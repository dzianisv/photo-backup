Method 1: SnapDisk on the five (the one-tap route)

On the five phones that clear Android 7.0, the automated route exists. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and all five clear it — the S41 runs 7.0 exactly, the S61 runs 8.0, the S62 Pro runs 10, the S53 runs 11, the S75 runs 12 — and all five list USB OTG:

  1. Install SnapDisk. Until the Play listing is live, install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on the S41, S61, S62 Pro, S53 and S75. It does not install on the S40, S50 or S60, which run Android 5.1, 4.4 and 6.0.
  2. Plug in the USB drive. On the S41 that is a micro-USB OTG adapter — the adapter was in the box when the phone was new. On the S61, S62 Pro, S53 and S75 a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and a regular USB drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter. Buy the adapter with the OTG wiring inside it, not a plain sync cable.
  3. Tap Backup in the notification. SnapDisk offers the backup when the drive connects, then copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  4. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. Its limits: it works only on the five phones that clear Android 7.0 — not on the S40, S50 or S60, where no backup app installs at all — and it needs a drive the phone can host, which all five list. On the S40, S50 and S60, Methods 2, 3 and 4 are the routes.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter (works on all eight)

This is the stock route, and the only phone route on the S40, S50 and S60. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive, and OTG is listed on all eight covered models:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with the right OTG adapter. On the S40, S50, S60 and S41 that is a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain micro-USB sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. On the S61, S62 Pro, S53 and S75 a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and a regular USB drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive.
  2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the stock file manager on a Bullitt/CAT phone, so use Files by Google or the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit. One S50-only warning: GSMArena, phonesdata and eIMEI24 list OTG or USB Host for the S50, but phonemore lists only microUSB 2.0 — so on the S50 the drive mounting is the test.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On these phones the photos are the 13 MP shots from the rear camera on the S60 and the S41 — 16 MP on the S61, 12 MP dual on the S62 Pro, 8 MP on the S40 and the S50 — plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on the five newer phones: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. On the S40, S50 and S60 there is no app route at all, because none of the three can run SnapDisk — so this manual copy, the computer copy, and the card-reader route are the only routes on those three.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the Bullitt phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. The S60's own manual describes exactly this: transfer media to a PC in Media Device MTP mode, connect the phone to the PC with the USB cable, touch File transfers to activate MTP mode, and copy the desired files. This works on every covered model, with no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. It is also the fallback for the S40, S50 and S60, which cannot run SnapDisk at all. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 1 or 2 is the route, or Method 4.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (works on every model)

Every covered Bullitt model takes a microSD card — up to 512 GB on the S53, up to 256 GB on the S41, S61 and S62 Pro, up to 128 GB or 256 GB on the S60 depending on the source, and up to 64 GB on the S40 and S50 — and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old phone manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. Two warnings: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in the file manager. And on the S53 the card shares the SIM slot, so a card and a second SIM compete for the same tray — on the S61 and the S62 Pro the slot is disputed, shared per GSMArena and smartprix and dedicated per phonemore and the CAT guide — so check the tray on your unit before assuming you can run two SIMs and a card at once.

Method 5: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

Bullitt offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — the per-model backup guides all point to a computer copy, Google Drive, or Google Photos — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead; and its "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p). 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and Google accounts only offer 5 GB of free storage by default since May 2026, with the full 15 GB available only after verifying a phone number on the account. 3. Google backup is what Android itself names: personal settings back up to Google's servers with a Google account, and are restored to a new phone when you sign in with that account. It is a real backup — but it backs up settings, not a folder of photo files on a drive you hold. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. And one Bullitt-only limit: no source found confirms the current Google Photos or Drive app still installs on the S40, S50 or S60. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2, 3 or 4 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What this does not do

A few honest limits, so the manual copy is not oversold:

  • No automated backup exists for the S40, S50 or S60 at all. All three run below SnapDisk's Android 7.0 minimum — 5.1, 4.4 and 6.0 — and below VaultDrive's Android 8 or later per its own site. The file-manager copy, the computer copy and the card-reader route are manual, one batch at a time, with no progress detail, no resume, and no verification.
  • No backup app exists for a Bullitt phone that cannot host a drive — but every covered model lists OTG. Unlike Plum, where two phones had unconfirmed OTG, all eight Bullitt models in this guide list OTG or USB Host — so the file-manager route exists on all eight, and the only blocker on the S40, S50 and S60 is the Android version, not the port.
  • Photos and videos only, from the folder you pick. By default that's DCIM — your camera photos. Photos in other folders (Downloads, app folders) only copy if you select them too, and photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the microSD card are not on the card for the Method 4 route.
  • Files over 4 GB on FAT32 are a problem. Very long videos on a FAT32 drive will not copy — copy those to a computer instead, or reformat the drive to exFAT, noting that a phone this old may not read exFAT, so FAT32 is the safer format for the phone route.
  • It never deletes. The file manager's Copy only copies. Freeing space on the device is copy, check the copy on the drive, then delete the originals yourself — never the other way round.
  • OTG is listed on all eight, but the S50's listing is two-sided. GSMArena, phonesdata and eIMEI24 list OTG or USB Host for the S50, while phonemore lists only microUSB 2.0 — so on the S50 the drive mounting is the test, and the computer copy in Method 3 is the reliable route.
  • The S61's Android version is two numbers in the sources found. GSMArena says Android 8.0 Oreo, upgradable to 9.0 Pie; the manual says Android 8.1. Either way it clears SnapDisk's 7.0 minimum — the number matters only if you are sizing VaultDrive, which needs Android 8 or later per its own site either way.
  • The S61's and S62 Pro's microSD slots are disputed. GSMArena says the S61's slot uses the shared SIM slot; phonemore says it is a dedicated slot. The CAT guide says the S62 Pro's slot is dedicated; smartprix and urbanglobal say it is a hybrid/shared slot. Check the tray on your unit before assuming you can run two SIMs and a card at once — and on the S53 there is no dispute, the slot uses the shared SIM slot.
  • The S60's and S41's microSD ceilings are two numbers each. The S60 is up to 128 GB per gadgets360 and up to 256 GB per phonemore; the S41 is up to 256 GB per Notebookcheck and Android Headlines and up to 2 TB per the woot listing. Check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 128 GB for the S60 or 256 GB for the S41.
  • Only one model has a sourced microSD ceiling of 512 GB. The S53 takes microSDXC up to 512 GB in the shared SIM slot; the S41, S61 and S62 Pro take up to 256 GB; the S60 takes up to 128 GB or 256 GB depending on the source; the S40 and the S50 take up to 64 GB. Check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 64 GB for an S40 or S50.
  • No source found names the stock file manager on a Bullitt/CAT phone. No source found names one, so Method 2 uses Files by Google or the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit.
  • A microSD card is a second copy, not a USB drive. All eight covered models take microSD, so a card is a second local copy you can count on here. But a card is not a USB drive: it does not plug into any computer on its own, and this article is about the drive — the card only becomes a backup once its photos are copied onto one.
  • If yours is a Bullitt phone that is not a CAT S-series, the route is different. This guide covers the CAT S40, S50, S60, S41, S61, S62 Pro, S53 and S75 only. If yours is a Land Rover Explore or a Motorola Defy, both also made by Bullitt, see the Land Rover Explore guide and the Motorola guide instead — and if yours is a different Bullitt model, check Settings > About phone for its Android version first: anything below Android 7.0 cannot run SnapDisk at all, and the computer copy in Method 3 is the reliable route.
  • Newer does not mean a newer Android version on your unit. Bullitt shut down on 26 January 2024, so no Bullitt phone gets updates any more — the S41 stays on Android 7.0, the S60 stays on 6.0, and a used S62 Pro may still be on Android 10 if its owner never took the upgrade to 11. Check Settings > About phone on your unit before assuming any version number.
  • The IP68 and IP69 ratings are old seals. The S50 is IP67, the S60 and the S41 are IP68, the S62 Pro is IP68/IP69 — but the seals on phones from 2014–2023 are years old. Do not test water resistance with a phone whose photos you have not backed up yet.

What to avoid

  • Assuming SnapDisk backs up to a drive on every Bullitt phone. SnapDisk installs on the S41, S61, S62 Pro, S53 and S75 — but it does not install at all on the S40, S50 or S60. Do not buy Pro for an S40, S50 or S60 expecting a drive backup, and do not trust a store listing that says "compatible" without checking the Android version first.
  • Assuming the S60 runs Android 7.0 or later. The S60 runs Android 6.0 Marshmallow, and Bullitt is gone, so no update is coming. Check Settings > About phone on your unit before planning anything around the app.
  • Assuming OTG because the phone is old and rugged. OTG is listed on all eight covered models — but on the S50 the listing is two-sided, GSMArena and phonesdata say OTG while phonemore says only microUSB 2.0. Plug in a drive and look for the mount notification before buying an adapter.
  • Buying the wrong adapter for the port. The S40, S50, S60 and S41 take micro-USB 2.0; the S61, S62 Pro, S53 and S75 take USB-C. A USB-C adapter does not fit a micro-USB port, and a micro-USB adapter does not fit a USB-C port — buy the adapter that matches your model's port, with the OTG wiring inside it.
  • Assuming the S61 or S62 Pro takes two SIMs and a card at once. The S61's slot is a shared SIM slot per GSMArena and a dedicated slot per phonemore; the S62 Pro's is dedicated per the CAT guide and hybrid/shared per smartprix and urbanglobal. Check the tray on your unit before buying a second SIM or a card.
  • Assuming the cloud still works on Android 4.4, 5.1 or 6.0. No source found confirms the current Google Photos or Drive app still installs on the S40, S50 or S60. If the cloud app is not already on your phone and working, do not plan a backup around installing it now.
  • Assuming your photos are on the microSD card. On these phones the camera may save to internal storage or to the card depending on the setting. If you pull the card for Method 4 without checking, you may copy an empty DCIM folder while the photos sit in internal storage. Check where the photos live first.
  • Assuming the S60's or S41's microSD ceiling or the S61's Android version. The S60 is up to 128 GB per gadgets360 and up to 256 GB per phonemore; the S41 is up to 256 GB per Notebookcheck and up to 2 TB per the woot listing; the S61 is Android 8.0 per GSMArena and 8.1 per its manual. Check Settings > About phone and the card slot on your unit before sizing anything around any of these numbers.
  • Assuming Bullitt has a backup app. No Bullitt backup app to a USB drive turned up in any source. The one-tap route on the five newer phones is SnapDisk, not a Bullitt app — and on the S40, S50 and S60 there is no app route at all.
  • Assuming an old IP68 or IP69 rating still holds. The S50 is IP67, the S60 and the S41 are IP68, the S62 Pro is IP68/IP69 — but the seals on phones from 2014–2023 are years old. Do not test water resistance with a phone whose photos you have not backed up yet.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the device — never the other way round. The file manager only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. A file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the device. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish before unplugging, and eject the drive from the notification or Settings > Storage first.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your device up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1, 2, 3 or 4, not a cloud backup. And Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and since May 2026 Google accounts get only 5 GB free by default, with the full 15 GB only after verifying a phone number. On the S40, S50 and S60 the cloud apps may not even install any more.
  • Mistaking a generic guide for a Bullitt guide. The thecomputerbasics transfer guide, the topselect USB-C drive guide and the itechguides memory-stick guide are real documents — but they are generic phone guides, and the maletphoto guide is about iPhones. None of them describes backing up a Bullitt phone's camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone. The generic Android guides in the search results are the closest thing to a phone-to-USB guide, and they say nothing about Bullitt.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the device and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy or a card reader is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On a Bullitt phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and on the S40, S50 and S60 those apps may not install at all any more.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip. On the S41, S61, S62 Pro, S53 or S75, SnapDisk picks them for you; on the S40, S50 or S60, no app installs at all, so copy the whole DCIM folder in one go with the file manager and an OTG adapter, check it on the drive, then delete from the device — or use the computer copy or the card reader, which work on every model.
