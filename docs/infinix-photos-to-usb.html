And Infinix's own backup tools back your photos up to the cloud or to a PC, not to a USB drive as plain files. A USB-C flash drive plugs straight into a modern Infinix. What you need

An Infinix phone (Android 7.0 or later for SnapDisk) that supports OTG — check your model's specs before buying anything; a USB drive your phone can read — a USB-C flash drive plugs straight into a modern Infinix, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter; older Infinix phones with a Micro-USB port need a Micro-USB OTG adapter instead; and enough space on the drive for the photos you want to keep. Three Infinix specifics: first, OTG is not a given. Infinix phones run XOS — Infinix's Android skin — and the drive mounts on plug-in on models that support OTG: look for a "USB storage added" or "USB drive connected" notification. One guide reports an OTG toggle at Settings > More Connections on the Zero 30, and that Infinix turns OTG off after 10 minutes with nothing connected — but that report is AI-generated and unverified, and it warns itself that firmware versions can move the toggle, so treat the path as a lead to check, not a fact. If the phone ignores the drive, check Settings > Storage. And not every Infinix phone has OTG at all — the Smart 5 doesn't, while the Note 12 G96, Zero 30 5G, and Hot 70 do — so for any other model check its specs, and don't assume a missing toggle means a broken phone. Second, Infinix's stock file manager is File Manager, which handles the phone's internal storage and connected external storage, and the stock gallery is AI Gallery — and Infinix camera photos live in DCIM/Camera — and whether your model has a microSD slot varies by model (the Hot 70 has a dedicated microSD slot), so check your own model's specs rather than assuming one either way; a USB drive is the plug-in storage that works on every OTG model. Third, Infinix phones ship with Google Play, so SnapDisk installs from the v0.1.2 APK on the GitHub release page today and from Play once the listing is live — there is no sideload-only situation here. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 can't hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit.

Method 2: File Manager (the stock route, no app needed)

Infinix phones ship with File Manager as the stock file manager, and it can copy your photos to the USB drive: 1. Plug in the USB drive — a notification should appear saying "USB storage added"; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage. 2. Open File Manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under external storage. 3. Go back to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. 4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). 5. Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until File Manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos. This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason SnapDisk exists: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. For a library that grows every day, the app's oldest-first background copy is the calmer option.

Method 3: Infinix's own tools (Google backup, XCloud, or a PC — not phone-to-USB)

If you want Infinix's own route, know what each tool actually does — none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive. Google backup is the route Infinix's guides point to first: Settings > Google > Backup, Back up to Google Drive on — it backs the phone up to your Google account so you can restore app settings and data to a new phone. It is a real backup — but the backup lives in Google's cloud and restores to a phone, not a folder of photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. XCloud is Infinix's own cloud backup, on phones that have it: Settings > System > Backup & Reset, Local Backup or XCloud Backup — it backs contacts, messages, photos, and settings up to your Infinix account. It is a real backup too — but it lives on Infinix's servers, needs an Infinix account and a connection, and is not on every model (one guide says newer phones with XOS above 7 usually have it), and it still doesn't give you a folder of photo files on a USB drive. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to Google's cloud, with 15 GB free with every Google account — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead. Coolmuster Android Backup Manager is the PC route the Infinix guides offer: a Windows tool that backs the whole phone up to a computer in one click — but it needs the computer, a USB cable, and USB debugging on. And the SD-card route copies DCIM to a memory card with File Manager — a real local copy, but it stays on a card in the phone (or needs a card reader to open anywhere else), not a USB drive you can plug into any computer. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1 or 2 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 17, 2026.

What this does not do

A few honest limits, so the automatic copy is not oversold:

  • Photos and videos only. SnapDisk copies image and video files. Documents, voice notes, and anything that isn't a photo or video is skipped — Google backup is the tool if you want contacts, messages, and settings in the same backup, not just photos.
  • Only the folder you pick. By default that's DCIM — your camera photos. Photos in other folders (Downloads, app folders) only copy if you point the From folder at them, or at a parent folder that contains them.
  • Free 5 GB per run. A library bigger than 5 GB stops at the cap; run it again and it picks up where it stopped. Pro ($9.99, one-time) removes the limit and starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk.
  • Files over 4 GB on FAT32 are skipped. Very long videos on a FAT32 drive are listed with their reason instead of failing the backup. Reformat the drive to exFAT if you need them.
  • It never deletes. SnapDisk only copies. Freeing space on the phone is copy, check the copy on the drive, then delete the originals yourself — never the other way round.
  • No OTG, no USB backup. On a model without OTG — the Smart 5 is the confirmed one — a USB drive simply never appears, and no app can change that. The cloud routes (Google Photos, XCloud) are the backup for those phones.

What to avoid

  • Buying an adapter before checking OTG. Not every Infinix phone has OTG — the Smart 5 doesn't, and no adapter or setting can enable it. Check your own model's specs first, or the drive will never appear no matter what you plug in.
  • Hunting for an OTG switch that may not exist. One guide reports a toggle at Settings > More Connections on the Zero 30, but that report is AI-generated and unverified, and XOS mounts the drive on plug-in with a "USB storage added" notification on models that support OTG. If your Infinix phone ignores a drive, check Settings > Storage — but a missing switch is not proof the phone is broken; some models simply have no OTG.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google backup, XCloud, and Google Photos back your phone up to Google's or Infinix's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1 or 2, not a cloud backup.
  • Mistaking a PC tool for a phone-to-USB copy. Coolmuster backs the phone up to a computer — it needs the PC, a cable, and USB debugging. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route, not a desktop tool.
  • Mistaking an SD-card copy for a USB backup. Copying DCIM to a memory card is a real local copy — but the card stays in the phone, and opening it anywhere else needs a card reader. A USB drive plugs into any computer as it is.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 17, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On Infinix the cloud routes are Google Photos, Google One, and XCloud — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. The file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the phone. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip. Automate the copy, check it on the drive, then delete from the phone.
