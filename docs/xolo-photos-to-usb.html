Method 1: SnapDisk on the four (the one-tap route, unproven)

On the four phones that clear Android 7.0 and might host a drive, the automated route may exist — but on none of them do the sources agree it does. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later — the ZX, Era 5X and Era 4X run 8.1, and the Era 3X, Era 3 and Era 2V run 7.0 exactly — and the one-tap backup needs the phone to host a drive. The test, on the ZX, Era 5X, Era 3X and Era 2V, is whether a drive mounts:

  1. Install SnapDisk. Until the Play listing is live, install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on the six phones that clear 7.0 — the ZX, Era 5X, Era 4X, Era 3X, Era 3 and Era 2V — but the one-tap backup to a drive only works where the phone hosts a drive: the Era 5X, Era 3X and Era 2V have disputed OTG, the ZX has no OTG stated, and the Era 4X and Era 3 list OTG No. It does not install on the other eight: they run Android 6.0 or older, below the minimum, so Method 3 or 4 is the route on those.
  2. Plug in the USB drive with a micro-USB OTG adapter. All four take micro-USB, so a regular USB drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A USB-C flash drive does not plug straight into any of them.
  3. Look for the mount notification, then tap Backup in the notification. If the drive mounts, SnapDisk offers the backup when the drive connects, then copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. If nothing mounts, the phone does not host the drive — the sources disagree on all four, so a no-mount settles it for your unit, and Method 3 or 4 is the route. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  4. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. Its limits: it works only on phones that run Android 7.0 and host a drive — and on no covered Xolo phone do the sources agree both are true. On the Era 4X and Era 3 it cannot work at all — both list OTG No — and on the eight below Android 7.0, only Methods 3 and 4 exist.

The copies are the original files. SnapDisk streams each file byte-for-byte from the device to the drive — the code reads the file and writes the same bytes out, with no decode, no recompression, and no resize — and then checks that the copy on the drive is the same size as the original. Other apps copy originals byte-for-byte too; what SnapDisk adds is the automated whole-library run: oldest-first order, progress with Pause and Stop, resume where it stopped, and skip-if-already-copied on the next run. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter (works on the four, if the drive mounts)

This is the stock route, and the only phone route on the four besides SnapDisk. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive, and OTG is disputed or unstated on all four:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with a micro-USB OTG adapter. All four take micro-USB, so a regular USB drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive — and if the drive never shows up, the phone does not host it, and Method 3 or 4 is the route.
  2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the stock file manager on a Xolo phone, so use Files by Google or the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On these phones the photos are the 13 MP shots from the rear cameras on the ZX, Era 5X and Era 3X and the 8 MP shots on the Era 2V, plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy, if the drive mounts at all. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on the four phones: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. And on the other ten it does not work at all as a phone route — the Era 4X and Era 3 list OTG No, and the eight below Android 7.0 cannot run a modern file-manager backup flow to a drive without an app that installs on them.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the Xolo phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no Android-version question and no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. It is also the only phone-to-drive route for the eight models below Android 7.0, which run Android 6.0 or older and cannot run SnapDisk at all, and for the two that list OTG No — the Era 4X and Era 3 — and for the four where the drive mounting is the test, if the drive never mounts. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 1 or 2 is the route on the four, or Method 4 on any of the fourteen.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (works on all fourteen)

All fourteen covered Xolo models take a microSD card — up to 256 GB on the ZX, up to 128 GB per gadgets360 and up to 256 GB per smartprix and giznext on the Era 5X, up to 64 GB on the Era 4X, up to 32 GB per gadgets360 and smartprix and up to 64 GB per gadgetsnow and 91mobiles on the Era 3X, Era 3 and Era 2V, and up to 32 GB on the other eight — and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old phone manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. Two warnings: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in the file manager. And the ceilings are disputed on four models — the Era 5X's slot is up to 128 GB per gadgets360 and up to 256 GB per smartprix and giznext, and the Era 3X's, Era 3's and Era 2V's slots are up to 32 GB per gadgets360 and smartprix and up to 64 GB per gadgetsnow and 91mobiles — so check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 32 GB for any of those four. Unlike Karbonn, where the Hexa takes no card at all, every covered Xolo phone takes one — so this route exists on all fourteen.

Method 5: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

Xolo offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no Xolo-specific backup advice turned up in any source beyond a computer copy, Google Drive, or Google Photos — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead; and its "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p). 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and Google accounts only offer 5 GB of free storage by default since May 2026, with the full 15 GB available only after verifying a phone number on the account. 3. Google backup is what Android itself names: personal settings back up to Google's servers with a Google account, and are restored to a new phone when you sign in with that account. It is a real backup — but it backs up settings, not a folder of photo files on a drive you hold. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2, 3 or 4 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What to avoid

  • Buying a USB-C OTG adapter for a Xolo phone. All fourteen covered models take micro-USB — there is no USB-C model in this guide — so a USB-C adapter does not fit any of them. Buy a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it.
  • Assuming the Era 5X's, Era 3X's or Era 2V's OTG listing is settled. Mobilegizmo and smartprix say OTG Yes and Hindustan Times says Yes for the 3X, but gadgets360's comparison tables say USB OTG No for all three. Plug a drive in and look for the mount notification before counting on the one-tap route on any of them.
  • Assuming the ZX's 9.0 listing means FotoSafe runs on it. Hindustan Times lists Android 9.0 for the ZX, but gadgets360, smartprix, themobileindian and umatechnology all say 8.1 — and FotoSafe needs Android 9 or later, so on the weight of the sources it does not run on the ZX either.
  • Assuming the Era 4G's Marshmallow update or the Era X's planned upgrade fixes the backup. The Era 4G got Android 6.0 per the Indian Express, and the Era X lists a planned upgrade to 6.0 — but 6.0 is still below SnapDisk's 7.0 minimum, so the upgrades change nothing about the backup.
  • Assuming the Q3000's OTG listing is settled. Gizbot, geeky-gadgets and umatechnology list USB OTG, but phonebunch leaves the OTG line blank. Either way the Q3000 runs Android 4.2 with a 4.4.2 update and cannot run SnapDisk, so the dispute is not a reason to buy an adapter for it.
  • Assuming the Era 2X's or Q1010's OTG listing is settled. Phonebunch lists OTG Supported for the Era 2X and OTG Yes for the Q1010, but gadgets360's comparison table and spec page list USB OTG No for both. Either way both run below Android 7.0 and cannot run SnapDisk, so the disputes are not a reason to buy an adapter for either of them.
  • Assuming the Era 3X's, Era 3's or Era 2V's 64 GB ceiling is settled. Gadgetsnow's review and 91mobiles say up to 64 GB, gadgets360 and smartprix say up to 32 GB. Check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 32 GB for any of those three.
  • Assuming VaultDrive or FotoSafe runs on an Android 7.0 Xolo. VaultDrive needs Android 8 or later per its own site, and FotoSafe needs Android 9 — so neither runs on the Era 3X, Era 3 or Era 2V, and FotoSafe runs on none of the fourteen, since the newest covered models run Android 8.1.
  • Mistaking a spec sheet or a product page for a phone-to-USB guide. The gadgets360 and smartprix listings are real spec pages — but they only list an Android version, a processor, a camera, or a USB port type. None of them describes copying your camera roll straight to a USB drive from the device.
  • Mistaking a drive-locked app for a general backup. Photo Backup Stick DC is a real one-tap backup app — but it only works with genuine Photo Backup Stick OTG drives. If the drive you already own is any other brand, that app cannot use it: SnapDisk, Photos to USB, and FotoSafe work with any drive.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the device and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026 — and on the eight models below Android 7.0, the two that list OTG No, and the four where the drive mounting is the test, that route may not exist, so the honest answer is that a computer may be needed.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On a Xolo phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the device — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. The file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the device. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip. Automate the copy, check it on the drive, then delete from the phone.
