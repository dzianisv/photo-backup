Method 1: SnapDisk on the one (the one-tap route)

On the one phone that clears Android 7.0 and lists USB OTG, the automated route exists. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later — the Cool 3 runs 9 — and the one-tap backup needs the phone to host a drive, which the Cool 3 lists. The other five phones that clear 7.0 — the Note 6, Note 8, Mega 5, Mega 5A and Cool Play 6 — have OTG half-stated or disputed, so on those the drive mounting is the test:

  1. Install SnapDisk. Until the Play listing is live, install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on the six phones that clear 7.0 — the Cool 3, Note 6, Note 8, Mega 5, Mega 5A and Cool Play 6 — and the one-tap backup to a drive works on the one that lists USB OTG, the Cool 3. On the other five, SnapDisk installs but the one-tap route is unproven until a drive mounts. It does not install on the other nine: they run Android 6.0 or older, below the minimum, so Method 3 or 4 is the route on those.
  2. Plug in the USB drive with a micro-USB OTG adapter. The Cool 3 takes micro-USB, so a regular USB drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A USB-C flash drive does not plug straight into it.
  3. Look for the mount notification, then tap Backup in the notification. If the drive mounts, SnapDisk offers the backup when the drive connects, then copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. On the nine below Android 7.0, only Methods 3 and 4 exist.

The copies are the original files. SnapDisk streams each file byte-for-byte from the device to the drive — the code reads the file and writes the same bytes out, with no decode, no recompression, and no resize — and then checks that the copy on the drive is the same size as the original. Other apps copy originals byte-for-byte too; what SnapDisk adds is the automated whole-library run: oldest-first order, progress with Pause and Stop, resume where it stopped, and skip-if-already-copied on the next run. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device.

Method 5: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

Coolpad offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no Coolpad-specific backup advice turned up in any source beyond a computer copy, Google Drive, or Google Photos — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open.
