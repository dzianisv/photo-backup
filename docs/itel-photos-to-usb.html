  • Cloud routes — Google Photos syncs your gallery to Google's servers, Google Drive backs your data up to Google's cloud, and Google backup restores your data to a new phone from Google's cloud, not to a drive you hold; Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p), and since May 2026 Google accounts get 5 GB free by default, with the full 15 GB available only after verifying a phone number on the account — so a cloud backup stops working the day you stop paying for storage. Neither route gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive. itel offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no itel-specific backup advice turned up in any source beyond a computer copy, Google Drive, or Google Photos — so there is no itel cloud route to compare either.

None of them is an itel-specific phone-to-USB photo-backup guide, and none of them offers an automated itel photo-library backup: nothing that takes the entire camera roll, copies it oldest-first in the background as plain files you can open anywhere, shows progress, pauses and resumes, and verifies every file. That is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026 — with the honest finding that the automated part exists on sixteen itel phones, the drive mounting is the test on one more, and one phone cannot host a drive at all.

Method 1: SnapDisk on the sixteen (the one-tap route)

On the sixteen phones that clear Android 7.0 and list USB OTG, the automated route exists. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and all sixteen clear it — the A44, A44 Pro, A44 Air and A44 Power run 7.0, 7.0, 8.1 Go and 8.1 Go, the A50 runs 14 Go, the A60 runs 11 or 12 Go, the A70 runs 13 Go, the A80 runs 14 Go, the S23 runs 12, the S24 runs 13, the S25 and S25 Ultra run 14, the P40 runs 12 Go, the P55 and P55 5G run 13, and the P65 runs 14 — and all sixteen list USB OTG:

  1. Install SnapDisk. Until the Play listing is live, install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on all sixteen — and on the A46 and the A48 too. It does not fail to install on any covered itel phone: every one of the eighteen clears Android 7.0.
  2. Plug in the USB drive. On the A50 (2024 model), A70, A80, S23, S24, S25, S25 Ultra, P40, P55, P55 5G and P65 a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and a regular USB drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter. On the A44, A44 Pro, A44 Air and A44 Power a micro-USB OTG adapter is the connection — the adapter was not in the box on these models, so buy the adapter with the OTG wiring inside it, not a plain sync cable.
  3. Tap Backup in the notification. SnapDisk offers the backup when the drive connects, then copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  4. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. Its limits: it works only on the sixteen phones that clear Android 7.0 and list OTG — not on the A46, where the drive mounting is the test, and not on the A48, which cannot host a drive at all. On the A46, plug a drive in and look for the mount notification: if it mounts, Method 1 works; if it does not, Methods 3 and 4 are the routes. On the A50, A80 and P40 the sources disagree about OTG, so the same test applies before counting on the one-tap route. On the A48, Methods 3 and 4 are the routes.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter (works on the sixteen)

This is the stock route, and the only phone route on the sixteen OTG models besides SnapDisk. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive, and OTG is listed on sixteen covered models:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with the right OTG adapter. On the A50 (2024 model), A70, A80, S23, S24, S25, S25 Ultra, P40, P55, P55 5G and P65 a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and a regular USB drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter. On the A44, A44 Pro, A44 Air and A44 Power that is a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain micro-USB sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive.
  2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the stock file manager on an itel phone, so use Files by Google or the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit. On the A46 — and on the A50, A80 and P40, where the sources disagree — this step is the test: if the drive shows up, the file-manager route works; if it does not, the phone does not host drives, and Methods 3 and 4 are the routes.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On these phones the photos are the 5 MP shots from the rear camera on the A44 — 8 MP on the A50, A60 and A46, 13 MP on the A70, 50 MP on the A80, S23, S25 Ultra, P55 and P65, 108 MP on the S24 — plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on the sixteen OTG phones: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. On the A48 there is no phone route at all, because it cannot host a drive — so the computer copy and the card-reader route are the only routes on that one.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the itel phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. It is also the fallback for the A48, which cannot host a drive, for the A46 if the drive does not mount, and for the A50, A80 and P40 if their OTG turns out to be No on your unit. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 1 or 2 is the route, or Method 4.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (works on every model with a card)

Almost every covered itel model takes a microSD card — up to 2 TB on the A70 per two sources, up to 1 TB on the S23, P55, P55 5G and P65, up to 512 GB on the P40 per two sources, up to 256 GB on the S25, up to 128 GB on the A46 and A48, and up to 32 GB on the A44 family — and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old phone manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. Two warnings: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in the file manager. And on the S25 Ultra the card slot itself is disputed — a dedicated slot per mobilo, no slot at all per smartprix and nanoreview — so check the tray on your unit before buying a card for that model.

Method 5: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

itel offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no itel-specific backup advice turned up in any source beyond a computer copy, Google Drive, or Google Photos — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead; and its "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p). 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and Google accounts only offer 5 GB of free storage by default since May 2026, with the full 15 GB available only after verifying a phone number on the account. 3. Google backup is what Android itself names: personal settings back up to Google's servers with a Google account, and are restored to a new phone when you sign in with that account. It is a real backup — but it backs up settings, not a folder of photo files on a drive you hold. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2, 3 or 4 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What to avoid

  • Assuming SnapDisk backs up to a drive on every itel phone. SnapDisk installs on all eighteen covered models — but the one-tap backup to a drive works only on the sixteen that list OTG. Do not buy Pro for an A48 expecting a drive backup, and do not trust a store listing that says "compatible" without checking the Android version first.
  • Assuming the A48 hosts a drive because it runs Android 10. It clears the Android minimum and can install SnapDisk — but whatmobile lists OTG No for it. Check the OTG line, not the Android version, before planning anything around the phone route.
  • Assuming OTG because the phone is new and the port is USB-C. OTG is listed on the A50, A70, A80, S23, S24, S25, S25 Ultra, P40, P55, P55 5G and P65 — but the A46 lists only microUSB 2.0 with no OTG line, and the A50, A80 and P40 each have a source saying OTG No. Plug in a drive and look for the mount notification before buying an adapter.
  • Buying the wrong adapter for the port. The A50 (2024 model), A70, A80, S23, S24, S25, S25 Ultra, P40, P55, P55 5G and P65 take USB-C; the A44, A44 Pro, A44 Air, A44 Power, A46, A48 and A60 take micro-USB 2.0. A USB-C adapter does not fit a micro-USB port, and a micro-USB adapter does not fit a USB-C port — buy the adapter that matches your model's port, with the OTG wiring inside it. On the A50, A80 and P40, check the port on your unit first, because the sources disagree.
  • Assuming the A50 in a spec table is your A50. Two phones share the name: the 2024 A50 runs Android 14 Go on a T603 with USB-C, while older listings describe an A50 on a SC9832E running Android 11 or 12 Go with 32 GB and microUSB. Check Settings > About phone on your unit before trusting any single spec table.
  • Assuming the S25 Ultra takes a microSD card. Mobilo says a dedicated slot; smartprix and nanoreview say no card slot at all. Check the tray on your unit before buying a card for that model.
  • Assuming your photos are on the microSD card. On these phones the camera may save to internal storage or to the card depending on the setting. If you pull the card for Method 4 without checking, you may copy an empty DCIM folder while the photos sit in internal storage. Check where the photos live first.
  • Assuming the A60's Android version or the A50's, A70's, S23's or P40's microSD ceiling. The A60 is Android 12 Go per beebom and Android 11 Go per mobileinto; the A50 is up to 512 GB per pricehunt, up to 2 TB per mobility, up to 1 TB per jkphones and up to 32 GB per gadgets360; the A70 is up to 2 TB per mobiledokan and 91mobiles and up to 1 TB per phonesstorekenya; the S23 is up to 1 TB per mysmartprice, up to 512 GB per cellularkenya and up to 256 GB per phonesstorekenya; the P40 is up to 512 GB per gadgets360 and 91mobiles and up to 128 GB per naijatechguide. Check Settings > About phone and the card slot on your unit before sizing anything around any of these numbers.
  • Assuming itel has a backup app. No itel backup app to a USB drive turned up in any source. The one-tap route on the sixteen OTG phones is SnapDisk, not an itel app — and on the A48 there is no app route at all.
  • Assuming a Go edition phone is too weak to run the app. The A44 Air, A44 Power, A48, A50, A60 and A70 run Android Go, and all six can install SnapDisk — the blocker on the A48 is the missing OTG, not the Go edition.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the device — never the other way round. The file manager only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. A file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the device. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish before unplugging, and eject the drive from the notification or Settings > Storage first.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your device up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1, 2, 3 or 4, not a cloud backup. And Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and since May 2026 Google accounts get only 5 GB free by default, with the full 15 GB only after verifying a phone number.
  • Mistaking a generic guide for an itel guide. The YouTube OTG video, the Dr.Fone guide, the thecomputerbasics guide and the thegadgetians guide are real documents — but they are generic Android guides. None of them describes backing up an itel phone's camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone. The generic Android guides in the search results are the closest thing to a phone-to-USB guide, and they say nothing about itel.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the device and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy or a card reader is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On an itel phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip.
