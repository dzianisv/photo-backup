A card bigger than the contested ceiling on any of the thirty-five contested phones without checking the phone first — the ceiling is two or three numbers on each of those thirty-five. A rival backup app expecting it to work on the shipped software of every covered phone — VaultDrive's AppBrain floor is Android 12.1, which thirty-eight clear on at least one listing, and FotoSafe's Android 9 floor is proven with OTG on sixty-seven.
  • What the drive must not be. An app for the phone — a backup app and a backup drive are two different things. Your old phone's card — a card that came out of another phone carries its photos, not yours. A drive with no space left — check the free space before you start, not halfway through.

Method 1: SnapDisk — proven on eighty-four

Method 1 is the one-tap version. It uses SnapDisk, the free backup app this site is about — SnapDisk. Free version: manual backup, up to 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase and adds auto-backup on connect plus unlimited size. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, which ninety-two covered UMIDIGI phones clear — the lowest clears it exactly, at 7.0 — so the app installs on ninety-two, and the one-tap backup is proven on eighty-four: each one lists USB OTG Yes.

  1. Check the Android version first. Settings > About phone > Android version. The S2 and the Z Pro run below 7.0 as shipped — SnapDisk will not install on those two, so stop here and use Method 3 or Method 4 instead. The Z Pro clears 7.0 after the immediate OTA it was offered at launch, so check for the update before giving up on it. The S2 Lite runs 7.0 only on the 7.0 listings — 6.0 per phonesdata — so check the version on the unit. Every other covered phone runs 7.0 or newer — the oldest clear it exactly, at 7.0, and the newest, the A75s and the A75x, run Android 16. If it says 6.0, you are on the S2 or the Z Pro — check the model name before buying a drive expecting the one-tap backup.
  2. Install SnapDisk. SnapDisk is not on Google Play yet — the listing is being prepared — so install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. On the phone, allow installing from unknown sources when asked. It needs Android 7.0 or later, which ninety-two covered UMIDIGI phones clear — the lowest clears it exactly, at 7.0 — so on a UMIDIGI phone the APK installs on ninety-two, and the one-tap backup is proven on eighty-four.
  3. Plug in the USB drive. On seventy-eight covered phones that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in — no adapter, no cable. On the eight micro-USB models that is a micro-USB OTG adapter between the phone and the drive. A plain sync cable is the wrong cable: it connects a phone to a computer, not a drive to a phone.
  4. Look for the mount notification. On the eighty-four proven phones Android notices the drive and offers it as storage, and SnapDisk picks it as the To folder. On the eight mounting-test phones — the A15, the Note 90, the A15 Pro 5G, the A15T, the A16 Pro, the A75s, the G100 5G and the G100x 5G — if nothing appears, check Settings > Storage: if the drive is listed there, tap it and mount it, then run the backup. If the drive never shows up on one of those eight, the phone does not host it, and Method 3 is the route.
  5. Check the copy, then delete the originals. Open the drive in Files and count: the backup is only done when every photo is on the drive and opens. Then delete the originals on the phone if you need the space — and only then.

SnapDisk copies the photos byte-for-byte: the file on the drive is the same file the phone took, with the same pixels. There is no re-encoding step, so nothing is lost in the copy — the only place quality gets lost is a cloud backup with a "Storage saver" setting.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter

Method 2 is the manual version of the same thing — no app, just the phone's file manager and a drive. The file manager on a UMIDIGI phone is Files, or Files by Google, per devicesfaq — so this method names it directly. It works on the eighty-four proven phones, and on the eight mounting-test phones if the drive shows up in Settings.

  1. Plug the drive into the phone. On seventy-eight covered phones that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in. On the eight micro-USB models that is a micro-USB OTG adapter between the phone and the drive.
  2. Open Files and find the DCIM folder. Your camera photos live in the DCIM folder — on most phones that is Internal storage > DCIM > Camera. Screenshots live in Pictures > Screenshots, and WhatsApp photos in Android > media > com.whatsapp > WhatsApp > Media > WhatsApp Images.
  3. Select the photos and copy them to the drive. Long-press one photo, select the rest, tap Copy, navigate to the USB drive and tap Paste. Copy, not Move — see "What to avoid" below.
  4. Wait, then check. Copying takes time — thousands of photos can take half an hour or more. Open the drive in Files and count before you delete anything.

This is the same copy Method 1 does, done by hand. It copies the original files as they are — no re-encoding, no quality loss.

Method 3: through a computer

Method 3 is the fallback — and the one the per-model backup FAQ calls the most economical method. It works on all ninety-five, including the two below-7.0 phones, the eight mounting-test phones if the drive never shows up on the phone, and the S2 Lite if the unit runs 6.0. Plug the phone into a computer with the USB cable, open the phone as a drive, and drag the DCIM folder onto a USB drive. Copy, not Move, and check the copy before deleting anything. It works — it just takes the manual step Method 1 does for you.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route

Method 4 is the card route, and on UMIDIGI it is a real one at full scale: a microSD card fits all ninety-five phones for certain, so this route is proven on all ninety-five — unlike Vernee, where it is proven on twenty of twenty-one, and unlike RedMagic, where it is proven on none of the fourteen.

  1. Put the card in the phone. Card ceilings are contested or unverified on thirty-five phones — so buy at or under the lower number if you want the ceiling every source agrees on. The worst are the X (128, 256 or 500 GB), the A7S (64, 128 or 256 GB) and the G5 Mecha (256 GB or 1 TB).
  2. Set the camera to save to the card. Open the Camera app, go to Settings, and set Storage to the SD card. New photos then land on the card, not the internal storage.
  3. Move the photos already on the phone. Open Files, select the DCIM folder, and move it to the card. The photos already on the phone do not move themselves — they live in internal storage until you move them.
  4. Take the card out and read it in a card reader. Plug the reader into the computer, or into the phone with an OTG adapter, and copy the photos to a USB drive.

Two warnings: the photos already on the phone live in internal storage, not on the card — so moving the card alone backs up nothing until you move them — and the card is only a backup if the photos end up on it, so check the card before you delete anything on the phone.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud

Method 5 is the cloud route — and the one UMIDIGI's per-model FAQ names for photos alone: Google Photos uploads them automatically when on Wi-Fi. It works on all ninety-five, it is free up to the Google account's 15 GB, and it is the only method here that backs up without a cable. But it is not a USB backup: the photos live on Google's servers, not on a drive you hold, and the "Storage saver" setting compresses them — so check that setting before trusting a cloud copy. If you want the originals on a drive you own, Method 1 or Method 2 is the route.

What this does not do

  • It does not install below Android 7.0. Two covered UMIDIGI phones run below Android 7.0 as shipped — the S2 and the Z Pro — so on those two there is no SnapDisk route at all as shipped (the Z Pro clears after its offered 7.0 OTA), and the S2 Lite clears only on the 7.0 listings; on a below-7.0 phone the only routes are Method 3 and Method 4. VaultDrive needs Android 8 and FotoSafe needs Android 9, so on a below-7.0 phone there is no rival-app route either.
  • It does not work if the phone does not host a drive. The eight mounting-test phones — the A15, the Note 90, the A15 Pro 5G, the A15T, the A16 Pro, the A75s, the G100 5G and the G100x 5G — may or may not mount a drive, because no source settles OTG on them. If the drive never shows up on one of those eight, Method 3 is the route.
  • It does not delete photos. A backup copies the photos to the drive; deleting the originals is a separate step, and only after you have checked the copy.
  • It does not back up your settings, contacts or messages. It backs up photos and videos — the files in your camera roll.
  • It does not back up photos from apps that hide them. Photos saved inside an app's private storage — WhatsApp's hidden media, or a locked album — may not be in the DCIM folder, so check the app's own export.
  • It does not compress or shrink your photos. The copy is the original file — the same pixels, the same size.

What to avoid

  • Don't move instead of copying. Move deletes the original as it goes — if the copy fails halfway, the photos are gone. Copy first, check the copy, then delete.
  • Don't unplug the drive until the copy is done. Unplugging mid-copy corrupts the files being written. Wait for the progress to finish, then eject the drive in Settings before pulling it out.
  • Don't buy a micro-USB adapter for a Type-C phone. Seventy-eight covered phones take USB Type-C — a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and the adapter is the wrong buy.
  • Don't buy a USB-C drive expecting it to plug into one of the eight micro-USB phones. Those eight are micro-USB, and a USB-C drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter between them.
  • Don't buy a card bigger than the contested ceiling without checking. Thirty-five phones have two or three card ceilings — so buy at or under the lower number if you want the ceiling every source agrees on.
  • Don't buy a card bigger than 128 GB for the X or the A7S without checking. The X's ceiling is three numbers (128, 256 and 500 GB) and the A7S's is three numbers (64, 128 and 256 GB) — 128 GB and 64 GB respectively are the only numbers every source agrees on for each.
  • Don't count on VaultDrive's AppBrain floor on every UMIDIGI phone. Its own site says Android 8, which eighty-three clear — but AppBrain lists Android 12.1, which thirty-eight clear on at least one listing, and five of those only on their higher listing.
  • Don't count on FotoSafe on shipped software on every UMIDIGI phone. It needs Android 9 with OTG — sixty-seven are proven, but the other twenty-eight are not.
  • Don't trust a single C2 listing. The C2 is two different phones — the 2017 C2 on Android 7.0 with micro-USB, and the 2023 C2 on Android 13 with USB-C. Check the year, not the name.
  • Don't trust a single A15, Note 90 or G100 5G listing either. The A15's OTG is Yes per one source and No per another, the Note 90's OTG is listed by some sources and not others, and the G100 5G's Android 15 is a blog mention, unverified as a shipped update.
  • Don't confuse the Note 100 with the Note 100 5G. The Note 100 5G is the February 2025 phone on the Unisoc T765 with a 1 TB card ceiling; the Note 100 4G is the October 2025 phone on the Unisoc T615 with an unverified ceiling. Same name, different phones.
  • Don't let one folder hold every photo. A backup is only a backup if the copy exists somewhere else — a drive, a card, or the cloud. One copy is not a backup.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a copy. A backup finds your photos, copies them all, and tells you when it's done.
