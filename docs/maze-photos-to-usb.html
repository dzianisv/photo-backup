What you need

  • A covered Maze phone. Which one matters less than it usually does here, because the line splits only two ways: three clear Android 7.0 on shipped software — the Alpha, the Alpha X and the Comet — and the one-tap backup is proven on all three outright. The Blade runs Android 6.0 as shipped, so SnapDisk does not install on it — but its USB OTG Yes listing means the file-manager copy in Method 2 still works on it.
  • A USB drive with enough free space — big enough for the whole photo library you want to copy, or enough room for the files you care about most. If the library is bigger than the drive, copy the year or the album you care about most first.
  • An adapter, or the right drive. Three take USB Type-C — the Alpha, the Alpha X and the Comet — so a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in on those three, and a regular USB-A drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter. One takes micro-USB — the Blade — so a regular USB-A drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter on it. A plain USB charging cable is not an OTG adapter — it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction.
  • Optional: a microSD card, for the card-reader route in Method 4. A card fits all four phones for certain — the Blade, the Alpha, the Alpha X and the Comet — so the card-reader route is proven on all four. Ceilings are stated on two — 256 GB on the Alpha and the Alpha X — and unstated on two — the Blade and the Comet — so check the card size against the phone before buying a large one.
  • What the drive must not be: if the drive has no free space, or the phone cannot see it at all, no method below works — and if you want to keep the backup, do not buy a drive with no space to grow into.
  • What you should not buy: do not buy a USB-C flash drive for a micro-USB phone, or a micro-USB adapter for a Type-C phone, and do not buy a cable and call it an adapter — a charging cable connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction.

Method 1: SnapDisk — proven on three

SnapDisk is the one-tap version. It needs Android 7.0 or later, which three covered Maze phones clear — and the one-tap backup is proven on all three of them: each one clears the floor and lists USB OTG Yes.

  1. Install SnapDisk v0.1.2 — it is not on Google Play yet, so install the APK from the GitHub release page. You do not need an account, and there are no ads.
  2. Plug the USB drive into the phone — straight in on a Type-C phone (the Alpha, the Alpha X and the Comet), through a USB-C OTG adapter for a regular USB-A drive.
  3. Open SnapDisk and tap Backup. SnapDisk copies photos and videos oldest-first from the phone to the drive, in the background, with live progress, Pause and Stop.
  4. Wait for the copy to finish, then open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

Free version: backs up manually, up to 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase — auto-backup on connect and unlimited size. On the Blade, SnapDisk does not install at all — it runs Android 6.0 — so use Method 2 or 4 instead.

Method 2: file manager + OTG adapter

Same plug, no app to install. On all four phones it works exactly as on any other Android phone with OTG support:

  1. Plug the USB drive into the phone — straight in on a Type-C phone, or through a micro-USB OTG adapter on the Blade.
  2. Open the phone's file manager and find the DCIM folder — that is where the camera photos live.
  3. Select the photos and videos, copy them to the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish.
  4. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

On the Blade the drive still mounts in the file manager even though SnapDisk does not install: the file-manager copy needs OTG, not Android 7.0 — and the Blade lists USB OTG Yes on phonesdata. This is the only method that works on all four phones with no app at all.

Method 3: through a computer

The fallback that works on all four covered phones, including the one no phone-to-USB method reaches with an app: the Blade, where SnapDisk does not install.

  1. Plug the phone into the computer with a USB cable — a plain charging cable works here, because the computer is the other end.
  2. On the phone, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer.
  3. On the computer, open the phone's DCIM folder and copy the photos to the computer, then to the USB drive.
  4. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

This is the method devicesfaq names as the most economical way to back up a Maze Blade — the computer copy, then Google Drive or Google Photos for the cloud. It needs the PC in between, so it is the fallback, not the route, when the phone and the drive are all you have.

Method 4: microSD card reader route

If the phone has a card slot — and all four do, for certain — the card is the backup, and the reader is the bridge to the USB drive:

  1. Put a microSD card in the phone and copy the DCIM folder to the card with the file manager.
  2. Take the card out, put it in a card reader, plug the reader into the computer, and copy the photos from the card to the USB drive.
  3. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

A microSD card fits all four phones for certain — the Blade, the Alpha, the Alpha X and the Comet — so the card-reader route is proven on all four. Ceilings are stated on two — 256 GB on the Alpha and the Alpha X — and unstated on two — the Blade and the Comet. On the Alpha X the card shares the second SIM slot, so the card means one SIM, not two. A card copy is a real local copy, but the card stays in the phone until you take it out, and opening it anywhere else needs the card reader — a USB drive plugs into any computer as it is.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud

The cloud is the last resort, and the one devicesfaq points to for Maze: the Blade FAQ names Google Drive as the default cloud service, and Google Photos is the photo version of the same idea. It works on all four phones — every one runs Android, so Google Photos runs on all four.

  1. Open Google Photos, turn on backup, and wait for the upload to finish — this needs Wi-Fi, and a big library takes a while.
  2. On a computer, open Google Photos, download the photos, and copy them to the USB drive.
  3. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

Two warnings. First, Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — check the setting before trusting a cloud copy. Second, a cloud backup is not a USB backup: your photos sit on Google's servers, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying for storage. If you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy — that is Method 1 or 2, not a cloud backup.

What this does not do

  • Does not install below Android 7.0. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and the Blade runs Android 6.0 as shipped, so SnapDisk does not install on it. No other Maze phone is below the floor.
  • Does not work if the phone does not host a drive. No Maze phone lists USB OTG No, and none needs the mounting test — every one of the four lists USB OTG Yes — so this limit does not bite on any Maze phone.
  • Does not delete photos. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Does not back up anything but photos and videos. SnapDisk copies image and video files only — no settings, no contacts, no messages, no app data.
  • Does not back up photos from apps that hide them. If an app keeps its photos in a private folder SnapDisk cannot read, those photos stay in the app.
  • Does not compress. SnapDisk copies the original files as they are — no recompression, no resizing, no quality loss.

What to avoid

  • Buying an adapter you don't need — or the wrong one. A USB-C flash drive plugs straight into three of the four phones — the Alpha, the Alpha X and the Comet — so an adapter is only needed for a regular USB-A drive, and it must be a USB-C OTG adapter on those three, a micro-USB OTG adapter on the Blade. A plain USB-C charging cable is not an OTG adapter: it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your phone up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1 or 2, not a cloud backup.
  • Mistaking a PC tool for a phone-to-USB copy. A computer copy with a USB cable moves files to a computer — but it is a copy to a computer, and the backup lands on the PC. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route, not a desktop copy.
  • Mistaking a spec table or a FAQ page for a phone-to-USB guide. The Maze spec pages are real Maze pages — but they only list an Android version, a chipset, or a USB connector type. None of them describes copying your camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone. The devicesfaq backup FAQ is the closest thing to Maze backup advice, and it names the computer copy.
  • Mistaking OTG support on one model for OTG support on the other. All four phones list USB OTG Yes — but that is this brand's luck, not the rule: on other brands in this series, most models do not list OTG at all. Do not assume the next brand's phones host a drive just because Maze's do.
  • Mistaking a microSD card copy for a USB backup. Copying DCIM to a microSD card is a real local copy — all four phones take a card — but the card stays in the phone, and opening it anywhere else needs a card reader; a USB drive plugs into any computer as it is.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 20, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On a Maze phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. The file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the phone. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.
  • Trusting one spec listing. The Comet is three numbers in three places — its battery at 4,000 vs 6,000 mAh, its storage at 64 vs 32 or 64 GB, its launch at September vs November 2017 — and the Alpha X's launch is two months — October vs November 2017 — so check the unit before buying a drive expecting a particular spec. The Android floor is settled on all four either way: three clear 7.0 and the Blade runs 6.0 on every source.

Rule of thumb: four phones, three clear Android 7.0, four list USB OTG Yes, four take a microSD card — the one-tap backup is proven on three, the file-manager copy works on all four, and the card-reader route is proven on all four.
