Method 1: SnapDisk — proven on none

SnapDisk is the one-tap version. It needs Android 7.0 or later, which twelve covered Doro phones clear — but the one-tap backup is proven on none of them: no source states USB OTG Yes for any Doro phone, so run the mounting test first.

  1. Install SnapDisk v0.1.2 — it is not on Google Play yet, so install the APK from the GitHub release page. You do not need an account, and there are no ads.
  2. Plug the USB drive into the phone — straight in if it is a USB-C drive and your phone is one of the ten Type-C models, or through a USB-C OTG adapter for a regular USB-A drive on those ten, or through a micro-USB OTG adapter on the 8035 and the 8040. On the five phones whose port is unstated and the Liberto 820, check the port before buying an adapter.
  3. Check the phone sees the drive before doing anything else. If nothing appears, the drive does not mount and neither this method nor Method 2 works on that phone.
  4. Open SnapDisk and tap Backup. SnapDisk copies photos and videos oldest-first from the phone to the drive, in the background, with live progress, Pause and Stop.
  5. Wait for the copy to finish, then open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

Free version: backs up manually, up to 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase — auto-backup on connect and unlimited size. On the twelve floor-clearing phones, SnapDisk installs — they clear Android 7.0 — but the one-tap backup is unproven until a drive mounts, so plug the drive in first and check the phone sees it. On the six below-floor phones, SnapDisk does not install at all, so use Method 3 or 4 instead.

Method 2: file manager + OTG adapter

Same plug, no app to install. On the twelve floor-clearing phones it works exactly as on any other Android phone with OTG support — once the drive mounts:

  1. Plug the USB drive into the phone through the right OTG adapter — USB-C on the ten Type-C models, micro-USB on the 8035 and the 8040.
  2. Check the phone sees the drive. If nothing appears, the drive does not mount and this method does not work on that phone.
  3. Open the phone's file manager and find the DCIM folder — that is where the camera photos live.
  4. Select the photos and videos, copy them to the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish.
  5. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

On all twelve floor-clearing phones the mounting test comes first — OTG is unstated on every one of them in every consulted source. On the six below-floor phones the file-manager copy is unproven too — none of them lists USB OTG Yes — so plug the drive in and check before trusting it there as well.

Method 3: through a computer

The fallback that works on all eighteen covered phones, including the ones no phone-to-USB method reaches with an app: the six below-floor phones, and any of the twelve where the drive does not mount.

  1. Plug the phone into the computer with a USB cable — a plain charging cable works here, because the computer is the other end.
  2. On the phone, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer.
  3. On the computer, open the phone's DCIM folder and copy the photos to the computer, then to the USB drive.
  4. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

This is the method Doro's own user manuals name — connect the phone to a computer and drag and drop the files — the computer copy. It needs the PC in between, so it is the fallback, not the route, when the phone and the drive are all you have.

Method 4: microSD card reader route

If the phone has a card slot — and all eighteen do, for certain — the card is the backup, and the reader is the bridge to the USB drive:

  1. Put a microSD card in the phone and copy the DCIM folder to the card with the file manager.
  2. Take the card out, put it in a card reader, plug the reader into the computer, and copy the photos from the card to the USB drive.
  3. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

A microSD card fits all eighteen phones for certain, so the card-reader route is proven on eighteen. Ceilings are stated on twelve, contested on one — the 8200 at 128 GB against up to 512 GB — and unstated on five. A card copy is a real local copy, but the card stays in the phone until you take it out, and opening it anywhere else needs the card reader — a USB drive plugs into any computer as it is.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud

The cloud is the last resort, and the one Doro's manuals point to alongside the computer copy: the 8031 manual's contents list a Backup section, which is the Google backup, and Google Photos is the photo version of the same idea. Doro offers no bundled cloud storage of its own, and Response by Doro is the safety app for the assistance button, not a backup. The cloud works on all twelve floor-clearing phones — every one runs Android, so Google Photos runs on all twelve — and on the six below-floor phones too, as far as their old Android versions still run it.

  1. Open Google Photos, turn on backup, and wait for the upload to finish — this needs Wi-Fi, and a big library takes a while.
  2. On a computer, open Google Photos, download the photos, and copy them to the USB drive.
  3. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

Two warnings. First, Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — check the setting before trusting a cloud copy. Second, a cloud backup is not a USB backup: your photos sit on Google's servers, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying for storage. If you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy — that is Method 1 or 2, not a cloud backup.

What this does not do

  • Does not install below Android 7.0. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and six covered Doro phones run below Android 7.0 as shipped — the PhoneEasy 740, the Liberto 820, the Liberto 820 Mini, the Liberto 825, the 8030 and the 8031 — so SnapDisk does not install on any of them. No other Doro phone is below the floor.
  • Does not work if the phone does not host a drive. No covered Doro phone lists USB OTG Yes in any consulted source, so on all twelve floor-clearing phones the drive mounting is the test: plug the drive in and check the phone sees it. On the six below-floor phones the file-manager copy is unproven too, for the same reason.
  • Does not delete photos. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Does not back up anything but photos and videos. SnapDisk copies image and video files only — no settings, no contacts, no messages, no app data.
  • Does not back up photos from apps that hide them. If an app keeps its photos in a private folder SnapDisk cannot read, those photos stay in the app.
  • Does not compress. SnapDisk copies the original files as they are — no recompression, no resizing, no quality loss.

  • Mistaking a PC tool for a phone-to-USB copy. A computer copy with a USB cable moves files to a computer — but it is a copy to a computer, and the backup lands on the PC. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route, not a desktop copy.
  • Mistaking a spec table or a manual page for a phone-to-USB guide. The Doro spec pages are real Doro pages — but they only list an Android version, a chipset, or a USB connector type.   • Mistaking a microSD card copy for a USB backup. Copying DCIM to a microSD card is a real local copy — all eighteen phones take a card — but the card stays in the phone, and opening it anywhere else needs a card reader; a USB drive plugs into any computer as it is.

  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step.
