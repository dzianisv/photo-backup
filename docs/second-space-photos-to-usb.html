Back up photos from Xiaomi Second Space to a USB drive

A First Space app cannot see your Second Space photos — Xiaomi keeps the space in a separate storage area with its own accounts. The backup has to start inside the space, or the photos have to come out to First Space first. Here are the routes that actually work, and the ones that don't.

November 23, 2026 · 6 min read

TL;DR: SnapDisk installed in First Space cannot back up Second Space photos — Second Space is a separate storage space with its own accounts (Settings → Special features → Second space, on MIUI and HyperOS), and a First Space app simply never sees the photos inside it. There are four honest routes, each with its own limit. (1) Copy inside Second Space with the space's own file manager — the only route that needs no crossing and no cloud — but whether a USB drive even appears in Second Space's Files app is unverified from Xiaomi's documentation or any guide found for this article, so try it and fall back if it doesn't show up. (2) Move the photos to First Space with the built-in "Transfer data between spaces" option — the supported way out, manual by design, nothing syncs automatically — and then back them up from First Space. (3) A cloud account signed in inside Second Space as a bridge — Xiaomi Cloud, or OneDrive — a fallback, not a USB backup, and the copy dies with that account if you lose access to it. (4) Installing a backup app inside Second Space — Xiaomi lets you install apps there, but SnapDisk isn't on Google Play at all and no one has run it inside Second Space, so do not plan a backup around this route. The sharpest motive is the delete flow: deleting Second Space erases everything left in it — the delete screen offers moving apps and contacts into First Space, not the photo library — and a forgotten Second Space password leaves deletion as the only way back in, so move the photos out before you delete the space, reset the phone, or switch phones. SnapDisk's one-tap copy works once the photos are in First Space — free up to 5 GB per run, Pro $9.99 once — and SnapDisk copies, it never deletes. As of November 23, 2026, no top search result offers a phone-direct USB-drive backup of Second Space photos — the generic Xiaomi USB guides never mention Second Space, and the Second Space guides back up only via a linked account or the cloud.

SnapDisk cannot see your Second Space photos — say it first

This is the fact the whole article turns on, so here it is with the evidence, not the claim. Second Space, Xiaomi's separate-space feature on MIUI and HyperOS, creates a second environment on the phone with its own apps, accounts, photos, files, and settings — as if you had two different phones in one. The Times of India's Gadgets Now guide puts the separation in plain words: "the Second Space works like an entire separate storage space and can be linked to Gmail or Xiaomi accounts different from the main one." Xiaomi's own MIUI 14 manual says the same thing in plainer words: "You can create a separate space in your tablet where you can use different accounts and IDs and store data. The data in the Second space does not affect the primary space." Each space has its own lock — the manual notes the passwords for the two spaces must be different — and you switch between them from the lock screen, from the Switch icon on the home screen, or from Settings → Special features → Second space. SnapDisk installed in First Space reads your First Space MediaStore library — the images and videos tables, oldest-first by date taken, verified in the code — and Second Space photos are not in that library. They are not hidden, or locked, or waiting for a permission: they are in a different space that your First Space apps cannot reach.

So the first step is not a backup method at all. It is finding out which space holds the photos. Photos taken with the camera app inside Second Space, saved inside a Second Space app, or moved into the space live in Second Space. Photos taken with your First Space camera, and everything in your First Space Gallery, live in First Space — and those are the ones SnapDisk can already back up. If you are not sure, switch into Second Space — tap the Switch icon, or enter the Second Space password on the lock screen — and look inside: the gallery or file manager installed there shows the photos the space holds. The rest of this article is about the photos that live in there.

One more scope fact before the methods, because it decides which of them SnapDisk can help with at all. SnapDisk copies image and video files only. On the default run it reads the phone's MediaStore images and videos tables, with no folder filter — the whole First Space library, oldest-first, whatever folder the photos sit in. On the From-picker route it walks the folder you picked and keeps only recognised photo and video extensions. A backup run that finishes green has copied your First Space photos — it has not touched Second Space, and the count it reports is the First Space count. If the number looks too small for what you expected, that is probably why: the Second Space photos were never in the run. Keep that in mind through Methods 1 to 4: the question is never "can SnapDisk copy these photos" in the abstract. It is "are these photos in the space SnapDisk runs in."

A short note on the neighbours, because search mixes them up: Second Space is not Dual Apps, which clones one app inside the same space — Second Space is a whole second environment, not one cloned app. It is not Android 15 Private Space either — that is Google's own separate-profile feature on Pixel phones, with its own rules; if your phone is a Pixel, see our Private Space guide instead. And it is not Google Photos Locked Folder, which keeps photos in the same account and has had an optional cloud backup since August 2023. Second Space has no equivalent: the guides document backup only through the space's own linked account or cloud service, never as a plain file copy out of the space. The rest of this article is only about Second Space.

What the search results actually offer

Search for this job three ways and the results split into two shapes, neither of them the narrow job — a phone-direct backup of Second Space photos onto a USB drive plugged into the phone, no computer, oldest-first, one tap, background with progress, Pause and Stop, resume and verification. The first shape is the generic Xiaomi OTG copy guide: renick.io's "Transferring photos from the Xiaomi smartphone to a USB stick", Wondershare Dr.Fone's "4 Practical Ways to Transfer Photos from Android to USB Flash Drive", Pixoneye, and our own Xiaomi article. They all describe the same steps — enable OTG, plug the drive in, open the file manager, copy the photos by hand — and none of them mentions Second Space at all. They assume every photo on the phone is visible to the file manager doing the copying, which is exactly what is false on a phone with photos in Second Space.

The second shape is the Second Space guide: Gadgets Now's "Second Space on Xiaomi smartphones: How to use, take backup and erase data", Dr.Fone's Second Space setup guides, and androidguias' "The Ultimate Guide to Second Space on Xiaomi". These are about the right feature — what Second Space is, how to switch, how to delete it — and they establish the key facts this article relies on: the space is an entire separate storage space with its own accounts; backup runs through the linked Gmail or Xiaomi account, Xiaomi's cloud backup service, or a personal cloud platform like OneDrive; and deleting the space offers moving apps and contacts into First Space. But none of them is a photo-backup-to-USB guide. The USB step never appears: Gadgets Now's backup section is cloud accounts only, and the MI Cloud export guide (umatechnology, "3 Ways to Transfer Files and Photos Out of MI Cloud") gets photos out of Xiaomi's cloud through the i.mi.com website or the MI Cloud app — a cloud download, not a drive plugged into the phone, and the web route needs a computer for the last step.

So the gap stands, but only on the narrow angle: the phone-direct USB-drive backup of Second Space photos, with the copy, the manual fallback, and the honest statement about what SnapDisk can and cannot do in one guide. That is the angle this article is written on.

What you need

A Xiaomi, Redmi, or POCO phone running MIUI or HyperOS with Second Space set up (Settings → Special features → Second space — if the entry is missing, your model doesn't support it: Second Space is not available on Android One models or very old versions); a USB drive your phone can read — a USB-C flash drive, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter (most modern Xiaomi phones support OTG); and enough space on the drive for the photos you want to keep. Two Xiaomi specifics. First, OTG is off by default on many MIUI and HyperOS phones — before the phone will see any drive, go to Settings > Additional settings > OTG and turn it on, with the drive already attached, because the switch turns itself off again after about 10 minutes without a drive. Second, the drive has to be one the phone can read — if nothing appears when you plug it in, check Settings > Storage, or try a different adapter. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 can't hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit.

Before any method, switch into Second Space and check which space holds the photos, as above — the gallery or file manager inside the space, or the First Space one. And one warning up front, because it applies to every route below: the two spaces have different passwords, and if you forget the Second Space password, the only recovery is deleting the space, which wipes everything inside it. Do not store the only copy of photos that matter in a space whose password you might forget.

Method 1: copy inside Second Space, by hand

This is the route to try first, because it is the only one that needs no crossing between spaces and no cloud account. Everything happens inside Second Space: the photos start there, the copy happens there, and the drive is the destination. Switch into Second Space, then plug the USB drive into the phone — with OTG on, as above. Open the Files app inside Second Space — the one in the space, not your First Space Files app — and look for the USB drive under storage. If it appears, browse to the photos (in the space's gallery, or the folder they sit in), select them, tap Copy to, and choose the USB drive as the destination. Wait for the copy to finish before unplugging, and then verify it: plug the drive into another device and open a few photos, and confirm they render.

The honest limit of this route is the one stated in the TL;DR, and it is worth spelling out because it is the difference between a guide and a guess. Whether a USB drive plugged into the phone appears in Second Space's Files app is unverified from Xiaomi's documentation or any guide found for this article: the MIUI manual confirms the space is separate with its own accounts and storage, Gadgets Now confirms backup runs through the linked account or the cloud, but no source says anything about whether a USB volume is visible per space, and no source found for this article answers it either way. It may appear, and on many phones it will — USB storage is device-wide hardware, not a per-space folder — but that is an expectation, not a verified fact. So treat this method as a test, not a promise: plug the drive in, open the Files app inside the space, and look. If the drive is there, copy by hand and you are done. If it is not there, the route is closed on your phone — do not keep tapping around hoping it appears — and the fallback is Method 2 or Method 3.

If the copy works, remember what it is: a manual copy. There is no oldest-first order, no progress bar with speed and time left, no Pause and Stop, and no resume if you unplug mid-copy. Copy in batches small enough to finish, check the count against what you selected, and open a few files on another device before you trust the drive. And the files on the drive are plain files — anyone who picks up the drive can open them — so if the photos are sensitive, this route protects nothing except against losing the phone. One more Second Space quirk: to view the photos at all inside the space you need a gallery or file manager installed there — the space does not share your First Space Gallery — so if the space has no gallery app, install one inside the space first.

Method 2: move the photos to First Space with "Transfer data between spaces"

If the drive doesn't appear inside Second Space, the next route is to move the photos across the boundary into First Space, where your First Space apps — and SnapDisk — can see them. This is Xiaomi's own route, and it is the one the guides document. Androidguias puts it plainly: "If you want to exchange files or applications between spaces, you'll need to do it manually from the 'Transfer data between spaces' option in the settings, as by default data is not automatically synchronized or shared." Nothing is shared or synced between the spaces unless you move it yourself — no apps, files, or accounts cross on their own.

Switch into Second Space first — the transfer runs from the space whose data you are moving. Then open Settings → Special features → Second space → Transfer data between spaces, pick the photos to move, and send them to First Space. Switch back to First Space and check the photos in the First Space Gallery before you delete anything in Second Space. Once they are First Space files, Method 4 below backs them up to the USB drive with one tap.

Two things to know if the transfer does work. First, check the moved copies before you rely on them: open a few in the First Space Gallery and confirm they are the full photos, not thumbnails, and that videos play. A transfer that silently downscaled or truncated a file is worse than a transfer that failed loudly. Second, do not confuse this with the delete flow. When you delete Second Space, Xiaomi offers moving apps and contacts into First Space — Gadgets Now documents the delete steps as "Choose the option to move into the First Space and tap 'Continue' to delete the Second Space", and its backup section names the same move for "installed apps and contacts". Photos are not in that list. So do not count on the delete screen to rescue the photo library: move the photos with Transfer data between spaces first, verify them in First Space, and only then delete the space. The transfer is the way out for photos; the delete flow is the way out for apps and contacts.

Method 3: a cloud account inside Second Space as a bridge — not a USB backup

The third route goes through a cloud account signed in inside Second Space — the space's linked Gmail or Xiaomi account, Xiaomi's cloud backup service, or a personal cloud platform like OneDrive, as Gadgets Now documents. Inside Second Space, open the cloud app signed in with the space's account, upload the photos, wait for the upload to finish, and then check from another device or a browser that the photos are actually in the account — a pending upload is not a verified copy, and a backup setting alone proves nothing. This gets the photos off the phone, which is the urgent part when a space deletion or a phone switch is coming.

Label this honestly: it is a fallback, not a USB backup. The photos leave the phone to an account, not to a drive you hold, and getting them back after the space is gone depends on keeping access to that account. If the cloud account is the space's dedicated account — Gadgets Now notes the space "can be linked to Gmail or Xiaomi accounts different from the main one" — keep its credentials available; if it is your main account, the photos are no longer private in any sense. And the MI Cloud export route is not a shortcut around this: umatechnology's guide gets photos out of MI Cloud through the i.mi.com website or the MI Cloud app, which downloads them to a computer or another device — a cloud download, not a drive plugged into the phone, and the web route needs a computer for the last step.

If the cloud copy is verified and you can still reach the account, the photos can come back down to First Space — download them there, or open them on a computer and copy them to the USB drive directly — and then Method 4 handles the drive copy. But if the only copy of the photos is in a cloud account you are about to lose, that is not a backup. It is a countdown. The USB drive is the copy that survives the phone ending; the cloud is the bridge that gets the photos there.

Method 4: SnapDisk's one-tap copy, once the photos are in First Space

This is the backup step itself — the part SnapDisk does — and it only starts once the photos are in First Space, through Method 2 or Method 3, or because they were First Space photos all along. Install SnapDisk from the v0.1.2 APK (it's not on Google Play yet), then:

  1. Plug in the drive. Turn OTG on first if it is off — Settings > Additional settings > OTG, with the drive already attached — then SnapDisk offers a backup in a notification: "External storage is connected. Backup photos?"
  2. Tap Backup. Photos and videos copy oldest-first — your earliest photos first — in the background, while you use the phone normally. The default run reads the whole First Space media library via MediaStore, not DCIM only, so every photo the First Space gallery sees is covered, whatever folder it sits in. This was verified in the code, not assumed: with no From folder picked, the app queries the MediaStore images and videos tables with no folder filter.
  3. Watch the progress notification — real percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left — with Pause and Stop. You can pause and resume any time.
  4. Wait for "Backup complete." Every file is size-verified after copying, so the counts are proof, not a promise. Files that already copied are never copied twice — so if the free 5 GB per-run limit stops a big library partway, just run it again and it picks up where it left off.

Copying only some photos? If you would rather copy just one folder than the whole library, use the From folder picker: tap it, navigate to the folder, and the run copies only that folder, recursively. The picker route keeps only recognised photo and video extensions. If the drive is unplugged mid-copy, the backup pauses with the full error shown and resumes where it stopped when the drive is back. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device.

And the check that matters for this article: after the run, compare the count against what you expected. A green backup that copied everything in the First Space library has still copied zero Second Space photos — they were never in the run. If the number looks too small, the missing photos are probably still inside Second Space, and Methods 1 to 3 are where they are. SnapDisk copies, it never deletes — the only files it ever deletes are its own half-finished .part files — so deleting the originals after the check is always your step, never the app's.

Method 5: installing SnapDisk inside Second Space

It sounds like the clean fix — put SnapDisk in Second Space, and it would see the space's photos the same way it sees First Space ones. Unlike a work profile, this route is not blocked by an admin: Second Space installs its own copies of apps with the space's own accounts, and there is no IT policy to ask. But do not plan a backup around it. SnapDisk is not on Google Play at all yet, so it cannot be installed from the Play Store in either space — there is no listing to install from, and the Play account inside Second Space is a separate account from the one in First Space. The other way in is side-loading the v0.1.2 APK inside the space, and that needs the install-from-unknown-sources allowance inside the space, which is a setting you control — but no one has installed SnapDisk in Second Space, and no test has run there, so whether the app's storage detection, notification, and background copy all work inside the space is unproven.

If it does work one day, then SnapDisk inside Second Space would read the space's MediaStore and back up its photos the same way it backs up First Space ones today — oldest-first, size-verified, Pause and Stop, 5 GB free per run, Pro $9.99 once. But that is a conditional, not a claim: until a real-device test runs SnapDisk inside the space, Method 5 is an experiment to try, not a backup to rely on. Methods 1 to 4 are the backup; this one is a question for a test phone.

Deleting Second Space, or switching phones? Back up first — there is no restore

This is the sharpest motive for the whole article, and the one with the least room for error. Gadgets Now states the delete rule plainly: deleting Second Space erases all the data in that space, and the delete flow's move into First Space covers installed apps and contacts. Androidguias says the same thing in plainer words: "deleting it will erase all files, apps, and data stored in that space, unless you manually transfer them before deletion" — and its tip before removing the space is direct: "If you need to keep photos, documents, or apps, manually transfer them to the main space before deleting the environment." The photos have no documented rescue on the delete screen. They have to be moved first, with Method 2, and verified in First Space before the space goes.

So the order matters, and it is not negotiable: move the photos out first, verify the copy on another device second, delete or switch or reset third. Open several photos from the drive on a computer and confirm they render; confirm the count matches what the gallery inside Second Space showed. Only then delete the space, reset the phone, or switch phones. The same order applies to the password: if you forget the Second Space password, the only recovery is deleting the space and losing what it contains — so a forgotten password is the same as a deletion, and the backup has to exist before either happens. If the photos matter — the ones you put behind a separate lock for a reason — the backup is the job, and the deletion is the deadline.

What to avoid

  • Assuming SnapDisk backs up Second Space photos. It cannot see them. A First Space app reads the First Space MediaStore, and the space's photos are in a different space. If the photos matter and they live in Second Space, Methods 1 to 3 are the routes — SnapDisk is the last step, not the first.
  • Trusting a green First Space backup to have covered the space's photos. A backup that copied everything in the First Space library copied zero Second Space photos. Check the count against what the gallery inside Second Space shows before you call it done.
  • Assuming the drive appears in Second Space's Files app. Unverified from Xiaomi's documentation or any guide found for this article. Plug it in, look, and if it isn't there, move to Method 2 or 3 instead of tapping around hoping it appears.
  • Counting on the delete screen to rescue the photos. The delete flow's move into First Space is documented for installed apps and contacts, not the photo library. Move the photos with Transfer data between spaces first — the delete screen is not a backup.
  • Forgetting the Second Space password. The two spaces have different passwords, and the only recovery from a forgotten Second Space password is deleting the space, which wipes it. Keep the password somewhere safe, or the photos are one forgotten password from gone.
  • Treating a cloud upload as a USB backup. A cloud copy inside the space is a bridge, not a backup: it dies with the account if you lose access to it, and the MI Cloud web route needs a computer for the last step. The USB drive is the copy that survives the phone ending.
  • Using Move when you meant Copy. When moving photos between spaces, keep the Second Space original until the USB backup is verified — if the space is then deleted or its password forgotten, a photo that exists only in the space exists nowhere. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Deleting the space before verifying. Deleting Second Space erases everything left in it. Copy, open a few photos on the drive on another device, then delete — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Assuming the copy keeps running with the screen off. Unverified — no wake lock, no battery-saver handling in the repo — so keep the phone awake for the run, or check it. Screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified, and a run that stopped when the screen went dark is the expected failure, not a surprise.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.
  • Forgetting OTG is off. On many MIUI and HyperOS phones OTG is off by default, and the switch turns itself off again after about 10 minutes without a drive. If the phone doesn't see the drive at all, check Settings > Additional settings > OTG before assuming the drive or the adapter is broken.

Rule of thumb: the space is the boundary — the backup has to start inside Second Space, or the photos have to come out to First Space first. Check which space holds the photos before you trust any backup, and move them out before you delete the space, reset the phone, or switch phones — the delete screen rescues apps and contacts, not the photo library.
