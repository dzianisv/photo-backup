SnapDisk vs Buffalo Photo Backup: Android USB backup

Two Android apps that back up your photos to a USB drive — one from Japan's biggest external-drive maker, one from a small team. Here is how they actually differ, from the facts we could verify as of November 2026.

November 21, 2026 · 4 min read

TL;DR: SnapDisk and Buffalo Photo Backup both back up Android photos to a USB drive, and both are free to download. The verified differences: Buffalo Photo Backup is on Google Play today (updated Dec 16, 2025, Android 13 or later, rated 4.5 from 61 reviews) and SnapDisk is not yet (v0.1.2 — install the APK from GitHub, Android 7.0 or later); Buffalo Photo Backup is free with no paid tier named in its listing, while SnapDisk Pro is $9.99 one-time for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size — so there is no Pro-versus-paid price comparison to make; Buffalo Photo Backup backs up photos and videos automatically when a drive is connected, keeps the originals, copies only new photos from the second time onward, lets you view the backed-up photos in the app, and restores all or selected photos — while SnapDisk copies photos and videos only, oldest-first, and never deletes, with no restore and no in-app viewer; Buffalo Photo Backup is built for Buffalo drives — its listing names verified Buffalo SSDs, USB flash drives, and card readers — while SnapDisk works with any drive Android can mount; Buffalo Photo Backup's listing says nothing about a copy order, Pause and Stop, or per-file verification, while SnapDisk copies oldest-first with Pause and Stop in the progress notification and checks the size of every file after copying; and Buffalo Photo Backup's listing says nothing about whether the copy keeps running with the screen off, while SnapDisk copies in the background as a foreground service, though screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified. One thing to know before you search: Buffalo's app is literally named "Photo Backup" — the same words as the job — which is why this app is called SnapDisk instead. As of November 21, 2026, no SnapDisk-vs-Buffalo head-to-head comparison turned up in search — this article is the first. This is the fifth head-to-head comparison in the series, after SnapDisk vs VaultDrive, SnapDisk vs FotoSafe, SnapDisk vs Photos to USB, and SnapDisk vs SanDisk app.

The two apps at a glance

SnapDisk is a free Android app (v0.1.2, not on Google Play yet) that backs up photos and videos to a USB disk. Plug in the disk, tap Backup in the notification, and photos copy oldest-first in the background, size-verified, with a progress notification showing percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left, plus Pause and Stop. Free: manual backup, 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup when a disk is connected and unlimited size. No cloud, no account, no ads, and no internet permission — your photos physically cannot leave the phone. It needs Android 7.0 or later.

Buffalo Photo Backup (jp.buffalo.photobackup, developer BUFFALO INC.) is on Google Play — updated Dec 16, 2025, Android 13 or later on a phone with a USB-C connector, rated 4.5 from 61 reviews. It is a free backup app from Buffalo, the No. 1 external SSD and card reader manufacturer in Japan according to a 2024 BCN survey. Open the app, connect an SSD, USB flash drive, or card reader, and backup starts automatically, keeping the original data. From the second time onward it detects newly added photos and videos, so later backups are incremental. You can view the backed-up photos and videos in the app, restore all or selected photos if they disappear from the phone, back up photos stored on the phone's internal SD card, and back up to multiple storage devices — the app remembers the backup status of up to 10 storage devices. Free — its listing shows a price of $0 and names no paid tier. Its verified devices are Buffalo SSDs, USB flash drives, and card readers.

Price: free vs free — and the paid tier only one of them names

Both apps are free to download. Buffalo Photo Backup's listing shows a price of $0, and the listing text we could read names no paid tier, no subscription, and no in-app purchase price at all. SnapDisk is free with no ads, and its Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase, verified in the repo's billing code (product id pro_unlock).

So this article makes no Pro-versus-paid price comparison: there is no Buffalo paid tier in the listing to compare against, and any price for one would be a guess. What can be compared is what the money buys on the one side where money is charged. SnapDisk's Pro buys features: auto-backup when a disk is connected, and unlimited size instead of the 5 GB free cap. Buffalo Photo Backup's automatic backup on connect — the feature SnapDisk charges $9.99 for — is in its free listing. If price is what decides it for you, both are free to start; the only priced thing in this comparison is SnapDisk Pro, at $9.99 once.

Neither app asks for a subscription at any point. The Buffalo listing names no subscription, and SnapDisk Pro is one-time.

The free tier: everything free vs 5 GB a run

Buffalo Photo Backup's free tier is the whole feature set as its listing describes it: automatic backup of photos and videos when a compatible drive is connected, incremental backup from the second time onward, viewing backed-up photos in the app, restore of all or selected photos, and backup to up to 10 remembered storage devices. Its listing states no size cap and no file-count cap. That is the free tier: everything it lists, free, with no limit stated.

SnapDisk's free tier is 5 GB per backup run, with no file-count cap, and runs can be repeated. Files already copied are never copied twice, so a second run picks up where the first stopped. A phone that has never been backed up often holds a library bigger than 5 GB, so it takes several taps — and that is the case Pro is for — but the free version can finish the whole library, run by run, without paying.

Which free tier goes further depends on your drive and your library: Buffalo Photo Backup's free tier covers the whole library in one automatic run, free, if your drive is a verified Buffalo one; SnapDisk's free tier takes several runs for a big library, but each run works with any drive, in the background with Pause and Stop, and there are no ads at all. If you own a Buffalo drive and want the whole library backed up free in one go, Buffalo Photo Backup's free tier is the usable one — SnapDisk's is not, until you repeat the run.

Before the copy: set it once vs pick your folders

Buffalo Photo Backup is the simpler start, if you have its drive. Its listing lays out the deal in one line: "Simply open the app and connect an SSD, USB Flash Drive, or card reader to automatically start backup." Open the app, plug a verified Buffalo drive in, and the backup runs. There is no folder picking in its listing and no preview: it backs up your photos and videos to the drive.

SnapDisk asks you to choose first. Pick the From and To folders in settings (the system folder picker, verified in the repo as OpenDocumentTree), plug the drive in, tap Backup in the notification — and the numbers appear once the copy is running, in the progress notification: percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left. The folders are your choice; the plan is not previewed.

Neither app shows a planned-backup summary before the run — FotoSafe does, in an earlier comparison in this series, but neither of these two does. The difference here is control against convenience: Buffalo Photo Backup decides what to back up (photos and videos) and runs it automatically; SnapDisk lets you pick the folders, and mirrors your source folder structure on the drive.

During the backup: automatic on connect vs background with Pause and Stop

This is the clearest behavioural difference, and it cuts both ways. Buffalo Photo Backup's automatic backup runs when the drive is connected — that is the deal for the whole-library free tier — and from the second time onward it copies only the photos added since the last backup, so later runs are faster. Its listing mentions no Pause and Stop controls, states no copy order, and says nothing about whether the copy keeps running with the screen off. It also says nothing about what happens if the drive is unplugged mid-backup, and nothing about files too large for the drive's format.

SnapDisk copies oldest-first — your earliest photos first, by the date the photo was taken — in the background, while you use the phone normally. The progress notification shows real percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left, with Pause and Stop. If you unplug the disk mid-copy, the backup pauses with the full error shown and resumes where it stopped when the disk is back; the file in flight resumes from its .part file, and files already copied are never copied twice. Files that cannot be copied — a single file of 4 GB or more on a FAT32 drive, for example — are skipped and listed with their reason instead of failing the whole backup.

No review text was readable in the listing this run — the rating (4.5 from 61 reviews) is shown, but no individual review — so no reviewer complaints are quoted here, unlike the SanDisk comparison in this series, where the rival's own reviewers report a FAT32 stall with no skip. That absence cuts both ways: there is no public record of Buffalo Photo Backup stalling on a large file, and no public record of it handling one either. Its listing says nothing about the case at all, so nothing is claimed for it.

After the copy: verification, restore, and what the drive holds

Buffalo Photo Backup verifiably wins this section on two points its listing states outright. First, restore: "If important photos or videos disappear from your Android smartphone, you can restore them all from your storage. You can also restore selected photos and videos that you temporarily deleted to free up space." Second, viewing: connect the drive and view the backed-up photos and videos in the app. It also remembers the backup status of each storage device, up to 10 of them, so a second drive is a second backup, not a restart. SnapDisk has no restore feature and no in-app viewer — you open the copied files in any viewer yourself — and it tracks no per-device backup status.

SnapDisk's answer is a different check. It checks the size of every file after copying: each copy writes to a .part file next to the destination and is renamed to the final file only after the size verification passes, so a file that did not arrive whole never pretends it did. Its destination mirrors your source folder structure — Camera, Screenshots, and the rest land where they were on the phone. Buffalo Photo Backup's listing says nothing about per-file verification, and nothing about the folder layout its backups land in — so neither is claimed for it, and neither is scored against it. Both apps keep the originals: Buffalo Photo Backup's listing says it backs up "while preserving their original data," and SnapDisk streams each file byte-for-byte with no recompression. Neither app backs up contacts — Buffalo Photo Backup backs up photos and videos, and SnapDisk backs up photos and videos only. Neither side replaces opening a few photos on the drive yourself — a backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy.

Freeing up space: delete after backup vs copy-only

Here is the honest part, and the one place the two apps genuinely differ on what they do to your phone. Buffalo Photo Backup's listing says it can help free up space — "Not only can you protect your precious memories, but you can also use it to free up space on your Android smartphone!" — but the app itself does not say it moves or deletes files: freeing space is deleting from the phone after the backup, which is your step. Its own footnote says the same thing, with a warning worth quoting: when deleting photos or videos from the phone, back them up to two or more storage devices first, just in case.

SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes anything (the only delete calls in its source touch .part files, never your photos) — so freeing space is a two-step flow you do yourself: copy to the drive, open a few photos on the drive to check they arrived, then delete the originals from your phone. That flow is covered step by step in How to free up space by moving photos to a USB drive.

So if freeing space is why you're choosing an app, the question is not which app moves the files — neither says it does. The difference is the safety net around the delete: Buffalo Photo Backup's listing tells you to hold two backups before you delete, and its multi-device memory (up to 10 devices) makes that second backup a normal step; SnapDisk's copy-only rule means a file is never gone from the phone until you have checked the copy yourself. Either way, check the copy on the drive before you delete anything — that order is what keeps your photos safe, whichever app does the copying.

What to avoid

  • Assuming Buffalo Photo Backup works with any drive. It does not promise that. Its verified devices are Buffalo SSDs, USB flash drives, and card readers, and its listing points to Buffalo's compatibility page for details. If your drive is another brand, its listing promises nothing about it — SnapDisk works with any drive Android can mount.
  • Buying a Buffalo drive you do not own yet just for the app. Buffalo Photo Backup is built for verified Buffalo drives. If your drive is another brand, its listing promises nothing about it — SnapDisk works with any drive Android can mount.
  • Paying before checking what the purchase buys. The Buffalo listing names no paid tier at all. SnapDisk's Pro buys auto-backup on connect and unlimited size for $9.99 once — and Buffalo Photo Backup's auto-backup on connect is in its free listing.
  • Assuming "free" means the same thing on both sides. Buffalo Photo Backup is free with no cap stated and automatic backup included; SnapDisk is free with no ads and 5 GB per run, repeatable. The trade is the cap against the drive lock-in.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. Buffalo's own listing says the same: back up to two or more devices before you delete.
  • Assuming the copy keeps running with the screen off. Neither listing promises it. SnapDisk's copy runs as a foreground service — the mechanism Android gives a copy that must keep running with the screen off — but the repo holds no wake lock and no battery-saver handling, and no comparison run has ever been tested, so screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified, and this article says so instead of promising it. Buffalo Photo Backup's listing says nothing about it either.
  • Using a FAT32 drive for long videos. A single file over 4 GB does not fit on FAT32. SnapDisk skips those files and tells you which ones. Buffalo Photo Backup's listing says nothing about files over 4 GB at all — so use exFAT for a library with very long videos.
  • Choosing on whether an app is on Play. Buffalo Photo Backup is on Google Play today and SnapDisk is not yet — that says where to download it, not how either app copies your photos.
  • Reading too much into the 4.5 rating. Buffalo Photo Backup is rated 4.5, but from only 61 reviews — a small base, so one unhappy user moves it a lot. It is a real rating, not a big one.
  • Assuming contacts come along. Neither app backs up contacts. Buffalo Photo Backup backs up photos and videos; SnapDisk backs up photos and videos only — your contacts need another route.
  • Searching for the wrong name. Buffalo's app is literally named "Photo Backup," so a search for "photo backup" finds it first — and finds a dozen other apps with the same name. That collision is why this app is called SnapDisk.

Rule of thumb: the app that wins is the one whose copy you trust enough to delete after. Compare the free tier, the preview, and the copy — then check the copy on the drive before you delete anything.
