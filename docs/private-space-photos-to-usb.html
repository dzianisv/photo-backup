Back up Private Space photos to a USB drive from Android

A personal-profile app cannot see your Private Space photos — Android keeps the space in a separate, locked profile with its own storage. The backup has to start inside the space, or the photos have to come out to personal first. Here are the routes that actually work, and the ones that don't.

November 23, 2026 · 6 min read

TL;DR: SnapDisk installed in your personal profile cannot back up Private Space photos — Private Space is a separate, locked profile (Android 15+, Settings → Security & privacy → Private space) with its own storage, and a personal-profile app simply never sees the photos inside it. There are four honest routes, each with its own limit. (1) Copy inside Private Space with the space's own file manager — the only route that needs no crossing and no cloud — but whether a USB drive even appears in Private Space's Files app is unverified from Google's documentation, so try it and fall back if it doesn't show up. (2) Share the photos out to a personal app while the space is unlocked — the share sheet shows a Private tab only while unlocked — and then back them up from the personal profile; sharing copies the file, so the original stays inside the space until you delete it. (3) A cloud account signed in inside Private Space as a bridge — a fallback, not a USB backup, and the copy dies with that account if you lose access to it. (4) Installing a backup app inside Private Space — Google allows installing apps there, including from installers other than Play, but SnapDisk isn't on Google Play at all and no one has run it inside Private Space, so do not plan a backup around this route. The sharpest motive is the one Google states plainly: Private Space has no backup and restore, so when you restore a device backup the space is not restored, and any on-device data in it is not backed up — move the photos out before you switch phones, reset the phone, or delete the space. SnapDisk's one-tap copy works once the photos are in the personal profile — free up to 5 GB per run, Pro $9.99 once — and SnapDisk copies, it never deletes. As of November 23, 2026, no top search result offers a phone-direct USB-drive backup of Private Space photos — the generic OTG guides never mention Private Space, and the phone-switch guides never take the USB step.

SnapDisk cannot see your Private Space photos — say it first

This is the fact the whole article turns on, so here it is with the evidence, not the claim. Private Space, added in Android 15, is a separate, lockable area on the phone for sensitive apps — technically a second Android profile, built on the same multi-user system as a work profile, with its own copies of apps and its own data. When it is locked, the apps inside are stopped and hidden from your app list, recent apps, notifications, search, and Settings. Google's own help page puts the separation in plain words: "Since private space is a completely separate space and profile on your device, it doesn't automatically read the accounts in your main space." SnapDisk installed in your personal profile reads your personal profile's MediaStore library — the images and videos tables, oldest-first by date taken, verified in the code — and Private Space photos are not in that library. They are not hidden, or locked, or waiting for a permission: they are in a different profile that your personal apps cannot reach.

So the first step is not a backup method at all. It is finding out which space holds the photos. Photos taken with the camera app inside Private Space, saved inside a Private Space app, or moved into the space with its Add files step live in Private Space. Photos taken with your personal camera, and everything in your personal Gallery, live in the personal profile — and those are the ones SnapDisk can already back up. If you are not sure, unlock Private Space (it sits at the bottom of All Apps) and look inside: the gallery or file manager installed there shows the photos the space holds. Files you add to the space land in its own Downloads folder. The rest of this article is about the photos that live in there.

One more scope fact before the methods, because it decides which of them SnapDisk can help with at all. SnapDisk copies image and video files only. On the default run it reads the phone's MediaStore images and videos tables, with no folder filter — the whole personal library, oldest-first, whatever folder the photos sit in. On the From-picker route it walks the folder you picked and keeps only recognised photo and video extensions. A backup run that finishes green has copied your personal photos — it has not touched Private Space, and the count it reports is the personal count. If the number looks too small for what you expected, that is probably why: the Private Space photos were never in the run. Keep that in mind through Methods 1 to 4: the question is never "can SnapDisk copy these photos" in the abstract. It is "are these photos in the profile SnapDisk runs in."

A short note on the neighbours, because search mixes them up: Private Space is not Google Photos Locked Folder and not Samsung Secure Folder. Locked Folder has had an optional backup since August 2023 — turn it on and the folder moves with your Google account. Secure Folder moves with Samsung Smart Switch. Private Space has neither: Google's documentation says it has no backup and restore, and that is the gap this article is written on. If your photos are in Locked Folder, turn its backup on or move them out; if they are in Secure Folder, Smart Switch is the supported move. The rest of this article is only about Private Space.

Search for this job three ways and the results split into two shapes, neither of them the narrow job — a phone-direct backup of Private Space photos onto a USB drive plugged into the phone, no computer, oldest-first, one tap, background with progress, Pause and Stop, resume and verification. The first shape is the generic OTG copy guide: groovypost's "How to Transfer Photos from Android to a USB Drive", Pixoneye, FotoSafe, and our own automatic-transfer article. They all describe the same steps — plug the drive in, open the file manager, copy DCIM by hand — and none of them mentions Private Space at all. They assume every photo on the phone is visible to the file manager doing the copying, which is exactly what is false on a phone with photos in Private Space.

The second shape is the phone-switch guide: Wondershare MobileTrans, dobr.app's "What happens to hidden photos when you get a new phone", llcloud's "Move Hidden Photos to a New Phone", and calculatorlockerapp. These are about the right problem — what happens to hidden photos when the phone changes — and they establish the key facts this article relies on: Google says Private Space has no backup and restore and is not part of device backup; apps inside it must be installed again on the new phone; files stored only inside the space stay on the old phone, so move them out first. But none of them is a photo-backup-to-USB guide. The USB leg appears only as a single export step — copy the photos out, then move them by cable or your usual transfer method — never as a workflow that ends with the photos on a drive plugged into the phone. The dobr guide states the rule plainly: "Copy anything you need out of it first." It just doesn't say how to get that copy onto a USB drive without a computer.

So the gap stands, but only on the narrow angle: the phone-direct USB-drive backup of Private Space photos, with the copy, the manual fallback, and the honest statement about what SnapDisk can and cannot do in one guide. That is the angle this article is written on.

What you need

An Android phone running Android 15 or later with Private Space set up (Settings → Security & privacy → Private space — if the entry is missing, your phone maker or an administrator has disabled it, or you are on a work-managed phone or a supervised child account on the main space, where Private Space is unavailable); a USB drive your phone can read — a USB-C flash drive, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter (most modern Android phones support OTG; if the phone doesn't see the drive, try a different adapter); and enough space on the drive for the photos you want to keep. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 can't hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit.

Before any method, unlock Private Space and check which space holds the photos, as above — the gallery or file manager inside the space, or the personal one. And one warning up front, because it applies to every route below: when Private Space is locked, its apps are stopped and hidden, and the share sheet shows no Private tab — every route below starts with unlocking the space. If you have forgotten the Private Space lock, there is no recovery: Google says if you forget your private space unlock factor, you won't be able to access the space, and the only way back in is deleting the space, which wipes everything inside it. Do not store the only copy of photos that matter in a space whose lock you might forget.

Method 1: copy inside Private Space, by hand

This is the route to try first, because it is the only one that needs no crossing between profiles and no cloud account. Everything happens inside Private Space: the photos start there, the copy happens there, and the drive is the destination. Unlock Private Space, then plug the USB drive into the phone. Open the Files app inside Private Space — the one in the space, not your personal Files app — and look for the USB drive under storage. If it appears, browse to the photos (in the space's gallery, or its Downloads folder, where files added to the space land), select them, tap Copy to, and choose the USB drive as the destination. Wait for the copy to finish before unplugging, and then verify it: plug the drive into another device and open a few photos, and confirm they render.

The honest limit of this route is the one stated in the TL;DR, and it is worth spelling out because it is the difference between a guide and a guess. Whether a USB drive plugged into the phone appears in Private Space's Files app is unverified from Google's documentation: the help page confirms the space is a separate profile with its own storage, but it says nothing about whether a USB volume is visible per profile, and no source found for this article answers it either way. It may appear, and on many phones it will — USB storage is device-wide hardware, not a per-profile folder — but that is an expectation, not a verified fact. So treat this method as a test, not a promise: plug the drive in, open the Files app inside the space, and look. If the drive is there, copy by hand and you are done. If it is not there, the route is closed on your phone — do not keep tapping around hoping it appears — and the fallback is Method 2 or Method 3.

If the copy works, remember what it is: a manual copy. There is no oldest-first order, no progress bar with speed and time left, no Pause and Stop, and no resume if you unplug mid-copy. Copy in batches small enough to finish, check the count against what you selected, and open a few files on another device before you trust the drive. And the files on the drive are plain files — anyone who picks up the drive can open them — so if the photos are sensitive, this route protects nothing except against losing the phone. One more Private Space quirk: to view the photos at all inside the space you need a gallery or file manager installed there — the space does not share your personal Gallery — so if the space has no gallery app, install one inside the space first.

Method 2: share the photos out to the personal profile

If the drive doesn't appear inside Private Space, the next route is to move the photos across the boundary into the personal profile, where your personal apps — and SnapDisk — can see them. Unlock Private Space first: while it is locked, the share sheet shows no Private tab and the space's apps are stopped. Then, in the gallery or file manager inside the space, select the photos, tap Share, and look for your personal apps in the share sheet. When the space is unlocked, Google says you will find a "Private" tab on sharing apps such as the share sheet, the Docs UI, and the photo picker — share to your personal Files app, or to your personal Gallery, and the photos are copied into the personal profile. Once they are personal-profile files, Method 4 below backs them up to the USB drive with one tap.

The honest limit: this route has no policy gate the way a work profile does — there is no IT admin deciding whether Private Space photos may cross — but it does have a direction problem worth stating plainly. Google documents moving files into Private Space in detail — open the space, tap + Add, tap Add files, choose Copy (the original stays) or Move (the original is deleted) — and files land in the space's Downloads folder. It documents no equivalent move-out step. Sharing is the way out, and sharing copies the file: the original stays inside Private Space until you delete it or the space itself, so you now have two copies, and the one inside the space is still at risk. That is not a flaw in the route — it is the reason to finish the job: share the photos out, back them up to the drive, verify the copy, and only then decide what to delete inside the space.

Two things to know if sharing does work. First, check the shared copies before you rely on them: open a few in the personal Gallery and confirm they are the full photos, not thumbnails, and that videos play. A share that silently downscaled or truncated a file is worse than a share that failed loudly. Second, mind the account: if you signed into Private Space with the same Google Account you use on the main space, synced photos, files, and emails can be visible outside the space — Google recommends a dedicated account for Private Space that hasn't been signed in elsewhere. If the photos you are moving out are ones you wanted kept separate, sharing them to a personal app that syncs to that same account defeats the separation. Share to a local app — personal Files, personal Gallery — not to a cloud-backed one, unless you mean to.

Method 3: a cloud account inside Private Space as a bridge — not a USB backup

The third route goes through a cloud account signed in inside Private Space — Google Photos with the space's account, or another cloud app installed in the space. Upload the photos from the app inside the space to that account, wait for the upload to finish, and then check from another device or a browser that the photos are actually in the account — a pending upload is not a verified copy, and a backup setting alone proves nothing. This gets the photos off the phone, which is the urgent part when a phone switch or a space deletion is coming.

Label this honestly: it is a fallback, not a USB backup. The photos leave the phone to an account, not to a drive you hold, and getting them back after the space is gone depends on keeping access to that account. Only data an app syncs to its own cloud comes back after you sign in again — that is Google's own wording for what survives. If the cloud account is the space's dedicated account, keep its credentials available; if it is your main account, the photos are no longer private in any sense. And do not rely on standard Android device backup as the route: Google says Private Space data is not part of device backup, and restoring a backup does not bring the space back.

If the cloud copy is verified and you can still reach the account, the photos can come back down to the personal profile — download them there, or open them on a computer and copy them to the USB drive directly — and then Method 4 handles the drive copy. But if the only copy of the photos is in a cloud account you are about to lose, that is not a backup. It is a countdown. The USB drive is the copy that survives the phone ending; the cloud is the bridge that gets the photos there.

Method 5: installing SnapDisk inside Private Space

It sounds like the clean fix — put SnapDisk in Private Space, and it would see the space's photos the same way it sees personal ones. Unlike a work profile, this route is not blocked by an admin: Google says you can install apps in Private Space from the Play Store or with different app installers, and there is no IT policy to ask. But do not plan a backup around it. SnapDisk is not on Google Play at all yet, so it cannot be installed from the Play Store in either profile — there is no listing to install from. The other way in is side-loading the v0.1.2 APK inside the space, and that needs the install-from-unknown-sources allowance inside the space, which is a setting you control — but no one has installed SnapDisk in Private Space, and no test has run there, so whether the app's storage detection, notification, and background copy all work inside the space is unproven.

If it does work one day, then SnapDisk inside Private Space would read the space's MediaStore and back up its photos the same way it backs up personal ones today — oldest-first, size-verified, Pause and Stop, 5 GB free per run, Pro $9.99 once. But that is a conditional, not a claim: until a real-device test runs SnapDisk inside the space, Method 5 is an experiment to try, not a backup to rely on. Methods 1 to 4 are the backup; this one is a question for a test phone.

Switching phones, or deleting the space? Back up first — there is no restore

This is the sharpest motive for the whole article, and the one with the least room for error. Google's help page states it three ways, and each one matters. First: "The app and its data are deleted with no backup." Second: "when you restore the device from a device backup, private space won't be restored. Any on-device data for private space isn't backed up." Third, the list of things you can't do in Private Space includes "Backup and restore app data as part of device back up." The phone-switch guides say the same thing in plainer words: apps inside the space must be installed again on the new phone, only data an app syncs to its own cloud comes back after you sign in, and anything stored only inside the space stays on the old phone.

So the order matters, and it is not negotiable: move the photos out first, verify the copy on another device second, switch or reset or delete third. Open several photos from the drive on a computer and confirm they render; confirm the count matches what the gallery inside the space showed. Only then switch phones, reset the old one, or delete the space. The same order applies to the lock: if you forget the Private Space lock, the only way back in is deleting the space, which wipes it — so a forgotten lock is the same as a deletion, and the backup has to exist before either happens. If the photos matter — the ones you put behind a lock for a reason — the backup is the job, and the switch is the deadline.

What to avoid

  • Assuming SnapDisk backs up Private Space photos. It cannot see them. A personal-profile app reads the personal profile's MediaStore, and the space's photos are in a different profile. If the photos matter and they live in Private Space, Methods 1 to 3 are the routes — SnapDisk is the last step, not the first.
  • Trusting a green personal backup to have covered the space's photos. A backup that copied everything in the personal library copied zero Private Space photos. Check the count against what the gallery inside the space shows before you call it done.
  • Assuming the drive appears in Private Space's Files app. Unverified from Google's documentation. Plug it in, look, and if it isn't there, move to Method 2 or 3 instead of tapping around hoping it appears.
  • Trying to share out of a locked space. When Private Space is locked, its apps are stopped and the share sheet shows no Private tab. Unlock the space first, every time — and if you have forgotten the lock, there is no recovery except deleting the space.
  • Treating a cloud upload as a USB backup. A cloud copy inside the space is a bridge, not a backup: only data the app syncs to its own cloud comes back, and it dies with the account if you lose access to it. The USB drive is the copy that survives the phone ending.
  • Using Move when you meant Copy. Adding files to Private Space offers Copy (the original stays) or Move (the original is deleted). Move deletes the personal original after a successful transfer — if the space is then deleted or its lock forgotten, that photo exists nowhere. Use Copy until the backup is verified.
  • Deleting the space before verifying. Deleting Private Space deletes the app and its data with no backup. Copy, open a few photos on the drive on another device, then switch, reset, or delete — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Assuming the copy keeps running with the screen off. Unverified — no wake lock, no battery-saver handling in the repo — so keep the phone awake for the run, or check it. Screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified, and a run that stopped when the screen went dark is the expected failure, not a surprise.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.

Rule of thumb: the profile is the boundary — the backup has to start inside Private Space, or the photos have to come out to personal first. Check which space holds the photos before you trust any backup, and move them out before you switch phones, reset the phone, or delete the space — there is no restore.
