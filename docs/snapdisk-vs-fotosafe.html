SnapDisk vs FotoSafe: back up Android photos to USB

Two Android apps that copy your photos to a USB drive — no cloud, no account, no upload. Here is how they actually differ, from the facts we could verify as of November 2026.

November 21, 2026 · 4 min read

TL;DR: SnapDisk and FotoSafe both back up Android photos to a USB drive offline, with no account and no subscription — and both only copy, never delete. The verified differences: FotoSafe is on Google Play today and SnapDisk is not yet (v0.1.2 — install the APK from GitHub); FotoSafe's free tier is one successful run of up to 100 media files, then its one-time Pro is required for further runs, while SnapDisk's free tier is 5 GB per run and repeatable — files already copied are never copied twice; SnapDisk Pro is $9.99 one-time, but FotoSafe's Pro price is published only in Google Play — its own site says "Google Play shows the current price" — and we could not verify a figure, so this article makes no price comparison; FotoSafe shows the planned backup before it starts, SnapDisk copies oldest-first with Pause and Stop in the progress notification, and SnapDisk checks the size of every file after copying while FotoSafe's own listing tells you to verify important backups on the drive yourself. FotoSafe needs Android 9 or later with USB host/OTG; SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, so it runs on older phones FotoSafe cannot. This is the second head-to-head comparison in the series, after SnapDisk vs VaultDrive.

The two apps at a glance

SnapDisk is a free Android app (v0.1.2, not on Google Play yet) that backs up photos and videos to a USB disk. Plug in the disk, tap Backup in the notification, and photos copy oldest-first in the background, size-verified, with a progress notification showing percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left, plus Pause and Stop. Free: manual backup, 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup when a disk is connected and unlimited size. No cloud, no account, no ads, and no internet permission — your photos physically cannot leave the phone. It needs Android 7.0 or later.

FotoSafe — Photo Backup to USB (at.weidi.fotobackup) is on Google Play. Connect the drive, select it as the destination in FotoSafe through Android's system picker, review the media, and start copying. Backups land in a /FotoSafe/ folder on the drive; files already backed up are skipped on later runs; a progress notification shows during longer backups, and result summaries follow each run. It works offline with no account, no ads, and no tracking, needs Android 9 or later with USB host/OTG support, and describes what it copies as photos and videos, without listing formats. Free: one successful trial run of up to 100 photos or videos. Pro is a one-time purchase that unlocks unlimited backup runs, with no subscription.

Price: both one-time Pro — but only one price is published

This is the part of the comparison that cannot be scored, and the honest thing is to say so. Both apps charge once, not monthly: SnapDisk Pro is $9.99 one-time, verified in the repo's billing code (product id pro_unlock), and FotoSafe Pro is a one-time Google Play purchase with no subscription, verified from FotoSafe's own site and its Play listing.

But only one of the two prices is published where it can be checked. FotoSafe's site says, in its own FAQ: "Google Play shows the current price." The Play listing text we could read states the price of the app itself (free) and that Pro is a one-time purchase, but it does not show a Pro price figure — and no other FotoSafe page we checked lists one. So this article makes no price comparison: any number for FotoSafe Pro would be a guess, and a guessed price in a comparison is worse than no price at all. If the Pro price is what decides it for you, open FotoSafe's Play listing and read the in-app purchase price there — it is the only source FotoSafe itself points to.

Both apps are free to start: FotoSafe is free to download from Play with the trial run included, and SnapDisk's v0.1.2 APK is free from GitHub. Neither asks for a subscription at any point.

The free tier: one run vs every run

The free tiers are shaped differently, and the difference matters more than the price neither side fully publishes. FotoSafe's free tier is one successful backup run of up to 100 media files — one run, not 100 files on every run. After that run, further runs require the one-time Pro purchase. It is a trial: enough to prove the flow works on your phone and your drive, not enough to back up a library.

SnapDisk's free tier is 5 GB per backup run, with no file-count cap, and runs can be repeated. Files already copied are never copied twice, so a second run picks up where the first stopped. A phone that has never been backed up often holds a library bigger than 5 GB, so it takes several taps — and that is the case Pro is for — but the free version can finish the whole library, run by run, without paying.

Which free tier goes further depends on your camera roll: 100 files is a small roll, and 5 GB fits a few hundred large photos or a few long videos. If you want to test before you pay, FotoSafe's trial is a complete run. If you want to back up a whole library without paying, SnapDisk's repeatable 5 GB runs are the usable free tier — FotoSafe's is not.

Before the copy: FotoSafe shows the plan first

This is where FotoSafe verifiably wins. Its guide says, plainly: "FotoSafe shows the planned backup before it starts." Before copying, you review the destination, the new files, the files already backed up, and the required space — then you start. You know what is about to happen before any file moves.

SnapDisk does not show a planned-backup summary before the run. The flow is: pick the From and To folders in settings (the system folder picker, verified in the repo as OpenDocumentTree), plug the drive in, tap Backup in the notification — and the numbers appear once the copy is running, in the progress notification: percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left. The folders are your choice; the plan is not previewed.

FotoSafe also offers an Expert mode for choosing additional folders beyond the guided flow, and its destination step runs through Android's system picker: you open the USB storage entry and tap "Use this folder" — the guide warns, in its own words, that the picker "must show USB storage, not internal storage", and documents a Samsung / One UI fallback (check the drive in My Files first, then pick the same entry). SnapDisk's folders are picked the same way, through the system picker — the difference is the preview, not the picker.

During the backup: oldest-first, Pause / Stop, resume

SnapDisk copies oldest-first — your earliest photos first, by the date the photo was taken — in the background, while you use the phone normally. The progress notification shows real percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left, with Pause and Stop. If you unplug the disk mid-copy, the backup pauses with the full error shown and resumes where it stopped when the disk is back; the file in flight resumes from its .part file, and files already copied are never copied twice.

FotoSafe shows a visible progress notification during longer backups and result summaries after each run, and it skips files already backed up on later runs — reconnect the same destination and it checks existing files, so a repeat run only copies what's new. That is resume-by-skipping for finished files, and it works. What FotoSafe does not advertise is the rest: its listing mentions a progress notification, not Pause and Stop controls; its guide does not state a copy order, so there is no oldest-first claim; and its own listing warns that if the phone, Android, cable, adapter, or USB drive fails during a backup, the backup "may remain incomplete" — it does not advertise resuming the file that was in flight when the failure happened.

After the copy: who checks that it worked

SnapDisk checks the size of every file after copying: each copy writes to a .part file next to the destination and is renamed to the final file only after the size verification passes, so a file that did not arrive whole never pretends it did. Files that cannot be copied — a single file of 4 GB or more on a FAT32 drive, for example — are skipped and listed with their reason instead of failing the whole backup.

FotoSafe puts the check in your hands. Its Play listing says: "Please verify important backups on the destination drive and keep at least one additional copy." Its guide says the same, more plainly: "A completion message is useful; a real file check is better" — open several new photos and large videos directly from the USB drive after the run, and verify before deleting anything elsewhere. Its result summaries tell you what happened; they are not a per-file verification claim, and FotoSafe does not advertise one.

Neither side replaces opening a few photos on the drive yourself. SnapDisk's size check proves the bytes arrived; FotoSafe's guide asks you to prove it by looking. Do the looking either way — a backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy.

Freeing up space: neither app deletes for you

Here is the honest part. Neither app frees up space on your phone today. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes anything (the only delete calls in its source touch .part files, never your photos) — so freeing space is a two-step flow you do yourself: copy to the drive, open a few photos on the drive to check they arrived, then delete the originals from your phone. That flow is covered step by step in How to free up space by moving photos to a USB drive.

FotoSafe is in the same place, by its own words. Its Play listing lists "no automatic deletion of your original files" among what the app does not do, and its guide says: "Copying is not moving. Originals remain on the phone." So if freeing space is why you're choosing an app, the question is not which app deletes — neither does — but which app's copy you trust enough to delete after. SnapDisk's answer is the copy-first-then-delete flow above, with every file size-verified after copying; FotoSafe's answer is the same flow, with the check done by you on the drive.

What to avoid

  • Paying before trying the free run. FotoSafe's trial is one successful run — make it count. Check the destination is the USB drive, not internal storage, before you start; its own guide warns that the picker must show USB storage.
  • Picking the wrong storage in the picker. If the top-level storage shows the phone name, "Internal storage", "Downloads", or "DCIM", cancel and start again from the USB entry — that is FotoSafe's own instruction, and it applies to any app that uses the system picker.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. This applies to both apps.
  • Assuming the free tier covers a whole library. FotoSafe's free tier is one run of up to 100 files; SnapDisk's is 5 GB per run, repeatable. A library bigger than either needs more runs — or Pro.
  • Assuming "offline" means the same thing everywhere. Both apps copy offline with no account and no cloud. SnapDisk goes one step further: it has no internet permission at all, so your photos physically cannot leave the device — it works with the phone in airplane mode. FotoSafe says no cloud and no tracking; its permission set is not something we verified, so this article does not claim it either way.
  • Choosing on whether an app is on Play. FotoSafe is on Google Play today and SnapDisk is not yet — that says where to download it, not how either app copies your photos.
  • Assuming the copy keeps running with the screen off. SnapDisk's copy runs as a foreground service — the mechanism Android gives a copy that must keep running with the screen off — but the repo holds no wake lock and no battery-saver handling, and no comparison run has ever been tested, so screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified, and this article says so instead of promising it. FotoSafe's guide says the same thing in its own way: keep the phone and drive connected until completion.

Rule of thumb: the app that wins is the one whose copy you trust enough to delete after. Compare the free tier, the preview, and the copy — then check the copy on the drive before you delete anything.

Which one fits you

If you want an app on Google Play today, a guided preview of the planned backup before anything copies, and an Expert mode for extra folders, that is FotoSafe: try the free run of up to 100 files first, then its one-time Pro for unlimited runs — the price is in its Play listing. If you want the repeatable free tier (5 GB per run), oldest-first order, Pause and Stop in the notification, per-file size verification, and an app that runs on Android 7.0 and later, that is SnapDisk: free manual backup up to 5 GB per run, Pro $9.99 once for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size. Either way, back up first, verify the copy, then delete — that order is what keeps your photos safe, whichever app does the copying.
