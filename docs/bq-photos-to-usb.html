Method 1: SnapDisk — proven on ten

SnapDisk is the one-tap version. It needs Android 7.0 or later, which eleven covered BQ phones clear — and the one-tap backup is proven on ten of them: each one clears the floor and lists USB OTG Yes.

  1. Install SnapDisk v0.1.2 — it is not on Google Play yet, so install the APK from the GitHub release page. You do not need an account, and there are no ads.
  2. Plug the USB drive into the phone — straight in on a Type-C phone (the X2 Pro, the X2, the X Pro, the X and the C), through a USB-C OTG adapter for a regular USB-A drive.
  3. Open SnapDisk and tap Backup. SnapDisk copies photos and videos oldest-first from the phone to the drive, in the background, with live progress, Pause and Stop.
  4. Wait for the copy to finish, then open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

Free version: backs up manually, up to 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase — auto-backup on connect and unlimited size. On the V Plus, SnapDisk installs — it clears Android 7.0 — but the one-tap backup is unproven until a drive mounts, so plug the drive in first and check the phone sees it. On the nineteen below-floor phones, SnapDisk does not install at all, so use Method 2 or 4 instead.

Method 2: file manager + OTG adapter

Same plug, no app to install. On twenty-four phones it works exactly as on any other Android phone with OTG support:

  1. Plug the USB drive into the phone — straight in on a Type-C phone, or through a micro-USB OTG adapter on a micro-USB phone.
  2. Open the phone's file manager and find the DCIM folder — that is where the camera photos live.
  3. Select the photos and videos, copy them to the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish.
  4. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

On the fourteen below-floor phones that list USB OTG Yes, the drive still mounts in the file manager even though SnapDisk does not install: the file-manager copy needs OTG, not Android 7.0. On the V Plus and the five below-floor phones with unstated OTG — the E5 4G, the E6, the E4, the 5.7 and the 4.5 — plug the drive in and check the phone sees it before trusting this method: if nothing appears, the drive does not mount and this method does not work on that phone.

Method 3: through a computer

The fallback that works on all thirty covered phones, including the ones no phone-to-USB method reaches with an app: the nineteen below-floor phones, and the six where OTG is unstated.

  1. Plug the phone into the computer with a USB cable — a plain charging cable works here, because the computer is the other end.
  2. On the phone, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer.
  3. On the computer, open the phone's DCIM folder and copy the photos to the computer, then to the USB drive.
  4. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

This is the method BQ's own user manuals name — connect the phone to a computer with the supplied USB cable and copy the files — the computer copy, then Google backup for the cloud. It needs the PC in between, so it is the fallback, not the route, when the phone and the drive are all you have.

Method 4: microSD card reader route

If the phone has a card slot — and twenty-nine do, for certain — the card is the backup, and the reader is the bridge to the USB drive:

  1. Put a microSD card in the phone and copy the DCIM folder to the card with the file manager.
  2. Take the card out, put it in a card reader, plug the reader into the computer, and copy the photos from the card to the USB drive.
  3. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

A microSD card fits twenty-nine phones for certain, so the card-reader route is proven on twenty-nine. Ceilings are stated on twenty-two, contested on one — the X5 at 32 GB against 64 GB — and unstated on six. The slot itself is unstated on the 5.7, so check that phone for a slot before buying a card for it. On the hybrid-tray models the card shares the second SIM slot, so the card means one SIM, not two. A card copy is a real local copy, but the card stays in the phone until you take it out, and opening it anywhere else needs the card reader — a USB drive plugs into any computer as it is.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud

The cloud is the last resort, and the one BQ's manuals point to alongside the computer copy: the manuals' Backup and Reset section is the Google backup, and Google Photos is the photo version of the same idea. It works on all thirty phones — every one runs Android, so Google Photos runs on all thirty. BQ On is BQ's own cloud storage, which you buy — it is storage, not a USB backup.

  1. Open Google Photos, turn on backup, and wait for the upload to finish — this needs Wi-Fi, and a big library takes a while.
  2. On a computer, open Google Photos, download the photos, and copy them to the USB drive.
  3. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

Two warnings. First, Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — check the setting before trusting a cloud copy. Second, a cloud backup is not a USB backup: your photos sit on Google's servers, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying for storage. If you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy — that is Method 1 or 2, not a cloud backup.

What this does not do

  • Does not install below Android 7.0. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and nineteen covered BQ phones run below Android 7.0 as shipped — the U Lite, the U, the U Plus, the X5 Plus, the E5s, the X5, the M5, the M5.5, the M4.5, the M10, the E4.5, the E5, the E5 4G, the E6, the E4, the A4.5, the 5, the 5.7 and the 4.5 — so SnapDisk does not install on any of them. No other BQ phone is below the floor.
  • Does not work if the phone does not host a drive. No BQ phone lists USB OTG No — but the V Plus and five below-floor phones (the E5 4G, the E6, the E4, the 5.7 and the 4.5) are unstated on OTG, so on those six the drive mounting is the test: plug the drive in and check the phone sees it.
  • Does not delete photos. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Does not back up anything but photos and videos. SnapDisk copies image and video files only — no settings, no contacts, no messages, no app data.
  • Does not back up photos from apps that hide them. If an app keeps its photos in a private folder SnapDisk cannot read, those photos stay in the app.
  • Does not compress. SnapDisk copies the original files as they are — no recompression, no resizing, no quality loss.

What to avoid

  • Buying an adapter you don't need — or the wrong one. A USB-C flash drive plugs straight into five of the thirty phones — the X2 Pro, the X2, the X Pro, the X and the C — so an adapter is only needed for a regular USB-A drive, and it must be a USB-C OTG adapter on those five, a micro-USB OTG adapter on the other twenty-five. A plain USB-C charging cable is not an OTG adapter: it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your phone up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1 or 2, not a cloud backup. BQ On is the same idea with BQ's name on it: storage you buy, not a USB backup.
  • Mistaking a PC tool for a phone-to-USB copy. A computer copy with a USB cable moves files to a computer — but it is a copy to a computer, and the backup lands on the PC. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route, not a desktop copy.
  • Mistaking a spec table or a manual page for a phone-to-USB guide. The BQ spec pages are real BQ pages — but they only list an Android version, a chipset, or a USB connector type. None of them describes copying your camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone. The BQ user manuals are the closest thing to BQ backup advice, and they name the computer copy.
  • Mistaking OTG support on one model for OTG support on the other. Twenty-four of the thirty phones list USB OTG Yes — but the V Plus does not list it at all, and neither do the E5 4G, the E6, the E4, the 5.7 or the 4.5. Do not assume the next BQ phone hosts a drive just because most of them do.
  • Mistaking a microSD card copy for a USB backup. Copying DCIM to a microSD card is a real local copy — twenty-nine phones take a card — but the card stays in the phone, and opening it anywhere else needs a card reader; a USB drive plugs into any computer as it is.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 20, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On a BQ phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive, plus BQ On — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. The file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the phone. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.
  • Trusting one spec listing. The X2 Pro is two Android numbers — 8.0 per phonesdata against 8.1 per smartprix — the M4.5 is three — 5.0, 5.1 and 5.1.1 — the 5.7 is two — 4.1 against 4.2.1 — and the X5's card is two ceilings — 32 GB against 64 GB — so check the unit before buying a drive expecting a particular spec. The Android floor is settled on every one either way: eleven clear 7.0 and nineteen run below it on every source.

Rule of thumb: thirty phones, eleven clear Android 7.0, ten list USB OTG Yes with the floor cleared, one needs the mounting test, nineteen below the floor, twenty-nine take a microSD card — the one-tap backup is proven on ten, the file-manager copy works on twenty-four, and the card-reader route is proven on twenty-nine.
