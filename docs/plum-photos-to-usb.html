Method 1: SnapDisk on the Gator 6 and Gator 7 (the one-tap route)

On the two OTG-listed phones, the automated route exists. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and both clear it — the Gator 6 runs Android 10 (Go edition), the Gator 7 runs Android 11 — and both list USB OTG:

  1. Install SnapDisk. Until the Play listing is live, install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on the Gator 5, the Gator 6 and the Gator 7 — and on the Gator 4 only if it is really on Android 7.0, not the 6.0 its manual states. It does not install on the original Gator or the Gator Plus, which run Android 4.2.2.
  2. Plug in the USB drive with a micro-USB OTG adapter. Both phones take the same micro-USB OTG adapter — a USB-C adapter does not fit either port. Buy the adapter with the OTG wiring inside it, not a plain sync cable.
  3. Tap Backup in the notification. SnapDisk offers the backup when the drive connects, then copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  4. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. Its limits: it works with a drive only on the two OTG-listed phones — not on the Gator 5 or the Gator 4, where OTG is unconfirmed — and it does not install at all on the original Gator or the Gator Plus. On the Gator 4, the Gator 5, the original Gator and the Gator Plus, Methods 3 and 4 are the routes.

Method 2: the built-in file manager and an OTG adapter (works on the two OTG phones)

This is the stock route, and the only phone route on the two OTG-listed phones besides SnapDisk. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive, and OTG is confirmed on the Gator 6 and the Gator 7:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with a micro-USB OTG adapter. On both phones that is a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain micro-USB sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive.
  2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the stock file manager on a Plum phone, so use the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On these phones the photos are the 8 MP shots from the rear camera on the Gator 6 and the Gator 7 — 13 MP on the Gator 5 and the Gator 4, 5 MP on the original Gator and the Gator Plus — plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on the OTG phones: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. On the Gator 5 and the Gator 4 there may be no phone route at all — the manual copy needs a drive the phone can host, and OTG is unconfirmed on both — and on the original Gator and the Gator Plus there is no app route at all, because neither can run SnapDisk.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the Plum phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. It is also the fallback for the original Gator and the Gator Plus, which cannot run SnapDisk at all, for the Gator 4 if it runs Android 6.0 as its manual states, and for the Gator 5, where OTG is unconfirmed. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 1 or 2 is the route, or Method 4.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (works on every model)

Every covered Plum model takes a microSD card — up to 128 GB on the Gator 7, up to 64 GB on the Gator 5 and the Gator 4 per the spec sites (32 GB per the Gator 4 manual), microSDXC with no ceiling stated on the Gator 6, and microSDHC up to 32 GB on the original Gator and the Gator Plus — and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old phone manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. One warning: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in the file manager.

Method 5: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

Plum offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — the only Plum-specific backup advice found is the devicesfaq page telling you to connect the phone to a computer with the USB cable and copy the files — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead; and its "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p). 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and Google accounts only offer 5 GB of free storage by default since May 2026, with the full 15 GB available only after verifying a phone number on the account. 3. Google backup is what Android itself names: personal settings back up to Google's servers with a Google account, and are restored to a new phone when you sign in with that account. It is a real backup — but it backs up settings, not a folder of photo files on a drive you hold. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. And one Plum-only limit: no source found confirms the current Google Photos or Drive app still installs on the Android 4.2.2 Gators. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2, 3 or 4 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What this does not do

A few honest limits, so the manual copy is not oversold:

  • No automated backup exists for the Gator 4 or the Gator 5 unless the drive mounts. The Gator 5 can run SnapDisk — it clears Android 7.0 — but no source states OTG for it, so the app may never see a drive; the Gator 4 may not even run the app, if its manual's Android 6.0 is right. The computer copy and the card-reader route are manual, one batch at a time, with no progress detail, no resume, and no verification.
  • No backup app exists for the original Gator or the Gator Plus at all. Both run Android 4.2.2, below SnapDisk's Android 7.0 minimum and below VaultDrive's Android 8 or later per its own site. The computer copy and the card-reader route are the only routes on those two.
  • Photos and videos only, from the folder you pick. By default that's DCIM — your camera photos. Photos in other folders (Downloads, app folders) only copy if you select them too, and photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the microSD card are not on the card for the Method 4 route.
  • Files over 4 GB on FAT32 are a problem. Very long videos on a FAT32 drive will not copy — copy those to a computer instead, or reformat the drive to exFAT, noting that a phone this old may not read exFAT, so FAT32 is the safer format for the phone route.
  • It never deletes. The file manager's Copy only copies. Freeing space on the device is copy, check the copy on the drive, then delete the originals yourself — never the other way round.
  • OTG is confirmed on two phones and unconfirmed on two more. The Gator 6 and the Gator 7 list OTG; the Gator 4 and the Gator 5 list only microUSB 2.0 with no OTG field in any source found — so on those two the drive mounting is the test, and the computer copy in Method 3 is the reliable route.
  • The Gator 4's Android version is two numbers in the sources found. The official manual says Android 6.0 Marshmallow; phonesdata, eIMEI24, devicesfaq and mobilexfiles say Android 7.0 Nougat. Check Settings > About phone on your unit before assuming either — if it is 6.0, SnapDisk cannot install at all, and VaultDrive cannot either.
  • The Gator 4's microSD ceiling is two numbers, too. The spec sites say up to 64 GB; the official manual says up to 32 GB. Check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 32 GB.
  • The Gator 5's IP68 is disputed. phonesdata lists IP68 dust/water proof (up to 1.2 m for 30 mins); the devicesfaq FAQ page says the Gator 5 has no water-resistance certification. Either way, the seals on a phone from 2018 are years old — do not test water resistance with a phone whose photos you have not backed up yet.
  • Only one model has a sourced microSD ceiling of 128 GB. The Gator 7 takes microSDXC up to 128 GB per the Amazon listing; the Gator 5 and the Gator 4 take up to 64 GB per the spec sites; the Gator 6 takes microSDXC with no ceiling stated; the original Gator and the Gator Plus take microSDHC up to 32 GB. Check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 32 GB.
  • No source found names the stock file manager on a Plum phone. No source found names one, so Method 2 uses the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit.
  • A microSD card is a second copy, not a USB drive. All six covered models take microSD, so a card is a second local copy you can count on here. But a card is not a USB drive: it does not plug into any computer on its own, and this article is about the drive — the card only becomes a backup once its photos are copied onto one.
  • If yours is a non-Gator Plum, the route is different. No source in this run's searches names a Gator 3, and this guide covers the Gator line only. If yours is a different Plum model, check Settings > About phone for its Android version first: anything below Android 7.0 cannot run SnapDisk at all, and the computer copy in Method 3 is the reliable route.
  • Newer does not mean OTG. The Gator 5 runs Android 8.1 — newer than the Gator 4's disputed 6.0/7.0 — but no source states OTG for it, while the Gator 6 on Android 10 Go lists it. A newer Android version is not a promise the port hosts drives — on any Plum model, plug in a drive and look for the mount notification.
  • The Gator 7's IP69 and the Gator 4, Gator 5 and Gator 6's IP68 ratings are old seals. The ratings are real — IP69 on the Gator 7 per phonesdata (IP68/IP69K per the Amazon listing), IP68 on the other three — but the seals on phones from 2017–2021 are years old. Do not test water resistance with a phone whose photos you have not backed up yet.

What to avoid

  • Assuming SnapDisk backs up to a drive on every Plum phone. SnapDisk installs on the Gator 4 (if it is really on 7.0), the Gator 5, the Gator 6 and the Gator 7 — but it can only see a drive on the two that list USB OTG: the Gator 6 and the Gator 7. Do not buy Pro for a Gator 5 or a Gator 4 expecting a drive backup, and do not trust a store listing that says "compatible" without checking the OTG listing first.
  • Assuming the Gator 4 runs Android 7.0. The spec sites say 7.0, but the official manual says 6.0 — and if the manual is right, SnapDisk cannot install at all. Check Settings > About phone on your unit before planning anything around the app.
  • Assuming OTG because the phone is new. The Gator 5 runs Android 8.1 and the Gator 7 runs Android 11, but only the Gator 7 lists OTG — the Gator 5 lists only microUSB 2.0. Plug in a drive and look for the mount notification before buying an adapter.
  • Buying a USB-C adapter for a Gator. Every covered model takes micro-USB 2.0 — the Gator 6 and the Gator 7 included. A USB-C adapter does not fit any Gator port; buy a micro-USB OTG adapter with the OTG wiring inside it.
  • Assuming the Gator 4 or Gator 5 hosts a drive. No source found states OTG for either phone. On those two the drive mounting is the test, and the computer copy in Method 3 is the reliable route.
  • Assuming the cloud still works on Android 4.2.2. No source found confirms the current Google Photos or Drive app still installs on the original Gator or the Gator Plus. If the cloud app is not already on your phone and working, do not plan a backup around installing it now.
  • Assuming your photos are on the microSD card. On these phones the camera may save to internal storage or to the card depending on the setting. If you pull the card for Method 4 without checking, you may copy an empty DCIM folder while the photos sit in internal storage. Check where the photos live first.
  • Assuming the Gator 4's microSD ceiling or the Gator 5's IP68. The Gator 4 is up to 64 GB per the spec sites and up to 32 GB per its manual; the Gator 5 is IP68 per phonesdata and uncertified per the devicesfaq FAQ page. Check Settings > About phone and the card slot on your unit before sizing anything around any of these numbers.
  • Assuming Plum has a backup app. No Plum backup app to a USB drive turned up in any source. The one-tap route on the two OTG phones is SnapDisk, not a Plum app — and on the original Gator and the Gator Plus there is no app route at all.
  • Assuming an old IP68 or IP69 rating still holds. The Gator 7 is IP69 per phonesdata (IP68/IP69K per the Amazon listing) and the Gator 4, Gator 5 and Gator 6 are IP68 — but the seals on phones from 2017–2021 are years old. Do not test water resistance with a phone whose photos you have not backed up yet.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the device — never the other way round. The file manager only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. A file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the device. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish before unplugging, and eject the drive from the notification or Settings > Storage first.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your device up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1, 2, 3 or 4, not a cloud backup. And Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and since May 2026 Google accounts get only 5 GB free by default, with the full 15 GB only after verifying a phone number. On the Android 4.2.2 Gators the cloud apps may not even install any more.
  • Mistaking a generic guide for a Plum guide. The softhandtech memory-stick guide and the Alibaba local-backup guide are real documents — but they are generic phone guides, and the MacRumors thread and the coolmuster guide are about iPhones. None of them describes backing up a Plum phone's camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone. The generic Android guides in the search results are the closest thing to a phone-to-USB guide, and they say nothing about Plum.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the device and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy or a card reader is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On a Plum phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and on the Android 4.2.2 Gators those apps may not install at all any more.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip. On the Gator 6 or the Gator 7, SnapDisk picks them for you; on the Gator 5 or the Gator 4, plug a drive in first and look for the mount notification before buying anything, and copy the whole DCIM folder in one go, check it on the drive, then delete from the device; on the original Gator or the Gator Plus, the computer copy or the card reader is the route — neither phone can run a backup app at all.
