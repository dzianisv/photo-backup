How to back up DCIM to OTG storage on Android

DCIM is where your Android phone keeps its camera photos — and OTG is how a USB drive plugs straight into that phone. Put the two together and your whole camera roll copies to a drive you can hold, with no computer and no upload in between.

November 14, 2026 · 4 min read

TL;DR: To back up DCIM to OTG storage on Android, plug a USB drive into your phone through an OTG adapter and copy the DCIM folder to it. The easy way is SnapDisk: plug in the drive, tap Backup in the notification, and your photos and videos copy oldest-first in the background, with live progress and Pause / Stop. The free version covers 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size. If you'd rather do it by hand, the Files app does the same copy — just slower and with no resume if you unplug mid-copy.

What DCIM and OTG mean

  • DCIM is the folder Android uses for camera photos and videos, at /sdcard/DCIM. Camera shots live in DCIM → Camera; screenshots in DCIM → Screenshots. If you back up DCIM, you back up the camera roll.
  • OTG (USB On-The-Go) lets your phone act as the host for a USB drive. Plug the drive in — directly if it's USB-C, or through a USB-C OTG adapter for a regular USB stick — and the phone reads it like a computer would. Most modern Android phones support OTG; if the phone doesn't see the drive, try a different adapter.

One format note: drives formatted FAT32 can't hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit.

What you need

  • An Android phone (Android 7.0 or later for SnapDisk).
  • An OTG drive your phone can read — a USB-C flash drive, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter. A spinning hard drive may need its own power.
  • Enough space on the drive for the DCIM folder you want to keep. Check the drive's free space first — a phone's camera roll is often tens of gigabytes.

Method 1: SnapDisk (one tap, background copy)

SnapDisk is a free Android app that does exactly this job and nothing else. Install it from the v0.1.2 APK (it's not on Google Play yet), then:

  1. Plug in the OTG drive. SnapDisk offers a backup in a notification: "External storage is connected. Backup photos?"
  2. Tap Backup. Photos and videos copy oldest-first — your earliest photos first — in the background, while you use the phone normally. By default it copies from /sdcard/DCIM to /disk/DCIM on the drive; both folders can be changed in Settings.
  3. Watch the progress notification — real percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left — with Pause and Stop. You can pause and resume any time.
  4. Wait for "Backup complete." Every file is size-verified after copying, so the counts are proof, not a promise. Files that already copied are never copied twice.

If you unplug the drive mid-copy, the backup pauses with the full error shown and resumes where it stopped when the drive is back. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup.

The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device.

Method 2: by hand in the Files app (no app needed)

If you'd rather not install anything, Android's built-in Files app can copy DCIM to the OTG drive:

  1. Plug in the OTG drive. A notification or a "USB drive" entry should appear in the Files app under Storage devices.
  2. Open Files, browse to Internal storage → DCIM. Long-press the DCIM folder to select the whole thing — or open DCIM → Camera and select the photos inside it.
  3. Tap ⋮ → Copy to (or Move to, if you want them off the phone), then choose the external drive as the destination.
  4. Wait for the copy to finish — and don't unplug the drive until the Files app says it's done.

This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason SnapDisk exists: no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time. For a full DCIM folder, the app's oldest-first background copy is the calmer option.

What to avoid

  • Moving instead of copying the first time. "Move to" deletes the originals as it goes. Copy first, check the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round.
  • Copying without verifying. A copy that reports "done" without checking sizes can silently leave you with half a file. Whatever method you use, spot-check a few photos on the drive before deleting them from the phone.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling an OTG drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.

Rule of thumb: DCIM is the whole camera roll in one folder. Back it up as a folder, check a few files open on the drive, and only then free the space on the phone.
