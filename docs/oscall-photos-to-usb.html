  4. Look for the mount notification. On the twenty-two proven phones Android notices the drive and offers it as storage, and SnapDisk picks it as the To folder. On the eight mounting-test phones — the C20, C20 Pro, S60 Pro, Tiger 8, Pilot 5, Flat 3C, Flat 3C Mini and Modern 8 — if nothing appears, check Settings > Storage: if the drive is listed there, tap it and mount it, then run the backup. If the drive never shows up on one of those eight, the phone does not host it, and Method 3 is the route — and on the C20 Pro, FindPare lists USB OTG No, so expect that outcome there.
  5. Check the copy, then delete the originals. Open the drive in Files and count: the backup is only done when every photo is on the drive and opens. Then delete the originals on the phone if you need the space — and only then.

SnapDisk copies the photos byte-for-byte: the file on the drive is the same file the phone took, with the same pixels. There is no re-encoding step, so nothing is lost in the copy — the only place quality gets lost is a cloud backup with a "Storage saver" setting.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter

Method 2 is the manual version of the same thing — no app, just the phone's file manager and a drive. No source names Oscall's bundled file manager, so this method says "the file manager" generically rather than naming one. It works on the twenty-two proven phones, and on the eight mounting-test phones if the drive shows up in Settings.

  1. Plug the drive into the phone. On twenty-six covered phones that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in. On the three micro-USB models that is a micro-USB OTG adapter between the phone and the drive.
  2. Open the file manager and find the DCIM folder. Your camera photos live in the DCIM folder — on most phones that is Internal storage > DCIM > Camera. Screenshots live in Pictures > Screenshots, and WhatsApp photos in Android > media > com.whatsapp > WhatsApp > Media > WhatsApp Images.
  3. Select the photos and copy them to the drive. Long-press one photo, select the rest, tap Copy, navigate to the USB drive and tap Paste. Copy, not Move — see "What to avoid" below.
  4. Wait, then check. Copying takes time — thousands of photos can take half an hour or more. Open the drive in the file manager and count before you delete anything.

This is the same copy Method 1 does, done by hand. It copies the original files as they are — no re-encoding, no quality loss.

Method 3: through a computer

Method 3 is the fallback — and the one the per-model backup FAQ calls the most economical method. It works on all thirty, including the eight mounting-test phones if the drive never shows up on the phone, and the C20 Pro if FindPare's USB OTG No is right. Plug the phone into a computer with the USB cable, open the phone as a drive, and drag the DCIM folder onto a USB drive. Copy, not Move, and check the copy before deleting anything. It works — it just takes the manual step Method 1 does for you.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route

Method 4 is the card route, and on Oscall it is a real one at full scale: a microSD card fits all thirty phones for certain, so this route is proven on all thirty — unlike Vernee, where it is proven on twenty of twenty-one, and unlike RedMagic, where it is proven on none of the fourteen.

  1. Put the card in the phone. Card ceilings are stated on twenty-two, contested on two — the C30 (256 or 512 GB) and the Pilot 3 (1 or 2 TB) — and unstated on six — the C60, the S70 Pro, the Tiger 8, the Flat 2, the Flat 2C and the Flat 3C — so buy at or under the lower number on the contested two if you want the ceiling every source agrees on.
  2. Set the camera to save to the card. Open the Camera app, go to Settings, and set Storage to the SD card. New photos then land on the card, not the internal storage.
  3. Move the photos already on the phone. Open the file manager, select the DCIM folder, and move it to the card. The photos already on the phone do not move themselves — they live in internal storage until you move them.
  4. Take the card out and read it in a card reader. Plug the reader into the computer, or into the phone with an OTG adapter, and copy the photos to a USB drive.

Two warnings: the photos already on the phone live in internal storage, not on the card — so moving the card alone backs up nothing until you move them — and the card is only a backup if the photos end up on it, so check the card before you delete anything on the phone.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud

Method 5 is the cloud route — and the one Oscall's per-model FAQ names for photos alone: Google Photos uploads them automatically when on Wi-Fi. It works on all thirty, it is free up to the Google account's 15 GB, and it is the only method here that backs up without a cable. But it is not a USB backup: the photos live on Google's servers, not on a drive you hold, and the "Storage saver" setting compresses them — so check that setting before trusting a cloud copy. If you want the originals on a drive you own, Method 1 or Method 2 is the route.

What this does not do

  • It does not install below Android 7.0. No covered Oscall phone runs below Android 7.0 as shipped — the lowest is Android 11 — so this limit bites none of the thirty. VaultDrive needs Android 8 and FotoSafe needs Android 9, so on an Oscall phone there is no rival-app version limit either — every covered phone clears both floors on shipped software.
  • It does not work if the phone does not host a drive. The eight mounting-test phones — the C20, C20 Pro, S60 Pro, Tiger 8, Pilot 5, Flat 3C, Flat 3C Mini and Modern 8 — may or may not mount a drive, because no source settles OTG on them. The C20 Pro carries the article's only USB OTG No listing (FindPare) — if that listing is right, only Methods 3, 4 and 5 work on it. If the drive never shows up on one of those eight, Method 3 is the route.
  • It does not delete photos. A backup copies the photos to the drive; deleting the originals is a separate step, and only after you have checked the copy.
  • It does not back up your settings, contacts or messages. It backs up photos and videos — the files in your camera roll.
  • It does not back up photos from apps that hide them. Photos saved inside an app's private storage — WhatsApp's hidden media, or a locked album — may not be in the DCIM folder, so check the app's own export.
  • It does not compress or shrink your photos. The copy is the original file — the same pixels, the same size.

What to avoid

  • Don't move instead of copying. Move deletes the original as it goes — if the copy fails halfway, the photos are gone. Copy first, check the copy, then delete.
  • Don't unplug the drive until the copy is done. Unplugging mid-copy corrupts the files being written. Wait for the progress to finish, then eject the drive in Settings before pulling it out.
  • Don't buy a micro-USB adapter for a Type-C phone. Twenty-six covered phones take USB Type-C — a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and the adapter is the wrong buy.
  • Don't buy a USB-C drive expecting it to plug into one of the three micro-USB phones. Those three — the C20 Pro, the C60 and the S60 Pro — are micro-USB, and a USB-C drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter between them.
  • Don't buy a card bigger than the contested ceiling without checking. Two phones have two card ceilings — the C30 (256 or 512 GB) and the Pilot 3 (1 or 2 TB) — so buy at or under the lower number if you want the ceiling every source agrees on.
  • Don't buy a card bigger than the unstated ceiling without checking. Six phones have no ceiling in any source — the C60, the S70 Pro, the Tiger 8, the Flat 2, the Flat 2C and the Flat 3C — so check the phone first before buying a big card for one of those six.
  • Don't count on VaultDrive's AppBrain floor on every Oscall phone. Its own site says Android 8, which all thirty clear — but AppBrain lists Android 12.1, which twenty-one clear, and the S70 Pro only on its higher (13) listing.
  • Don't count on FotoSafe on every Oscall phone. It needs Android 9 with OTG — twenty-two are proven, but the other eight need the mounting test first.
  • Don't trust a single S70 Pro listing. The S70 Pro is Android 13 per PhoneArena and 12 per phonesdata, gsm-arena, mobiledokan and mobilemaya. Check the version on the unit, not the name.
  • Don't trust a single C30 or Pilot 3 ceiling. The C30's ceiling is 256 GB per Oscall's own site against 512 GB per philnews, and the Pilot 3's is 1 TB per PhoneArena against 2 TB per ruggedzone.
  • Don't trust a single C20 Pro OTG listing either. FindPare lists USB OTG No for the C20 Pro, and every other source is silent on its OTG — plug a drive in and see, and keep Methods 3 to 5 ready.
  • Don't trust a single Flat 1C DokeOS listing either. The Flat 1C is DokeOS 3.0 Go per Oscall's own site against 4.0 Go per manuals.plus.
  • Don't trust mobilemaya on the Modern 8. It claims USB OTG support for the Modern 8, but the same source contradicts itself on the card slot — so this article does not credit the OTG claim, and the Modern 8 stays in the mounting-test group.
  • Don't let one folder hold every photo. A backup is only a backup if the copy exists somewhere else — a drive, a card, or the cloud. One copy is not a backup.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a copy. A backup finds your photos, copies them all, and tells you when it's done. On an Oscall phone the honest position is simple: thirty phones, thirty clear Android 7.0, twenty-two list USB OTG Yes, thirty take a microSD card — the one-tap backup is proven on twenty-two, and the card-reader route is proven on all thirty.
