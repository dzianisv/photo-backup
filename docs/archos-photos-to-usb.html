Method 1: SnapDisk on the Saphir 50X, 50 Graphite and 55 Graphite (the one-tap route)

On the three Android 7.0 phones, the automated route exists. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and these three run exactly 7.0 — the floor, not a comfortable margin — and all three list USB OTG:

  1. Install SnapDisk. Until the Play listing is live, install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on the Saphir 50X, the 50 Graphite and the 55 Graphite — and not on the 50 Saphir.
  2. Plug in the USB drive with the right OTG adapter. The Saphir 50X takes a micro-USB OTG adapter — one ships in the box. On the Graphites, check your unit's port first: the connector is disputed between microUSB and Type-C, so buy the adapter that fits the port you can see, not the one a spec table guesses.
  3. Tap Backup in the notification. SnapDisk offers the backup when the drive connects, then copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  4. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. Its limits: it installs only on the three Android 7.0 phones — not on the 50 Saphir, not on any Archos phone running Android 6.0 or older — and on the 101 Saphir tablet it installs but the drive may never mount, because OTG is disputed there. On the 50 Saphir, Method 2 is the route.

Method 2: the built-in file manager and an OTG adapter (works on all four phones)

This is the stock route, and the only phone route on the 50 Saphir. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive, and OTG is confirmed on all four covered phones:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with an OTG adapter that fits your unit. On the 50 Saphir and the Saphir 50X that is a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain micro-USB sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. On the Graphites, check the port first: microUSB per eIMEI24, Type-C per MobileGizmo and the launch coverage. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive.
  2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the stock file manager on an Archos phone, so use the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit — Archos ships near-stock Android with a few custom apps.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On these phones the photos are the 13 MP shots from the rear camera, plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on the newer phones: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. On the 50 Saphir there is no app that removes those limits — the manual copy is the best the phone offers.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the Archos phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. It is also the fallback for any Archos phone whose USB port never hosts a drive, and for the 101 Saphir tablet, where OTG is disputed. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 1 or 2 is the route, or Method 4.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (works on every model)

Every covered Archos model takes a microSD card up to 128 GB per most sources (one source says 64 GB for the 50X), and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old Archos manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. One warning: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in the file manager.

Method 5: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

Archos offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no Archos backup app turned up in any source — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead; and its "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p). 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and Google accounts only offer 5 GB of free storage by default since May 2026, with the full 15 GB available only after verifying a phone number on the account. 3. Google backup is what Android itself names: personal settings back up to Google's servers with a Google account, and are restored to a new phone when you sign in with that account. It is a real backup — but it backs up settings, not a folder of photo files on a drive you hold. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. And one Archos-only limit: no source found confirms the current Google Photos or Drive app still installs on the 50 Saphir's Android 6.0. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2, 3 or 4 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What this does not do

A few honest limits, so the manual copy is not oversold:

  • No automated backup exists for the 50 Saphir. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 and VaultDrive needs Android 8 — the 50 Saphir runs Android 6.0, and no Android 7.0 update was ever announced — so no current backup app installs on the rugged phone most people mean by "Archos". The file-manager copy is manual, one batch at a time, with no progress detail, no resume, and no verification.
  • Photos and videos only, from the folder you pick. By default that's DCIM — your camera photos. Photos in other folders (Downloads, app folders) only copy if you select them too, and photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the microSD card are not on the card for the Method 4 route.
  • Files over 4 GB on FAT32 are a problem. Very long videos on a FAT32 drive will not copy — copy those to a computer instead, or reformat the drive to exFAT, noting that a phone this old may not read exFAT, so FAT32 is the safer format for the phone route.
  • It never deletes. The file manager's Copy only copies. Freeing space on the device is copy, check the copy on the drive, then delete the originals yourself — never the other way round.
  • OTG is confirmed on all four phones — but the Graphites' connector is disputed, and the tablet's OTG is disputed too. eIMEI24 lists microUSB 2.0 with OTG for both Graphites, while MobileGizmo lists Type-C with OTG for the 50 Graphite and the launch coverage says Type-C for the 55 Graphite — check your unit's port before buying an adapter. On the 101 Saphir tablet, gadgets360 lists USB OTG No and gadgetsnow lists Yes, so plug in a drive and look for the mount notification before buying anything.
  • The 50 Saphir is below SnapDisk's Android 7.0 minimum — and the three phones that clear it do so exactly. The Saphir 50X, the 50 Graphite and the 55 Graphite run Android 7.0, which is SnapDisk's floor (API 24), not a comfortable margin. None of the four phones can run VaultDrive, which needs Android 8 or later per its own site — AppBrain lists 12.1 or later, which no covered Archos phone clears either.
  • The 50 Saphir's price and IP68 depth are two numbers in the sources found. The launch price is €199 per the launch coverage and €230 for Notebookcheck's test model; the immersion depth is 1 m for 30 minutes per Notebookcheck and Android Headlines and 1.5 m per accio. Either way it is a rugged phone with a decade-old seal — do not test water resistance with a phone whose photos you have not backed up yet.
  • The Saphir 50X's microSD ceiling and processor speed are two numbers in the sources found, too. The card ceiling is up to 128 GB per phonesdata and eIMEI24 and up to 64 GB per productindetail; the processor is 1.3 GHz per eIMEI24 and 1.5 GHz per MobileGizmo. Check Settings > About phone and the card slot on your unit before sizing anything around either number.
  • The 55 Graphite's screen is two numbers in the sources found. gadgets360 lists 720x1280 and the launch coverage lists Full HD 1920x1080. The screen changes nothing about the backup — but check your unit before trusting either number for anything else.
  • The 50 Graphite's battery is two numbers in the sources found. The launch coverage, MobileGizmo and phonesreview list 2400 mAh, and gadgets360 lists 3000 mAh. Either way it is a small battery next to the 50 Saphir's 5000 mAh — keep the phone charged while copying, and do not start a big copy on a low battery.
  • No source found names the stock file manager on an Archos phone. No source found names one, so Method 2 uses the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit — on these phones that is the stock file manager under Archos's near-stock Android.
  • A microSD card is a second copy, not a USB drive. All five covered models take microSD up to 128 GB per most sources, so a card is a second local copy you can count on here. But a card is not a USB drive: it does not plug into any computer on its own, and this article is about the drive — the card only becomes a backup once its photos are copied onto one.
  • If yours is an ARCHOS media player or an old tablet, the route is different. The search results for this query are an AV 700 media-player manual and an ARCHOS 70 tablet doc: both describe copying photos INTO the device from a camera or a drive, not out to a drive. A media player has no phone file manager and no backup app, so the route is a card reader or a computer, as those manuals describe. Nothing in this article's phone methods applies to a media player.
  • Newer Archos phones can still fail to host a drive. The Oxygen 63 runs Android 9.0 but its USB lists only Charging and Mass storage, and the Access 50S runs Android 8.1 Go with USB OTG listed as No. A newer Android version is not a promise the port hosts drives — on any Archos model, plug in a drive and look for the mount notification.

What to avoid

  • Assuming a backup app installs on the 50 Saphir. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 and VaultDrive needs Android 8 — the 50 Saphir runs Android 6.0, and no Android 7.0 update was ever announced. Do not buy Pro, do not sideload an APK built for a newer Android, and do not trust a store listing that says "compatible" without checking the Android floor first.
  • Assuming OTG because the port is micro-USB — or because the phone is new. All four covered phones list OTG, but the Graphites' connector is disputed between microUSB and Type-C, and the Oxygen 63 and Access 50S prove a newer Archos can still lack OTG entirely. Plug in a drive and look for the mount notification before buying an adapter.
  • Buying the wrong adapter for a Graphite. eIMEI24 lists microUSB 2.0 with OTG for both Graphites, while MobileGizmo lists Type-C with OTG for the 50 Graphite and the launch coverage says Type-C for the 55 Graphite. Look at the port on your unit before buying — a micro-USB adapter does not fit a Type-C port, and a Type-C adapter does not fit a micro-USB port.
  • Assuming the 101 Saphir tablet hosts a drive. gadgets360 lists USB OTG No for the tablet and gadgetsnow lists Yes, and the connector is disputed too — Type-C per gadgetsnow, microUSB per PhoneArena. On the tablet the drive mounting is the test, and the computer copy in Method 3 is the reliable route.
  • Assuming the cloud still works on Android 6.0. No source found confirms the current Google Photos or Drive app still installs on the 50 Saphir's Android 6.0. If the cloud app is not already on your phone and working, do not plan a backup around installing it now.
  • Assuming your photos are on the microSD card. On these phones the camera may save to internal storage or to the card depending on the setting. If you pull the card for Method 4 without checking, you may copy an empty DCIM folder while the photos sit in internal storage. Check where the photos live first.
  • Assuming the 50 Saphir's price, the 50X's card ceiling, or the 55 Graphite's screen. The 50 Saphir is €199 at launch and €230 for Notebookcheck's test model; the 50X takes microSD up to 128 GB per most sources and up to 64 GB per one; the 55 Graphite is 720x1280 per gadgets360 and Full HD per the launch coverage. Check Settings > About phone and the card slot on your unit before sizing anything around any of these numbers.
  • Assuming Archos has a backup app. No Archos backup app to a USB drive turned up in any source. The one-tap route on the three Android 7.0 phones is SnapDisk, not an Archos app — and on the 50 Saphir there is no app route at all.
  • Assuming the 50 Saphir's IP68 rating still holds. The immersion depth is 1 m for 30 minutes per Notebookcheck and Android Headlines and 1.5 m per accio — but the seals on a phone from 2017 are years old. Do not test water resistance with a phone whose photos you have not backed up yet.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the device — never the other way round. The file manager only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. A file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the device. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish before unplugging, and eject the drive from the notification or Settings > Storage first.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your device up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1, 2, 3 or 4, not a cloud backup. And Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and since May 2026 Google accounts get only 5 GB free by default, with the full 15 GB only after verifying a phone number. On the 50 Saphir the cloud apps may not even install any more.
  • Mistaking a media-player manual or a tablet doc for a phone-to-USB guide. The AV 700 manual and the ARCHOS 70 doc are real documents — but they describe transferring photos INTO the device from a camera or a drive. Neither describes copying your phone's camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone. The generic Android guides in the search results are the closest thing to a phone-to-USB guide, and they say nothing about Archos.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the device and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy or a card reader is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On an Archos phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and on the 50 Saphir those apps may not install at all any more.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip. On the Saphir 50X, the 50 Graphite or the 55 Graphite, SnapDisk picks them for you; on the 50 Saphir there is no app that does, so copy the whole DCIM folder in one go, check it on the drive, then delete from the device.
