Method 1: SnapDisk on all fourteen (the one-tap route)

On all fourteen phones that clear Android 7.0 and list USB OTG, the automated route exists. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and all fourteen clear it — the Yuva 2 and Yuva 2 Pro run 12, the Agni 2 5G, Blaze 2 5G, Blaze Curve 5G, Storm 5G, Yuva 3 and Yuva 3 Pro run 13, the Agni 3 5G, Blaze 3 5G, Blaze X 5G, Yuva 2 5G and Yuva 4 run 14, and the Storm Play 5G runs 15 — and all fourteen list USB OTG:

  1. Install SnapDisk. Until the Play listing is live, install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on all fourteen. It does not fail to install on any covered Lava phone: every one of the fourteen clears Android 7.0 by five versions or more.
  2. Plug in the USB drive. On all fourteen a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and a regular USB drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter. There is no micro-USB model in this guide, so buy the adapter with the USB-C plug and the OTG wiring inside it, not a plain sync cable.
  3. Tap Backup in the notification. SnapDisk offers the backup when the drive connects, then copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  4. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. Its limits: it works only on phones that host a drive — and every covered Lava phone lists OTG, so there is no covered model it does not work on. The honest limit in this guide is the card, not the drive: on the Agni 3 5G, the Blaze Curve 5G and the Blaze X 5G there is no card slot, so the USB drive is the only local copy and there is no card-reader fallback.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter (works on all fourteen)

This is the stock route, and the only phone route on all fourteen besides SnapDisk. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive, and OTG is listed on all fourteen covered models:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter. On all fourteen a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and a regular USB drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain USB-C sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive.
  2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the stock file manager on a Lava phone, so use Files by Google or the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On these phones the photos are the 13 MP shots from the rear camera on the Yuva 2, Yuva 2 Pro and Yuva 3 — 50 MP on the Agni 2 5G, Agni 3 5G, Blaze 2 5G, Blaze 3 5G, Storm 5G, Storm Play 5G, Yuva 2 5G, Yuva 3 Pro and Yuva 4, 64 MP on the Blaze Curve 5G and Blaze X 5G — plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on all fourteen phones: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the Lava phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. It is also the fallback for the Agni 3 5G, the Blaze Curve 5G and the Blaze X 5G if you want a second copy beyond the drive, since those three have no card slot for the card-reader route. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 1 or 2 is the route, or Method 4.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (works on the ten with a card)

Ten of the fourteen covered Lava models take a microSD card — up to 1 TB on the Blaze 3 5G, Storm 5G, Storm Play 5G and Yuva 4, up to 512 GB on the Yuva 2 and Yuva 2 5G, up to 256 GB on the Yuva 2 Pro — and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old phone manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. Two warnings: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in the file manager. And this route does not exist on the Agni 3 5G, the Blaze Curve 5G or the Blaze X 5G, which take no card at all, and the Agni 2 5G's slot is disputed — a hybrid slot per smartprix, no slot at all per mobiledokan and mysmartprice — so check the tray on your unit before buying a card for that model.

Method 5: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

Lava offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no Lava-specific backup advice turned up in any source beyond a computer copy, Google Drive, or Google Photos — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead; and its "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p). 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and Google accounts only offer 5 GB of free storage by default since May 2026, with the full 15 GB available only after verifying a phone number on the account. 3. Google backup is what Android itself names: personal settings back up to Google's servers with a Google account, and are restored to a new phone when you sign in with that account. It is a real backup — but it backs up settings, not a folder of photo files on a drive you hold. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2, 3 or 4 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

  • Buying the wrong adapter for the port. All fourteen covered models take USB Type-C 2.0, so buy a USB-C OTG adapter or a USB-C flash drive. A micro-USB adapter does not fit a USB-C port — and there is no micro-USB model in this guide.

  • Assuming your photos are on the microSD card. On these phones the camera may save to internal storage or to the card depending on the setting.   • Assuming the Blaze 2 5G's, Yuva 3's or Yuva 3 Pro's microSD ceiling. All three take microSD, but no source found states a ceiling for any of them. Check the card slot on your unit before sizing anything around a ceiling number for those three.
  • Assuming Lava has a backup app. No Lava backup app to a USB drive turned up in any source. The one-tap route on all fourteen phones is SnapDisk, not a Lava app.
  • Assuming the cheapest Yuva is too weak to run the app. The Yuva 2 runs Android 12 on a Unisoc T606 with 3 GB of RAM, and it can install SnapDisk — the Android version is what matters, not the price.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the device — never the other way round. The file manager only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. A file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the device. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish before unplugging, and eject the drive from the notification or Settings > Storage first.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your device up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1, 2, 3 or 4, not a cloud backup.
