Method 1: SnapDisk — proven on five, test on two, blocked on four

On the five proven phones — the Z18, Z20, Z30 Pro, Z40 Pro and Z60 Ultra — SnapDisk installs and the drive mounts, so the one-tap backup works as designed. On the Z17 and the Z50 SnapDisk installs but the OTG is contested in the sources, so the drive mounting is the test before you count on it. On the four oldest — the Z5, Z7, Z9 and Z11 — SnapDisk does not install at all: every one of them runs Android 6.0.1 or older, below SnapDisk's Android 7.0 minimum, so the installer refuses it, and no source found documents a custom-ROM route past the floor for any of them:

  1. Check your Android version first. Settings > About phone shows the version. If it says 6.0.1 or lower, SnapDisk will not install — do not buy a drive expecting the one-tap backup. If it says 7.1.1 or higher, you are on one of the seven that clear the floor: skip to step 2.
  2. Install SnapDisk from the GitHub APK. The Play listing is being prepared, so until then install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on the Z17, Z18, Z20, Z30 Pro, Z40 Pro, Z50 and Z60 Ultra.
  3. Plug in the USB drive. On the five proven phones and the two test phones that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction.
  4. Look for the mount notification. Tap Backup in the notification and SnapDisk copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  5. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything. On the Z60 Ultra the drive can be formatted FAT32, exFAT or NTFS — if a single file is over 4 GB, FAT32 will refuse it, so exFAT is the safer format there.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification — and it is proven on five of the eleven, needs a mounting test on two more, and is blocked on the four oldest by the version floor itself: SnapDisk needs Android 7.0, and the Z5, Z7, Z9 and Z11 do not have it.

The copies are the original files. SnapDisk streams each file byte-for-byte from the device to the drive — the code reads the file and writes the same bytes out, with no decode, no recompression, and no resize — and then checks that the copy on the drive is the same size as the original. Other apps copy originals byte-for-byte too; what SnapDisk adds is the automated whole-library run: oldest-first order, progress with Pause and Stop, resume where it stopped, and skip-if-already-copied on the next run. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter (works wherever the drive mounts)

This is the stock route, and the only phone route that works on the four oldest Nubia phones at all. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive. On the eight that list OTG Yes — the Z7, Z9, Z11, Z18, Z20, Z30 Pro, Z40 Pro and Z60 Ultra — the drive should mount. On the Z5 no source states OTG at all, and on the Z17 and the Z50 the sources disagree on OTG, so on those three the drive mounting is the test:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with an OTG adapter. On the Z5, Z7 and Z9 that is a micro-USB OTG adapter; on the Z11 onward it is a USB-C OTG adapter, or a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in, with the OTG wiring inside the adapter — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive — and if the drive never shows up, the phone does not host it, and Method 3 is the route.
  2. Open File Manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. Nubia's UI ships its own File Manager app, so open File Manager on your unit.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On the eight where this method should work, the photos are the 13 MP shots from the rear camera on the Z7, the 16 MP shots on the Z9 and Z11, the 24 MP + 16 MP shots on the Z18, the 48 MP shots on the Z20, the 64 MP shots on the Z30 Pro, Z40 Pro and Z50, and the 50 MP shots on the Z60 Ultra, plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy, if the drive mounts at all. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on the five phones where SnapDisk runs: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. And it is proven on eight of the eleven — on the Z5, the Z17 and the Z50 the mounting is the test.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the Nubia phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no Android-version question and no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. It is also the only phone-to-drive route for a Z5 if the drive never mounts on your unit, and the route for a Z17 or a Z50 if the drive never mounts on yours. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 2 is the route on the eight where the drive mounts, or Method 4 on the one model that takes a card for certain.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (proven on one of eleven)

Only one covered Nubia model takes a microSD card for certain — the Z11, on the SIM 2 slot, with a ceiling that is three numbers: up to 200 GB per MobileGizmo, Smartprix and 91mobiles, up to 256 GB per aivanet, up to 512 GB per GSMArena and phonesdata — and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old phone manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. Two warnings: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in File Manager. And the card is contested on three more models — the Z7, the Z9 and the Z17 — so on those, check your unit's SIM tray before counting on this route. Unlike most brands in this series, where the route is proven on nearly every model, only one of eleven Nubia flagships takes a card for certain — so for this brand the card-reader route is the exception, not the fallback.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud — not phone-to-USB plain files

Nubia offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no Nubia-specific backup advice turned up in any source beyond a computer copy or Google Photos — so the stock options are the clouds, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is the backup on the models that shipped with Google services: turn backup on in the Photos app and your photos save to your Google Account — but that is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and the Storage saver setting compresses photos. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2 or 4 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What this does not do

  • It does not back up your settings or your contacts. SnapDisk copies photos, videos, and any files you pick — the files themselves, exactly as they are. App settings, call logs, texts, and your accounts stay with your phone and your Nubia account. For those, Android's built-in backup is the tool — it backs up personal settings to Google's servers with a Google account — not SnapDisk.
  • It does not delete photos from your phone after copying. Deleting is always your choice, after you check the copy on the drive. SnapDisk never deletes originals — it only copies.
  • It does not install on the four oldest Nubia flagships. The Z5, Z7, Z9 and Z11 run Android 6.0.1 or older, below SnapDisk's 7.0 floor. No source found documents a custom-ROM route past the floor for any of them.
  • It does not work if your phone does not host a drive. On the Z5 no source states OTG at all, and on the Z17 and the Z50 the sources disagree on OTG, so if the drive never mounts on your unit, no backup app of any kind can copy your photos straight to a USB drive on it. If the drive never mounts, that settles it for your unit — use Method 3 instead.
  • It does not back up photos from apps that hide them. Some apps keep photos in private folders SnapDisk cannot read. Those stay on the phone — check each app's own export or share option.
  • It does not compress your photos. It copies the original files byte-for-byte, so the copy is the same size as the original. On the Z20, Z30 Pro, Z40 Pro, Z50 and Z60 Ultra, with their 48 MP and 64 MP cameras, that is real storage on the drive — bring a drive with room.

What to avoid

  • Don't move files instead of copying them. The file manager's Move deletes the original as it goes. Copy first, check the copy on the drive, and only then delete the originals from the phone if you need the space.
  • Don't unplug the drive until the copy is done. If the copy is still running — SnapDisk shows progress, speed and ETA — unplugging leaves half-copied files. Wait for it to finish, then eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it.
  • Don't buy the wrong adapter for your phone's port. The Z5, Z7 and Z9 take micro-USB, so those three need a micro-USB OTG adapter; the Z11 onward take USB Type-C. A micro-USB adapter does not fit a Z11 or anything newer, and a USB-C adapter does not fit a Z5, Z7 or Z9.
  • Don't buy a drive expecting it to mount on the Z5, the Z17 or the Z50 without testing. No source states OTG for the Z5, and the sources disagree on OTG for the Z17 and the Z50, so a no-mount settles it for your unit. Use Method 3 instead.
  • Don't buy a drive expecting SnapDisk to install on the four oldest flagships. The Z5, Z7, Z9 and Z11 run Android 6.0.1 or older, below SnapDisk's 7.0 floor — so the APK will not install as the phone shipped.
  • Don't buy a card for the Z11 on a ceiling figure. The ceiling is three numbers — 200 GB, 256 GB and 512 GB — so check your unit's slot before buying a large card, and do not count on the card route at all on the Z7, Z9 or Z17, where the card itself is contested.
  • Don't let one folder hold every photo. SnapDisk copies oldest-first, and the copy is the same size as the original, so a full camera roll on a phone with a 48 MP or 64 MP camera — the Z20, Z30 Pro, Z40 Pro, Z50 and Z60 Ultra — fills a small drive fast. Bring a drive with room, or back up in batches and check the copy before deleting anything.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a copy. A backup finds your photos, copies them all, and tells you when it's done. On a Nubia phone the honest position splits by age: on the five newest flagships the one-tap backup is proven, on the Z17 and the Z50 it waits on a mounting test, and on the four oldest the version floor blocks it entirely — everywhere else the computer copy or the file-manager copy is the backup, and only the Z11 has a card-reader route at all.
