What you need

  • A covered BlackBerry Android phone. Method 1: SnapDisk — proven on six

SnapDisk is the one-tap version. It needs Android 7.0 or later, which seven covered BlackBerry phones clear — and the one-tap backup is proven on six of them: each one clears the floor and lists USB OTG Yes. The Motion is the one exception — its OTG support is contested — so run the mounting test on the Motion first.

  1. Install SnapDisk v0.1.2 — it is not on Google Play yet, so install the APK from the GitHub release page. You do not need an account, and there are no ads.
  2. Plug the USB drive into the phone — straight in if it is a USB-C drive on a Type-C phone, or through a USB-C OTG adapter for a regular USB-A drive; on the Aurora, through a micro-USB OTG adapter instead. The Priv, the DTEK50 and the DTEK60 cannot run SnapDisk at all, so this method does not exist on those three.
  3. Check the phone sees the drive before doing anything else. If nothing appears, the drive does not mount and neither this method nor Method 2 works on that phone — on the Motion, this is the test that settles the contest.
  4. Open SnapDisk and tap Backup. SnapDisk copies photos and videos oldest-first from the phone to the drive, in the background, with live progress, Pause and Stop.
  5. Wait for the copy to finish, then open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

Free version: backs up manually, up to 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase — auto-backup on connect and unlimited size. On the six proven phones, SnapDisk installs and the one-tap backup works — they clear Android 7.0 and list USB OTG Yes. On the Motion, SnapDisk installs — it runs Android 7.1 — but the one-tap backup is unproven until a drive mounts, so plug the drive in first and check the phone sees it. On the Priv, the DTEK50 and the DTEK60, SnapDisk does not install at all — they run Android 5.1.1 and 6.0.1 — so use Method 2 or Method 4 on those three instead.

Method 2: file manager + OTG adapter

Same plug, no app to install. On the six proven phones it works exactly as on any other Android phone with OTG support — once the drive mounts — and it also works on the DTEK50 and the DTEK60, which list USB OTG Yes even though SnapDisk cannot install on them:

  1. Plug the USB drive into the phone through an OTG adapter — USB-C on seven phones, micro-USB on the Priv, the DTEK50 and the Aurora — or straight in, if it is a USB-C drive on a Type-C phone.
  2. Check the phone sees the drive. If nothing appears, the drive does not mount and this method does not work on that phone.
  3. Open the phone's file manager and find the DCIM folder — that is where the camera photos live.
  4. Select the photos and videos, copy them to the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish.
  5. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

On the Motion the mounting test comes first — its OTG is contested, Yes per Gadgets360 and MobileGizmo against Notebookcheck's review saying the port serves solely for charging and transferring data — so plug the drive in and check before trusting it there. On the Priv the mounting test comes first too — its OTG is contested, Yes per Gadgets360, Notebookcheck and malaysianwireless against No per FoneArena's review — so plug the drive in and check before trusting it there. On the other eight the file-manager copy is proven alongside the one-tap backup — or, on the DTEK50 and the DTEK60, in place of it.

Method 3: through a computer

The fallback that works on all ten covered phones, including the three no phone-to-USB method reaches with certainty: the Priv, if the drive does not mount on it, and the DTEK50 and the DTEK60, which SnapDisk cannot install on.

  1. Plug the phone into the computer with a USB cable — a plain charging cable works here, because the computer is the other end.
  2. On the phone, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer.
  3. On the computer, open the phone's DCIM folder and copy the photos to the computer, then to the USB drive.
  4. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

This is the method BlackBerry's own manuals sit next to — the BlackBerry 10 manuals describe USB mass storage to a computer, and the older phones' Desktop Software copies files to a computer — and the computer copy is the plain-files version of the same idea. It needs the PC in between, so it is the fallback, not the route, when the phone and the drive are all you have.

Method 4: microSD card reader route

If the phone has a card slot — and all ten do, for certain — the card is the backup, and the reader is the bridge to the USB drive:

  1. Put a microSD card in the phone and copy the DCIM folder to the card with the file manager.
  2. Take the card out, put it in a card reader, plug the reader into the computer, and copy the photos from the card to the USB drive.
  3. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

A microSD card fits all ten phones for certain, so the card-reader route is proven on all ten — the only method in this article that reaches every phone, including the Priv, the DTEK50 and the DTEK60. Ceilings are stated on all ten, contested on three — the Priv, the Motion and the Evolve X — so check the card size against the phone before buying a large one. On the six shared-slot phones the card takes the second SIM's place while it is in. A card copy is a real local copy, but the card stays in the phone until you take it out, and opening it anywhere else needs the card reader — a USB drive plugs into any computer as it is.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud

The cloud is the last resort, and the one BlackBerry's own tool points to: the BlackBerry Content Transfer app moves your data to a new phone over Google Drive or a media card. BlackBerry offers no bundled cloud storage of its own. The cloud works on all ten covered phones — every one runs Android, so Google Photos runs on all ten.

  1. Open Google Photos, turn on backup, and wait for the upload to finish — this needs Wi-Fi, and a big library takes a while.
  2. On a computer, open Google Photos, download the photos, and copy them to the USB drive.
  3. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

Two warnings. First, Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — check the setting before trusting a cloud copy. Second, a cloud backup is not a USB backup: your photos sit on Google's servers, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying for storage. If you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy — that is Method 1 or 2, not a cloud backup. And the Content Transfer app is a phone-to-phone move, not a backup at all: it moves your data to a new phone, it does not leave a copy on a drive you can keep.

What this does not do

  • Does not install below Android 7.0. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and three covered BlackBerry phones run below Android 7.0 as shipped — the Priv on 5.1.1 and the DTEK50 and the DTEK60 on 6.0.1 — so SnapDisk does not install on any of them. No other BlackBerry phone is below the floor.
  • Does not work if the phone does not host a drive. Six covered BlackBerry phones list USB OTG Yes, so on those six the drive mounting is a formality — but the Motion's OTG is contested, Yes per Gadgets360 and MobileGizmo against Notebookcheck's review saying the port serves solely for charging and transferring data, so on the Motion plug the drive in and check the phone sees it before trusting either the one-tap backup or the file-manager copy. The Priv's OTG is contested the same way, so the same test applies there — and the DTEK50 and the DTEK60 list USB OTG Yes, so the file-manager copy works on those two even though SnapDisk does not install on them.
  • Does not delete photos. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Does not back up anything but photos and videos. SnapDisk copies image and video files only — no settings, no contacts, no messages, no app data.
  • Does not back up photos from apps that hide them. If an app keeps its photos in a private folder SnapDisk cannot read, those photos stay in the app.
  • Does not compress. SnapDisk copies the original files as they are — no recompression, no resizing, no quality loss.

What to avoid

  • Buying an adapter you don't need — or the wrong one. Seven phones take USB Type-C, so a USB-C flash drive plugs straight into those seven, and a micro-USB OTG adapter is the wrong adapter for every one of them. Three take micro-USB — the Priv, the DTEK50 and the Aurora — so a USB-C OTG adapter is the wrong adapter for those three. A plain USB charging cable is not an OTG adapter: it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your phone up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1 or 2, not a cloud backup. BlackBerry has no bundled cloud of its own, and the Content Transfer app moves your data to a new phone over Google Drive — it does not leave a copy on a drive you can keep — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.
  • Mistaking a PC tool for a phone-to-USB copy. A computer copy with a USB cable moves files to a computer — but it is a copy to a computer, and the backup lands on the PC. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route, not a desktop copy. BlackBerry Link and the Desktop Software are PC tools in exactly this sense.
  • Mistaking a spec table or a manual page for a phone-to-USB guide. The BlackBerry spec pages are real BlackBerry pages — but they only list an Android version, a chipset, or a USB connector type. None of them describes copying your camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone. The BlackBerry 10 manuals are the closest thing to BlackBerry backup advice, and they describe USB mass storage to a computer.
  • Mistaking OTG support on one model for OTG support on the other. Six BlackBerry phones list USB OTG Yes — but the Motion is contested, Yes per Gadgets360 and MobileGizmo against Notebookcheck's review saying the port serves solely for charging and transferring data — and the Priv is contested too, Yes per Gadgets360, Notebookcheck and malaysianwireless against No per FoneArena's review — so do not assume either one hosts a drive just because it clears Android 7.0, and do not assume a phone that clears Android 7.0 hosts a drive either. Plug a drive in and check, on the Motion and the Priv at least.
  • Mistaking one KEYone for another. The KEYone Limited Edition Black and the Bronze Edition are variants of the KEYone, not separate models — same hardware, same specs — so a spec, an adapter or a card ceiling for the KEYone holds for all three, and there is no separate Black Edition to look up.
  • Mistaking a rebrand for a different phone. The DTEK50 is the Alcatel Idol 4 and the DTEK60 is the Alcatel Idol 4S, rebadged — so the Alcatel article's Idol rows describe the same hardware, and a DTEK50 or DTEK60 answer that only names Alcatel is still an answer about your phone.
  • Mistaking a microSD card copy for a USB backup. Copying DCIM to a microSD card is a real local copy — all ten phones take a card — but the card stays in the phone, and opening it anywhere else needs a card reader; a USB drive plugs into any computer as it is. And on the six shared-slot phones the card takes the second SIM's place while it is in.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 20, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On a BlackBerry phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. The file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the phone. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.
  • Trusting one spec listing. The Priv's OTG is two claims — Yes per Gadgets360, Notebookcheck and malaysianwireless against No per FoneArena — the Motion's OTG is two claims — Yes per Gadgets360 and MobileGizmo against Notebookcheck's review saying the port serves solely for charging and transferring data — the Aurora's OTG is an omission — Gadgets360 lists USB OTG Yes, ubergizmo and gsm-arena list the port without OTG — the Priv's card is two ceilings — 256 GB per Gadgets360 against 2 TB per FoneArena and Notebookcheck — the Motion's card is two ceilings — 256 GB per gsm.cool, gsmarena.com.bd and Techaeris against 2 TB per Notebookcheck, PhoneArena and Gadgets360 — the Evolve X's card is two ceilings — 256 GB per Gadgets360 against 2 TB per Times Now — and the Motion's Android is three labels — 7.1 against 7.1.1 against 7.1.2 — so check the unit before buying a drive expecting a particular spec. The Android floor is settled on every one either way: seven clear 7.0 and three run below it on every source.

Rule of thumb: ten phones, seven clear Android 7.0, six list USB OTG Yes, seven take USB Type-C, all ten take a microSD card — the one-tap backup is proven on six, the Motion needs the mounting test, and the card-reader route is proven on all ten.
