Method 1: SnapDisk — proven on all fourteen

On all fourteen covered phones SnapDisk installs and the drive mounts, so the one-tap backup works as designed. There is no test phone and no blocked phone in this article:

  1. Check your Android version first. Settings > About phone shows the version. Every covered RedMagic flagship runs Android 10 or newer, so if yours is one of the fourteen, you clear the floor: skip to step 2. If it says 6.0.1 or lower, you are not on a covered model — check the model name before buying a drive expecting the one-tap backup.
  2. Install SnapDisk from the GitHub APK. The Play listing is being prepared, so until then install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on all fourteen covered models.
  3. Plug in the USB drive. On every covered phone that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction.
  4. Look for the mount notification. Tap Backup in the notification and SnapDisk copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  5. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything. If a single file is over 4 GB, FAT32 will refuse it, so exFAT is the safer format for the drive.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification — and it is proven on all fourteen of the covered RedMagic flagships, with no mounting test and no version block anywhere: SnapDisk needs Android 7.0, and the lowest covered RedMagic runs Android 10.

The copies are the original files. SnapDisk streams each file byte-for-byte from the device to the drive — the code reads the file and writes the same bytes out, with no decode, no recompression, and no resize — and then checks that the copy on the drive is the same size as the original. Other apps copy originals byte-for-byte too; what SnapDisk adds is the automated whole-library run: oldest-first order, progress with Pause and Stop, resume where it stopped, and skip-if-already-copied on the next run. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter (works wherever the drive mounts)

This is the stock route, and it works on all fourteen covered phones, because every one of them lists USB OTG Yes:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with an OTG adapter. On every covered phone that is a USB-C OTG adapter, or a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in, with the OTG wiring inside the adapter — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive — and if the drive never shows up, the phone does not host it, and Method 3 is the route.
  2. Open Files and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. The file manager on a RedMagic phone is Files, or Files by Google, per devicesfaq, so open Files on your unit.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On the fourteen where this method works, the photos are the 64 MP shots from the rear camera on the 5G, 5S, 6, 6 Pro, 6S Pro, 7, 7 Pro and 7S Pro, and the 50 MP shots on the 8 Pro, 8S Pro, 9 Pro, 9S Pro, 10 Pro and 10S Pro, plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on the fourteen phones where SnapDisk runs: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. And it is proven on all fourteen — there is no mounting test in this article.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the RedMagic phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no Android-version question and no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. The per-model backup FAQ names this the most economical method. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 2 is the route on all fourteen, since the drive mounts on every one of them.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (proven on none of fourteen)

No covered RedMagic model takes a microSD card for certain — GSMArena lists no card slot on all fourteen — so the card-reader route is proven on none of them, and it needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app where it does exist: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old phone manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. Two warnings: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in Files. And the card is contested on three models — the 5G, the 5S and the 9S Pro — so on those, check your unit's SIM tray before counting on this route. Unlike most brands in this series, where the route is proven on nearly every model, no RedMagic flagship takes a card for certain — so for this brand the card-reader route does not exist as a fallback at all.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud — not phone-to-USB plain files

RedMagic offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no RedMagic-specific backup advice turned up in any source beyond a computer copy, Google Drive or Google Photos — so the stock options are the clouds, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is the backup for photos alone per the per-model backup FAQ: turn backup on in the Photos app and your photos save to your Google Account — but that is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and the Storage saver setting compresses photos. 2. Google Drive is the default built-in cloud backup service on Android per the same FAQ, and REDMAGIC's own update guide says to back up all data externally or on the cloud before updating. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1 or 2 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What this does not do

  • It does not back up your settings or your contacts. SnapDisk copies photos, videos, and any files you pick — the files themselves, exactly as they are. App settings, call logs, texts, and your accounts stay with your phone and your RedMagic account. For those, Android's built-in backup is the tool — it backs up personal settings to Google's servers with a Google account — not SnapDisk.
  • It does not delete photos from your phone after copying. Deleting is always your choice, after you check the copy on the drive. SnapDisk never deletes originals — it only copies.
  • It does not install on a phone below Android 7.0. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0, and no covered RedMagic flagship runs below Android 10 — the lowest is the 5G and the 5S on Android 10 — so this limit does not bite on any of the fourteen. If your phone runs 6.0.1 or lower, it is not one of the covered models.
  • It does not work if your phone does not host a drive. Every covered model lists USB OTG Yes, so if the drive never mounts on your unit, that settles it for your unit — use Method 3 instead.
  • It does not back up photos from apps that hide them. Some apps keep photos in private folders SnapDisk cannot read. Those stay on the phone — check each app's own export or share option.
  • It does not compress your photos. It copies the original files byte-for-byte, so the copy is the same size as the original. On all fourteen, with their 64 MP and 50 MP cameras, that is real storage on the drive — bring a drive with room.

What to avoid

  • Don't move files instead of copying them. The file manager's Move deletes the original as it goes. Copy first, check the copy on the drive, and only then delete the originals from the phone if you need the space.
  • Don't unplug the drive until the copy is done. If the copy is still running — SnapDisk shows progress, speed and ETA — unplugging leaves half-copied files. Wait for it to finish, then eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it.
  • Don't buy a micro-USB adapter for your phone's port. All fourteen take USB Type-C, so a USB-C flash drive plugs straight into any of them. A micro-USB adapter does not fit any covered RedMagic phone.
  • Don't buy a card expecting the card-reader route. No covered RedMagic model takes a microSD card for certain — the card is contested on the 5G, the 5S and the 9S Pro and No on the other eleven — so do not count on the card route on any of them.
  • Don't count on VaultDrive's AppBrain floor on the 7-series. The 7, 7 Pro and 7S Pro run Android 12, below AppBrain's 12.1 listing for VaultDrive — so under that stricter listing VaultDrive is not proven there, even though the one-tap SnapDisk route is.
  • Don't let one folder hold every photo. SnapDisk copies oldest-first, and the copy is the same size as the original, so a full camera roll on a phone with a 64 MP or 50 MP camera — all fourteen — fills a small drive fast. Bring a drive with room, or back up in batches and check the copy before deleting anything.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a copy. A backup finds your photos, copies them all, and tells you when it's done. On a RedMagic phone the honest position is the simplest in this series: the one-tap backup is proven on all fourteen flagships — every one clears Android 7.0 and lists USB OTG Yes — and the only route that does not exist here is the card reader, because no RedMagic phone takes a microSD card for certain.
