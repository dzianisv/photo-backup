SnapDisk vs VaultDrive: USB photo backup on Android, compared

Two Android apps that copy your photos to a USB drive — no cloud, no account, no upload. Here is how they actually differ, from the facts we could verify as of November 2026.

November 16, 2026 · 4 min read

TL;DR: SnapDisk and VaultDrive both back up Android photos to a USB drive offline, with no account and no subscription. The verified differences: SnapDisk Pro is $9.99 one-time, half of VaultDrive Pro Lifetime at $19.99 once; SnapDisk's free tier covers 5 GB per backup run, VaultDrive's covers 200 files or 2 GB per backup; SnapDisk copies oldest-first with Pause and Stop in the progress notification; and neither app deletes photos from your phone — VaultDrive's own FAQ says freeing up space is "planned for an upcoming release", while SnapDisk's free-up-space flow is copy first, check the copy, then delete the originals yourself. VaultDrive launched in October 2026 and had about 870 installs as of November 14, 2026, with no reviews yet; SnapDisk is not on Google Play yet (v0.1.2).

The two apps at a glance

SnapDisk is a free Android app (v0.1.2, not on Google Play yet) that backs up photos and videos to a USB disk. Plug in the disk, tap Backup in the notification, and photos copy oldest-first in the background, size-verified, with a progress notification showing percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left, plus Pause and Stop. Free: manual backup, 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup when a disk is connected and unlimited size. No cloud, no account, no ads, and no internet permission — your photos physically cannot leave the phone.

VaultDrive: USB Photo Backup (com.coticsy.vaultdrive, by Coticsy LLC) launched in October 2026 and had about 870 installs as of November 14, 2026, with no reviews yet. Plug in a USB-C flash drive, SSD, or SD card reader, choose what to back up, and tap Start. It copies at original quality, byte for byte, keeping dates and places; offers Fresh or Incremental backup (Pro copies only what's new); resumes after unplug; checks every copy and lists skipped files with reasons; and sorts folders by date or by album. It works offline with no account, needs Android 8 or later, and shows no ads. Free tier: up to 200 files or 2 GB per backup. Pro Lifetime is $19.99 once, with no subscription, ever.

Price: $9.99 vs $19.99, both one-time

This is the clearest difference, and both sides are one-time purchases — neither app charges a subscription. SnapDisk Pro is $9.99 once: auto-backup on connect plus unlimited backup size. VaultDrive Pro Lifetime is $19.99 once, also with no subscription, ever — verified November 16, 2026 from the vendor site (coticsy.com) and the App Store in-app purchase list. Same model, half the price: $9.99 against $19.99.

Both free versions are genuinely usable, not demos. SnapDisk free backs up manually up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. VaultDrive free covers up to 200 files or 2 GB per backup. Which free tier goes further depends on your camera roll: a 5 GB run fits more than a 200-file / 2 GB run if your photos are large, and SnapDisk's runs can be repeated — files already copied are never copied twice, so a second run picks up where the first stopped.

During the backup: oldest-first, Pause / Stop, resume

SnapDisk copies oldest-first — your earliest photos first — in the background, while you use the phone normally. The progress notification shows real percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left, with Pause and Stop. If you unplug the disk mid-copy, the backup pauses with the full error shown and resumes where it stopped when the disk is back; files already copied are never copied twice.

VaultDrive also resumes after unplug, checks every copy, and lists skipped files with reasons. On Android it keeps running in the background with a progress notification. Its Pro tier adds Incremental backup — copy only what's new, skip files already on the drive. SnapDisk does the same skip for free: files already copied are never copied twice, so a repeat run only copies what's new.

Freeing up space: neither app deletes for you — yet

Here is the honest part. Neither app frees up space on your phone today. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes anything — so freeing space is a two-step flow you do yourself: copy to the drive, open a few photos on the drive to check they arrived, then delete the originals from your phone. That flow is covered step by step in How to free up space by moving photos to a USB drive.

VaultDrive is in the same place, by its own words. Its FAQ says: "Not yet. Today VaultDrive only copies, so nothing is removed from your phone. Freeing up space … is planned for an upcoming release." So if freeing space is why you're choosing an app, the question is not which app deletes — neither does — but which app's copy you trust enough to delete after. SnapDisk's answer is the copy-first-then-delete flow above, with every file size-verified after copying.

What to avoid

  • Choosing on install counts. VaultDrive had about 870 installs as of November 14, 2026, with no reviews yet — it launched in October 2026, so there is almost no track record to judge it by. SnapDisk is not on Google Play yet, so it has no install count at all. Neither number tells you how either app copies your photos.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. This applies to both apps.
  • Assuming "offline" means the same thing everywhere. Both apps copy offline with no account. SnapDisk goes one step further: it has no internet permission at all, so your photos physically cannot leave the device — it works with the phone in airplane mode.
  • Paying for a subscription you didn't want. Neither app has one: SnapDisk Pro is $9.99 once, VaultDrive Pro Lifetime is $19.99 once. If a backup app asks for a monthly fee, it is a different model from both of these.

Rule of thumb: the app that wins is the one whose copy you trust enough to delete after. Compare the price, the free tier, and the copy — then check the copy on the drive before you delete anything.

Which one fits you

If you want the cheaper one-time Pro, the larger free tier per run, oldest-first order, and Pause / Stop in the notification, that is SnapDisk: free manual backup up to 5 GB per run, Pro $9.99 once for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size. If you want folder organisation by date or by album, backup reminders, and an App Lock, those are VaultDrive features — check them against its free tier of 200 files or 2 GB per backup, and its $19.99 Pro Lifetime. Either way, back up first, verify the copy, then delete — that order is what keeps your photos safe, whichever app does the copying.
