SnapDisk vs SanDisk app: back up Android photos to USB

Two Android apps that back up your photos to a USB drive — one from the biggest name in flash storage, one from a small team. Here is how they actually differ, from the facts we could verify as of November 2026.

November 21, 2026 · 4 min read

TL;DR: SnapDisk and the SANDISK app both back up Android photos to a USB drive, and both are free to download. The verified differences: the SANDISK app is on Google Play today (updated Oct 12, 2026, Android 8 or later, rated 4.76 from about 37,900 ratings) and SnapDisk is not yet (v0.1.2 — install the APK from GitHub, Android 7.0 or later); the SANDISK app is free with no paid tier named in its listing, while SnapDisk Pro is $9.99 one-time for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size — so there is no Pro-versus-paid price comparison to make; the SANDISK app is built for SanDisk drives — its listing names compatible SanDisk USB flash drives, external SSDs, and microSD cards, sold separately — while SnapDisk works with any drive Android can mount; the SANDISK app backs up photos, videos, and contacts automatically when a drive is connected, and can move and delete files and encrypt them, while SnapDisk copies photos and videos only, oldest-first, and never deletes; the SANDISK app's listing says nothing about a copy order, Pause and Stop, or per-file verification, while SnapDisk copies oldest-first with Pause and Stop in the progress notification and checks the size of every file after copying; and the SANDISK app's listing says nothing about whether the copy keeps running with the screen off, while SnapDisk copies in the background as a foreground service, though screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified. One correction to a number that floats around this niche: the current SANDISK listing passed 1,000,000 installs in July 2026 — the 10,000,000+ figure belongs to its predecessor, Memory Zone, which was removed from Play in April 2025. As of November 21, 2026, no SnapDisk-vs-SanDisk head-to-head comparison turned up in search — this article is the first. This is the fourth head-to-head comparison in the series, after SnapDisk vs VaultDrive, SnapDisk vs FotoSafe, and SnapDisk vs Photos to USB.

The two apps at a glance

SnapDisk is a free Android app (v0.1.2, not on Google Play yet) that backs up photos and videos to a USB disk. Plug in the disk, tap Backup in the notification, and photos copy oldest-first in the background, size-verified, with a progress notification showing percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left, plus Pause and Stop. Free: manual backup, 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup when a disk is connected and unlimited size. No cloud, no account, no ads, and no internet permission — your photos physically cannot leave the phone. It needs Android 7.0 or later.

SANDISK (com.sandisk.smz2, developer © Sandisk Corporation or its affiliates, formerly SANDISK Memory Zone™) is on Google Play — updated Oct 12, 2026, Android 8 or later, rated 4.76 from about 37,900 ratings, over 1,000,000 installs on its current listing. It is a file manager for compatible SanDisk drives: browse, move, copy, or delete files, a media timeline and grid, storage insights, and encryption with password protection for files you want secured. Set automatic backup once and your photos, videos, and contacts back up when a drive is connected. Free — its listing shows a price of $0 and names no paid tier. It is built for SanDisk USB flash drives such as the SanDisk Phone Drive, SanDisk external solid-state drives, and SanDisk microSD cards, sold separately.

Price: free vs free — and the paid tier only one of them names

Both apps are free to download. The SANDISK app's listing shows a price of $0, and the listing text we could read names no paid tier, no subscription, and no in-app purchase price at all. SnapDisk is free with no ads, and its Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase, verified in the repo's billing code (product id pro_unlock).

So this article makes no Pro-versus-paid price comparison: there is no SANDISK paid tier in the listing to compare against, and any price for one would be a guess. What can be compared is what the money buys on the one side where money is charged. SnapDisk's Pro buys features: auto-backup when a disk is connected, and unlimited size instead of the 5 GB free cap. The SANDISK app's automatic backup on connect — the feature SnapDisk charges $9.99 for — is in its free listing. If price is what decides it for you, both are free to start; the only priced thing in this comparison is SnapDisk Pro, at $9.99 once.

Neither app asks for a subscription at any point. The SANDISK listing names no subscription, and SnapDisk Pro is one-time.

The free tier: everything free vs 5 GB a run

The SANDISK app's free tier is the whole feature set as its listing describes it: automatic backup of photos, videos, and contacts when a compatible drive is connected, plus file management, storage insights, and encryption. Its listing states no size cap and no file-count cap. That is the free tier: everything it lists, free, with no limit stated.

SnapDisk's free tier is 5 GB per backup run, with no file-count cap, and runs can be repeated. Files already copied are never copied twice, so a second run picks up where the first stopped. A phone that has never been backed up often holds a library bigger than 5 GB, so it takes several taps — and that is the case Pro is for — but the free version can finish the whole library, run by run, without paying.

Which free tier goes further depends on your drive and your library: the SANDISK app's free tier covers the whole library in one automatic run, free, if your drive is a compatible SanDisk one; SnapDisk's free tier takes several runs for a big library, but each run works with any drive, in the background with Pause and Stop, and there are no ads at all. If you own a SanDisk drive and want the whole library backed up free in one go, the SANDISK app's free tier is the usable one — SnapDisk's is not, until you repeat the run.

Before the copy: set it once vs pick your folders

The SANDISK app is the simpler start, if you have its drive. Its listing lays out the deal in one line: "Set it once and your photos, videos, and contacts will automatically back up when your device is connected." Set up automatic backup in the app settings, plug a compatible SanDisk drive in, and the backup runs. There is no folder picking in its listing and no preview: it backs up your photos, videos, and contacts to the drive.

SnapDisk asks you to choose first. Pick the From and To folders in settings (the system folder picker, verified in the repo as OpenDocumentTree), plug the drive in, tap Backup in the notification — and the numbers appear once the copy is running, in the progress notification: percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left. The folders are your choice; the plan is not previewed.

Neither app shows a planned-backup summary before the run — FotoSafe does, in an earlier comparison in this series, but neither of these two does. The difference here is control against convenience: the SANDISK app decides what to back up (photos, videos, contacts) and runs it automatically; SnapDisk lets you pick the folders, and mirrors your source folder structure on the drive.

During the backup: automatic on connect vs background with Pause and Stop

This is the clearest behavioural difference, and it cuts both ways. The SANDISK app's automatic backup runs when the drive is connected — that is the deal for the whole-library free tier. Its listing mentions no Pause and Stop controls, states no copy order, and says nothing about whether the copy keeps running with the screen off. It also says nothing about what happens if the drive is unplugged mid-backup.

SnapDisk copies oldest-first — your earliest photos first, by the date the photo was taken — in the background, while you use the phone normally. The progress notification shows real percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left, with Pause and Stop. If you unplug the disk mid-copy, the backup pauses with the full error shown and resumes where it stopped when the disk is back; the file in flight resumes from its .part file, and files already copied are never copied twice. Files that cannot be copied — a single file of 4 GB or more on a FAT32 drive, for example — are skipped and listed with their reason instead of failing the whole backup.

That last point matters against the SANDISK app's own record. Reviewers on its Play listing report the opposite behaviour: "The copy/backup process stops when it encounters a file larger than 4GB because the drives are formatted as FAT32, and there is no feature … to skip the file and continue." (March 2026, as quoted in our Nov 14 competitor study). Another reports a slow copy: "I found it very slow. A 2 minute transfer took more than 20 minutes." (January 2025, same source). And a current-version reviewer reports broken drive detection on newer phones: "ZERO IMPROVEMENTS ON A17/OUI9. NO DRIVE CONNECT! BROKEN MULTI-PORT/MULTI-DRIVE OTG DETECT!" (v3.4.2, October 2026, same source). These are reviewer reports, not our tests — but they are the complaints the SANDISK app's own users raise, and they are exactly the failures SnapDisk's skip-and-continue rule and resume are built to avoid.

After the copy: verification, encryption, and what the drive holds

The SANDISK app verifiably wins this section on two points its listing states outright. First, encryption: "Add an extra layer of protection with encryption and password-protection for your most important content." Your files on the drive can be locked. Second, scope: it backs up contacts as well as photos and videos, and it is a full file manager — browse, move, copy, or delete — with a media timeline and grid and storage insights showing available, used, and total space. SnapDisk backs up photos and videos only, and has no encryption, no contacts backup, and no file manager.

SnapDisk's answer is a different check. It checks the size of every file after copying: each copy writes to a .part file next to the destination and is renamed to the final file only after the size verification passes, so a file that did not arrive whole never pretends it did. Its destination mirrors your source folder structure — Camera, Screenshots, and the rest land where they were on the phone. The SANDISK app's listing says nothing about per-file verification, and nothing about the folder layout its backups land in — so neither is claimed for it, and neither is scored against it. Neither side replaces opening a few photos on the drive yourself — a backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy.

Freeing up space: move vs copy-only

Here is the honest part, and the one place the two apps genuinely differ on what they do to your phone. The SANDISK app can move files: its listing says "Browse, move, copy, or delete files" and "Quickly transfer files from your Android phone to a compatible storage device so you can make room for what matters most." Moving a file off the phone frees the space in the same step.

SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes anything (the only delete calls in its source touch .part files, never your photos) — so freeing space is a two-step flow you do yourself: copy to the drive, open a few photos on the drive to check they arrived, then delete the originals from your phone. That flow is covered step by step in How to free up space by moving photos to a USB drive.

So if freeing space is why you're choosing an app, the question is whether you want the app to move the files for you. The SANDISK app says it does; SnapDisk does not, by design — the copy-first-then-delete flow means a file is never gone from the phone until you have checked the copy yourself. The SANDISK app's move is the convenient one; SnapDisk's copy-only is the cautious one. Either way, check the copy on the drive before you delete anything — that order is what keeps your photos safe, whichever app does the copying.

What to avoid

  • Assuming the 10,000,000+ installs belong to the current app. They do not. The current SANDISK listing passed 1,000,000 installs in July 2026; the 10,000,000+ figure is the old Memory Zone listing, removed from Play in April 2025. The current app is big, not ten-million big.
  • Buying a SanDisk drive you do not own yet just for the app. The SANDISK app is built for compatible SanDisk drives, sold separately. If your drive is another brand, its listing promises nothing about it — SnapDisk works with any drive Android can mount.
  • Paying before checking what the purchase buys. The SANDISK listing names no paid tier at all. SnapDisk's Pro buys auto-backup on connect and unlimited size for $9.99 once — and the SANDISK app's auto-backup on connect is in its free listing.
  • Assuming "free" means the same thing on both sides. The SANDISK app is free with no cap stated and automatic backup included; SnapDisk is free with no ads and 5 GB per run, repeatable. The trade is the cap against the drive lock-in.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. This applies to both apps, and doubly to the one that can move files for you.
  • Assuming the copy keeps running with the screen off. Neither listing promises it. SnapDisk's copy runs as a foreground service — the mechanism Android gives a copy that must keep running with the screen off — but the repo holds no wake lock and no battery-saver handling, and no comparison run has ever been tested, so screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified, and this article says so instead of promising it. The SANDISK app's listing says nothing about it either.
  • Using a FAT32 drive for long videos. A single file over 4 GB does not fit on FAT32. SnapDisk skips those files and tells you which ones. The SANDISK app's own reviewers report its copy stops entirely on such a file, with no skip — use exFAT for a library with very long videos.
  • Choosing on whether an app is on Play. The SANDISK app is on Google Play today and SnapDisk is not yet — that says where to download it, not how either app copies your photos.
  • Assuming contacts come along. The SANDISK app backs up contacts as well as photos and videos. SnapDisk backs up photos and videos only — your contacts need another route.

Rule of thumb: the app that wins is the one whose copy you trust enough to delete after. Compare the free tier, the preview, and the copy — then check the copy on the drive before you delete anything.

Which one fits you

If you want a repeatable free tier (5 GB per run), oldest-first order, Pause and Stop in the notification, per-file size verification, a background copy that works with any drive, and no ads at all, that is SnapDisk: free manual backup up to 5 GB per run, Pro $9.99 once for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size. Either way, back up first, verify the copy, then delete — that order is what keeps your photos safe, whichever app does the copying.
