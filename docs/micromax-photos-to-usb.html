Method 1: SnapDisk on the six (the one-tap route)

On the six phones that clear Android 7.0 and list USB OTG, the automated route exists. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and all nine clear it — the Canvas 1, Canvas 2 (2017), Canvas 2 2018 and Canvas 2 Plus (2018) run 7.0 exactly, the Canvas Infinity runs 7.0 or 7.1, the IN 1 and IN 1b and IN Note 1 run 10, and the IN 2b runs 11 — but only the six that list OTG get the one-tap backup:

  1. Install SnapDisk. Until the Play listing is live, install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on all nine. It does not fail to install on any covered Micromax phone: every one of the nine clears Android 7.0.
  2. Plug in the USB drive. On the four IN phones a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and a regular USB drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter; on the Canvas 1 and Canvas Infinity a regular USB drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter. Buy the adapter with the plug that matches your phone — micro-USB for a Canvas, USB-C for an IN — and the OTG wiring inside it, not a plain sync cable.
  3. Tap Backup in the notification. SnapDisk offers the backup when the drive connects, then copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  4. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. Its limits: it works only on phones that host a drive — and three covered Micromax phones do not clearly do that. The Canvas 2 (2017) lists OTG No, so there is no phone route on it at all; the Canvas 2 2018's OTG is disputed, so plug a drive in and see whether the phone mounts it before counting on the one-tap route; the Canvas 2 Plus (2018)'s OTG is unconfirmed, so the same test applies. On those three, Method 3 or Method 4 is the route.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter (works on the six)

This is the stock route, and the only phone route on the six besides SnapDisk. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive, and OTG is listed on the six covered models that get it:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with an OTG adapter that matches your port. On the four IN phones a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and a regular USB drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter; on the Canvas 1 and Canvas Infinity a regular USB drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive.
  2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the stock file manager on a Micromax phone, so use Files by Google or the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On these phones the photos are the 8 MP shots from the rear camera on the Canvas 1 — 13 MP on the Canvas Infinity, Canvas 2 (2017), Canvas 2 2018, Canvas 2 Plus (2018), IN 1b and IN 2b, 48 MP on the IN 1 and IN Note 1 — plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on the six phones: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. And on the Canvas 2 (2017) it does not work at all — that phone lists OTG No, so no drive mounts — while on the Canvas 2 2018 and the Canvas 2 Plus (2018) it works only if the drive mounts, which is the test.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the Micromax phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. It is also the only phone-to-drive route for the Canvas 2 (2017), which lists OTG No, and the fallback for the Canvas 2 2018 and the Canvas 2 Plus (2018) if the drive does not mount on the phone. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 1 or 2 is the route on the six, or Method 4.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (works on all nine)

All nine covered Micromax models take a microSD card — up to 256 GB on the IN 1, IN Note 1 and IN 2b, up to 128 GB on the Canvas Infinity, Canvas 2 Plus (2018) and IN 1b, up to 64 GB on the Canvas 2 (2017) and Canvas 2 2018 — and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old phone manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. Two warnings: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in the file manager. And the Canvas 1's ceiling is disputed — up to 32 GB per gadgets360, indianexpress and gadgetsnow, up to 64 GB per gadgetguide4u and phoneradar — so check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 32 GB for that model, and the IN Note 1's ceiling is disputed too — up to 256 GB per mysmartprice, cashify and hindustantimes, up to 1 TB per techtoreview's summary — so the same check applies there before buying a card larger than 256 GB.

Method 5: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

Micromax offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no Micromax-specific backup advice turned up in any source beyond a computer copy, Google Drive, or Google Photos — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead; and its "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p). 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and Google accounts only offer 5 GB of free storage by default since May 2026, with the full 15 GB available only after verifying a phone number on the account. 3. Google backup is what Android itself names: personal settings back up to Google's servers with a Google account, and are restored to a new phone when you sign in with that account. It is a real backup — but it backs up settings, not a folder of photo files on a drive you hold. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2, 3 or 4 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What this does not do

A few honest limits, so the manual copy is not oversold:

  • No automated backup exists for a Micromax phone that cannot host a drive — and one covered phone is one. The Canvas 2 (2017) lists OTG No per gadgets360 and hardreset99, so there is no phone route on it at all, even though it clears Android 7.0 and can install SnapDisk. The Canvas 2 2018's OTG is disputed and the Canvas 2 Plus (2018)'s is unconfirmed, so on those two the drive mounting is the test. The honest limit in this guide is the OTG line, not the Android version: all nine clear 7.0.
  • Three models do not clearly host a drive, so the one-tap route is not promised on them. The Canvas 2 (2017) lists OTG No, the Canvas 2 2018's OTG is disputed — bmobile says OTG support, findpare says No — and the Canvas 2 Plus (2018)'s OTG is unconfirmed — gadgets360 blank, smartprix only USB charging. The computer copy in Method 3 and the card-reader route in Method 4 are the routes on those three.
  • Photos and videos only, from the folder you pick. By default that's DCIM — your camera photos. Photos in other folders (Downloads, app folders) only copy if you select them too, and photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the microSD card are not on the card for the Method 4 route.
  • Files over 4 GB on FAT32 are a problem. Very long videos on a FAT32 drive will not copy — copy those to a computer instead, or reformat the drive to exFAT, noting that Android 13 and later read exFAT natively, and no covered Micromax phone runs Android 13, so FAT32 is the safer format for the phone route on all nine.
  • It never deletes. The file manager's Copy only copies. Freeing space on the device is copy, check the copy on the drive, then delete the originals yourself — never the other way round.
  • The Canvas 2 2018's OTG is disputed, not Yes. Bmobile's listing says Micro USB 2.0 with O-T-G function support, but findpare says USB OTG No. Plug a drive in and see whether the phone mounts it before planning anything around the phone route on that model.
  • The Canvas 2 Plus (2018)'s OTG is unconfirmed, not No. Gadgets360 leaves the OTG line blank and smartprix lists only USB charging, so no source found states it either way. The same drive-mounting test applies before counting on the one-tap route on that model.
  • The Canvas 1's microSD ceiling is two numbers in the sources found. Up to 32 GB per gadgets360, indianexpress and gadgetsnow, up to 64 GB per gadgetguide4u and phoneradar. Check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 32 GB for that model.
  • The IN Note 1's microSD ceiling is two numbers too. Up to 256 GB in a dedicated slot per mysmartprice, cashify and hindustantimes, up to 1 TB per techtoreview's summary — and the same techtoreview page lists its card slot as No in one table and names FunTouch 10.5 as its OS, which is Vivo's interface, not Micromax's, so that page is not used as a source here. Check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 256 GB for that model.
  • The IN Note 1's front camera is two numbers in the sources found. 16 MP per phonebunch, 5 MP per ubergizmo. Either way the phone shoots 48 MP main photos — the selfie camera matters only if you are comparing camera specs, not for the backup route.
  • The Canvas Infinity's Android version and battery are two numbers each in the sources found. Android 7.0 at launch per gadgets360's launch report, 7.1.2 per smartprix, 7.1 upgradeable to 8.0 per mobilegizmo; 2900 mAh per gadgets360, 2980 mAh per smartprix, phonesdata and phonemore. Either way it clears 7.0 and lists OTG, so the backup route is the same.
  • Three spec pages describe second phones that do not match the launch record. Phonepik lists a Canvas1 released 15 June 2023 on Android 14, a Canvas Infinity released 15 June 2023 with a 6.67-inch display and an 8 MP ultrawide, and an IN 2B 6GB RAM on Android 14 with a June 2023 launch — against the July 2017 Canvas 1 launch on Android 7.0, the August 2017 Canvas Infinity launch with a 5.7-inch display and a single 13 MP rear camera, and the August 2021 IN 2b launch on Android 11 that every other source lists. None of the three listings is used as a source in this guide.
  • No source found states the charging wattage on five of the nine. The Canvas 1, Canvas 2 (2017), Canvas 2 2018, Canvas 2 Plus (2018) and IN Note 1 have no sourced charging wattage — only the IN 1 lists one, 18W — so this guide does not guess one for the other five.
  • No source found names the stock file manager on a Micromax phone. No source found names one, so Method 2 uses Files by Google or the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit.
  • A microSD card is a second copy, not a USB drive. All nine take microSD, so a card is a second local copy you can count on everywhere. But a card is not a USB drive: it does not plug into any computer on its own, and this article is about the drive — the card only becomes a backup once its photos are copied onto one.
  • If yours is a Micromax phone that is not in this guide, the route is different. This guide covers the Canvas 1, Canvas Infinity, Canvas 2 (2017), Canvas 2 2018, Canvas 2 Plus (2018), IN 1, IN 1b, IN Note 1 and IN 2b only. If yours is a different Micromax model — a Canvas 6, Canvas 5, Canvas Nitro, Canvas Spark, Canvas Mega, Bolt, or an older Canvas 2, Canvas 4 or Canvas Turbo — check Settings > About phone for its Android version first: anything below Android 7.0 cannot run SnapDisk at all, and the computer copy in Method 3 is the reliable route.
  • Newer does not mean a newer Android version on your unit. Micromax promises updates on some models — the IN 2b's listings carry a 2 OS updates + 3 years security commitment — but a used phone may still be on its launch version if its owner never took the update. Check Settings > About phone on your unit before assuming any version number.
  • Five covered Micromax phones cannot run VaultDrive at all. VaultDrive needs Android 8 or later per its own site, so the Canvas 1, Canvas Infinity, Canvas 2 (2017), Canvas 2 2018 and Canvas 2 Plus (2018) — all on Android 7.0 or 7.1 — cannot run it, while the four IN phones on Android 10 or 11 can. And AppBrain lists 12.1 or later, which no covered Micromax phone clears, since the newest covered models run Android 11.

What to avoid

  • Assuming SnapDisk backs up to a drive on every Micromax phone. SnapDisk installs on all nine covered models but the one-tap backup works on six — the Canvas 2 (2017) lists OTG No, the Canvas 2 2018's OTG is disputed, and the Canvas 2 Plus (2018)'s is unconfirmed. A Micromax phone outside this guide may not list it either, so check the OTG line before planning anything around the phone route.
  • Assuming a Micromax phone hosts a drive because it runs Android 7.0. All nine clear 7.0, but the Canvas 2 (2017) lists OTG No anyway. The Android version is not the test — the OTG line is.
  • Buying the wrong adapter for the port. The five Canvas models take micro-USB 2.0 and the four IN models take USB-C, so buy a micro-USB OTG adapter for a Canvas and a USB-C OTG adapter or USB-C flash drive for an IN. A USB-C adapter does not fit a micro-USB port, and a micro-USB adapter does not fit a USB-C port.
  • Assuming the Canvas 2 2018 hosts a drive. Bmobile's listing says Micro USB 2.0 with O-T-G function support, but findpare says USB OTG No. Plug a drive in and see whether the phone mounts it before buying anything around the phone route for that model.
  • Assuming the Canvas 2 Plus (2018) hosts a drive. No source found states its OTG either way — gadgets360 leaves the line blank and smartprix lists only USB charging. The same drive-mounting test applies.
  • Assuming the Canvas 1 takes a 64 GB card. Gadgets360, indianexpress and gadgetsnow say up to 32 GB; only gadgetguide4u and phoneradar say up to 64 GB. Check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 32 GB for that model.
  • Assuming the IN Note 1 takes a 1 TB card. Mysmartprice, cashify and hindustantimes say up to 256 GB in a dedicated slot; only techtoreview's summary says up to 1 TB — and the same page lists its card slot as No in one table. Check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 256 GB for that model.
  • Assuming your photos are on the microSD card. On these phones the camera may save to internal storage or to the card depending on the setting. If you pull the card for Method 4 without checking, you may copy an empty DCIM folder while the photos sit in internal storage. Check where the photos live first.
  • Assuming Micromax has a backup app. No Micromax backup app to a USB drive turned up in any source. The one-tap route on the six phones is SnapDisk, not a Micromax app.
  • Assuming the cheapest Canvas is too weak to run the app. The Canvas 1 runs Android 7.0 on a MediaTek MT6737 with 2 GB of RAM, and it can install SnapDisk and host a drive — the Android version is what matters, not the price.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the device — never the other way round. The file manager only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. A file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the device. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish before unplugging, and eject the drive from the notification or Settings > Storage first.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your device up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1, 2, 3 or 4, not a cloud backup. And Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and since May 2026 Google accounts get only 5 GB free by default, with the full 15 GB only after verifying a phone number.
  • Mistaking a generic guide for a Micromax guide. The YouTube OTG video, the Photos to USB Play listing, the Android Central forum thread and the three softhandtech and thegadgetians guides are real documents — but they are generic Android guides. None of them describes backing up a Micromax phone's camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone. The generic Android guides in the search results are the closest thing to a phone-to-USB guide, and they say nothing about Micromax.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the device and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy or a card reader is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On a Micromax phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip. On any of the six covered Micromax phones that list OTG, SnapDisk picks them for you; on the Canvas 2 (2017) there is no phone route at all, so copy through a computer or pull the microSD card — every covered Micromax phone takes one.
