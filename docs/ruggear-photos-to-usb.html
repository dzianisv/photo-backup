Back up RugGear photos to a USB drive (no computer)

RugGear's Android phones (RG655, RG850, and RG880) have USB host support on at least the RG655 — the RG655 spec table lists USB OTG as Yes, the RG310 lists USB on-the-go, and the RG935 tablet lists OTG as supported — and RugGear has no phone-to-USB backup tool of its own: the RG655 manual gives no backup advice at all, and the stock photo backup is Google Photos. One honest caveat up front: the RG850 and RG880 datasheets I found do not list OTG either way, so check with a drive before relying on it, and the RG655 has a micro USB port, so it needs a micro-USB OTG adapter while the RG850 and RG880 are USB-C. Here is the manual way, and the one-tap version.

What you need

A RugGear Android phone (Android 7.0 or later for SnapDisk) — the RG655 runs Android 11, the RG850 runs Android 8.1 Oreo, and the RG880 runs Android 13 — so no adapter is needed for a USB-C flash drive on the RG850 and the RG880, while the RG655 has a micro USB port and needs a micro-USB OTG adapter for a regular USB drive — and enough space on the drive for the photos you want to keep. Three RugGear specifics: first, OTG is confirmed on the RG655 — the spec table lists USB OTG as Yes — and on the RG310 and the RG935 tablet, but the RG850 and RG880 datasheets I found do not list OTG either way, so plug a drive in and check Settings > Storage before relying on it on those models. Second, RugGear has no phone-to-USB backup tool of its own — the RG655 manual gives no backup advice at all; the stock photo backup is Google Photos; RugGear camera photos live in DCIM/Camera, as on any Android phone. The RG655 takes a microSD card up to 128GB in FAT32 format, the RG850 takes one up to 128GB, and the RG880 takes one too, but on a hybrid SIM slot — so a card is an option, and it costs you a second SIM, and the card still stays in the phone and opening it anywhere else needs a card reader, while a USB drive plugs into any computer as it is. Third, SnapDisk installs from the v0.1.2 APK on the GitHub release page today and from Play once the listing is live. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 can't hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit. Note the RG655's own card reader does not support exFAT, so a microSD card over 4 GB per file is not an option on that phone either — another reason to use a USB drive.

Method 1: SnapDisk (one tap, background copy)

SnapDisk is a free Android app that does exactly this job and nothing else. Install it from the v0.1.2 APK (it's not on Google Play yet), then:

  1. Plug in the USB drive. It mounts on plug-in on most RugGear Android phones — look for a "USB storage added" notification. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive. The RG655 has OTG, so the drive should appear on it; on the RG850 and the RG880, whose datasheets do not list OTG, check Settings > Storage to confirm the drive mounted.
  2. Open SnapDisk and check the folders. The app opens to a settings screen with a From folder and a To folder, each with a folder picker. The defaults are already right for most people: From /sdcard/DCIM (your camera photos — RugGear camera photos live in DCIM/Camera), To /disk/DCIM on the drive. If your photos live somewhere else, tap the From picker and choose that folder — the picker is the Android system folder picker, and the app keeps access to it.
  3. Tap Backup. SnapDisk offers a backup in a notification when the drive connects: "External storage is connected. Backup photos?" Tap it and your whole library copies oldest-first — your earliest photos first — in the background, while you use the phone normally. The copy mirrors the source folder structure on the drive, so DCIM stays DCIM.
  4. Watch the progress notification — real percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left — with Pause and Stop. You can pause and resume any time.
  5. Wait for "Backup complete." Every file is size-verified after copying, so the counts are proof, not a promise. Files that already copied are never copied twice — so if the free 5 GB per-run limit stops a big library partway, just run it again and it picks up where it left off.

The copies are the original files. SnapDisk streams each file byte-for-byte from the phone to the drive — the code reads the file and writes the same bytes out, with no decode, no recompression, and no resize — and then checks that the copy on the drive is the same size as the original. Other apps copy originals byte-for-byte too; what SnapDisk adds is the automated whole-library run: oldest-first order, progress with Pause and Stop, resume where it stopped, and skip-if-already-copied on the next run. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device.

Method 2: a file manager (the stock route, no app needed)

A file manager can copy your photos to the USB drive — Dr.Fone's route is an OTG adapter copy, and AnExplorer's route is the same, and either is the manual route for external storage: 1. Plug in the USB drive — a notification should appear saying "USB storage added"; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive. 2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. 3. Go back to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. 4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). 5. Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos. This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason SnapDisk exists: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. For a library that grows every day, the app's oldest-first background copy is the calmer option.

Method 3: RugGear's own tools (Google Photos, Google Drive, or a microSD card — not phone-to-USB)

If you want RugGear's own route, know what each tool actually does — the RG655 manual gives no backup advice at all, so there is no RugGear backup page to point at, and none of the stock tools writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app, and it is the stock photo backup on RugGear Android phones: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead. 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — "on Android devices the default built-in service is Google Drive. With Google Drive you have 15Gb free" — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and Google accounts only offer 15 GB of free storage. 3. A microSD card is the local option RugGear documents — the RG655 takes a card up to 128GB in FAT32 format, the RG850 takes one up to 128GB, and the RG880 takes one too. It is a real local copy — but the card stays in the phone, the card shares the SIM tray with the second SIM, the RG655's card reader does not support exFAT, and opening the card anywhere else needs a card reader. 4. A computer copy is the other way to move files off the phone — connect the phone to a computer using the USB cable and make a copy of the files using the file browser. It is a real backup — but it needs a PC in between, and the backup lands on the PC, not on a USB drive as plain files straight from the phone. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1 or 2 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 18, 2026.

What this does not do

A few honest limits, so the automatic copy is not oversold:

  • Photos and videos only. SnapDisk copies image and video files. Documents, voice notes, and anything that isn't a photo or video is skipped — Google backup is the tool if you want contacts, messages, and settings in the same backup, not just photos.
  • Only the folder you pick. By default that's DCIM — your camera photos. Photos in other folders (Downloads, app folders) only copy if you point the From folder at them, or at a parent folder that contains them.
  • Free 5 GB per run. A library bigger than 5 GB stops at the cap; run it again and it picks up where it stopped. Pro ($9.99, one-time) removes the limit and starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk.
  • Files over 4 GB on FAT32 are skipped. Very long videos on a FAT32 drive are listed with their reason instead of failing the backup. Reformat the drive to exFAT if you need them.
  • It never deletes. SnapDisk only copies. Freeing space on the phone is copy, check the copy on the drive, then delete the originals yourself — never the other way round.
  • The RG170 and RG360 are not Android phones. RugGear's compact PTT handsets run a proprietary OS, not Android, so SnapDisk — which needs Android 7.0 or later — cannot install on them at all. This article is for the Android RugGear phones (RG655, RG850, RG880) and the RG935 tablet.
  • OTG is not confirmed on every RugGear model. The RG655 spec table lists USB OTG as Yes, the RG310 lists USB on-the-go, and the RG935 tablet lists OTG as supported — but the RG850 and RG880 datasheets I found do not list OTG either way, so confirm the drive mounts in Settings > Storage on those models before relying on it. SnapDisk still needs Android 7.0 or later.

What to avoid

  • Buying an adapter you don't need — or the wrong one. A USB-C flash drive plugs straight into the RG850 and the RG880, which have USB-C — so an adapter is only needed for a regular USB-A drive. The RG655 has a micro USB port, so it needs a micro-USB OTG adapter, not a USB-C one. A plain micro-USB charging cable is not an OTG adapter: it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your phone up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1 or 2, not a cloud backup.
  • Mistaking a PC tool for a phone-to-USB copy. Dr.Fone's PC software backs your phone up — but it is a copy to a computer, and the backup lands on the PC. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route, not a desktop copy.
  • Mistaking a spec table or a user manual for a phone-to-USB guide. The FindPare spec table for the RG655 and the RG655 user manual are real RugGear pages — but the spec table only lists OTG as Yes, and the manual gives no backup advice at all. Neither of them describes copying your camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone.
  • Mistaking a microSD card copy for a USB backup. Copying DCIM to a microSD card is a real local copy — the RG655 takes a card up to 128GB — but the card stays in the phone, it shares the SIM tray with the second SIM, the RG655's card reader does not support exFAT, and opening it anywhere else needs a card reader; a USB drive plugs into any computer as it is.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 18, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On RugGear the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.
  • Installing SnapDisk on a PTT handset. The RG170 and the RG360 run a proprietary OS, not Android — SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later and cannot install on them. If your RugGear has a 2.4-inch or 3-inch screen and a physical keypad with no Play Store, it is one of those handsets, not an Android phone.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. The file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the phone. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip. Automate the copy, check it on the drive, then delete from the phone.
