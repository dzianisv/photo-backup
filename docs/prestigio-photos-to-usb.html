Method 1: SnapDisk — proven on nine

SnapDisk is the one-tap version. It needs Android 7.0 or later, which twenty-eight covered Prestigio phones clear — and the one-tap backup is proven on nine of them: each one clears the floor and lists USB OTG Yes.

  1. Install SnapDisk v0.1.2 — it is not on Google Play yet, so install the APK from the GitHub release page. You do not need an account, and there are no ads.
  2. Plug the USB drive into the phone through a micro-USB OTG adapter — no Prestigio phone takes a USB-C drive straight in.
  3. Open SnapDisk and tap Backup. SnapDisk copies photos and videos oldest-first from the phone to the drive, in the background, with live progress, Pause and Stop.
  4. Wait for the copy to finish, then open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

Free version: backs up manually, up to 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase — auto-backup on connect and unlimited size. On the eleven mounting-test phones, SnapDisk installs — they clear Android 7.0 — but the one-tap backup is unproven until a drive mounts, so plug the drive in first and check the phone sees it. On the eight OTG-No phones, SnapDisk installs too, but the drive will not mount, so the one-tap backup does not work on those eight at all. On the twelve below-floor phones, SnapDisk does not install at all, so use Method 3 or 4 instead.

Method 2: file manager + OTG adapter

Same plug, no app to install. On nine phones it works exactly as on any other Android phone with OTG support:

  1. Plug the USB drive into the phone through a micro-USB OTG adapter.
  2. Open the phone's file manager and find the DCIM folder — that is where the camera photos live.
  3. Select the photos and videos, copy them to the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish.
  4. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

On the eleven mounting-test phones — the three contested and the eight unstated on OTG — plug the drive in and check the phone sees it before trusting this method: if nothing appears, the drive does not mount and this method does not work on that phone. On the eight OTG-No phones the drive will not mount at all, so this method does not work on those eight. On the twelve below-floor phones the file-manager copy is unproven too — none of them lists USB OTG Yes — so plug the drive in and check before trusting it there as well.

Method 3: through a computer

The fallback that works on all forty covered phones, including the ones no phone-to-USB method reaches with an app: the twelve below-floor phones, the eight OTG-No phones, and the eleven where OTG is contested or unstated.

  1. Plug the phone into the computer with a USB cable — a plain charging cable works here, because the computer is the other end.
  2. On the phone, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer.
  3. On the computer, open the phone's DCIM folder and copy the photos to the computer, then to the USB drive.
  4. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

This is the method Prestigio's own user manuals name — connect the phone to a PC and load files from your PC — the computer copy, then Google Drive for the cloud. It needs the PC in between, so it is the fallback, not the route, when the phone and the drive are all you have.

Method 4: microSD card reader route

If the phone has a card slot — and thirty-two do, for certain — the card is the backup, and the reader is the bridge to the USB drive:

  1. Put a microSD card in the phone and copy the DCIM folder to the card with the file manager.
  2. Take the card out, put it in a card reader, plug the reader into the computer, and copy the photos from the card to the USB drive.
  3. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

A microSD card fits thirty-two phones for certain, so the card-reader route is proven on thirty-two. Ceilings are stated on seventeen, contested on one — the Muze G7 LTE at 32 GB against up to 64 GB — and unstated on fourteen. The slot itself is unstated on eight, so check those phones for a slot before buying a card for them. A card copy is a real local copy, but the card stays in the phone until you take it out, and opening it anywhere else needs the card reader — a USB drive plugs into any computer as it is.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud

The cloud is the last resort, and the one Prestigio's manuals point to alongside the computer copy: the per-model backup FAQ names the computer copy as the most economical and Google Drive as the default cloud, and Google Photos is the photo version of the same idea. Prestigio Multi Cloud — 200 GB of bundled cloud storage — is the same idea with Prestigio's name on it. It works on all forty phones — every one runs Android, so Google Photos runs on all forty.

  1. Open Google Photos, turn on backup, and wait for the upload to finish — this needs Wi-Fi, and a big library takes a while.
  2. On a computer, open Google Photos, download the photos, and copy them to the USB drive.
  3. Open a few photos on the drive before deleting anything from the phone.

Two warnings. First, Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — check the setting before trusting a cloud copy. Second, a cloud backup is not a USB backup: your photos sit on Google's servers, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying for storage. If you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy — that is Method 1 or 2, not a cloud backup.

What this does not do

  • Does not install below Android 7.0. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and twelve covered Prestigio phones run below Android 7.0 as shipped — the Muze B3, the Wize G3, the Muze G3 LTE, the Wize NV3, the Wize P3, the Wize R3, the Wize NK3, the Wize OK3, the Wize PX3, the Grace R7, the Grace Z5 and the Muze B7 — so SnapDisk does not install on any of them. No other Prestigio phone is below the floor.
  • Does not work if the phone does not host a drive. Eight covered Prestigio phones list USB OTG No — the Wize Y3, the Grace V7 LTE, the Muze H3, the Muze K3 LTE, the Wize U3, the Wize Q3, the Muze J3 and the Grace P5 — so on those eight the drive will not mount at all. Eleven more are contested or unstated on OTG, so on those eleven the drive mounting is the test: plug the drive in and check the phone sees it.
  • Does not delete photos. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Does not back up anything but photos and videos. SnapDisk copies image and video files only — no settings, no contacts, no messages, no app data.
  • Does not back up photos from apps that hide them. If an app keeps its photos in a private folder SnapDisk cannot read, those photos stay in the app.
  • Does not compress. SnapDisk copies the original files as they are — no recompression, no resizing, no quality loss.

What to avoid

  • Buying an adapter you don't need — or the wrong one. No Prestigio phone takes USB Type-C, so a USB-C flash drive does not plug straight into any of the forty, and a USB-C OTG adapter is the wrong adapter for every one of them. A regular USB-A drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter on the thirty-five phones whose port is settled — and the port is unstated on five, so check the port on those five first. A plain USB charging cable is not an OTG adapter: it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your phone up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1 or 2, not a cloud backup. Prestigio Multi Cloud is the same idea with Prestigio's name on it: 200 GB of bundled cloud storage, not a USB backup — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.
  • Mistaking a PC tool for a phone-to-USB copy. A computer copy with a USB cable moves files to a computer — but it is a copy to a computer, and the backup lands on the PC. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route, not a desktop copy.
  • Mistaking a spec table or a manual page for a phone-to-USB guide. The Prestigio spec pages are real Prestigio pages — but they only list an Android version, a chipset, or a USB connector type. None of them describes copying your camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone. The Prestigio user manuals are the closest thing to Prestigio backup advice, and they name the computer copy.
  • Mistaking OTG support on one model for OTG support on the other. Nine of the forty phones list USB OTG Yes — but eight list USB OTG No, three are contested between two sources and eight more do not list it at all. Do not assume the next Prestigio phone hosts a drive just because nine of them do — and do not assume a phone that clears Android 7.0 hosts a drive either, because eight floor-clearing phones list OTG No.
  • Mistaking a microSD card copy for a USB backup. Copying DCIM to a microSD card is a real local copy — thirty-two phones take a card — but the card stays in the phone, and opening it anywhere else needs a card reader; a USB drive plugs into any computer as it is.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 20, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On a Prestigio phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive, plus Prestigio Multi Cloud — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. The file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the phone. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.
  • Trusting one spec listing. The Muze G5 LTE is two Android numbers — 7.0 per hazelphone against 8.1 per devicesfaq and mobilexfiles — the Muze V3 LTE is two release dates — November 2018 against August 2018 — the Muze B5 is two storage numbers — 8 GB against 16 GB — the Muze B7 is two Android numbers — 6.0 against 7.0 — and the Muze G7 LTE's card is two ceilings — 32 GB against up to 64 GB — so check the unit before buying a drive expecting a particular spec. One source is flat-out wrong on the Muze J5: devicesfaq's FAQ page says Android 5.1, 3 GB RAM and 32 GB, against 8.1, 2 GB and 16 GB in the other sources — and mobilespecs' Muze G5 LTE unlock page is garbled, listing a 5.5-inch display and an August 2017 date against 5.2 inches and September 2018 elsewhere. The Android floor is settled on every one either way: twenty-eight clear 7.0 and twelve run below it on every source.
