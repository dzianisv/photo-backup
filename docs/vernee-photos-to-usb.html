Method 1: SnapDisk — proven on twelve

Method 1 is the one-tap version. It uses SnapDisk, the free backup app this site is about — SnapDisk. Free version: manual backup, up to 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase and adds auto-backup on connect plus unlimited size. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, which sixteen covered Vernee phones clear — the lowest clears it exactly, at 7.0 — so the app installs on sixteen, and the one-tap backup is proven on twelve: each one lists USB OTG Yes.

  1. Check the Android version first. Settings > About phone > Android version. The Apollo, the Apollo Lite and the Mars run 6.0 — SnapDisk will not install on those three, so stop here and use Method 3 or Method 4 instead. The Thor runs 6.0 shipped with a 7.0 update reported, and the Apollo X is listed at 6.0 by Smartprix and 7.0 by UL Benchmarks — if either one says 7.0 on the unit, the one-tap backup works. The Mars Pro, Thor E, Thor Plus, Mix 2, Active, X, X1, V2 Pro, M5, M6, M7, M8 Pro, T3 Pro, X2, M3 and Apollo 2 all run 7.0 or newer — the Mars Pro, Thor E, Thor Plus, Mix 2, Active, X, X1, M5 and M6 run 7.0 or 7.1; the V2 Pro runs 8.1; the M7 runs 8.0, 8.1 or 9.0 depending on the listing; the M8 Pro runs 8.0 or 8.1; the T3 Pro runs 8.0 or 8.1; the X2 runs 8.0 or 9.0; the M3 runs 8.0 or 8.1; and the Apollo 2 runs 8.0 or 8.1. If it says 6.0, you are on one of the three below-floor phones — check the model name before buying a drive expecting the one-tap backup.
  2. Install SnapDisk. SnapDisk is not on Google Play yet — the listing is being prepared — so install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. On the phone, allow installing from unknown sources when asked. It needs Android 7.0 or later, which sixteen covered Vernee phones clear — the lowest clears it exactly, at 7.0 — so on a Vernee phone the APK installs on sixteen, and the one-tap backup is proven on twelve.
  3. Plug in the USB drive. On twelve covered phones that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in — no adapter, no cable. On the nine micro-USB models — the Thor, Mix 2, Thor E, Thor Plus, M5, M6, T3 Pro, M3 and X2 — that is a micro-USB OTG adapter between the phone and the drive. A plain sync cable is the wrong cable: it connects a phone to a computer, not a drive to a phone.
  4. Look for the mount notification. On the twelve proven phones — the Mars Pro, Mix 2, Thor E, Thor Plus, Active, X, X1, V2 Pro, M7, M8 Pro, Apollo 2 and T3 Pro — Android notices the drive and offers it as storage, and SnapDisk picks it as the To folder. On the Thor and the Apollo X, the same applies if the unit runs 7.0. On the four mounting-test phones — the M5, M6, M3 and X2 — if nothing appears, check Settings > Storage: if the drive is listed there, tap it and mount it, then run the backup. If the drive never shows up on one of those four, the phone does not host it, and Method 3 is the route.
  5. Check the copy, then delete the originals. Open the drive in Files and count: the backup is only done when every photo is on the drive and opens. Then delete the originals on the phone if you need the space — and only then.

SnapDisk copies the photos byte-for-byte: the file on the drive is the same file the phone took, with the same pixels. There is no re-encoding step, so nothing is lost in the copy — the only place quality gets lost is a cloud backup with a "Storage saver" setting.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter

Method 2 is the manual version of the same thing — no app, just the phone's file manager and a drive. The file manager on a Vernee phone is Files, or Files by Google, per devicesfaq — so this method names it directly. It works on the twelve proven phones, on the Thor and the Apollo X if the unit runs 7.0, and on the four mounting-test phones if the drive shows up in Settings.

  1. Plug the drive into the phone. On twelve covered phones that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in. On the nine micro-USB models — the Thor, Mix 2, Thor E, Thor Plus, M5, M6, T3 Pro, M3 and X2 — that is a micro-USB OTG adapter between the phone and the drive.
  2. Open Files and find the DCIM folder. Your camera photos live in the DCIM folder — on most phones that is Internal storage > DCIM > Camera. Screenshots live in Pictures > Screenshots, and WhatsApp photos in Android > media > com.whatsapp > WhatsApp > Media > WhatsApp Images.
  3. Select the photos and copy them to the drive. Long-press one photo, select the rest, tap Copy, navigate to the USB drive and tap Paste. Copy, not Move — see "What to avoid" below.
  4. Wait, then check. Copying takes time — thousands of photos can take half an hour or more. Open the drive in Files and count before you delete anything.

This is the same copy Method 1 does, done by hand. It copies the original files as they are — no re-encoding, no quality loss.

Method 3: through a computer

Method 3 is the fallback — and the one the per-model backup FAQ calls the most economical method. It works on all twenty-one, including the three below-7.0 phones and the four mounting-test phones if the drive never shows up on the phone. Plug the phone into a computer with the USB cable, open the phone as a drive, and drag the DCIM folder onto a USB drive. Copy, not Move, and check the copy before deleting anything. It works — it just takes the manual step Method 1 does for you.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route

Method 4 is the card route, and on Vernee it is a real one: a microSD card fits twenty phones for certain, so this route is proven on twenty — unlike RedMagic, where it is proven on none of the fourteen. It is contested on the Apollo 2, so check the slot on the unit before buying a card for that one.

  1. Put the card in the phone. The slot is dedicated on the Active and the V2 Pro. It is shared with the second SIM on the Mars Pro, M7, M8 Pro, X2, M5, M6 and Thor Plus. The Mix 2 and the X1 are contested — dedicated per Smartprix, shared per Kimovil. Card ceilings are contested on six phones — the V2 Pro (128 GB per Smartprix, Phoneinfo, Gizchina's review and phonecorridor's own full table, 256 GB per phonecorridor's key specs), the Mars (128 GB per Notebookcheck, TechAdvisor and the body of the AndroidHeadlines review, 256 GB per the specs section of that same review and per Gizmochina), the Apollo Lite (128 GB per Smartprix, Notebookcheck and Gizchina's "9 main selling points", 256 GB per Gizchina's review), the M7 (128 GB per Smartprix, devicesfaq and mobilexfiles, 256 GB per unite4buy), the M3 (64 GB per unite4buy, 128 GB per Gizchina's presale piece and Mobilespecs) and the Apollo X (32 GB per Smartprix, 128 GB per Gizchina's CEO piece) — so buy at or under the lower number if you want the ceiling every source agrees on. The T3 Pro takes up to 32 GB per every source, so a bigger card is wasted on it.
  2. Set the camera to save to the card. Open the Camera app, go to Settings, and set Storage to the SD card. New photos then land on the card, not the internal storage.
  3. Move the photos already on the phone. Open Files, select the DCIM folder, and move it to the card. The photos already on the phone do not move themselves — they live in internal storage until you move them.
  4. Take the card out and read it in a card reader. Plug the reader into the computer, or into the phone with an OTG adapter, and copy the photos to a USB drive.

Two warnings: the photos already on the phone live in internal storage, not on the card — so moving the card alone backs up nothing until you move them — and the card is only a backup if the photos end up on it, so check the card before you delete anything on the phone.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud

Method 5 is the cloud route — and the one Vernee's own per-model FAQ names for photos alone: Google Photos uploads them automatically when on Wi-Fi. It works on all twenty-one, it is free up to the Google account's 15 GB, and it is the only method here that backs up without a cable. But it is not a USB backup: the photos live on Google's servers, not on a drive you hold, and the "Storage saver" setting compresses them — so check that setting before trusting a cloud copy. If you want the originals on a drive you own, Method 1 or Method 2 is the route.

What this does not do

  • It does not install below Android 7.0. Three covered Vernee phones run Android 6.0 — the Apollo, the Apollo Lite and the Mars — so on those three there is no SnapDisk route at all, and no rival-app route either: VaultDrive needs Android 8 and FotoSafe needs Android 9, so on a 6.0 phone the only routes are Method 3 and Method 4.
  • It does not work if the phone does not host a drive. The four mounting-test phones — the M5, M6, M3 and X2 — may or may not mount a drive, because no source settles OTG on them. If the drive never shows up on one of those four, Method 3 is the route.
  • It does not delete photos. A backup copies the photos to the drive; deleting the originals is a separate step, and only after you have checked the copy.
  • It does not back up your settings, contacts or messages. It backs up photos and videos — the files in your camera roll.
  • It does not back up photos from apps that hide them. Photos saved inside an app's private storage — WhatsApp's hidden media, or a locked album — may not be in the DCIM folder, so check the app's own export.
  • It does not compress or shrink your photos. The copy is the original file — the same pixels, the same size.

What to avoid

  • Don't move instead of copying. Move deletes the original as it goes — if the copy fails halfway, the photos are gone. Copy first, check the copy, then delete.
  • Don't unplug the drive until the copy is done. Unplugging mid-copy corrupts the files being written. Wait for the progress to finish, then eject the drive in Settings before pulling it out.
  • Don't buy a micro-USB adapter for a Type-C phone. Twelve covered phones take USB Type-C — a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and the adapter is the wrong buy.
  • Don't buy a USB-C drive expecting it to plug into the Thor, Mix 2, Thor E, Thor Plus, M5, M6, T3 Pro, M3 or X2. Those nine are micro-USB, and a USB-C drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter between them.
  • Don't buy a card bigger than the contested ceiling without checking. The V2 Pro, Mars, Apollo Lite, M7, M3 and Apollo X each have two card ceilings — so buy at or under the lower number if you want the ceiling every source agrees on.
  • Don't buy a card at all for the Apollo 2 without checking the slot on the unit first. The slot itself is contested on that one — devicesfaq, Kimovil, Phoneinfo and mobilexfiles say a slot, while Smartprix and Mobilespecs say no slot.
  • Don't buy a card bigger than 32 GB for the T3 Pro. Every source agrees it takes up to 32 GB — a bigger card is wasted on it.
  • Don't count on VaultDrive's AppBrain floor on any Vernee phone. Its own site says Android 8, which seven clear — but AppBrain lists Android 12.1, which none of the twenty-one clears, since the highest shipped version is 9.0.
  • Don't count on FotoSafe on shipped software on any Vernee phone. It needs Android 9 — the M7 clears only on the 9.0 listings, and the X2 clears the version floor only on its 9.0 listings with OTG unstated in every source.
  • Don't trust a single M7 listing. Its Android version, chipset, display, battery, port, release date and card ceiling are all two or three numbers — check the unit, not the listing.
  • Don't let one folder hold every photo. A backup is only a backup if the copy exists somewhere else — a drive, a card, or the cloud. One copy is not a backup.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a copy. A backup finds your photos, copies them all, and tells you when it's done. On a Vernee phone the honest position is simple: twenty-one phones, sixteen clear Android 7.0, twelve list USB OTG Yes, twenty take a microSD card — the one-tap backup is proven on twelve, and the card-reader route is proven on twenty.
