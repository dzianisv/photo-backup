TL;DR: Thirteen Wiko phones can run SnapDisk with the one-tap backup: the View, View Prime, View 2, View 3, View 3 Lite, View 4, View 4 Lite, View 5, View 5 Plus, Power U20, Power U30, Y80 and Y81 all clear Android 7.0 and list USB OTG — so plug a drive in, tap the notification, and SnapDisk copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, free up to 5 GB per run, Pro $9.99 one-time for auto-backup and unlimited size. Six more clear Android 7.0 but no source found states OTG for them — the View XL, Lenny 4, Y50, Y52, Y62 and Y82 — so on those the drive mounting is the test: SnapDisk installs, but plug a drive in and look for the mount notification before counting on the one-tap route. The Y60 and the Y61 list OTG No in Wiko's own datasheets, so no phone route exists on those two — and the Highway 4G runs Android 4.4.2, so no backup app installs on it at all. What works on the OTG models is the manual route: open the file manager and copy DCIM/Camera to the drive, or use the computer copy, or pull the microSD card and copy it with a card reader. The copies are the original files either way: a file-manager copy moves the files as they are, never recompresses them, and the only place quality gets lost is a cloud backup with a "Storage saver" setting.   • Highway 4G (2014 phone): Android 4.4.2 KitKat. Cannot run SnapDisk — Android 4.4.2 is below the 7.0 minimum.

Five Wiko specifics: first, the connector splits the range in two — the View 5, View 5 Plus and Power U30 take USB-C, so a USB-C flash drive plugs straight into those three and a regular USB drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter, while every other covered model takes micro-USB 2.0 and needs a micro-USB OTG adapter — and a USB-C adapter does not fit a micro-USB port, so check your unit's port before buying anything. Second, OTG is the real split, not the Android version — thirteen phones list it, six more clear Android 7.0 with OTG unconfirmed, and the Y60 and the Y61 list OTG No in Wiko's own datasheets, so on those two the phone cannot host a drive at all and the computer copy and the card-reader route are the only routes. Third, Wiko's own datasheets are the source for most of the OTG answers in this guide — the Y60 and Y61 say OTG No, the Y81, Y80, View 4 Lite, View 5, View 5 Plus, Power U20 and Power U30 say OTG Yes — while the Y52's datasheet shows a USB 2.0 port with no OTG line at all, so its OTG is unconfirmed rather than No. Fourth, almost every covered model takes microSD — up to 512 GB on the View 5 per one source, up to 256 GB on the View 4, View 4 Lite, View 5, Power U20, Power U30 and Y61, up to 128 GB on the View, Y60 and Y81, up to 64 GB on the View 2 and Y52, and up to 32 GB on the Lenny 4 — so a card is a second local copy you can count on here, and the card-reader route in Method 4 works on every model with a card, with no OTG question at all. Fifth, no source found names the stock file manager on a Wiko phone, so Method 2 uses Files by Google or the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 can't hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — copy those to a computer instead, or reformat the drive to exFAT, which has no such limit — and note that a phone this old may not read exFAT, so FAT32 is the safer format for the phone route.

Method 1: SnapDisk on the thirteen (the one-tap route)

On the thirteen phones that clear Android 7.0 and list USB OTG, the automated route exists. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and all thirteen clear it — the View runs 7.1, the View Prime runs 7.1, the View 2 runs 8.0, the View 3 and View 3 Lite run 9.0, the View 4 and View 4 Lite run 10, the View 5 and View 5 Plus run 10, the Power U20 and Power U30 run 11, the Y80 runs 9.0 and the Y81 runs 10 Go — and all thirteen list USB OTG:

  1. Install SnapDisk. Until the Play listing is live, install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on all thirteen — and on the six more that clear 7.0 with OTG unconfirmed, and on the Y60 and Y61 too. It does not install on the Highway 4G, which runs Android 4.4.2, or on the early Wiko lines, which predate Android 7.0.
  2. Plug in the USB drive. On the View 5, View 5 Plus and Power U30 a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and a regular USB drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter. On the other ten a micro-USB OTG adapter is the connection — the adapter was not in the box on these models, so buy the adapter with the OTG wiring inside it, not a plain sync cable.
  3. Tap Backup in the notification. SnapDisk offers the backup when the drive connects, then copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  4. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. Its limits: it works only on the thirteen phones that clear Android 7.0 and list OTG — not on the six with unconfirmed OTG, where the drive mounting is the test, not on the Y60 or Y61, which cannot host a drive at all, and not on the Highway 4G, where no backup app installs at all. On the six unconfirmed models, plug a drive in and look for the mount notification: if it mounts, Method 1 works; if it does not, Methods 3 and 4 are the routes. On the Y60, Y61 and Highway 4G, Methods 3 and 4 are the routes.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter (works on the thirteen)

This is the stock route, and the only phone route on the thirteen OTG models besides SnapDisk. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive, and OTG is listed on thirteen covered models:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with the right OTG adapter. On the View 5, View 5 Plus and Power U30 a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and a regular USB drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter. On the other ten that is a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain micro-USB sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive.
  2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the stock file manager on a Wiko phone, so use Files by Google or the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit. On the six unconfirmed models — the View XL, Lenny 4, Y50, Y52, Y62 and Y82 — this step is the test: if the drive shows up, the file-manager route works; if it does not, the phone does not host drives, and Methods 3 and 4 are the routes.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On these phones the photos are the 13 MP shots from the rear camera on the View and the View 4 — 48 MP on the View 5 and View 5 Plus, 13 MP on the Power U20 and the Y81, 8 MP on the Y61 and the Y62, 5 MP on the Y52 — plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on the thirteen OTG phones: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. On the Y60 and the Y61 there is no phone route at all, because neither can host a drive — so the computer copy and the card-reader route are the only routes on those two — and on the Highway 4G there is no app route at all, because it cannot run SnapDisk.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the Wiko phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. It is also the fallback for the Y60 and the Y61, which cannot host a drive, for the six unconfirmed models if the drive does not mount, and for the Highway 4G, which cannot run SnapDisk at all. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 1 or 2 is the route, or Method 4.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (works on every model with a card)

Almost every covered Wiko model takes a microSD card — up to 512 GB on the View 5 per one source, up to 256 GB on the View 4, View 4 Lite, View 5, Power U20, Power U30 and Y61, up to 128 GB on the View, Y60 and Y81, up to 64 GB on the View 2 and Y52, and up to 32 GB on the Lenny 4 — and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old phone manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. Two warnings: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in the file manager. And on the Power U20 the card shares the SIM slot, so a card and a second SIM compete for the same tray — on the Power U30 there is no dispute, the slot is dedicated — so check the tray on your unit before assuming you can run two SIMs and a card at once.

Method 5: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

Wiko offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no Wiko-specific backup advice turned up in any source beyond a computer copy, Google Drive, or Google Photos — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead; and its "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p). 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and Google accounts only offer 5 GB of free storage by default since May 2026, with the full 15 GB available only after verifying a phone number on the account. 3. Google backup is what Android itself names: personal settings back up to Google's servers with a Google account, and are restored to a new phone when you sign in with that account. It is a real backup — but it backs up settings, not a folder of photo files on a drive you hold. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. And one Wiko-only limit: no source found confirms the current Google Photos or Drive app still installs on the Highway 4G or the other early Wiko phones. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2, 3 or 4 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What this does not do

A few honest limits, so the manual copy is not oversold:

  • No automated backup exists for the Y60, the Y61 or the Highway 4G at all. The Y60 and the Y61 list OTG No in Wiko's own datasheets, so no phone route exists on either — and the Highway 4G runs Android 4.4.2, below SnapDisk's Android 7.0 minimum and below VaultDrive's Android 8 or later per its own site. The computer copy and the card-reader route are manual, one batch at a time, with no progress detail, no resume, and no verification.
  • No backup app exists for a Wiko phone that cannot host a drive — and two covered models cannot. Unlike Bullitt, where all eight models listed OTG, the Y60 and the Y61 list OTG No — so on those two the file-manager route does not exist either, and only the computer copy and the card-reader route work. On the six unconfirmed models the drive mounting is the test.
  • Photos and videos only, from the folder you pick. By default that's DCIM — your camera photos. Photos in other folders (Downloads, app folders) only copy if you select them too, and photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the microSD card are not on the card for the Method 4 route.

  • It never deletes. The file manager's Copy only copies. Freeing space on the device is copy, check the copy on the drive, then delete the originals yourself — never the other way round.
  • OTG is unconfirmed on six models, not No. The View XL, Lenny 4, Y50, Y52, Y62 and Y82 clear Android 7.0, so SnapDisk installs on all six — but no source found states OTG or USB host for any of them, and the Y52's own datasheet shows a USB 2.0 port with no OTG line at all. The View 4, View 4 Lite, Power U20, Power U30 and Y61 take up to 256 GB; the View, Y60 and Y81 take up to 128 GB; the View 2 and Y52 take up to 64 GB; the Lenny 4 takes up to 32 GB; the View 3 Lite, View 5 Plus, Y50, Y62, Y80 and Y82 list a microSD slot with no ceiling stated in any source found. What to avoid

  • Assuming SnapDisk backs up to a drive on every Wiko phone. SnapDisk installs on nineteen of the twenty-two covered models — but the one-tap backup to a drive works only on the thirteen that list OTG. Do not buy Pro for a Y60, Y61 or Highway 4G expecting a drive backup, and do not trust a store listing that says "compatible" without checking the Android version first.
  • Assuming the Y60 or Y61 hosts a drive because it runs Android 9 or 10. Both clear the Android minimum and both can install SnapDisk — but Wiko's own datasheets list OTG No for both. Check the datasheet, not the Android version, before planning anything around the phone route.
  • Assuming OTG because the phone is new and the port is USB-C. OTG is listed on the View 5, View 5 Plus and Power U30 — but the Y52's datasheet shows a USB 2.0 port with no OTG line, and the Y50, Y62 and Y82 list only microUSB 2.0. Plug in a drive and look for the mount notification before buying an adapter.
  • Buying the wrong adapter for the port. The View 5, View 5 Plus and Power U30 take USB-C; every other covered model takes micro-USB 2.0. A USB-C adapter does not fit a micro-USB port, and a micro-USB adapter does not fit a USB-C port — buy the adapter that matches your model's port, with the OTG wiring inside it.
  • Assuming the Power U20 takes two SIMs and a card at once. The ohsem review says the Power U20's card uses the shared SIM slot; droidafrica says the Power U30's slot is dedicated. Check the tray on your unit before buying a second SIM or a card.
  • Assuming the cloud still works on Android 4.4. No source found confirms the current Google Photos or Drive app still installs on the Highway 4G or the other early Wiko phones. If the cloud app is not already on your phone and working, do not plan a backup around installing it now.
  • Assuming your photos are on the microSD card. On these phones the camera may save to internal storage or to the card depending on the setting. If you pull the card for Method 4 without checking, you may copy an empty DCIM folder while the photos sit in internal storage. Check where the photos live first.
  • Assuming the View 4's Android version or the View 5's or Y60's microSD ceiling. The View 4 is Android 10 per five sources and Android 9 per versus; the View 5 is up to 256 GB per the droidafrica spec table and up to 512 GB per its overview; the Y60 is up to 128 GB per Wiko's datasheet and up to 32 GB per droidafrica. Check Settings > About phone and the card slot on your unit before sizing anything around any of these numbers.
  • Assuming Wiko has a backup app. No Wiko backup app to a USB drive turned up in any source. The one-tap route on the thirteen OTG phones is SnapDisk, not a Wiko app — and on the Y60, Y61 and Highway 4G there is no app route at all.
  • Assuming a Go edition phone is too weak to run the app. The Y60 and Y61 run Android Go with 1 GB of RAM and both can install SnapDisk — the blocker on those two is the missing OTG, not the Go edition. The Y50, Y52 and Y81 are Go edition too, and all three clear the minimum.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the device — never the other way round. The file manager only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. A file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the device. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish before unplugging, and eject the drive from the notification or Settings > Storage first.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your device up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1, 2, 3 or 4, not a cloud backup. And Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and since May 2026 Google accounts get only 5 GB free by default, with the full 15 GB only after verifying a phone number. On the Highway 4G and the early Wiko phones the cloud apps may not even install any more.
  • Mistaking a generic guide for a Wiko guide. The YouTube OTG video, the Dr.Fone guide, the epictechtips guide and the softhandtech memory-stick guide are real documents — but they are generic Android guides, and the Android Central thread is a forum thread. None of them describes backing up a Wiko phone's camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone. The generic Android guides in the search results are the closest thing to a phone-to-USB guide, and they say nothing about Wiko.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the device and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy or a card reader is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On a Wiko phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and on the Highway 4G and the early Wiko phones those apps may not install at all any more.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip. On the View, View Prime, View 2, View 3, View 3 Lite, View 4, View 4 Lite, View 5, View 5 Plus, Power U20, Power U30, Y80 or Y81, SnapDisk picks them for you; on the View XL, Lenny 4, Y50, Y52, Y62 or Y82, plug a drive in first and see if it mounts; on the Y60 or Y61, no phone route exists at all, so copy the whole DCIM folder with the computer copy or the card reader, check it on the drive, then delete from the device — and on the Highway 4G, no app installs at all, so the computer copy or the card reader is the route.
