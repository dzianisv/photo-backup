Method 1: the built-in file manager and a micro-USB OTG adapter (the stock route, no app needed)

There is no one-tap app route on a Casio phone, so the file manager is Method 1, not the fallback. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive:

  1. Test whether the phone hosts a drive before buying anything else. Borrow a micro-USB OTG adapter and a USB drive, plug the drive in, and look for a mount notification or a USB storage entry in Settings > Storage. No source found confirms OTG on any Casio phone, so this test is the whole answer: if nothing mounts, skip to Method 2 or 3 — no adapter purchase fixes a phone that never hosts a drive.
  2. Plug in the USB drive with the micro-USB OTG adapter. A plain micro-USB sync cable is the wrong cable — it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. You need an adapter with the OTG wiring inside it. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive.
  3. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the stock file manager on a Casio phone, so use the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit.
  4. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On these phones the photos are the 5 MP shots from the Commando's rear camera or the 8 MP shots from the Commando 4G LTE, the TYPE-L and the CA-201L, plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  5. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on newer phones: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. On a Casio phone there is no app that removes those limits — the manual copy is the best the phone offers.

Method 2: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the Casio phone into the computer with its micro-USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. The manuals for these phones describe exactly this connection — "Connecting to a PC via USB" — so it is the one USB route Casio itself documents. It is also the fallback for any Casio phone whose USB port never hosts a drive. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 1 or 3 is the route. For a Casio camera rather than a phone, the same idea applies with a card reader: the Exilim manuals describe Mass Storage transfer to a computer, or moving the images with a memory card.

Method 3: the microSD card reader route (works on every model)

Every covered G'zOne takes a microSD card up to 32 GB, and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card — on the Commando the card slot sits underneath the battery, so take the battery out first. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the camera guides in the search results describe — remove the memory card from the camera, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. One warning: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in the file manager.

Method 4: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

Casio offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — its own software on these phones, G'zGEAR, is an outdoor sensor suite, not a photo backup — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead; and its "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p). 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and Google accounts only offer 5 GB of free storage by default since May 2026, with the full 15 GB available only after verifying a phone number on the account. 3. Google backup is what Android itself names: personal settings back up to Google's servers with a Google account, and are restored to a new phone when you sign in with that account. It is a real backup — but it backs up settings, not a folder of photo files on a drive you hold. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. And one Casio-only limit, the sharpest one: no source found confirms the current Google Photos or Drive app still installs on Android 4.0 or 4.1 — WhatsApp already refuses the CA-201L because it needs Android 6 or later — so the cloud routes may not even be on your phone any more. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2 or 3 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What this does not do

A few honest limits, so the manual copy is not oversold:

  • No automated backup exists for a Casio phone. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 and VaultDrive needs Android 8 — every G'zOne runs Android 2.2 to 4.1 — so no current backup app installs on these phones at all. The file-manager copy is manual, one batch at a time, with no progress detail, no resume, and no verification.
  • Photos and videos only, from the folder you pick. By default that's DCIM — your camera photos. Photos in other folders (Downloads, app folders) only copy if you select them too, and photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the microSD card are not on the card for the Method 3 route.
  • Files over 4 GB on FAT32 are a problem. Very long videos on a FAT32 drive will not copy — copy those to a computer instead, or reformat the drive to exFAT, noting that a phone this old may not read exFAT, so FAT32 is the safer format for the phone route.

  • OTG is unconfirmed on every Casio phone — and on any G'zOne the drive may not attach at all. What to avoid

  • Assuming a backup app installs on a G'zOne. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 and VaultDrive needs Android 8 — every Casio Android phone runs Android 2.2 to 4.1. Do not buy Pro, do not sideload an APK built for a newer Android, and do not trust a store listing that says "compatible" without checking the Android floor first.
  • Assuming OTG because the port is micro-USB. All four Casio models have a micro-USB 2.0 port, and no source found labels any of them OTG or USB host — a micro-USB port is not a promise the device hosts drives. Plug in a drive and look for the mount notification before buying an adapter.
  • Buying a USB-C adapter for a Casio phone. Every G'zOne uses micro-USB 2.0, not USB-C. A USB-C drive or adapter does not fit the phone at all — you need a micro-USB OTG adapter with the OTG wiring inside it, not a plain sync cable.
  • Assuming a newer Casio phone exists. The TYPE-L was the final NEC Casio handset in 2013, and the mobile phone business had fully ceased by 2014. There is no Casio phone with a newer Android version to upgrade to — the newest one ever made runs Android 4.0.4.
  • Assuming the cloud still works on Android 4.x. WhatsApp already refuses the CA-201L because it needs Android 6 or later, and no source found confirms the current Google Photos or Drive app still installs on Android 4.0 or 4.1. If the cloud app is not already on your phone and working, do not plan a backup around installing it now.
  • Assuming your photos are on the microSD card. On these phones the camera may save to internal storage or to the card depending on the setting. If you pull the card for Method 3 without checking, you may copy an empty DCIM folder while the photos sit in internal storage. Check where the photos live first.
  • Assuming the TYPE-L's battery or the US variant's Android version. The battery is 1,800 mAh per most sources and 1,850 mAh per one; the US variant is Android 4.0.4 on the reviewed unit and 4.1 per one source. Check Settings > About phone on your unit before sizing anything around either number.
  • Assuming G'zGEAR backs up photos. G'zGEAR is Casio's outdoor sensor suite — temperature, orientation, acceleration, atmospheric data — for the outdoors. It never touches a USB drive and it is not a photo backup.
  • Assuming the Commando 4G LTE's immersion rating still holds. The Verizon announcement says immersion 1 m for 30 minutes and one launch report says 1.5 m — but the seals on a phone from 2013 are a decade old. Do not test water resistance with a phone whose photos you have not backed up yet.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the device — never the other way round. The file manager only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. A file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the device. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish before unplugging, and eject the drive from the notification or Settings > Storage first.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your device up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1, 2 or 3, not a cloud backup. And Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and since May 2026 Google accounts get only 5 GB free by default, with the full 15 GB only after verifying a phone number. On a G'zOne the cloud apps may not even install any more.
  • Mistaking a camera manual or a product page for a phone-to-USB guide. The Exilim manuals and the projector guide are real documents — but they describe transferring camera pictures to a computer, or projecting from a USB stick. None of them describes copying your phone's camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone. The generic thumb-drive guide in the search results is the closest thing to a phone-to-USB guide, and it says nothing about Casio.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the device and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy or a card reader is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On a Casio phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and on a G'zOne those apps may not install at all any more.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip. On a Casio phone there is no app that picks them for you, so copy the whole DCIM folder in one go, check it on the drive, then delete from the device.
