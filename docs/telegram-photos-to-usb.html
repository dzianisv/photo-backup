Back up Telegram photos and videos to USB from Android

Your Telegram photos are files on your phone — in Telegram's own folders, which you can point a backup app at. Plug a USB drive into the phone and copy them straight to it, no computer, no upload.

November 22, 2026 · 5 min read

TL;DR: Plug a USB drive into your Android phone, point SnapDisk's From folder at the Telegram Images folder (or the parent Telegram folder for images and videos together), and tap Backup — your Telegram photos and videos copy oldest-first to the drive, with no computer and no upload in between. The honest limits, up front: SnapDisk copies media files only — the photos and videos in the folder you pick. It does not back up chat history (your cloud chats reappear when you sign in anywhere; secret chats are device-bound and never sync), voice messages, documents, stickers outside the picked folder, or the cache. And there is one wall to know about before you start: on Android 11 and later, Telegram's working data lives under Android/data/org.telegram.messenger, and Android's own folder picker refuses that folder — so if your Telegram media only exists there, the one-tap route cannot reach it, and the fallback is Telegram's own Save to Downloads or the PC route, both described below. SnapDisk copies, it never deletes — deleting from the phone afterwards is your step, only after verifying a few photos on the drive. Free version: 5 GB per run; run it again past the cap and it picks up where it stopped, because files already copied are never copied twice. Screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified — keep the phone awake for the run. Where Telegram keeps your photos and videos

Telegram saves every photo and video you download as an ordinary file in a folder on your phone. Where that folder sits depends on the Android version and how Telegram was installed, so check which one exists on your phone before you start:

  • Older setups: Internal storage > Telegram > Telegram Images (photos), Telegram Video (videos), Telegram Documents, Telegram Audio.
  • Android 11 and later: Telegram's working data lives under Internal storage > Android > data > org.telegram.messenger. The built-in file manager may refuse to open Android/data — that is the wall the next-but-one section is about.

Videos live in Telegram Video next to Telegram Images, so picking the parent Telegram folder gets both in one run. You do not need to memorise either path — in the steps below you navigate to the folder in a picker and tap it, so you just follow whichever one exists on your phone. Two things worth knowing before you start. First, if a photo never downloaded (the blurred thumbnail with a download arrow), it is not on your phone yet — open the chat and download it first, or there is nothing to copy. Second, viewing a photo is not downloading it: Telegram keeps a cache so you can reopen what you viewed, and the cache is fragments keyed to the installation, not your photos in any usable sense — do not copy the cache folder and call it a backup. Only files you deliberately downloaded sit in the Telegram Images and Telegram Video folders.

What you need

An Android phone (Android 7.0 or later for SnapDisk); a USB drive your phone can read — a USB-C flash drive, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter (most modern Android phones support OTG; if the phone doesn't see the drive, try a different adapter); and enough space on the drive for the Telegram photos and videos you want to keep. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 can't hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit. And one check before you start: open the Files app, look at the Telegram folder sizes, and divide by 5 GB — that is how many free runs the copy needs, and the arithmetic belongs before you plug the drive in, not halfway through the copy.

The Android/data wall — and what to do about it

This is the one configuration where Method 1 cannot work, and it is worth a section of its own because the search results name the folder without naming the wall. On Android 11 and later, you cannot use the system folder picker to select files under Android/data or Android/obb — Android's own developer documentation says so, for both picking single files and picking a folder tree. SnapDisk's From picker is that system picker (the Storage Access Framework's folder picker), so if you navigate to Android/data/org.telegram.messenger and tap it, the picker answers "Can't use this folder. To protect your privacy, choose another folder." That is the platform's rule, not the app refusing, and no setting in SnapDisk changes it — the restriction applies to every third-party app that asks for a folder, including the built-in file manager, which is why NetworkUstad tells you to connect the phone to a PC instead. What to do depends on where your Telegram media actually sits, so check first in the Files app: if a Telegram folder exists outside Android/data — the top-level Telegram folder with Telegram Images and Telegram Video — pick that one and Method 1 works as written. If the media only exists under Android/data, use one of the two fallbacks. The first is Telegram's own route: in each chat, save the photos and videos you want to keep with Save to Downloads or Save to Gallery, which puts ordinary copies in folders the picker can reach — slow for a whole library, but exact for the files that matter. The second is the PC route from NetworkUstad's guide: connect the phone to a computer in File Transfer mode, copy the Telegram folders to the computer, then copy them from the computer to the USB drive. Neither fallback is one-tap, and this article does not pretend otherwise — the one-tap route covers the Telegram folders the picker can reach, and the wall is the reason the folder check comes before the plug-in, not after.

Method 2: by hand in the Files app (no app needed)

If you'd rather not install anything, Android's built-in Files app can copy the Telegram folder to the USB drive: 1. Plug in the USB drive. A notification or a "USB drive" entry should appear in the Files app under Storage devices. 2. Open Files, browse to Internal storage and find the Telegram folder — Telegram > Telegram Images on older setups, or the folder you found in the check above. 3. Select the photos (or long-press the Telegram Images folder to select the whole thing), tap ⋮ → Copy to, then choose the USB drive as the destination. 4. Wait for the copy to finish — and don't unplug the drive until the Files app says it's done. This works fine for a one-off copy, and it is also the fallback for the one configuration SnapDisk's route gets wrong: if your Telegram media sits under Android/data and the picker refuses it, the Files app at least lets you look — and if it refuses too, the PC route in the section above is the way in. Its limits are the reason SnapDisk exists: no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time Telegram photos pile up. For a Telegram folder that grows every day, the app's oldest-first background copy is the calmer option.

What this does not back up

This is the part people get wrong, so here it is plainly. Copying the Telegram Images and Telegram Video folders backs up the files — the photos and videos. It does not back up:

  • Chat history. Your messages live in Telegram's cloud, not in the Images folder, and they reappear when you sign in on any device. SnapDisk only copies image and video files. For a chat archive, use Telegram Desktop's Export chat history on a computer.
  • Secret chats. Secret chats are device-bound and never sync — they do not travel to a new phone by signing in, and they are not files in the Telegram folders. This article's route does not back them up.
  • Voice messages and audio. Voice messages are .opus audio files, and Telegram Audio holds audio — SnapDisk copies image and video files only (verified in the repo: .jpg, .jpeg, .png, .heic, .heif, .webp, .gif, .bmp, .dng, .raw for images; .mp4, .mov, .mkv, .webm, .3gp, .m4v, .avi for videos), so audio files are skipped.
  • Documents. PDFs and other files sent in Telegram live in Telegram Documents, and they are not image or video files, so they are skipped. Copy them by hand in the Files app if they matter.
  • The cache. Files Telegram downloaded only to show you once sit in the cache, not in the media folders, and the cache is rebuilt per installation. Copying it achieves nothing — the proper copies are in Telegram Images and Telegram Video, or re-downloadable from the chats.
  • Stickers outside the picked folder. Stickers live in their own folder; they only copy if they sit inside the folder you picked, and only if they are image files.

If you want the whole Telegram — chats and media together — the Telegram Desktop export is the route, and it is the only one Telegram itself supports. This article's route is for the photos and videos as files on a drive you hold.

What to avoid

  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. The top search results for moving Telegram files assume a computer in between — NetworkUstad's guide routes through a PC, and MobileTrans is a Windows/Mac program at heart. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 22, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. TeleDrive and Telegrive store your files in Telegram — that is a cloud shelf, not a USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Assuming the chats came along. The photo files are not the conversation. Cloud chats reappear on sign-in, secret chats never leave the device, and neither is in the folders this article copies. If the chats matter, run Telegram Desktop's export too — the folder copy does not replace it.
  • Copying the cache and calling it a backup. Cache files are fragments keyed to the old installation. The proper copies are in Telegram Images and Telegram Video, or re-downloadable from the chats — the cache is not a backup of either.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.
  • Assuming the copy keeps running with the screen off. Unverified — no wake lock, no battery-saver handling in the repo. Keep the phone awake for the run or check it.

Rule of thumb: the photos are files in a folder — back up the folder. The chats are in the cloud — they follow your sign-in. The secret chats are on the device — they follow nothing. Do the folder copy, check the copy on the drive before you delete anything, and export the chats on a computer if the conversation itself matters.
