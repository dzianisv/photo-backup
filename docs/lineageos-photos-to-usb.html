Back up photos from a LineageOS phone to a USB drive

LineageOS frees your photos from Google — but it gives you no plain-files backup in return. Seedvault writes an encrypted archive; the USB copy is yours to do.

November 23, 2026 · 7 min read

TL;DR: LineageOS ships without Google apps by default, so there is no Google Photos and no Google cloud backup on the phone — your photos live only on the device until you copy them off. The backup LineageOS does ship, Seedvault, is not a photo backup: it writes a whole-device backup, encrypted with a 12-word recovery phrase, to a USB drive, Nextcloud, or local storage, and you can only get the photos back by restoring through Seedvault on a compatible ROM — you cannot plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures. Glimpse, the LineageOS gallery app, only shows your photos; it does not copy them anywhere. There is no locked container to move out of here — unlike a private space, the photos sit in the open, in DCIM and Pictures, visible to any app with media permission — so the job is the plain-files copy itself, and no guide covers that copy for LineageOS. The route is on the phone, no computer: plug in a USB drive and copy the library oldest-first with SnapDisk, whose free core needs no Google Play services (the code has no Play Services dependency and no internet permission), or copy the folders by hand in the Files app. Three things are unverified and are marked as such in this article: whether USB OTG works on your particular device and LineageOS build, whether SnapDisk's backup notification appears unchanged on LineageOS builds, and whether the $9.99 Pro purchase — a Google Play Billing purchase — works at all on a de-Googled build without Play services. The free core works either way, with a 5 GB cap per run, and running it again picks up where it stopped. As of November 23, 2026, no top search result offers a dedicated LineageOS-photos-to-USB how-to: the Seedvault guides cover the encrypted archive, the generic guides cover stock Android or need a computer, and none joins the plain-files copy to a LineageOS phone.

The wall: no Google Photos — and Seedvault is an archive, not a photo copy

Start with what LineageOS takes away, because it is the whole job and it is easy to underestimate. LineageOS is a de-Googled Android: it ships without Google apps by default, and without the Google services behind them. On a stock phone, Google Photos quietly copies your photos to the cloud the moment you are on Wi-Fi, and Google's backup quietly carries app data with it — you can argue about the privacy cost, but the copy exists. On LineageOS, neither exists. There is no Google Photos to open, no Google Drive backup to fall back on, and no cloud copy of anything unless you installed one yourself. Your photos live in one place: the phone's own storage, in DCIM and Pictures and the messaging-app folders. Lose the phone, break the screen, or flash a bad build, and the photos go with it — unless you copied them off first. That is the wall: not a locked space hiding the photos from backup apps, but the absence of any backup at all.

The backup LineageOS does ship is Seedvault, and it is worth understanding precisely, because it looks like the answer and it is not — not for photos. Seedvault has been LineageOS's built-in backup since LineageOS 18.1: go to Settings > System > Backup, change the backup provider to Seedvault, and you can back up to a USB drive, a Nextcloud instance, or local storage, on a schedule, including automatically when you plug the backup drive in. The backup is encrypted client-side with AES/GCM and unlocked with a 12-word recovery phrase. That is a good design for a device backup. It is the wrong shape for a photo backup. The output is an encrypted archive, not a folder of .jpg files: you cannot plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you cannot hand the drive to a family member and have them look at the photos, and you can only get the photos back by restoring through Seedvault on a compatible ROM. Lose the 12-word phrase and the archive is unreadable — there is no reset. And the file-backup part of Seedvault is a beta feature: the CalyxOS documentation says it "may not work as expected" and recommends making a copy of your files by another means, "such as by connecting to a PC." So Seedvault is a restore point for the device. It is not a copy of your photos.

Glimpse is the other half of the confusion, and it is worth naming because every LineageOS user meets it on day one. Glimpse is LineageOS's gallery app — fast, bare-bones by design, reading image folders without layering on extra metadata or any cloud. It is a genuinely good way to look at your photos. It is not a backup tool. It has no export, no copy-to-drive, and no backup of any kind: it shows the photos that are already on the phone, and that is all it does. The same is true of the camera app, which takes the photos, and of the Files app, which can move them — none of them backs anything up. So the LineageOS photo situation is this: the photos are fully visible and fully unprotected. There is no locked container to move out of, no second space to escape, and no hidden album to unlock — the photos sit in the open, in the standard folders, readable by any app with media permission. The job is not getting access to the photos. The job is the plain-files copy itself: getting the library onto a USB drive as files you can open anywhere, on the phone, with no computer — and no guide covers that copy for LineageOS.

One more scope fact, because it decides which of the methods below SnapDisk can help with at all. SnapDisk copies image and video files only. On the default run it reads the phone's MediaStore images and videos tables, with no folder filter — the whole library, oldest-first, whatever folder the photos sit in. That matters on LineageOS because there is no single folder to point at: photos live in DCIM/Camera, in Pictures/Screenshots, in the messaging-app folders, and wherever else the camera and the apps put them. A backup that only copied DCIM would miss them. SnapDisk's default run does not only copy DCIM — it reads the whole MediaStore library — so the whole library is covered by the same one-tap run, with no folder to pick and no setting to change. And the free core needs no Google Play services to do it: the code has zero matches for Google Play Services, no internet permission in the manifest, and a minimum Android version of 7.0 — so the manual oldest-first backup runs on a de-Googled build. What the code cannot prove is the device half of the job, and this article marks it as unverified rather than assuming it: whether your particular device and LineageOS build sees the USB drive at all, and whether the backup notification appears unchanged on LineageOS builds. Both are covered in their own sections below.

What you need

A LineageOS phone — any build on Android 7.0 or later, which is SnapDisk's minimum version, and in practice every LineageOS build still in use is far newer than that — and one USB drive your phone can read: a USB-C flash drive, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter. SnapDisk itself is installed from the v0.1.2 APK — it is not on Google Play yet — so install it by allowing "install unknown apps" for the browser or Files app you download it with, which is standard Android and works on LineageOS. Check the drive's space first: look at how many photos are in Glimpse or Settings > Storage, add headroom, and remember the drive holds a second copy of the library, not a replacement for it, until you delete the originals — which this article never asks you to do. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 cannot hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit, and exFAT is the format to use for a photo drive.

Before any method, settle the one question that decides whether the phone route works at all: does your device and build see the USB drive? Plug the drive in and open Settings > Connected devices or Settings > Storage — the drive should appear there. That check is the test, and it takes ten seconds, because OTG support varies by device and LineageOS build and no guide tests it across builds — this article marks it unverified rather than assuming your phone sees the drive. If the drive does not appear, try a different adapter first — cheap OTG adapters are the usual failure — then a different drive, and a powered hub if the drive is a spinning hard drive rather than a flash drive. And one warning up front, because it applies to every route below: do not delete the photos from the phone until the USB copy is done and verified. On a LineageOS phone there is no Google Photos copy to fall back on — the phone copy and the drive copy are the only two copies in the world, and deleting the phone copy before the drive copy is verified leaves you with one copy on a losable drive.

Method 2: Copy by hand in the Files app

If you'd rather not install anything, the manual copy works — Glimpse cannot do it, but the Files app can. Plug the drive in, confirm it mounted in Settings > Connected devices or Settings > Storage, then open the Files app that ships with your build and confirm the drive shows up there too. Browse to Internal storage, find the photo folders — DCIM for the camera, Pictures for screenshots and downloads, and the messaging-app folders for the photos people sent you — select them (long-press the first, tap the rest, or tap Select all), tap Copy, navigate to the drive, and paste. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos — and on a LineageOS phone there is no cloud copy to fall back on. Wait for the copy to finish before unplugging, and then verify it: plug the drive into another device and open a few photos, and confirm they render.

The honest limits of this route are the ones SnapDisk exists to remove. There is no oldest-first order, no progress bar with speed and time left, no Pause and Stop, and no resume if you unplug mid-copy — a manual copy interrupted halfway has to be started again, and working out which files already landed is your job, by comparing counts. There is no per-file verification: the Files app does not check sizes after copying, so a file that copied short looks finished until you open it. And the folder walk is manual: the library lives in DCIM, Pictures, Screenshots, and messaging-app folders, and every folder you forget is a set of photos that never left the phone, and there is no MediaStore whole-library run to catch them. Copy in batches small enough to finish, check the count against what you selected, and open a few files on another device before you trust the drive. And the files on the drive are plain files — anyone who picks up the drive can open them — so if the photos matter enough to back up, this route protects them against losing the phone and nothing else.

Method 3: Seedvault (the encrypted whole-device archive)

If you want LineageOS's own route, Seedvault is already on the phone — but note what it writes and what it is for. Go to Settings, System, Backup, change the backup provider to Seedvault if it is not already the provider, and configure it: pick the storage — a USB drive, a Nextcloud instance, or local storage — and write down the 12-word recovery phrase it shows you. Then run the backup. The data is encrypted and written to the drive, and the phrase is required to restore it — through Seedvault, on a compatible ROM. This works fine as a device backup: it carries apps and their data along with files, and it can run automatically when you plug the backup drive in. Its limits, for photos specifically, are the ones this article is built around: the backup is an archive, not a folder of photos — you cannot plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures; you can only restore them through Seedvault, and only onto a compatible ROM; the file-backup part is beta and the documentation warns it may not work as expected; and if you lose the recovery phrase, the backup is unreadable.

So Seedvault and Method 1 are answers to different questions, and it is worth saying which question you are asking before you pick one. If what you want is a restore point for the whole device — apps, data, and files together, encrypted, on a drive you keep at home — Seedvault is the right tool, and the recovery phrase goes somewhere safe: without it the archive cannot be restored, and there is no reset. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere — on any computer, on any phone, in ten years — Method 1 or 2 is the route, and the archive is the wrong shape. The two are not rivals: a LineageOS phone with no cloud backup at all is exactly the phone that benefits from both — the archive for the device, and the plain-files copy for the photos. Run Seedvault for the restore point, and run the plain copy for the pictures, and keep the 12-word phrase with the drive.

Will the drive mount and the notification appear on your build?

The first half is the drive mount: does your device and LineageOS build see a USB drive plugged into the phone? OTG support varies by device and by build — the kernel and the device tree decide it, and a build for one phone can mount drives that a build for another phone cannot. No guide tests it across builds, and the search results for this job contain no report of anyone plugging a drive into a LineageOS phone and seeing — or not seeing — the drive mount there. The test takes ten seconds: plug the drive in, open Settings > Connected devices or Settings > Storage, and look for the drive. If it is there, the phone route works. If it is not, try a different adapter first, then a different drive, then a powered hub for a spinning hard drive — and if none of those works, the drive may simply not mount on that build, and the computer copy is the fallback.

So the honest position is this: on a LineageOS build without Play services, plan on the free runs. They are the route that always works, and running it again picks up where it stopped, because files already copied are never copied twice. If you are on a LineageOS build with Play services installed — some users install them — the purchase may work, but that is untested too, and the free runs are still the safe plan until someone verifies it.

There is a second, quieter point here, and it is worth stating because it decides whether the Pro question even matters for a photo backup. Checking the drive after the copy

The check has two halves, and they have to happen in order: first that the drive mounted and the run covered the library, then that the files on the drive open. After the run finishes, open the drive on a computer or on the phone itself and count: the drive should hold the number of photos and videos Glimpse shows, and a few opened at random should render fully, videos included. Open at least one photo from each folder you care about — DCIM, Pictures, and the messaging-app folders — not just any photo on the drive, because a run that covered DCIM only would still look complete on a random check while the rest of the library was missing. If the count is short, the run stopped early — the 5 GB free cap is the usual cause, and running it again picks up where it stopped. If the drive's free space ran out mid-run, the run stops with the full error shown; free space by moving the drive's contents to a bigger drive first, or back up to a second drive. And the check that matters most for a phone with no cloud copy anywhere: confirm the drive copy before you delete anything from the phone. The drive is a plain folder of plain files. Anyone who picks up the drive can open them. That is the price of a copy you can open anywhere, and it is worth stating before the photos leave the phone.

What to avoid

  • Mistaking a Seedvault archive for a photo copy. Seedvault writes an encrypted whole-device backup that only Seedvault can restore — you cannot plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures. If you want plain files, you need Method 1 or 2, not Seedvault. And if you do use Seedvault, keep the 12-word recovery phrase somewhere safe: without it the archive cannot be restored.
  • Assuming Google Photos exists on LineageOS. It does not, and neither does Google cloud backup. There is no cloud copy of your photos unless you installed one yourself — the phone copy and the drive copy are the only two copies in the world. Do not delete the phone copy before the drive copy is verified.
  • Assuming the $9.99 Pro purchase works without Play services. Unverified — Pro is a Google Play Billing purchase, and a de-Googled build has no Play services. Plan on the free runs, which need no purchase and pick up where they stopped.
  • Assuming the USB drive will mount on every LineageOS device. Unverified — OTG support varies by device and build. Plug the drive in and check Settings > Connected devices or Settings > Storage before you plan the backup around it.
  • Assuming the backup notification appears on LineageOS. Unverified — the offer is a standard Android notification and nothing in the code is Play-specific, but no test confirms it renders unchanged on LineageOS builds. If it does not appear, open SnapDisk and start the backup from the app.
  • Assuming Glimpse backs anything up. Glimpse is a gallery app — it reads image folders and shows your photos, and it does not copy them anywhere. Looking at a photo is not backing it up.
  • Using an ADB backup guide when the point is no computer. The ADB backup guides need a computer and rooted debugging, and they write a backup archive, not plain photo files. They solve a different job — this article is the phone-direct route.
  • Checking the drive by opening any photo. Open a photo from each folder you care about — DCIM, Pictures, and the messaging-app folders. A random check passes even when a whole folder was missed, because the photos that did copy render fine.
  • Using a FAT32 drive for a library with long videos. FAT32 cannot hold a single file over 4 GB, and a library with long 4K clips will hold one. SnapDisk skips those files and lists them — use exFAT.
  • Assuming the free 5 GB cap covers the whole library. It is per run. A library over 5 GB needs several free runs — run it again and it picks up where it stopped — or one $9.99 Pro purchase on a build where the purchase works.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this. An unplug mid-copy leaves a half-copied library that looks finished until you count it.
  • Confusing Seedvault with a cloud backup. Seedvault is local and encrypted — it writes to a drive you hold, a Nextcloud you run, or local storage. It is not Google Photos, and it is not a cloud. The distinction is the point of running LineageOS.

Rule of thumb: Seedvault is the restore point, the plain copy is the photo backup — LineageOS gives you the archive and no cloud, so the drive holds the only copy of your photos outside the phone. Copy them as plain files, verify the drive, and only then decide about deleting anything.
