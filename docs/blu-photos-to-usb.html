  • The right adapter, if your phone needs one. Fourteen covered phones take USB Type-C, so a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in. Two take micro-USB — the Vivo XI and the Vivo XL4 — so on those two, a micro-USB OTG adapter is the cable, and a USB-C flash drive does not plug straight in.
  • Optional: a microSD card. A microSD card fits fourteen phones for certain — each has a slot — so the card-reader route in Method 4 is proven on fourteen. A card bigger than the contested ceiling on the G9 Pro, Vivo XI or Vivo XI+ without checking the phone first — the ceiling is two numbers on each of those three. A card at all for the Bold N2 or the Bold N3 without checking the slot on the unit first — the slot itself is contested on those two. A rival backup app expecting it to work on the shipped software of every covered phone — VaultDrive's AppBrain floor is Android 12.1, which only the Bold N3 and the G93 clear, and FotoSafe's Android 9 floor is not met on the shipped software of the Vivo XI, the Vivo XI+ or the Vivo XL4.
  • What the drive must not be. An app for the phone — a backup app and a backup drive are two different things. Your old phone's card — a card that came out of another phone carries its photos, not yours. A drive with no space left — check the free space before you start, not halfway through.

And a card-reader route the RedMagic article could not offer: a microSD card fits fourteen BLU phones for certain, so the card-reader route is proven on fourteen here, where on RedMagic it was proven on none of the fourteen. The updates twist is the other way round from the usual story: the three oldest phones in this article, the 2018 Vivos, shipped on Android 8.1 and have a planned Android 9.0 upgrade on two of them — the Vivo XI and the Vivo XI+ — while the Vivo XL4 has no Android 9 upgrade on record, so FotoSafe is not proven on it. The F91's twist is in the listings, not the phone: it is BLU's first 5G phone, launched in April 2022 with Android 11, and one pre-release listing calls it Android 12, so check the version on the unit, not the listing.

Method 1: SnapDisk — proven on eleven

Method 1 is the one-tap version. It uses SnapDisk, the free backup app this site is about — SnapDisk. Free version: manual backup, up to 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase and adds auto-backup on connect plus unlimited size. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, which all sixteen covered BLU phones clear — the lowest runs Android 8.1 — so the app installs on all sixteen, and the one-tap backup is proven on eleven: each one lists USB OTG Yes.

  1. Check the Android version first. Settings > About phone > Android version. All sixteen covered BLU phones run Android 8.1 or newer — the Vivo XI, Vivo XI+ and Vivo XL4 run 8.1; the Bold N1, G9 and G9 Pro run 9.0; the G90, G90 Pro and G91 run 10; the Bold N2, G91 Pro, G91 Max and F91 run 11; the S91 Pro runs 12; the Bold N3 and G93 run 13. If it says 8.0 or lower, you are not on a covered model — check the model name before buying a drive expecting the one-tap backup.
  2. Install SnapDisk. SnapDisk is not on Google Play yet — the listing is being prepared — so install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. On the phone, allow installing from unknown sources when asked. It needs Android 7.0 or later, which all sixteen covered BLU phones clear — the lowest runs Android 8.1 — so on a BLU phone the APK installs on all sixteen, and the one-tap backup is proven on eleven.
  3. Plug in the USB drive. On fourteen covered phones that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in — no adapter, no cable. On the two micro-USB models, the Vivo XI and the Vivo XL4, that is a micro-USB OTG adapter between the phone and the drive. A plain sync cable is the wrong cable: it connects a phone to a computer, not a drive to a phone.
  4. Look for the mount notification. On the eleven proven phones — the Bold N1, Bold N2, Bold N3, G9 Pro, G90, G90 Pro, G91 Pro, G91 Max, G93, F91 and S91 Pro — Android notices the drive and offers it as storage, and SnapDisk picks it as the To folder. On the five mounting-test phones — the G9, G91, Vivo XI, Vivo XI+ and Vivo XL4 — if nothing appears, check Settings > Storage: if the drive is listed there, tap it and mount it, then run the backup. If the drive never shows up on one of those five, the phone does not host it, and Method 3 is the route.
  5. Check the copy, then delete the originals. Open the drive in Files and count: the backup is only done when every photo is on the drive and opens. Then delete the originals on the phone if you need the space — and only then.

SnapDisk copies the photos byte-for-byte: the file on the drive is the same file the phone took, with the same pixels. There is no re-encoding step, so nothing is lost in the copy — the only place quality gets lost is a cloud backup with a "Storage saver" setting.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter

Method 2 is the manual version of the same thing — no app, just the phone's file manager and a drive. The file manager on a BLU phone is Files, or Files by Google, per devicesfaq — so this method names it directly. It works on the eleven proven phones, and on the five mounting-test phones if the drive shows up in Settings.

  1. Plug the drive into the phone. On fourteen covered phones that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in. On the Vivo XI and the Vivo XL4, that is a micro-USB OTG adapter between the phone and the drive.
  2. Open Files and find the DCIM folder. Your camera photos live in the DCIM folder — on most phones that is Internal storage > DCIM > Camera. Screenshots live in Pictures > Screenshots, and WhatsApp photos in Android > media > com.whatsapp > WhatsApp > Media > WhatsApp Images.
  3. Select the photos and copy them to the drive. Long-press one photo, select the rest, tap Copy, navigate to the USB drive and tap Paste. Copy, not Move — see "What to avoid" below.
  4. Wait, then check. Copying takes time — thousands of photos can take half an hour or more. Open the drive in Files and count before you delete anything.

This is the same copy Method 1 does, done by hand. It copies the original files as they are — no re-encoding, no quality loss.

Method 3: through a computer

Method 3 is the fallback — and the one the per-model backup FAQ calls the most economical method. It works on all sixteen, including the five mounting-test phones if the drive never shows up on the phone. Plug the phone into a computer with the USB cable, open the phone as a drive, and drag the DCIM folder onto a USB drive. Copy, not Move, and check the copy before deleting anything. It works — it just takes the manual step Method 1 does for you.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route

Method 4 is the card route, and on BLU it is a real one: a microSD card fits fourteen phones for certain, so this route is proven on fourteen — unlike RedMagic, where it is proven on none of the fourteen. It is contested on the Bold N2 and the Bold N3, so check the slot on the unit before buying a card for one of those two.

  1. Put the card in the phone. The slot is dedicated on the Bold N1, G91, G91 Pro and S91 Pro. Card ceilings are contested on three phones — the G9 Pro (128 GB per five sources, 512 GB per PhoneArena), the Vivo XI (128 GB per BLU's own page, gsm-arena and Smartprix, 512 GB per phonemore) and the Vivo XI+ (128 GB per GSMArena, PhoneArena, phonesdata and gsm-arena, 512 GB per phonemore) — so buy at or under 128 GB if you want the ceiling every source agrees on.
  2. Set the camera to save to the card. Open the Camera app, go to Settings, and set Storage to the SD card. New photos then land on the card, not the internal storage.
  3. Move the photos already on the phone. Open Files, select the DCIM folder, and move it to the card. The photos already on the phone do not move themselves — they live in internal storage until you move them.
  4. Take the card out and read it in a card reader. Plug the reader into the computer, or into the phone with an OTG adapter, and copy the photos to a USB drive.

Two warnings: the photos already on the phone live in internal storage, not on the card — so moving the card alone backs up nothing until you move them — and the card is only a backup if the photos end up on it, so check the card before you delete anything on the phone.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud

Method 5 is the cloud route — and the one BLU's own per-model FAQ names for photos alone: Google Photos uploads them automatically when on Wi-Fi. It works on all sixteen, it is free up to the Google account's 15 GB, and it is the only method here that backs up without a cable. But it is not a USB backup: the photos live on Google's servers, not on a drive you hold, and the "Storage saver" setting compresses them — so check that setting before trusting a cloud copy. If you want the originals on a drive you own, Method 1 or Method 2 is the route.

What this does not do

  • It does not install below Android 7.0. No covered BLU phone runs below Android 8.1 — so on a covered phone, this does not come up — but if the phone in your hand runs Android 6.0 or lower, it is not a covered model.
  • It does not work if the phone does not host a drive. The five mounting-test phones — the G9, G91, Vivo XI, Vivo XI+ and Vivo XL4 — may or may not mount a drive, because no source settles OTG on them. If the drive never shows up on one of those five, Method 3 is the route.
  • It does not delete photos. A backup copies the photos to the drive; deleting the originals is a separate step, and only after you have checked the copy.
  • It does not back up your settings, contacts or messages. It backs up photos and videos — the files in your camera roll.
  • It does not back up photos from apps that hide them. Photos saved inside an app's private storage — WhatsApp's hidden media, or a locked album — may not be in the DCIM folder, so check the app's own export.
  • It does not compress or shrink your photos. The copy is the original file — the same pixels, the same size.

What to avoid

  • Don't move instead of copying. Move deletes the original as it goes — if the copy fails halfway, the photos are gone. Copy first, check the copy, then delete.
  • Don't unplug the drive until the copy is done. Unplugging mid-copy corrupts the files being written. Wait for the progress to finish, then eject the drive in Settings before pulling it out.
  • Don't buy a micro-USB adapter for a Type-C phone. Fourteen covered phones take USB Type-C — a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and the adapter is the wrong buy.
  • Don't buy a USB-C drive expecting it to plug into the Vivo XI or the Vivo XL4. Those two are micro-USB, and a USB-C drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter between them.
  • Don't buy a card bigger than the contested ceiling without checking. The G9 Pro, Vivo XI and Vivo XI+ each have two card ceilings — 128 GB per most sources, 512 GB per one — so buy at or under 128 GB if you want the ceiling every source agrees on.
  • Don't buy a card at all for the Bold N2 or the Bold N3 without checking the slot on the unit first. The slot itself is contested on those two — phonemore says no slot on the N2 and gsmfind, phonesdata, Smartprix and Assuredzone say no slot on the N3, while droidafrica says up to 2 TB and 512 GB respectively.
  • Don't count on VaultDrive's AppBrain floor except on the Bold N3 and the G93. Its own site says Android 8, which all sixteen clear — but AppBrain lists Android 12.1, which only the Bold N3 and the G93, both on Android 13, clear.
  • Don't count on FotoSafe on shipped software on the three 2018 Vivos. It needs Android 9 — the Vivo XI and the Vivo XI+ clear only after the planned Android 9 upgrade, and the Vivo XL4 has no Android 9 upgrade on record.
  • Don't trust the F91's pre-release Android 12 listing. It ships Android 11 — check the version on the unit, not the listing.
  • Don't let one folder hold every photo. A backup is only a backup if the copy exists somewhere else — a drive, a card, or the cloud. One copy is not a backup.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a copy. A backup finds your photos, copies them all, and tells you when it's done. On a BLU phone the honest position is simple: sixteen phones, all clear Android 7.0, eleven list USB OTG Yes, fourteen take a microSD card — the one-tap backup is proven on eleven, and the card-reader route is proven on fourteen.
