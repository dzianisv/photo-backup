And the name trap: VKworld is not VK Mobile — VK Mobile was a South Korean maker, bankrupt in 2006, whose VK4000, VK700, VK800, VK560, VK2020 and VK2000 are 2000s feature phones — and the line has a rebrand in it, the F2 being the Cagabi One Plus after VKworld bought Cagabi in 2017. The line is finished, with the last phones, the K1 and the Mix3, both 2018 models — and no live brand site was verified from this run.

Method 1: SnapDisk — proven on five

Method 1 is the one-tap version. It uses SnapDisk, the free backup app this site is about — SnapDisk. Free version: manual backup, up to 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase and adds auto-backup on connect plus unlimited size. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, which nine covered VKworld phones clear — the lowest is Android 7.0 — so the app installs on those nine, and the one-tap backup is proven on five: each one lists USB OTG Yes.

  1. Check the Android version first. Settings > About phone > Android version. Nine covered phones run Android 7.0 or newer — the oldest clear ones are the S8, Mix Plus, S3, Mix and VK7000 on Android 7.0, and the newest, the K1 and VK4000, run Android 8.0 and 8.1. If it says anything below 7.0, you are on one of the twenty-three below-floor phones — check the model name before buying a drive expecting the one-tap backup. If it says 5.0 on a T2 Plus, you have the older listing's unit; if it says 7.0, you have the refresh.
  2. Install SnapDisk. SnapDisk is not on Google Play yet — the listing is being prepared — so install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. On the phone, allow installing from unknown sources when asked. It needs Android 7.0 or later, which nine covered VKworld phones clear — the lowest is Android 7.0 — so on a VKworld phone the APK installs on those nine, and the one-tap backup is proven on five.
  3. Plug in the USB drive. On two covered phones that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in — no adapter, no cable. On the six micro-USB models that is a micro-USB OTG adapter between the phone and the drive. On the other twenty-five, check the port first — no source states the connector on those. A plain sync cable is the wrong cable: it connects a phone to a computer, not a drive to a phone.
  4. Look for the mount notification. On the five proven phones Android notices the drive and offers it as storage, and SnapDisk picks it as the To folder. On the four mounting-test phones — the S3, Mix, VK7000 and VK4000 — and on the contested T2 Plus, if nothing appears, check Settings > Storage: if the drive is listed there, tap it and mount it, then run the backup. If the drive never shows up on one of those five, the phone does not host it, and Method 3 is the route — and on the twenty-three below-floor phones and the three feature phones, expect that outcome.
  5. Check the copy, then delete the originals. Open the drive in Files and count: the backup is only done when every photo is on the drive and opens. Then delete the originals on the phone if you need the space — and only then.

SnapDisk copies the photos byte-for-byte: the file on the drive is the same file the phone took, with the same pixels. There is no re-encoding step, so nothing is lost in the copy — the only place quality gets lost is a cloud backup with a "Storage saver" setting.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter

Method 2 is the manual version of the same thing — no app, just the phone's file manager and a drive. No source names VKworld's bundled file manager, so this method says "the file manager" generically rather than naming one. It works on the five proven phones, and on the four mounting-test phones and the contested T2 Plus if the drive shows up in Settings.

  1. Plug the drive into the phone. On two covered phones that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in. On the six micro-USB models that is a micro-USB OTG adapter between the phone and the drive.
  2. Open the file manager and find the DCIM folder. Your camera photos live in the DCIM folder — on most phones that is Internal storage > DCIM > Camera. Screenshots live in Pictures > Screenshots, and WhatsApp photos in Android > media > com.whatsapp > WhatsApp > Media > WhatsApp Images.
  3. Select the photos and copy them to the drive. Long-press one photo, select the rest, tap Copy, navigate to the USB drive and tap Paste. Copy, not Move — see "What to avoid" below.
  4. Wait, then check. Copying takes time — thousands of photos can take half an hour or more. Open the drive in the file manager and count before you delete anything.

This is the same copy Method 1 does, done by hand. It copies the original files as they are — no re-encoding, no quality loss.

Method 3: through a computer

Method 3 is the fallback — and the one the per-model backup FAQ calls the most economical method. It works on all thirty-six, including the four mounting-test phones and the T2 Plus if the drive never shows up on the phone, the twenty-three below-floor phones and the three feature phones. Plug the phone into a computer with the USB cable, open the phone as a drive, and drag the DCIM folder onto a USB drive — on the three feature phones, copy the phone's photo folder instead, since there is no DCIM folder on a phone with no Android. Copy, not Move, and check the copy before deleting anything. It works — it just takes the manual step Method 1 does for you.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route

Method 4 is the card route, and on VKworld it is a real one on twenty of the thirty-six: a microSD card fits twenty phones for certain, so this route is proven on those twenty — unlike RedMagic, where it is proven on none of the fourteen.

  1. Put the card in the phone. Card ceilings are stated on thirteen, contested on two — the Mix Plus (128 or 32 GB) and the Mix (128 or 64 GB) — and unstated on five — the VK800X, G1 Giant, Discovery S1, Discovery S2 and Stone V3 Plus — while the slot itself is unstated on sixteen — so buy at or under the lower number on the contested two if you want the ceiling every source agrees on, and check the phone first on the sixteen where the slot is unstated.
  2. Set the camera to save to the card. Open the Camera app, go to Settings, and set Storage to the SD card. New photos then land on the card, not the internal storage.
  3. Move the photos already on the phone. Open the file manager, select the DCIM folder, and move it to the card. The photos already on the phone do not move themselves — they live in internal storage until you move them.
  4. Take the card out and read it in a card reader. Plug the reader into the computer, or into the phone with an OTG adapter, and copy the photos to a USB drive.

Two warnings: the photos already on the phone live in internal storage, not on the card — so moving the card alone backs up nothing until you move them — and the card is only a backup if the photos end up on it, so check the card before you delete anything on the phone.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud

Method 5 is the cloud route — and the one VKworld's per-model FAQ names for photos alone: Google Photos uploads them automatically when on Wi-Fi. It works on all thirty-three smartphones, it is free up to the Google account's 15 GB, and it is the only method here that backs up without a cable. But it is not a USB backup: the photos live on Google's servers, not on a drive you hold, and the "Storage saver" setting compresses them — so check that setting before trusting a cloud copy. If you want the originals on a drive you own, Method 1 or Method 2 is the route. On the three feature phones there is no Google Photos at all — Methods 3 and 4 are the routes there.

What this does not do

  • It does not install below Android 7.0. Twenty-three covered VKworld phones run below Android 7.0 as shipped — the oldest is the VK 1000 4G on Android 4.4 — so on those twenty-three SnapDisk does not install at all, and only Methods 3, 4 and 5 work. VaultDrive needs Android 8 and FotoSafe needs Android 9, so on a below-floor VKworld phone there is no rival-app route either.
  • It does not install on a feature phone. Three covered VKworld phones — the New Stone V3, Stone V3 Plus and Stone V3S — have no Android at all, so SnapDisk cannot install on those either, and only the card-reader route in Method 4 and the computer copy in Method 3 work on them.
  • It does not work if the phone does not host a drive. The four mounting-test phones — the S3, Mix, VK7000 and VK4000 — and the contested T2 Plus may or may not mount a drive, because no source settles OTG on them. If the drive never shows up on one of those five, Method 3 is the route.
  • It does not delete photos. A backup copies the photos to the drive; deleting the originals is a separate step, and only after you have checked the copy.
  • It does not back up your settings, contacts or messages. It backs up photos and videos — the files in your camera roll.
  • It does not back up photos from apps that hide them. Photos saved inside an app's private storage — WhatsApp's hidden media, or a locked album — may not be in the DCIM folder, so check the app's own export.
  • It does not compress or shrink your photos. The copy is the original file — the same pixels, the same size.

What to avoid

  • Don't move instead of copying. Move deletes the original as it goes — if the copy fails halfway, the photos are gone. Copy first, check the copy, then delete.
  • Don't unplug the drive until the copy is done. Unplugging mid-copy corrupts the files being written. Wait for the progress to finish, then eject the drive in Settings before pulling it out.
  • Don't buy a micro-USB adapter for a Type-C phone. Two covered phones take USB Type-C — the Mix3 and the VK7000 — a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and the adapter is the wrong buy.
  • Don't buy a USB-C drive expecting it to plug into one of the six micro-USB phones. Those six are micro-USB, and a USB-C drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter between them.
  • Don't buy a card bigger than the contested ceiling without checking. Two phones have two card ceilings — the Mix Plus (128 or 32 GB) and the Mix (128 or 64 GB) — so buy at or under the lower number if you want the ceiling every source agrees on.
  • Don't buy a card bigger than the unstated ceiling without checking. Five phones have no ceiling in any source — the VK800X, G1 Giant, Discovery S1, Discovery S2 and Stone V3 Plus — and sixteen more have no slot stated at all — so check the phone first before buying a big card for one of those.
  • Don't count on VaultDrive on every VKworld phone. Its own site says Android 8, which three clear — the K1, the VK4000 and the VK7000 on its higher listing — but AppBrain lists Android 12.1, which none clear, and only the K1 is proven with OTG Yes.
  • Don't count on FotoSafe on every VKworld phone. It needs Android 9 with OTG — none is proven, and the highest shipped version is Android 8.1.
  • Don't trust a single T2 Plus listing. Devicesfaq, techyne, majordroid and unite4buy list Android 7.0, while phonesdata and FindPare list Android 5.0 with the older T2's specs. Check the version on the unit, and keep Methods 3 to 5 ready.
  • Don't trust a single VK7000 spec. Gizmochina's spec page says Android 7.0, a 1080×2160 display and a 7000 mAh battery; smartprix says Android 8.0, and a gizmochina giveaway post says Oreo with a 720p screen and a 5600 mAh battery. Check the unit, not the name.
  • Don't trust a single Android listing on the nine two-number phones. The Mix 2 is 7.0 vs 7.1.1, the VK7000 is 7.0 vs 8.0, the K1 is 8.0 vs 8.1, the T2 Plus is 5.0 vs 7.0, the VK700 is 4.4 vs 5.0, the VK700X is 5.0 vs 5.1, the VK800X is 5.0 vs 5.1, the G1 Giant is 5.0 vs 5.1 and the T5 SE is 5.1 vs 6.0. Only the T2 Plus flips the 7.0 floor — check the version on the unit, not the name.
  • Don't trust a single launch date on the two contested phones. The K1 was announced June 2018 per phonecorridor and released September 2018 per phonesdata; the T2 Plus's February 2016 date belongs to the older T2's listing.
  • Don't expect the one-tap backup on a below-7.0 phone or a feature phone. Twenty-three run below Android 7.0 as shipped and three have no Android at all — SnapDisk does not install on those at all — so Methods 3, 4 and 5 are the routes there.
  • Don't confuse VKworld with VK Mobile. VK Mobile was a South Korean maker, bankrupt in 2006 — its VK4000, VK700, VK800, VK560, VK2020 and VK2000 are 2000s feature phones, not VKworld phones. A VKworld VK4000 runs Android 8.1; a VK Mobile VK4000 is a 2006 slide phone.
  • Don't let one folder hold every photo. A backup is only a backup if the copy exists somewhere else — a drive, a card, or the cloud. One copy is not a backup.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a copy. A backup finds your photos, copies them all, and tells you when it's done. On a VKworld phone the honest position is simple: thirty-six phones, nine clear Android 7.0, five list USB OTG Yes, twenty take a microSD card — the one-tap backup is proven on five, and the card-reader route is proven on twenty.
