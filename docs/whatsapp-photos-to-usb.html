Back up WhatsApp photos to a USB drive on Android (no computer)

Your WhatsApp photos are files on your phone — in a folder you can point a backup app at. Plug a USB drive into the phone and copy them straight to it, no computer, no upload.

Where WhatsApp keeps your photos

WhatsApp saves every photo you receive (and send, if you keep the default) as an ordinary file in a folder on your phone. The folder's location depends on the Android version and how WhatsApp was installed, so check which one exists on your phone:

  • Newer Android: Internal storage > Android > media > com.whatsapp > WhatsApp > Media > WhatsApp Images
  • Older installs: Internal storage > WhatsApp > Media > WhatsApp Images

Videos live next door, in WhatsApp Video under the same Media folder. You do not need to memorise either path — in the steps below you navigate to the folder in a picker and tap it, so you just follow whichever one exists on your phone. One more thing worth knowing before you start: if a photo never downloaded (the blurred thumbnail with a download arrow), it is not on your phone yet — open the chat and download it first, or there is nothing to copy.

What you need

An Android phone (Android 7.0 or later for SnapDisk); a USB drive your phone can read — a USB-C flash drive, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter (most modern Android phones support OTG; if the phone doesn't see the drive, try a different adapter); and enough space on the drive for the WhatsApp photos you want to keep. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 can't hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit.

Method 2: by hand in the Files app (no app needed)

If you'd rather not install anything, Android's built-in Files app can copy the WhatsApp folder to the USB drive: 1. Plug in the USB drive. A notification or a "USB drive" entry should appear in the Files app under Storage devices. 2. Open Files, browse to Internal storage and find the WhatsApp folder — Android > media > com.whatsapp > WhatsApp > Media > WhatsApp Images on newer Android, or WhatsApp > Media > WhatsApp Images on older installs. 3. Select the photos (or long-press the WhatsApp Images folder to select the whole thing), tap ⋮ → Copy to, then choose the USB drive as the destination. 4. Wait for the copy to finish — and don't unplug the drive until the Files app says it's done. This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason SnapDisk exists: no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time WhatsApp photos pile up. For a WhatsApp folder that grows every day, the app's oldest-first background copy is the calmer option.

What this does not back up

This is the part people get wrong, so here it is plainly. Copying the WhatsApp Images folder backs up the files — the photos and videos. It does not back up:

  • Chat history. Your messages live in a database file (msgstore.db.crypt14), not in the Images folder, and SnapDisk only copies image and video files. Back up chats with WhatsApp's own Settings > Chats > Chat backup, which backs up to Google Drive.
  • Voice notes. Voice messages are .opus audio files, which SnapDisk does not copy.
  • Stickers outside the picked folder. Stickers live in their own folder; they only copy if they sit inside the folder you picked, and only if they are image files.
  • Documents. PDFs and other files sent in WhatsApp are not image or video files, so they are skipped.

If you want the whole WhatsApp — chats and media together — the Google Drive chat backup is the route, and it is the only one WhatsApp itself supports. This article's route is for the photos and videos as files on a drive you hold.

What to avoid

  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. The top search results for this job are Windows/Mac programs (Wondershare MobileTrans and similar) that need a computer in between. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 16, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Assuming the chats came along. The photo files are not the conversation. If the chats matter, run WhatsApp's own Chat backup too — the folder copy does not replace it.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.

Rule of thumb: the photos are files in a folder — back up the folder. The chats are a database — back them up in WhatsApp. Do both, and check the copy on the drive before you delete anything from the phone.
