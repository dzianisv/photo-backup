The copies are the original files. SnapDisk streams each file byte-for-byte from the device to the drive — the code reads the file and writes the same bytes out, with no decode, no recompression, and no resize — and then checks that the copy on the drive is the same size as the original. Other apps copy originals byte-for-byte too; what SnapDisk adds is the automated whole-library run: oldest-first order, progress with Pause and Stop, resume where it stopped, and skip-if-already-copied on the next run. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. What this does not do

  • It does not back up your settings or your contacts. SnapDisk copies photos, videos, and any files you pick — the files themselves, exactly as they are. App settings, call logs, texts, and your accounts stay with your phone and your Google account. For those, Android's built-in backup is the tool — it backs up personal settings to Google's servers with a Google account — not SnapDisk.
  • It does not delete photos from your phone after copying. Deleting is always your choice, after you check the copy on the drive. SnapDisk never deletes originals — it only copies.
  • It does not work if your phone does not host a drive. On the Art 3 Lite the sources agree the phone does not support OTG, so no backup app of any kind can copy your photos straight to a USB drive on that one. If the drive never mounts, that settles it for your unit — use Method 3 or 4 instead.
  • It does not back up photos from apps that hide them. Some apps keep photos in private folders SnapDisk cannot read. Those stay on the phone — check each app's own export or share option.
  • It does not compress your photos. It copies the original files byte-for-byte, so the copy is the same size as the original. On the Art 1 Pro, with its 16 MP camera, the Art 2 Pro, with its 16 MP camera, and the Art 3S, with its 16 MP camera, that is real storage on the drive — bring a drive with room.

What to avoid

  • Don't move files instead of copying them. The file manager's Move deletes the original as it goes. Copy first, check the copy on the drive, and only then delete the originals from the phone if you need the space.
  • Don't unplug the drive until the copy is done. If the copy is still running — SnapDisk shows progress, speed and ETA — unplugging leaves half-copied files. Wait for it to finish, then eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it.
  • Don't buy an adapter before checking your phone's port. Fifteen of the nineteen phones that clear Android 7.0 take micro-USB; only the Art 2 Pro, Art 3 Lite, Art 3S and Max 3 Ultra take USB Type-C. A USB-C adapter does not fit a micro-USB port — and on the Liv 1s the sources disagree about the connector itself, eIMEI24 saying microUSB 2.0 and the manuals.plus user manual saying to connect the Type-C end of the charger's cable to the phone — so check your unit's port before buying anything.
  • Don't assume the drive will mount. On all nineteen phones that clear Android 7.0 the drive mounting is the test — no source lists OTG for eighteen of them, and the Art 3 Lite lists OTG No — so on those eighteen a no-mount settles it for your unit. And on the Art 3 Lite the sources agree it does not mount, so there is no phone route on that one at all — use Method 3 or 4.
  • Don't let one folder hold every photo. SnapDisk copies oldest-first, and the copy is the same size as the original, so a full camera roll on a phone with a 16 MP camera — the Art 1 Pro, the Art 2 Pro or the Art 3S — fills a small drive fast. Bring a drive with room, or back up in batches and check the copy before deleting anything.
