Back up Snapchat photos and videos to USB from Android

Your Snapchat photos live in Memories, on Snapchat's servers — not as files on your phone. Export them to the phone first, then copy them straight to a USB drive, no computer, no upload.

November 22, 2026 · 6 min read

TL;DR: Plug a USB drive into your Android phone, export your Snapchat Memories to the phone, and tap Backup: your Snapchat photos and videos copy oldest-first to the drive, with no computer and no upload in between. The honest limits, up front: Memories live in Snapchat's cloud until you export them — nothing on the phone copies a Memory that is still only inside Snapchat, so the export is the job and the USB copy is the second half. Exporting puts the file on your phone, and the guides disagree about which folder it lands in — a Snapchat album in your gallery, a Snapchat folder inside Pictures or DCIM, or Downloads, depending on your phone — so this article tells you to check the gallery first and the folders second instead of naming one folder and being wrong on your phone. The good news on scope, verified in the code this run: SnapDisk's default run copies your whole media library via MediaStore — not DCIM only — so exported Snaps your gallery shows are covered whatever folder they sit in; the From-picker folder route below is for copying only the Snapchat folder, or the unzipped export folder, instead of the whole library. Your whole library at once is a different job: Snapchat's My Data export sends you a ZIP of every Memory, but the ZIP must be unzipped before anything can copy to USB, and its download link expires after 7 days — that two-step route is Method 2 below. And there is a deadline with a real date on it: Snapchat's own help page says Memories more than a year old outside your oldest 5 GB will be archived starting in January 2027, and opening an archived Memory then takes a paid plan — downloading them first is the free way out. SnapDisk copies, it never deletes — deleting from the phone afterwards is your step, only after verifying a few photos on the drive. Free version: 5 GB per run; run it again past the cap and it picks up where it stopped, because files already copied are never copied twice. Screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified — keep the phone awake for the run. As of November 22, 2026, no top search result offers exported-Memories-to-USB backup in one tap: the top results export Memories to the phone or the computer only, save them to the gallery only, back them up to Google Drive only, or copy by hand in a file manager without naming Snapchat at all.

What the search results actually offer

Search for this job and the results split into five shapes, none of them the one-tap exported-Memories-to-USB copy. The first shape is the Memories export guide: the Medium guide "Want to Save Your Snapchat Memories? Here's How" and itechguides' "How to Use Snapchat Memories to Download, Delete, & Back Up Snaps" both teach the two Snapchat routes — press-hold a Snap in Memories and tap Export, or request a Memories-only My Data export — and both stop at the phone or the computer: the file is exported, the guide ends, and no USB drive is ever mentioned. The second shape is the generic OTG guide: the two YouTube OTG walkthroughs ("How to Connect USB Drive to Android Phone to Move Photos & Videos" and "How to Transfer Photos, Videos and any Files from Android Phone to USB Pendrive") and the EyeFi forum thread all copy by hand in the Files app — select in DCIM, Copy to, paste on the drive — and Snapchat is never named, no Snapchat folder path is given, and the copy is manual select-copy-paste with no progress, no resume, and no verification. The third shape is our own SnapDisk videos article — the same one-tap copy, oldest-first in the background with progress, Pause and Stop, and resume — but generic, Snapchat never named. The fourth shape is the Memories importer app, and it is the closest near-miss on the phone side: Memories Import on Google Play ($4.99 one-time) takes your My Data export ZIP and saves your whole Snapchat library to the phone's gallery, with original dates and locations preserved — but it saves to the gallery, and the USB step is missing entirely: the Memories are on the phone, and the drive is still untouched. The fifth shape is the cloud backup app: Backup for Snapchat Memories on AppBrain imports your export and backs selected files up to your own Google Drive — a cloud copy, not a USB copy, and the opposite of a drive you hold. A sixth guide, getmemoriesout's "How to Back Up Snapchat Memories (Free, All of Them)", does the first half thoroughly — check Backup Progress, request the My Data export, download every ZIP — and then points at a computer, an external drive via its own large-export guide, iCloud, or Google Photos, without a one-tap phone-to-USB step of its own. Another guide in the same shape, backupmemories.com's "How to backup Snapchat Memories, and where to keep them", covers the export and then points at Google Photos, the phone gallery, or a computer or hard drive — again with no one-tap phone-to-USB step. That is the gap: the export is taught to the phone or the computer, the USB copy is taught by hand for camera photos, the importer apps stop at the gallery or the cloud, and nobody puts the two together in one tap.

Where Snapchat keeps your photos and videos

Snapchat keeps your saved Snaps in Memories, and Memories is not a folder on your phone. It is Snapchat's account-linked storage area, on Snapchat's servers: a Snap saved to Memories appears inside the app when you swipe up from the camera screen, and it stays visible after you log in on a new phone as long as its backup is complete. A Snap being visible in Memories does not mean a file exists on your phone — and until a file exists on your phone, no backup app, SnapDisk included, has anything to copy. Two things put a file on the phone:

  • Export from Memories. Open Memories, press and hold a Snap, tap Export, and choose where to save it — or tap the selector icon, pick several Snaps, and export them together. Snapchat's own help article says the same for one Snap and for a batch. The export lands in your gallery — but the guides disagree about the folder underneath, and the honest thing is to say so rather than pick one and be wrong on your phone. Macmyths says the file may appear in "a folder such as Snapchat, Pictures, or DCIM, depending on the device". The Condia says "on Android, they go to your gallery, sometimes in a Snapchat folder depending on your phone's brand". Watsspace says "you'll typically find them in the system Photos or Gallery app, often in a 'Snapchat' album". Check your gallery first for a Snapchat album, then look in the Files app in Pictures, in DCIM, and in Downloads — the file is in one of them, and the gallery album is the reliable check, not the folder path.
  • The My Data export. Snapchat's official export (Settings → My Data, or accounts.snapchat.com) sends your whole Memories library as a ZIP, or several ZIPs of about 2 GB each for a multi-year account. The ZIP downloads to your browser's Downloads folder — and the download link expires after 7 days, so download it promptly. The ZIP is not photos yet: it must be unzipped first (Method 2 below), and only the unzipped files copy to a drive.

Three more facts about where things are. First, check Backup Progress before anything else: in Snapchat, open your profile, tap the gear, go to App & Privacy → My App → Memories, and look at Backup Progress — it must say Complete before you log out, switch phones, or uninstall Snapchat, because Snapchat's own help says Memories that weren't backed up can't be recovered. Second, My Eyes Only is separate: Snaps in My Eyes Only are protected by a passcode, stay inside Snapchat, and are not in the export unless you unlock them and move them out first. Third, future Snaps can save themselves: in Settings → Memories, set the Save Button to Memories & Camera Roll and new Snaps land in your gallery automatically as you save them — then the next backup is a one-tap copy instead of another export session. And the deadline: Snapchat's own help page, "How do I manage my Memories storage?", says you get up to 5 GB of complimentary Memories storage, that Snapchat "will not automatically delete your Memories because you do not upgrade", and that "starting in January 2027, Memories that are more than one year old and are not part of your oldest 5 GB of Memories will be archived and remain stored on Snapchat" — archived Memories stay as thumbnails, and opening, editing, or sharing one then takes a paid plan. No exact day is given, and Snapchat says it will give advance notice in the app first. The free way out is the download: export your Memories before they are archived, and keep your own copy on a drive.

What you need

An Android phone (Android 7.0 or later for SnapDisk); a USB drive your phone can read — a USB-C flash drive, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter (most modern Android phones support OTG; if the phone doesn't see the drive, try a different adapter); and enough space on the drive for the Snapchat photos and videos you want to keep. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 can't hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit. And one check before you start: open Snapchat, check Memories → Backup Progress says Complete, then export the Snaps you want — or request the My Data export if you want all of them — and check in your gallery that the Snapchat album actually holds them. The export is the step people skip, and a backup run over a phone with no exported files copies nothing and proves nothing.

Method 1: SnapDisk (one tap, background copy)

SnapDisk is a free Android app that does exactly this job and nothing else. Install it from the v0.1.2 APK (it's not on Google Play yet), then:

  1. Export the Memories first — do not run the backup on an empty phone. In Snapchat, swipe up to Memories, press and hold a Snap, tap Export, and choose Save to Gallery. For a batch, tap the selector icon at the top of Memories, pick the Snaps, and export them together — about 100 Snaps per batch, so a big library takes several batches. Then open your gallery and check the Snapchat album holds them. If you set Save Button to Memories & Camera Roll first, future Snaps save to the gallery as you take them and this step shrinks to nothing.
  2. Run the default run — it covers the whole library, not just DCIM. SnapDisk's default run copies your whole media library via MediaStore — every photo and video the gallery sees, oldest first — so exported Snaps are covered whatever folder they sit in: Pictures/Snapchat, DCIM/Snapchat, or Downloads. The "/sdcard/DCIM" label on the settings screen is only the display label until you pick a folder; the copy itself is the whole library. Do not pick a folder unless you want only the Snapchat files — see step 3.
  3. Or point From at just the Snapchat folder. If you want only your Snapchat photos on the drive — not the whole camera library — open SnapDisk's settings, tap the From folder picker, and navigate to the folder that holds the exported Snaps (Internal storage > Pictures > Snapchat, or Internal storage > DCIM > Snapchat, or Internal storage > Download — whichever the Files app shows holding them). The copy walks the folder recursively and mirrors its structure on the drive. Run each folder that holds Snaps in turn; files already copied are never copied twice.
  4. Set the To folder to the USB drive. Plug in the USB drive, tap the To folder picker, and choose a folder on the drive.
  5. Plug in the USB drive. SnapDisk offers a backup in a notification: "External storage is connected. Backup photos?"
  6. Tap Backup. Photos and videos copy oldest-first — your earliest exported Snaps first — in the background, while you use the phone normally.
  7. Watch the progress notification — real percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left — with Pause and Stop. You can pause and resume any time.
  8. Wait for "Backup complete." Every file is size-verified after copying, so the counts are proof, not a promise. Files that already copied are never copied twice — so if the free 5 GB per-run limit stops a big Snapchat folder partway, just run it again and it picks up where it left off.

If you unplug the drive mid-copy, the backup pauses with the full error shown and resumes where it stopped when the drive is back. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device. What SnapDisk adds over the rival that also does phone-to-USB — VaultDrive, Pro $29.99 once, free up to 200 files or 2 GB per backup, "2,000+ installs on the App Store and Google Play" — is the oldest-first order, the detailed progress with Pause and Stop, per-file size verification, a free tier of 5 GB per run with no file count, and about a third of the Pro price. And what the importer apps add that SnapDisk does not: Memories Import ($4.99 once) turns your My Data export ZIP into gallery files with original dates and locations — it is the tool for getting Memories onto the phone, and SnapDisk is the tool for getting them from the phone onto a drive.

Method 2: the My Data export — your whole Memories library as a zip

Method 1 copies the Memories already on your phone as files. If you want everything — every Memory you ever saved, including the ones you never exported one by one — the only bulk route Snapchat itself offers is the My Data export, and it is a two-step job: the export gets the files onto your phone, and then they still have to be copied to the drive. Here is the whole route:

  1. Check Backup Progress first. In Snapchat, open your profile, tap the gear, go to App & Privacy → My App → Memories, and check Backup Progress says Complete. Memories that weren't backed up can't be recovered, and the export cannot fetch what was never backed up.
  2. Request the export. In Snapchat, go to Settings → My Data, or sign in at accounts.snapchat.com and open My Data. Under "Select data to include", turn on Export your Memories, tap Request Only Memories, pick the whole date range (All time, so every year is included), and tap Submit. Snapchat's own help says the same steps, and that longer date ranges take longer to process.
  3. Wait for the email. Snapchat emails you a download link when the export is ready — usually between one hour and 7 days after you ask, longer for a large export. The download link expires after 7 days, so download the ZIP as soon as the email arrives, or request it again.
  4. Download and unzip the ZIP on your phone. The ZIP downloads to your phone — your browser's Downloads folder. A multi-year account arrives as several ZIPs of about 2 GB each, so download every one. Open each ZIP in the Files app and extract it: the photos and videos inside are ordinary files, viewable through the index.html Snapchat puts in the export. The ZIP itself is not an image or video file, so SnapDisk would skip it — unzipping is not optional, it is the step that turns the export into a backup.
  5. Point SnapDisk at the unzipped folder and run Method 1 on it: From picker to the unzipped folder, To picker to the drive, tap Backup. The copy runs oldest-first with progress, Pause, and verification, exactly as above. Or run the default run — the unzipped files are media files on your phone, and the whole-library copy picks them up too.

Two honest notes. First, the export is slow and partial by nature: you wait on Snapchat, the link expires in 7 days, and what arrives is your Memories — the export does not include Snaps still sitting in My Eyes Only unless you moved them out first, and Snapchat's own help warns to try a computer rather than a phone if the download gives trouble. Second, the export's files can lose their dates: the exported files may not carry their original dates inside the ZIP, so the gallery may show them by download date rather than capture date — the photos are all there, but the order in the gallery is the download order, not the memory order. That is a Snapchat export property, not a SnapDisk one: SnapDisk copies the files byte-for-byte and verifies them, and copies oldest-first by the date the file carries.

Method 3: by hand in the Files app (no app needed)

If you'd rather not install anything, Android's built-in Files app can copy the exported Snaps to the USB drive — after the export step, which no file manager can do for you: 1. Export the Memories first, exactly as in Method 1 — press-hold in Memories, Export, Save to Gallery — and check the Snapchat album in your gallery. 2. Plug in the USB drive. A notification or a "USB drive" entry should appear in the Files app under Storage devices. 3. Open Files, browse to the folder that holds the exported Snaps — Internal storage > Pictures > Snapchat, Internal storage > DCIM > Snapchat, or Internal storage > Download, whichever holds them — select the photos (or long-press the Snapchat folder to select the whole thing), tap ⋮ → Copy to, then choose the USB drive as the destination. 4. Wait for the copy to finish — and don't unplug the drive until the Files app says it's done. This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason SnapDisk exists: no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time Memories pile up — and you have to remember which folder holds the exports, because the Files app will not tell you that Pictures is not where Snapchat puts them on every phone. For a Memories library that grows every day, the app's oldest-first background copy is the calmer option.

The 5 GB free cap, and how it shapes the routine

SnapDisk's free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run — verified in the repo as FREE_BACKUP_BYTES_LIMIT, 5 GiB — and runs can be repeated. Files already copied are never copied twice, so a second run picks up where the first stopped. For Snapchat, 5 GB goes further than it does for a camera library: exported Snaps are the compressed photos and short videos Snapchat stores, and a folder of exported Snaps is often one free run. The videos are the part that eats the cap — a Memories library full of long saved videos can pass 5 GB on its own, and that is the count to do before you start: open the Files app, look at the size of the Snapchat folder (or the unzipped export folder), divide by 5 GB, and that is how many free runs the copy needs. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (product id pro_unlock, verified in the repo's billing code) that auto-starts the backup when a disk is connected and removes the size limit, so a whole Memories library copies in one run — and the auto-start is what makes the repeat actually happen, because the copy starts when the drive connects instead of waiting for you to remember. One more size note, and it cuts the other way for Snapchat: your free 5 GB of Memories storage on Snapchat's side is a separate limit from SnapDisk's 5 GB per run — the first is how much Snapchat keeps for free before archiving starts in January 2027, the second is how much SnapDisk copies per free run. Do not confuse the two; the article keeps them apart on purpose. A file of 4 GB or more on a FAT32 drive is skipped with its reason and the job continues — verified in the repo's BackupService. A single Snap never reaches that, but a long saved video can — and that file is skipped and listed, the photos around it copy normally, and the fix is the same exFAT format recommended above.

What this does not back up

This is the part people get wrong, so here it is plainly. Copying the exported Snaps backs up the files — the photos and videos. It does not back up:

  • Your Snapchat account. Your profile, your friends, your Snap score, and your account itself live on Snapchat's servers, not in a folder on your phone. Copying files does not copy the account, and if the account is deleted or banned, the drive holds the photos, not the account.
  • Chats and messages. Chat messages are text in Snapchat's database, not files in a folder. The My Data export in Method 2 includes your data as an archive — SnapDisk copies image and video files only, so the chat history stays on the phone.
  • Memories still only in Snapchat. A Memory you never exported — never pressed Export on, never requested in a My Data export — is not a file on your phone. Method 1 copies what is there; Method 2 is the route for the rest. If you delete Snapchat or log out before Backup Progress says Complete, the un-backed-up Memories are gone — Snapchat's own help says so.
  • My Eyes Only, as it sits. Snaps in My Eyes Only are protected by a passcode and are not in the export. Unlock them and move them out of My Eyes Only first, then export — keeping in mind that exporting removes the extra privacy layer the passcode gave them, so the drive copy is only as private as the drive.
  • Other people's Snaps. A Snap someone sent you, or a Story you only viewed, is not yours to export — it lives in their account or in the chat, and no export on your side fetches it. This article covers your own Memories already on your phone.
  • Anything Snapchat archives in January 2027 that you never downloaded. Archived Memories stay on Snapchat as thumbnails, and opening one takes a paid plan. The download in Method 2 is free, and it is the only way to hold the file yourself — but only if you do it before the archive takes effect for that Memory.

If you want the whole account — Memories, chats, and account data together — the My Data export in Method 2 is the route, and it is the only one Snapchat itself supports. This article's one-tap route is for the photos and videos as files on a drive you hold.

What to avoid

  • Running the backup before exporting anything. Memories live in Snapchat's cloud, not on your phone. A backup run over a phone with no exported Snaps copies nothing — and finishes green, which looks like a completed backup of an empty set. Export first, check the gallery album, then back up.
  • Trusting one guide's folder and skipping the check. The guides disagree — a Snapchat album in the gallery, a Snapchat folder in Pictures or DCIM, or Downloads — and your phone decides. Open the gallery and the Files app and look before you start, or you will back up an empty folder and think the job is done.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Downloading the export and stopping there. The My Data export ZIP on your phone is not a backup — it is one file on the same phone that could be lost, and its download link expires in 7 days. Unzip it and copy the files to the drive, or the export was only a download.
  • Copying the ZIP and calling it a backup. SnapDisk copies image and video files, not zip archives — the export ZIP would be skipped. Unzip first, then copy the folder.
  • Letting the 7-day link lapse. The My Data download link expires after 7 days. Download the ZIP the day the email arrives — a lapsed link means requesting the whole export again and waiting hours to days for a new one.
  • Waiting past January 2027. Snapchat's own help page says Memories more than a year old outside your oldest 5 GB will be archived starting in January 2027, and opening an archived Memory then takes a paid plan. The download is free now; after archiving, the same Memory costs a subscription to open. Export before the deadline, not after it.
  • Assuming the copy keeps running with the screen off. Unverified — no wake lock, no battery-saver handling in the repo. Keep the phone awake for the run or check it.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.
  • Forgetting My Eyes Only. Snaps in My Eyes Only are not in the export and not in the gallery. If they matter, unlock them and move them out before you export — otherwise the backup silently misses them and you find out when the phone is gone.

Rule of thumb: the Memories are in Snapchat's cloud — export them to the phone first, then the drive. The folder is whichever one your phone chose — check the gallery album, not one guide's path. The archive deadline is January 2027 — download before it, not after it. Do the export, check the copy on the drive before you delete anything, and run the My Data export as well if the Memories you never exported one by one matter.
