Back up Instagram photos and videos to USB from Android

Your Instagram photos are files on your phone — in the Instagram folder and the Downloads folder, which you can point a backup app at. Plug a USB drive into the phone and copy them straight to it, no computer, no upload.

November 22, 2026 · 6 min read

TL;DR: Plug a USB drive into your Android phone, point SnapDisk's From folder at the Instagram folder (Pictures/Instagram) — and at Downloads for Reels saved through a browser — and tap Backup: your Instagram photos and videos copy oldest-first to the drive, with no computer and no upload in between. The honest limits, up front: SnapDisk copies media files only — the photos and videos in the folder you pick. It does not back up your Instagram account itself, your messages, comments, or the posts you only bookmarked inside the app (Instagram's Save is a bookmark, not a file — it stays inside Instagram and vanishes if the creator deletes the post). And the route matters: SnapDisk's default run copies DCIM only, and Instagram content is not in DCIM — so the Instagram route is the From-picker folder route, not the default run, and this article says so in the steps, not in a footnote. Your whole account at once is a different job: Meta's official export sends you a zip of everything, but the zip lands on your phone and must be unzipped before anything can copy to USB — that two-step route is Method 2 below. There is no bulk on-device Instagram-to-USB export: nobody offers one, and this article does not pretend SnapDisk is one. SnapDisk copies, it never deletes — deleting from the phone afterwards is your step, only after verifying a few photos on the drive. Free version: 5 GB per run; run it again past the cap and it picks up where it stopped, because files already copied are never copied twice. Screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified — keep the phone awake for the run. As of November 22, 2026, no top search result offers phone-to-USB Instagram backup in one tap: the top results download Instagram media to the phone only, export it as a zip that still needs a copy step, or copy by hand in a file manager without naming Instagram's folders.

What the search results actually offer

Search for this job and the results split into four shapes, none of them the one-tap phone-to-USB copy. The first shape downloads Instagram media to the phone and stops there. The clearest example is en.androidayuda's InstaPP guide: it downloads one photo at a time into a folder on the phone called "InstaPP" — a phone folder, not a drive, and one tap per photo, not a whole set. The same shape covers umatechnology's InstaSave and FastSave guides, which copy a post link and save the photo or video into the phone's gallery, and the web downloaders (ViewInsta, DownloadGram) that save a browser download into Downloads or Pictures. The second shape is Meta's official export, covered by HowToGeek and by FastSaveMedia's backup guide: Accounts Center → Your information and permissions → Download Your Information, choose "Download to your device", wait for the email, and download a zip within four days. That is the only bulk route Instagram itself offers — but the zip lands on the phone, and the guides stop there: the zip still has to be unzipped and copied to a drive by hand, and FastSaveMedia's own storage advice is the 3-2-1 rule with the export on a computer or an external drive — no phone-to-USB step in either. The third shape is the generic OTG guide: YouTube walkthroughs and Wondershare MobileTrans' phone-to-USB guide copy by hand in the Files app — select in DCIM, Copy to, paste on the drive — and Instagram is never named, no Instagram folder path is given, and the copy is manual select-copy-paste with no progress, no resume, and no verification. The fourth shape is the bookmark: Instagram's own Save adds a post to a private list inside the app, and several guides present it as "saving" — but it saves nothing to the phone at all, so there is nothing to copy to a drive. Two one-tap whole-library apps do appear in the wider results — PhotoStick Mobile and Photos to USB: OTG Backup, both of which copy the whole gallery in one tap — but neither names Instagram or its folders, and neither is a guide for the Instagram folders. VaultDrive, the rival this site compares against, also does phone-to-USB with no computer and lets you pick files and folders — and its page, read in full this run, never mentions Instagram either, so there is no Instagram-specific guide there. That is the gap: the folders are named in download-to-phone guides, the USB copy is done by hand in generic guides, and nobody puts the two together in one tap.

Where Instagram keeps your photos and videos

Instagram saves what it saves on your phone as ordinary files — but in two different folders, depending on how the file got there, so check both before you start:

  • Pictures/Instagram — the Instagram folder in your gallery. This is where your own posted photos land when the Save Original Photos toggle is on (Settings → Archiving and downloading), and where photos you save from the app land. It is also where a Reel downloaded with Instagram's own Download option lands — the option in the Share menu on public Reels, where the creator allows it.
  • Downloads — where a Reel or photo saved through a browser downloader lands. If you copy a post link and paste it into a web downloader in Chrome, the file downloads like any browser download, into the Downloads folder — and most gallery apps show it in a Download album.

What is not in either folder is just as important. DCIM is the camera folder — your phone's own photos live there, not Instagram's — so a backup that only copies DCIM misses your Instagram media entirely, and that is why the Instagram route below picks folders instead of running the default. Instagram's Save (the bookmark icon) saves nothing to the phone: it adds the post to a private list inside Instagram, and if the creator deletes the post it vanishes from that list too — a bookmark is not a file, and it cannot be copied to a drive. Stories you want to keep need the archive on: Settings → Archiving and downloading → Save story to archive — otherwise an expired Story is gone, and no folder on the phone holds it. And a photo you only viewed is not on the phone at all: Instagram shows you a copy from its servers, and only a deliberate save or download puts a file in either folder above. Check the two folders in the Files app first — if Pictures/Instagram does not exist on your phone, nothing has been saved there yet, and the toggle above is the fix for future posts, not for past ones.

What you need

An Android phone (Android 7.0 or later for SnapDisk); a USB drive your phone can read — a USB-C flash drive, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter (most modern Android phones support OTG; if the phone doesn't see the drive, try a different adapter); and enough space on the drive for the Instagram photos and videos you want to keep. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 can't hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit. And one check before you start: open the Files app, look at the Pictures/Instagram and Downloads folder sizes, and divide by 5 GB — that is how many free runs the copy needs, and the arithmetic belongs before you plug the drive in, not halfway through the copy.

Method 2: the Meta export — your whole account as a zip

Method 1 copies the Instagram media that is already on your phone as files. If you want everything you ever posted — including Stories, Reels, and the originals of posts you never saved to the phone — the only bulk route Instagram itself offers is Meta's official export, and it is a two-step job: the export gets the files onto your phone, and then they still have to be copied to the drive. Here is the whole route:

  1. Request the export. In Instagram, open your profile, tap the menu, tap Accounts Center, then Your information and permissions, then Export your information (some versions say Download your information). Tap Create export, pick your Instagram profile, and choose Export to device — not the transfer to Google Drive or Dropbox, which sends your archive to a cloud account instead of your phone. Pick what goes in it (Content covers your posts, Reels, and Stories), all time, and high media quality, then start the export and enter your password when asked.
  2. Wait for the email. Meta prepares the archive and emails you when it is ready. Most arrive within hours to a few days; Meta allows up to 30 days. The download link in that email is valid for four days — download the zip as soon as the email arrives, or request it again.
  3. Download and unzip the zip on your phone. The zip downloads to your phone — usually the Downloads folder. Open it in the Files app and extract it: the photos and videos inside are ordinary files in folders, and only now are they files SnapDisk can copy. The zip itself is not an image or video file, so SnapDisk would skip it — unzipping is not optional, it is the step that turns the export into a backup.
  4. Point SnapDisk at the unzipped folder and run Method 1 on it: From picker to the unzipped folder, To picker to the drive, tap Backup. The copy runs oldest-first with progress, Pause, and verification, exactly as above.

Two honest notes. First, the export is slow and partial by nature: you wait on Meta, the link expires, and what arrives is your content plus messages, comments, and account data — the messages and comments are text in the archive, not photos, and SnapDisk copies only the image and video files, so they stay on the phone. Second, the export is the only route that reaches posts you never saved to the phone — Method 1 can only copy files that are already there. If your Instagram folder is empty because you never turned on Save Original Photos, the export is the route; turn the toggle on as well, so future posts save to the phone and the next backup is a one-tap folder copy instead of another export.

Method 3: by hand in the Files app (no app needed)

If you'd rather not install anything, Android's built-in Files app can copy the Instagram folders to the USB drive: 1. Plug in the USB drive. A notification or a "USB drive" entry should appear in the Files app under Storage devices. 2. Open Files, browse to Internal storage > Pictures > Instagram — and, for the browser-saved Reels, Internal storage > Download. 3. Select the photos (or long-press the Instagram folder to select the whole thing), tap ⋮ → Copy to, then choose the USB drive as the destination. 4. Wait for the copy to finish — and don't unplug the drive until the Files app says it's done. This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason SnapDisk exists: no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time Instagram photos pile up — and you have to remember both folders, because the Files app will not tell you that DCIM is not where Instagram keeps its files. For an Instagram folder that grows every day, the app's oldest-first background copy is the calmer option.

What this does not back up

This is the part people get wrong, so here it is plainly. Copying the Pictures/Instagram and Downloads folders backs up the files — the photos and videos. It does not back up:

  • Your Instagram account. Your profile, your follower list, and your account itself live on Instagram's servers, not in a folder on your phone. Copying files does not copy the account, and if the account is deleted or banned, the drive holds the photos, not the account.
  • Messages and comments. Direct messages and comments are text in Instagram's database, not files in the Instagram folder. The Meta export in Method 2 includes them in the archive as text — SnapDisk copies image and video files only, so they stay on the phone.
  • Bookmarked posts. Instagram's Save is a bookmark inside the app, not a file. A saved post from someone else's account is not on your phone at all, and it vanishes from your Saved list if the creator deletes it. This article's route does not back it up — it was never yours to copy.
  • Other people's posts. Saver apps like InstaSave and FastSave download other people's posts onto your phone, one link at a time. This article covers your own media already on your phone. Downloading someone else's posts without their permission is against Instagram's terms, and a drive full of other people's photos is not a backup of your account.
  • Stories you never archived. A Story that expired without Save story to archive turned on is gone — no folder on the phone holds it, and no copy can bring it back. The export in Method 2 reaches archived Stories; it cannot reach ones that were never archived.
  • Anything still only on Instagram's servers. A post you never saved to the phone, never downloaded, and never exported is not a file on your phone. Method 1 copies what is there; Method 2 is the route for the rest.

If you want the whole account — posts, Stories, messages, and media together — the Meta export in Method 2 is the route, and it is the only one Instagram itself supports. This article's one-tap route is for the photos and videos as files on a drive you hold.

What to avoid

  • Running the default and thinking Instagram came along. The default run copies DCIM — the camera folder. Your Instagram media is in Pictures/Instagram and Downloads. A default run that finishes green has backed up your camera roll, not your Instagram — check the folder before you delete anything.
  • Treating the Save bookmark as a backup. The bookmark icon saves a post to a list inside Instagram, not to your phone. If the creator deletes the post, it vanishes from the list. A backup is a file on a drive you hold — a bookmark is a link to someone else's server.
  • Downloading the export and stopping there. The Meta export zip on your phone is not a backup — it is one file on the same phone that could be lost, and its download link expires in four days. Unzip it and copy the files to the drive, or the export was only a download.
  • Copying the zip and calling it a backup. SnapDisk copies image and video files, not zip archives — the export zip would be skipped. Unzip first, then copy the folder.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Assuming the copy keeps running with the screen off. Unverified — no wake lock, no battery-saver handling in the repo. Keep the phone awake for the run or check it.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.
  • Backing up other people's posts and calling it your archive. Saver apps make it easy to pull anyone's public post onto your phone. Your archive is your own media — the posts, Reels, and Stories you made. Someone else's post is theirs, and Instagram's terms say so.

Rule of thumb: the photos are files in two folders — back up both folders. The account is on Instagram's servers — it follows your sign-in, not your drive. The bookmark is a link — it follows nothing. Do the folder copy, check the copy on the drive before you delete anything, and run the Meta export as well if the posts you never saved to the phone matter.
