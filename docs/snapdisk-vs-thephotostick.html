SnapDisk vs ThePhotoStick Omni: Android USB backup, compared

One is a $9.99 app that copies to any USB drive; the other is a $69.99–$149.99 stick that only works with its own app. Here is how they actually differ, from the facts we could verify as of November 2026.

November 22, 2026 · 4 min read

TL;DR: SnapDisk and ThePhotoStick Omni both back up Android photos to a USB drive offline, with no account and no subscription. The verified differences: the Omni is hardware — $69.99 to $149.99 by size on its offer page (checked November 8 and 11, 2026), and its free app works only with Omni drives; SnapDisk is a $9.99 one-time app that works with any drive Android can mount, free up to 5 GB per backup run. The Omni backs up photos, videos, music, documents, and contacts, sorts them into folders, and restores files to the phone; SnapDisk copies photos and videos only, oldest-first, with Pause and Stop in the progress notification and a size check on every file — and it never deletes. The Omni's Play listing was updated November 21, 2026 and rates 3.9 from 1,689 ratings; its most recent top review describes a five-hour backup that copied nothing. SnapDisk is not on Google Play yet (v0.1.2).

The two apps at a glance

SnapDisk is a free Android app (v0.1.2, not on Google Play yet) that backs up photos and videos to a USB disk. Plug in the disk, tap Backup in the notification, and photos copy oldest-first in the background, size-verified, with a progress notification showing percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left, plus Pause and Stop. Free: manual backup, 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup when a disk is connected and unlimited size. No cloud, no account, no ads, and no internet permission — your photos physically cannot leave the phone.

ThePhotoStick Omni (com.prairieit.thephotostick, by ThePhotoStick / PRAIRIE IT, Inc) is a USB stick with a three-way adapter head (Lightning, USB-C, micro-USB) plus a free companion app, for phones and computers. Plug it in, tap GO, and it finds and copies photos, videos, music, documents, and contacts, sorting them into folders; files can be moved to a Windows or Mac computer or restored to the phone. The app has been on Google Play since June 2021, was updated November 21, 2026 (version 4.3.5), and rates 3.9 from 1,689 ratings; AppBrain counts about 380,000 installs total, around 180 a day over the last 30 days. It needs Android 8.0 or later, shows no ads, and carries a 30-day money-back guarantee. The catch, in the Play listing's own words: "Designed to work with only ThePhotoStick Omni devices."

Price: $69.99–$149.99 of hardware vs $9.99 once for the app

This comparison is not app against app — it is app against hardware plus app. SnapDisk Pro is $9.99 once: auto-backup on connect plus unlimited backup size. A ThePhotoStick Omni stick costs $69.99 for 32 GB, $89.99 for 64 GB, $109.99 for 128 GB, and $149.99 for 256 GB on its offer page, checked November 8 and again November 11, 2026; the 32 GB size also carried a $7.99 shipping charge, the larger sizes shipped free. The cheapest Omni stick costs seven times SnapDisk Pro — and it is a one-time purchase too, with no subscription on either side.

One price discrepancy, resolved honestly: the 2026 "best photo backup software" roundups quote $44.99 one-time for ThePhotoStick, and the vendor's own blog page shows "Price: $99.99 $44.99". That $44.99 is the entry price on the brand's own store page — which listed the same four sizes at $44.99, $59.99, $84.99, and $138.99 on November 8, 2026 — while the offer page that actually takes the order charged $69.99–$149.99 on the same dates. Prices move between the brand's pages and sales, so the range to budget on is $69.99–$149.99, and check the page you buy from before ordering. There is also a newer model, the Omni X, launched in April 2026 at $59.99–$149.99 by size — the same hardware-locked idea, a different stick.

Both free versions are genuinely usable, not demos — with one big difference in what "free" buys. SnapDisk free backs up manually up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud, to any drive you already own. ThePhotoStick Omni's app is free too, but it does nothing without the stick: the $69.99 minimum is the price of entry, and a drive you already own cannot be substituted.

The free tier: a free app with a hardware toll vs 5 GB a run

ThePhotoStick Omni's free tier is the app itself: download it free, and it finds, copies, and organises your photos, videos, music, documents, and contacts onto the stick. What it does not do is work with a drive you already have — the app is locked to Omni sticks, and the cheapest one is $69.99. If you already own a USB drive, the free app is worth nothing until you buy the hardware.

SnapDisk's free tier is the opposite shape: the app is free, the drive is yours. Manual backup up to 5 GB per run, no ads, no account, no cloud, to any USB drive Android can mount. Which free tier goes further depends on what you already own: if you have a drive in a drawer, SnapDisk's free tier backs up today for $0; if you have no drive at all, the Omni's $69.99 buys the drive and the app in one box.

Before the copy: one button on the stick vs any drive, oldest-first

ThePhotoStick Omni is built around one tap: plug the stick in, open the app, tap GO, and it searches the phone for photos and videos and copies them. It sorts what it finds into folders, and it also picks up music, documents, and contacts — file types SnapDisk does not touch. Its listing also promises the stick works with Windows and Mac computers, so the same stick can back up a phone and an old laptop.

SnapDisk's setup is the opposite: there is no stick to buy and no folder to pick. The default run reads the whole MediaStore library — every photo and video the gallery sees, oldest-first — with no folder filter, verified in the code. If you want only one album, pick its folder in the From picker instead. The copy order is the difference you feel: oldest-first means your earliest photos land on the drive first, so a half-finished run still holds the oldest memories; the Omni's listing names no copy order at all.

During the backup: tap GO vs background with Pause and Stop

SnapDisk copies in the background as a foreground service, while you use the phone normally. The progress notification shows real percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left, with Pause and Stop. If you unplug the disk mid-copy, the backup pauses with the full error shown and resumes where it stopped when the disk is back; files already copied are never copied twice. Every file is size-verified after copying, and a file that fails verification is retried once, then recorded in the skipped list with its reason.

The Omni's listing promises the same one-button simplicity — plug in, tap GO, wait — but says nothing about a progress notification, Pause and Stop, or per-file verification. What its reviewers report instead is the failure mode SnapDisk's verification exists to catch. The most recent top review on the Play listing, from October 29, 2026, reads: "A total mess. I followed the tutorial exactly, it ran for over 5 hours, saying it was downloading my pix. I finally paused it to charge my phone. I put the stick in my lap top, not one single photo was on it. I am NOT happy." An earlier review, quoted in the competitor file, describes the same shape at three hours: "It proceeds like it is saving my photos. Today it was uploading for 3 hours … I plugged it into my laptop to see if any pictures downloaded. Not one picture. Each folder says it is empty." (182 people found that one helpful.) A backup that reports progress for hours and copies nothing is the worst outcome in this category — and it is the Omni's top complaint, not a one-off.

After the copy: verification, restore, and what the drive holds

After the copy, the Omni has two things SnapDisk does not: it restores files back to the phone, and it sorts the backup into folders you can browse. Its listing also says the files can be moved to a Windows or Mac computer — the stick is a bridge between phone and computer, not just a phone backup. What the listing does not promise is any check that the copy actually arrived: no per-file verification is named anywhere in it.

SnapDisk's answer after the copy is narrower and stricter. Every file's size is checked after copying, a failed file is retried once, and the run ends with a completion notification — photos copied, size, and how many failed — so you know what actually arrived before you delete anything. SnapDisk v0.1.2 has no restore function, verified in the code, and it keeps no folder structure of its own: the copies are the original files, byte for byte, on the drive. If restoring to the phone matters to you, that is a real Omni advantage; if knowing the copy is complete matters more, that is SnapDisk's.

Freeing up space: delete after backup vs copy-only

Here is the honest part. Neither app frees up space on your phone by itself. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes anything — so freeing space is a two-step flow you do yourself: copy to the drive, open a few photos on the drive to check they arrived, then delete the originals from your phone. That flow is covered step by step in How to free up space by moving photos to a USB drive.

ThePhotoStick Omni's listing says nothing about deleting from the phone either — its job is the copy, and the delete is yours. One Omni reviewer, rated five stars in July 2026, asks exactly the question both apps leave open: "once I put my photos videos from my phone on the photo stick can I delete what's on my phone". The answer is the same for both: yes, but only after you check the copy on the drive — open a few photos there first, then delete from the phone, never the other way round. And with the Omni, check the copy especially carefully: its top complaint is a run that looked finished and copied nothing.

What to avoid

  • Buying the stick before checking the app's reviews. The Omni rates 3.9 from 1,689 ratings, and its top recent review is a five-hour backup that copied nothing. Read the reviews on the Play listing before paying $69.99 and up.
  • Assuming any USB drive will do. The Omni's app works only with Omni sticks — a drive you already own will not work with it. SnapDisk works with any drive Android can mount, so the drive in your drawer is already the right one.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. This applies to both apps, and doubly to the Omni, whose reviewers report empty folders after hours of "copying".
  • Assuming "offline" means the same thing everywhere. Both apps copy offline with no account. SnapDisk goes one step further: it has no internet permission at all, so your photos physically cannot leave the device — it works with the phone in airplane mode.
  • Assuming the copy keeps running with the screen off. Neither listing promises it. SnapDisk's copy runs as a foreground service — the mechanism Android gives a copy that must keep running with the screen off — but the repo holds no wake lock and no battery-saver handling, and no comparison run has ever been tested, so screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified, and this article says so instead of promising it. The Omni's listing says nothing about it either.
  • Paying for a subscription you didn't want. Neither app has one: SnapDisk Pro is $9.99 once, and the Omni is a one-time hardware purchase with a free app. If a backup app asks for a monthly fee, it is a different model from both of these.

Rule of thumb: the app that wins is the one whose copy you trust enough to delete after. Compare the price, the hardware lock, and the copy — then check the copy on the drive before you delete anything.

Which one fits you

If you already own a USB drive, want the cheaper route, and care most about knowing the copy actually arrived, that is SnapDisk: free manual backup up to 5 GB per run, Pro $9.99 once for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size, oldest-first, Pause and Stop in the notification, and a size check on every file. If you own no drive at all, want one purchase that covers a phone and a computer, and want restore and folder sorting, that is the Omni — $69.99–$149.99 by size on its offer page — with the warning to read its reviews first and to check the copy on a computer before deleting anything from the phone. Either way, back up first, verify the copy, then delete — that order is what keeps your photos safe, whichever app does the copying.
