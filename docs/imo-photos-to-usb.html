Back up IMO photos to USB from Android

IMO photos are not on your phone until you save them — but unlike Kik, IMO has a switch for that. Turn it on once, and every photo you receive saves itself.

November 25, 2026 · 12 min read

TL;DR: IMO does not save the photos you receive — not automatically, not ever — unless you switch it on. The switch is real and it is the whole story: open IMO, go to Me, Settings, Chats, Auto-Save After Receive, and turn on both Photos and Videos. From then on every photo and video you receive saves itself to your gallery automatically, with no tap per photo. For the photos already sitting in your chats, the save is still one file at a time: open the photo full-screen, press and hold it, and save it — IMO's own listing says "simply press and hold any file to save it" — and there is no save-all button for a chat. The exact on-disk folder for the saved copies is not pinned down by any public source, so this article gives a check order, not a single path: look for an IMO album in your gallery first, then Pictures, then search the file manager. From there the backup is the easy half: SnapDisk's default run reads Android's media index with no folder filter and copies the whole library — not DCIM only — so plug in the drive, tap Backup, and the saved IMO photos copy along with everything else, no computer. The honest limits, up front: the switch only covers what you receive after you turn it on, never the photos already in your chats; a photo you only viewed lives inside IMO, not in any folder, and no backup app can reach it; a photo that lives only in imo Cloud has to be downloaded and saved first; a photo in a Privacy Chat may not be saveable at all, because IMO lets Privacy Chat block downloads; and screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified — the repo holds no wake lock and no battery-saver handling — so keep the phone awake for the run or check it. As of November 25, 2026, no top search result offers a dedicated IMO-photos-to-USB how-to: the results are generic OTG copy guides that never name IMO, our own generic backup articles that never mention it, and a Facebook reel that shows the transfer without the save step.

The wall: IMO saves nothing by itself unless you switch it on — a photo you only viewed lives in the chat, not on the phone

Start with the fact that decides the whole job, because IMO sits exactly between the two cases this series has already covered. WhatsApp has an auto-download switch that most people leave on, so its photos are usually in the gallery already. Kik has no switch at all, so its photos are never on the phone until you save each one by hand. IMO has the switch — Settings, Chats, Auto-Save After Receive, with separate toggles for Photos and Videos — and it ships off. An August 2026 walkthrough, filmed on a real phone, shows the path plainly: tap IMO, open the Me profile page, enter Settings, then Chats, then Auto-Save After Receive, where both toggles sit switched off — and the demonstrator switches both on, with on-screen text explaining that received media will save to the phone's gallery automatically. That video is the live proof the switch exists and what it does, and it is also the proof of the default: off, until you turn it on. Until you do, IMO behaves exactly like Kik: a photo you only viewed in a chat lives inside IMO, not in any folder on the phone, and no backup app, file manager, or USB drive can reach it, because there is nothing to reach.

That is the wall, and it has two faces, which is why this article gives the switch first and the one-by-one save second. The first face is the past: every photo already sitting in your chats was received while the switch was off, so none of it was saved, and the switch does not reach backwards. Those photos need the manual save, one file at a time — open the photo full-screen, press and hold it, save it — because IMO has no save-all button for a chat, no select-all, no export conversation. The second face is the future: once the switch is on, the wall is gone for everything you receive from then on, because every photo and video saves itself to the gallery the moment it arrives. That is what makes IMO different from Kik, and it is the reason this row ships instead of being killed the way the LINE and KakaoTalk rows were: those apps have only the one-by-one save and nothing else to add, while IMO has a switch that changes the job from an evening of tapping into a setting you flip once. A chat that has collected hundreds of photos over months is still a long evening of tapping for the photos already in it — this article does not pretend otherwise — but every photo that arrives after today saves itself, and that is a real difference, not a wording trick.

Two scope facts, because they decide what this article can and cannot promise. First, IMO syncs your message history and files to imo Cloud — its own Play listing says so, in so many words: all of your message history and files can be securely synced in imo Cloud to free up your phone storage. That cloud copy is not a copy on your phone. A photo that IMO freed from the phone's storage lives in the cloud until you download it, and the download is the save: open it in the chat, press and hold, save it, and only then is there a file on the phone to back up. A backup run on a phone whose IMO photos are cloud-only copies zero IMO photos, for the same reason a run on a phone whose photos were never saved copies zero: from the media index's point of view there are no IMO photos to copy. Second, the save itself can be refused, not just forgotten: IMO's own listing says Privacy Chat can block screenshot, copy, share, and download. A photo in a Privacy Chat may not be saveable at all — no toggle in Settings changes that, because the block is the other person's privacy setting, not yours. If a photo matters and it sits in a Privacy Chat, the honest answer is that the phone route in this article may not reach it, and a screenshot is the only copy left — a picture of the screen, not the photo, lower quality, carrying the app chrome around it. This article says that in the TL;DR, in the method, and in "What to avoid", because a guide that promised every IMO photo is saveable would be wrong on exactly the chats where the save looks broken.

And a word on whose photos these are, because IMO photos are usually your own conversations, not someone else's catalogue. The photos this article covers are the ones people sent you — a picture a friend took, a photo from a group chat, an image someone shared that you want to keep — and the ones you sent, which you usually still have as originals. Saving them to your own drive for your own records is the job. They are also someone else's photos in a plain sense: a friend's picture of their own life, a photo someone sent you in confidence. Saving a file does not grant permission to reuse it — so re-posting a photo someone sent you in a private chat, or sharing it onward as if it were yours, is a different act, and the same courtesy applies here as anywhere: the person who sent it is the person to ask before the photo leaves your phone for anywhere else. This article's methods copy the file to your drive; what you do with it afterwards is yours to decide, and asking is the honest step before anything leaves your phone. Only save photos you have permission to keep — and this article makes no claim about recovering photos from a chat you have already deleted, because IMO's cloud copy is a sync of the account, not a backup you can pull single photos out of onto a drive.

Where saved IMO photos land — the gallery first, the exact folder unverified

This section exists because the folder is the one fact no source settles, and a guide that picks one of them as certain would be guessing — and because IMO's own history gives the gallery answer without giving the path. The earliest record is from 2014, when IMO's beta added the gallery saving: photos shared in one-to-one chats are also saved in a photo gallery, which makes them far easier to access at any time. That is twelve years old, and this article does not pretend it is current evidence on its own — but the current evidence says the same thing in the present tense: the August 2026 walkthrough switches the Auto-Save toggles on with on-screen text explaining that received media will save to the phone's gallery automatically. Two sources, twelve years apart, agree on the destination: the gallery. Neither of them names the on-disk folder. No guide, no forum thread, and no IMO help page found this run names the folder for saved IMO photos — not Pictures/imo, not DCIM/imo, not a folder under Android/media, nothing. So this article does not state one path as the path. It gives the check order instead, and the check order is the same on every phone.

Check the gallery first. Open your gallery app and look for an "IMO" album. If it is there, the save works — and this is also the check that proves the default run will see the photos, because the gallery and SnapDisk read the same media index. If the album is not there yet, no photo has been saved: turn on Auto-Save as in Method 1, or save one photo by pressing and holding it, and look again. Then check Pictures in the file manager: Internal storage, Pictures — the phone makers' own help pages describe the pattern as Pictures/[app] for photos saved from a messaging app, and that is the most likely shape for the saved copies, but it is a pattern, not a confirmed path for IMO, and this article says so. Then search the file manager: search for a photo by its date, or browse Internal storage for a folder with IMO in the name — if the folder exists under a name you did not expect, the gallery album name is the clue to what it is called. If the photo is in none of those places, check that IMO has permission to save photos — without it, the app cannot write to the gallery at all — and save one photo again, watching for the confirmation that it saved.

One more scope fact for this section, because it decides what the drive copy holds: the saved file is the photo as IMO delivered it in the chat, not necessarily the original the sender took. IMO's own listing says files share in their original quality, and the save keeps the copy IMO delivered — SnapDisk copies that file byte-for-byte, no conversion, no quality change — but it cannot recover quality IMO already removed, and a photo sent as a chat picture may not be the full-resolution file the sender's camera took. If the photo you want is one you sent, the original is usually still in your camera roll in DCIM/Camera at full quality, as an ordinary indexed file the default run already covers; back that up instead. The IMO route for a photo you sent is only needed when the original is gone. And the photos you took inside IMO's own camera are a separate case again: a photo you took in IMO is a gallery file already once it is saved, and the save step in this article is for the photos other people sent you. A photo that lives only in imo Cloud is a third case: the cloud copy is not a file on the phone, and the save is the step that makes it one — open it in the chat, press and hold, save it, and only then look for it in the gallery.

What you need

An Android phone — Android 7.0 or later, which is SnapDisk's minimum version — with the IMO app installed and signed in, plus one USB drive your phone can read: a USB-C flash drive, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter. SnapDisk itself is installed from the v0.1.2 APK — it is not on Google Play yet — so install it by allowing "install unknown apps" for the browser or Files app you download it with, which is standard Android. You need nothing else for the main route: no file manager with hidden files, no extra app, no account. The save step is inside IMO itself, and the backup is SnapDisk's default run. Check the drive's space first: IMO photos are small — a chat photo is a few megabytes at most — so space is almost never the problem here, but look anyway, because a whole-library default run copies everything, not just IMO. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 cannot hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit, and exFAT is the format to use for a photo drive.

Before any method, settle the one question that decides whether the phone route works at all: does your phone see the USB drive? Plug the drive in and open Settings > Connected devices or Settings > Storage — the drive should appear there. That check is the test, and it takes ten seconds. If the drive does not appear, try a different adapter first — cheap OTG adapters are the usual failure — then a different drive, and a powered hub if the drive is a spinning hard drive rather than a flash drive. And one warning up front, because it applies to every route below: do not delete anything from the phone until the USB copy is done and verified. The photos in the chat are IMO's copy, and the photos in the gallery are yours — but until the drive copy is checked, the gallery is the only copy outside IMO, and deleting from the gallery before the check is how a photo ends up nowhere at all. And do not log out of IMO, delete the app, or sign in on another device until the save and the copy are both done: the in-chat photos depend on the account staying on the phone, and the cloud copy is a sync, not a backup you can pull single photos out of onto a drive.

Method 1: Turn on Auto-Save, then back up with SnapDisk

This is the route to use, because it is the only one that joins the whole job — the IMO photo, out of the chat and into the gallery, onto a USB drive plugged into the phone, no computer — using only the apps already on the phone plus SnapDisk. It has two halves, and they have to happen in order: the save, which puts the photos on the phone as files, and the SnapDisk copy, which puts them on the drive. The first half is the work, and it has two parts of its own: the switch, which covers everything you receive from now on, and the one-by-one save, which covers the photos already in your chats. The second half is the backup. Skipping the first half is the mistake this article exists to prevent: running SnapDisk's default backup with nothing saved copies every photo in the gallery — and zero IMO photos, because from the media index's point of view there are no IMO photos to copy.

  1. Turn on Auto-Save After Receive. Open IMO, tap Me to open your profile page, enter Settings, then Chats, then Auto-Save After Receive — and switch on both Photos and Videos. Both toggles ship off, so if you have never opened this screen, nothing you received was ever saved. This is the one setting that changes the job: from the moment both toggles are on, every photo and video you receive saves itself to the gallery automatically, with no tap per photo. The switch does not reach backwards — the photos already sitting in your chats were received while it was off, so step 2 still applies to them — but every photo that arrives after today saves itself, and that is the bulk route IMO actually has. Check it worked: ask someone to send you a photo, or wait for the next one to arrive, and look for it in the gallery. If it is there, the switch is on and working.
  2. Save the photos already in your chats, one at a time. Open the chat, open the photo full-screen, press and hold it, and save it — IMO's own listing says "simply press and hold any file to save it". Either way the photo is saved to the gallery. There is no save-all button — every photo is one tap — so a chat with a hundred photos is a long job, and it is worth doing in sittings rather than all at once. Save the ones that matter first; the rest can wait for the next sitting, because the switch is now catching everything new. If a photo will not save, check IMO's storage permission first — Settings > Apps > IMO > Permissions, Storage or Files and media, Allow — and consider updating the IMO app, because a save can fail on an outdated version. If the photo is in a Privacy Chat, it may not be saveable at all: IMO lets Privacy Chat block downloads, and no setting of yours overrides the other person's privacy setting — a screenshot is the only copy left for that photo, and a screenshot is a picture of the screen, not the photo: it is lower quality, it carries the app chrome around it, and it lands in the Screenshots folder, not with the saved photos. If a video will not save, screen-record it instead: open the video full-screen, start the phone's built-in screen recorder, play the video with the sound on, stop the recording, and trim the start and end in the gallery — a recording is a copy of the screen, not the video file, so it is lower quality and carries the app chrome around it.
  3. Check the IMO album appeared. Open your gallery app, go to Albums, and look for "IMO". If it is there, the save works — and this is also the check that proves the default run will see the photos, because the gallery and SnapDisk read the same media index. If the album is not there, work through the check order in the section above: Pictures, then a file-manager search for the photo by name or date. If the files are in a folder but not in the gallery, media-scanner lag is the suspect: restart the phone to force a rescan. If the IMO folder does not exist at all, the permissions cause is the suspect: allow storage permission and save one photo again, watching for the confirmation that it saved.
  4. Plug in the drive and run SnapDisk. Plug the USB drive into the phone. SnapDisk offers the backup in a notification — tap Backup — or open the app and tap Start. The run copies oldest-first, verifies every file by size after copying, and shows progress with Pause and Stop in the notification. The free run covers up to 5 GB, and IMO photos are small, so one run is almost always enough for the IMO half — though a whole-library run copies everything else too, and that is the point: the saved IMO photos come along with the rest, with no folder to pick and no step to remember. Screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified — the repo holds no wake lock and no battery-saver handling — so keep the phone awake for the run or check it.
  5. Do not plan around the photos you never saved. A photo you only viewed in the chat is still inside IMO, and no step in this article changes that: the switch covers only what arrives after you turn it on, there is no save-all, and there is no export. If a photo matters, save it from the chat now, while the chat is still on this phone and the account is still signed in — logging out, deleting the app, or signing in on another device can delete the in-chat photos, and imo Cloud is a sync, not a backup you can pull single photos out of onto a drive. A screenshot is the only copy left for a photo the built-in save will not give you — and a screenshot is a picture of the screen, not the photo: it is lower quality, it carries the app chrome around it, and it lands in the Screenshots folder, not with the saved photos. Do not plan a backup around saving what IMO will not let you save.

Two honest limits. First, the switch is not retroactive in the other direction either: turning it on today does not put the photos from a chat you already deleted onto the phone — a deleted chat is gone, and the cloud copy is a sync of the account, not a drawer you can open. That is step 2's job, and it is a manual job: one tap per photo, or the bulk route in Method 3 for the ones you are willing to take to a computer. Second, the 5 GB free cap is per run — an IMO album will never reach it, but a whole-library default run might, and running it again picks up where it stopped, because files already copied are never copied twice. Third, SnapDisk copies, it never deletes: deleting the photos from the gallery after the check is always your step, never the app's — and deleting them from the chat is IMO's, not SnapDisk's, and it does not free the drive copy. The drive copy is the backup; the gallery copy is the original until you decide otherwise.

Copying only some IMO photos? If you would rather copy just a few photos than the whole album, the From folder picker still takes a folder, not single files — so copy the ones you want into their own folder first (in the file manager, or by saving them to the gallery and selecting them there), then point the picker at that folder. The picker route keeps only recognised photo and video extensions. If the drive is unplugged mid-copy, the backup pauses with the full error shown and resumes where it stopped when the drive is back. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device.

And the check that matters for this article: after the run, compare the count against the album. A green backup that copied the whole library has still copied zero IMO photos if nothing was ever saved from the chat — which is exactly what happens when the photos were only viewed. Open the drive and look for the IMO folder by name: the default run mirrors the source tree, so the album should be there as its own folder. If it is missing, the save is the suspect, not the run — turn on Auto-Save, save the photos from the chat, then run again. SnapDisk copies, it never deletes — the only files it ever deletes are its own half-finished .part files — so deleting the originals after the check is always your step, never the app's.

Method 2: Point the From picker at the IMO folder

If you would rather copy just the IMO photos and nothing else — and the photos are already saved — point SnapDisk's From folder picker at the folder that holds them. Open SnapDisk, tap the From folder picker, and navigate to the folder you found them in, using the check order in the folder section above: the gallery's IMO album first, then Pictures, then the folder your file-manager search found — and tap Backup. To get everything in Pictures at once, pick the parent Pictures folder instead: SnapDisk walks the folder recursively, so one run copies the IMO folder and any other album that lives under Pictures together. The same 5 GB free cap applies, and the same oldest-first order, size verification, Pause and Stop.

The honest limit on this route is that it is the code-supported route, not the test-proven one — and this article says so rather than claiming a test that never ran — and the folder itself is the second honest limit. The picker is the system's folder picker, and the code has no IMO path handling anywhere — zero matches for "imo" in the whole source tree — so the navigation is yours, step by step, through folders whose names are ordinary. But the exact folder name is not pinned down by any public source, so this article does not state one folder path as the path, and no SnapDisk test has picked this exact folder yet. Neither candidate name starts with a dot, so neither is hidden from the picker the way a dot-folder would be, and the folder sits in shared storage, not under Android/data, so the scoped-storage wall that blocks the Telegram folder does not apply here at all. And the folder walk recurses into every subfolder it can list, so picking the parent Pictures folder reaches every album under it. But the end-to-end pick is untested, and this article marks it as such. If the picker will not show the folder on your phone, fall back to Method 1: turn on Auto-Save, save the photos to the gallery first, then run the default backup, which is the route that is proven to work. And one more scope fact for this route: the folder walk keeps only recognised photo and video extensions — a document in the folder is simply skipped, and a chat is never copied, because a chat is not a photo or a video. For a whole chat, use Method 3 — this article is only about the photo and video files.

One IMO-specific reason Method 2 matters more here than in the other messaging-app articles: the folder is unknown, and the picker is the tool that does not need to know it. The default run reads the media index, and it covers a saved IMO photo only if the scanner indexed it — which it does when the photo shows in the gallery, and the gallery album is the evidence. But if the saved photos sit in a folder the scanner has not picked up yet, the default run cannot see them, even though the files are on the disk. The From picker route does not read the media index at all: it walks the folder tree directly and lists the files it finds, so a photo the gallery has not picked up yet is still a file the picker can copy. That makes the picker the fallback when the gallery will not show the IMO album yet — pick the folder and copy it directly, then restart the phone so the default run sees the photos next time. This is a code-grounded difference, not a guess: the picker path lists files from the document tree, while the default path queries MediaStore — and the media scanner is the only thing between the folder and the index. The pick itself is still untested end to end on a real phone, and this article marks it as such — but the reason it should work where the default run cannot is the code's own two paths, stated as such.

Method 3: imo Cloud and the PC route — and why neither is a phone-to-USB job

IMO has a cloud, and the cloud is worth naming honestly, because it is the first thing a search offers when the phone route looks hard — and it is not this job. IMO syncs your message history and files to imo Cloud to free up your phone storage, in its own listing's words. That is a sync, not a backup to a drive: the files live on IMO's servers, reachable when you sign in, and freeing the phone's storage is the point — the phone copy is the one that gets deleted. A photo that lives only in imo Cloud has no file on the phone for SnapDisk to copy, and the way to get it back onto the phone is the save in Method 1: open it in the chat, press and hold, save it, and only then is there a gallery file to back up. The cloud does not put photos on a USB drive, and it does not put them in a folder a picker can reach. Use it for what it is: the way to keep a chat alive across phones and to free storage when the phone is full. For plain photo files on a drive you can open anywhere, save the photos to the gallery first — Method 1 — and copy them to a USB drive with SnapDisk, no computer.

The PC route is the same shape with a different middleman, and it is worth naming for the same reason: it is the route the generic guides offer when the phone route looks hard, and it is not this job either. Connect the phone to a computer, copy the saved photos from the phone's storage to the computer, plug the drive into the computer, and copy them again onto the drive. That works — the saved photos are ordinary files, and a computer copies ordinary files — but it is two copies through a machine you do not need, and it still starts with the save: a photo that was never saved from the chat is not in any folder on the phone, so the computer has nothing to copy either. The PC route changes nothing about the first half of the job; it only changes the second half from one tap on the phone into two copies on a desk. If you are already at a computer and the photos are already saved, the file-manager copy on the computer is the certain route for the stragglers — the ones the default run did not carry — but for the job as a whole, the phone route in Method 1 is shorter, and this article is about the phone route. And the same permission rule applies to both of them: only save photos you have permission to keep, and do not use a backup tool to pull someone else's photos out of a chat you were never meant to keep.

Can SnapDisk read the IMO folder directly?

This is the question the whole article turns on, and it deserves a straight answer in three parts — what the code proves it can do, what is untested, and what would prove the rest. The first part is proven, from the code at the current base and from how Android's media index works — and it is the same shape as the Messenger and Viber cases, with one IMO-specific condition. SnapDisk's default run reads the phone's media index — it queries the MediaStore images and videos tables, oldest-first by date taken, with no folder filter. An IMO photo saved to the gallery is an ordinary indexed file: it shows in the gallery as an "IMO" album, and gallery apps read the media index. The album's existence in the gallery is the evidence the folder is indexed. The query is in PhotoQuery.kt, the no-folder-filter shape is the code's own design — the query call passes no selection at all — and the gallery evidence is the platform fact that closes the loop. So the default run sees saved IMO photos that show in the gallery, and it sees them with no step beyond the save. That is not an assumption: it is the code's query shape plus the gallery evidence, stated as such. And the default run copies the whole library, not DCIM only — the query reads every indexed image and video on the phone, whatever folder it sits in, so the IMO album is covered the same way DCIM is.

The condition is the folder itself, and it is the part that makes IMO different from Kik in this series: the default run's coverage is conditional on the photos showing in the gallery, and the folder they sit in is not pinned down by any public source. If the saved photos sit in a folder the media scanner has not picked up yet, the photos are on the disk but not in the media index, and the default run cannot see them — not because the code is wrong, but because the index it reads has nothing to return. The fix is the ordinary one: restart the phone to force a rescan, and check the gallery again. Until that is done, the default run copies zero IMO photos while the files sit in the folder. This article states the condition in the TL;DR, in the folder section, in Method 1 step 3, and here — because a guide that promised the default run covers every saved IMO photo, without the condition, would be wrong on exactly the phones where the save looks broken.

The second part is untested, and this article marks it as such rather than guessing in either direction: whether SnapDisk's From folder picker reaches the IMO folder end to end on a real phone. The picker is the system's folder picker — ActivityResultContracts.OpenDocumentTree, in MainActivity.kt — and it opens with no folder pre-selected: there is no initial-folder hint anywhere in the code, and no IMO path handling anywhere in the app — zero matches for "imo" in the whole source tree. The navigation chain is ordinary folders — the gallery album, Pictures, or the folder your file-manager search found — none of them dot-prefixed, so none of them is hidden from the picker the way a dot-folder would be, and the folder sits in shared storage, outside Android/data, so the scoped-storage wall that blocks the Telegram folder does not apply here. And the folder walk recurses into every subfolder it can list, so picking the parent Pictures folder should reach every album under it. But the exact folder name is not pinned down by any public source, and no SnapDisk test has picked this exact folder yet, so the end-to-end pick is untested — do not plan a backup around it as the only route, and do not assume it fails either. The joined workflow in Method 1 is the route that is proven to work, and it is the route this article gives first. The picker has one code-grounded advantage the default run does not, stated in Method 2: it walks the folder tree directly instead of reading the media index, so a photo the scanner has not picked up yet is still a file it can copy — but that advantage is itself untested end to end, and this article marks it as such too.

The third part is what the code does prove about the rest of the job, so the limits above are not mistaken for a wider doubt. The manifest requests the media-read permissions (READ_MEDIA_IMAGES and READ_MEDIA_VIDEO, plus the older READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE capped at Android 12), the notification permission, and foreground-service permissions — and no INTERNET permission at all, zero occurrences, so the photos physically cannot leave the device. It does not request all-files access — no MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE anywhere — which is the permission a file manager uses to browse another app's folders freely, and the absence is the reason SnapDisk cannot simply walk into the IMO folder the way a file manager can; the folder picker is the route instead, and it needs your tap. The backup itself is the verified core: oldest-first by date taken with a date-modified fallback (PhotoQuery.kt), every file size-verified after copying (BackupEngine.kt), Pause and Stop in the notification (NotificationHelper.kt, BackupService.kt), files of 4 GB or more on a FAT32 drive skipped with their reason instead of stalling the job (BackupService.kt), and the free run capped at 5 GB with Pro a one-time $9.99 purchase (ProPrefs.kt). None of that changes the picker verdict — it bounds it. The backup engine is proven; the end-to-end pick of this exact folder is not yet tested.

Photos you sent vs photos you received

The route is the same for both, but the starting point is not, and it is worth saying which one you are saving before you start. Photos you received — the case this article is really about — are the ones the switch governs: with Auto-Save on they land in the gallery by themselves, and without it they are not on the phone at all, and the methods above are written for them. Photos you sent are different, because you usually have a better original: a photo you sent on IMO is almost certainly still in your camera roll, in DCIM/Camera, at full quality, as an ordinary indexed file that SnapDisk's default run already covers — and the default run copies the whole library, not DCIM only, so it is covered either way. Back that up instead. The copy in the chat is a version IMO made for sending, and the original on your camera roll is the one worth keeping. That matters more than it sounds, because the photos you send are often the ones you care about most: a picture of the finished work for a client, a photo of the goods before dispatch, the shot you took to show someone what something looks like. The original is the one they will ask for again. The IMO route for a photo you sent is only needed when the original is gone — you deleted it from the camera roll, or it was a photo you took inside IMO's camera and never saved — and then the chat copy is the only one left: save it from the chat the same way as a received photo, and the default run picks it up like any other indexed photo, or point the From picker at the folder and copy it directly.

One related corner is worth a line, because IMO users hit it and it looks like a shortcut that is not one: IMO keeps its working data under Android/data, and a guide may tell you to dig in Android/data for a cached photo. That is a different thing from saved photos — the cached copies are fragments keyed to the installation, not your photos in any usable sense — and Android/data is guarded by scoped storage, so a file manager may not even show it. Do not go digging in Android/data for your IMO photos: they are not there. The photos are in the gallery once saved, or nowhere at all when they are not, and those are the only two places this article's methods touch. A photo that lives only in imo Cloud is not in Android/data either — it is on IMO's servers, and the save is the step that brings it onto the phone as a file.

Checking the drive after the copy

The check has two halves, and they have to happen in order: first that the save actually produced a file in the gallery, then that the drive holds it. After the SnapDisk run finishes, open the drive on a computer or on the phone itself and count: the drive should hold the IMO photos you saved, in an IMO folder of their own — the default run mirrors the source tree, so the album should be there by name — and a few opened at random should render fully, videos included. Open an IMO photo itself — not just any photo on the drive — because a run that covered the rest of the library would still look complete on a random check while the one album you cared about was missing. If the count is short, the run is not the first suspect — the save is. Check the gallery: is the "IMO" album actually there, and does it hold the photos you expected? If the album is empty, the photos were never saved — turn on Auto-Save, save the ones that matter from the chat, and run again. If the album holds them and the drive does not, the run is the suspect, and the run needs repeating, not a different folder. If the files are in a folder but not in the gallery, media-scanner lag is the suspect: restart the phone to force a rescan, and run again — or pick the folder directly with the From picker, which does not depend on the gallery.

If the drive's free space ran out mid-run, the run stops with the full error shown; free space by moving the drive's contents to a bigger drive first, or back up to a second drive. If the run stopped at the 5 GB free cap — unlikely for an IMO album alone, possible on a whole-library default run — run it again and it picks up where it stopped, because files already copied are never copied twice. And the check that matters most for a photo that only existed inside IMO: confirm the drive copy before you delete anything from the phone. The copy in the gallery is an ordinary file and will survive; the copy in the chat depends on IMO keeping it, and a logged-out account, a deleted app, or a new phone ends that. The drive is a plain folder of plain files. Anyone who picks up the drive can open them. That is the price of a copy you can open anywhere, and it is worth stating before the photos leave the phone.

What to avoid

  • Assuming IMO saves the photos you receive. It does not — not until you turn on Auto-Save After Receive in Settings, Chats, for both Photos and Videos. The toggles ship off. A one-tap backup copies zero IMO photos until the switch is on or the photos are saved from the chat first.
  • Assuming the switch reaches backwards. It does not — Auto-Save covers only what you receive after you turn it on, never the photos already sitting in your chats. Those still need the one-by-one save: open the photo, press and hold, save it.
  • Assuming the save covers a whole chat. It does not — the built-in save is one photo at a time, and there is no save-all button. A chat with a hundred photos is a long job of one tap each; the switch is the bulk route, and it only covers the future.
  • Assuming a deleted chat can be recovered. It cannot — imo Cloud is a sync of the account, not a backup you can pull single photos out of onto a drive. Save the photos that matter while the chat is still on this phone.
  • Assuming logging out or deleting the app is safe. It is not — the in-chat photos depend on the account staying on the phone. Save first, copy second, and only then change anything about the account.
  • Assuming one folder path for saved IMO photos. UNVERIFIED — no public source names the on-disk folder for saved IMO photos. Check the gallery first, then Pictures, then search the file manager — do not plan a backup around a single path no source agrees on.
  • Assuming a cloud-only photo is on the phone. It is not — IMO syncs history and files to imo Cloud to free phone storage, and a photo freed from the phone lives in the cloud until you download it. Open it in the chat and save it by pressing and holding, then look in the gallery.
  • Assuming a Privacy Chat photo can be saved. It may not be — IMO lets Privacy Chat block downloads, along with screenshot, copy, and share. No setting of yours overrides the other person's privacy setting; a screenshot is the only copy left, and it is a picture of the screen, not the photo.
  • Assuming the default SnapDisk run covers photos you only viewed. It cannot — proven from the code and the platform: the default run reads the media index, and a photo that was never saved never enters the index. A green backup copies zero IMO photos from chats you never saved from.
  • Assuming the default run covers saved photos the scanner has not picked up yet. It cannot — the default run reads the index, and a file the scanner has not indexed is not in it. Restart the phone to force a rescan, or use the From picker, which walks the folder directly.
  • Assuming the From picker route is test-proven. Untested — no SnapDisk test has picked the IMO folder yet, and the exact folder name is not pinned down by any public source. The code supports it and the folder names are ordinary, but do not plan a backup around it as the only route; Method 1's switch-and-save workflow is the proven one.
  • Assuming the chat copy is the original. A photo you sent is usually still in DCIM/Camera at full quality. The chat copy is the version IMO delivered; back the original up, and use the chat copy only when the original is gone.
  • Looking in Android/data for your IMO photos. That folder holds IMO's working data — cached fragments, not your photos — and scoped storage guards it. The photos are in the gallery once saved, or nowhere at all when they are not.
  • Using imo Cloud and thinking the USB job is done. The cloud keeps the photos on IMO's servers, not on a drive — the files live in the sync and still need the save to reach the phone and the copy to reach a USB drive. The USB leg is still SnapDisk's job — the cloud replaces nothing in this article.
  • Using a screenshot and thinking it is the photo. It is not — a screenshot is a picture of the screen, lower quality, carrying the app chrome around it, and it lands in the Screenshots folder, not with the saved photos. Save the photo itself when the save works, and screenshot only when it does not.
  • Assuming screen-off continuation under battery saver. UNVERIFIED — the repo holds no wake lock and no battery-saver handling. Keep the phone awake for the run, or check it.
  • Using a FAT32 drive for a library with long videos. FAT32 cannot hold a single file over 4 GB, and a library with long 4K clips will hold one. SnapDisk skips those files and lists them — use exFAT.
  • Assuming the free 5 GB cap covers the whole library. It is per run. A library over 5 GB needs several free runs — run it again and it picks up where it stopped — or one $9.99 Pro purchase.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this. An unplug mid-copy leaves a half-copied library that looks finished until you count it.
  • Checking the drive by opening any photo. Open an IMO photo itself — not just any photo on the drive. A random check passes even when the one album you cared about was never copied, because the photos that did copy render fine.

Rule of thumb: Switch it on, save what is already there, then back it up — IMO saves nothing until Auto-Save is on, the switch only covers the future, and the gallery is the bridge to the drive. The switch is one tap; the photos already in your chats are one tap each; the backup after that is one tap for all of them.
