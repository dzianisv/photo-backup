Method 1: SnapDisk on the five (the one-tap route)

On the five phones that clear Android 7.0 and list USB OTG, the automated route exists. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later — the F205 Pro runs 8.1, and the F6, S10, S11 and M7 run 7.0 or 7.1 — and the one-tap backup needs the phone to host a drive, which all five list. The F205 is the sixth phone that clears 7.0, but its OTG is half-stated — gadgets360 lists USB OTG, gadgetsnow and smartprix leave the line blank — so on it the drive mounting is the test:

  1. Install SnapDisk. Until the Play listing is live, install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on the six phones that clear 7.0 — the F205 Pro, F6, F205, S10, S11 and M7 — and the one-tap backup to a drive works on the five that list USB OTG. On the F205, SnapDisk installs but the one-tap route is unproven until a drive mounts. It does not install on the other eight: they run Android 6.0 or older, below the minimum, so Method 3 or 4 is the route on those.
  2. Plug in the USB drive with a micro-USB OTG adapter. All five take micro-USB, so a regular USB drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A USB-C flash drive does not plug straight into any of them.
  3. Look for the mount notification, then tap Backup in the notification. If the drive mounts, SnapDisk offers the backup when the drive connects, then copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. If nothing mounts on the F205, the phone does not host the drive — the sources disagree on it, so a no-mount settles it for your unit, and Method 3 or 4 is the route. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  4. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. Its limits: it works only on phones that run Android 7.0 and host a drive — and on the F205 the sources do not agree the phone hosts one. On the eight below Android 7.0, only Methods 3 and 4 exist.

The copies are the original files. SnapDisk streams each file byte-for-byte from the device to the drive — the code reads the file and writes the same bytes out, with no decode, no recompression, and no resize — and then checks that the copy on the drive is the same size as the original. Other apps copy originals byte-for-byte too; what SnapDisk adds is the automated whole-library run: oldest-first order, progress with Pause and Stop, resume where it stopped, and skip-if-already-copied on the next run. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device.

  2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the stock file manager on a Gionee phone, so use Files by Google or the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit.

Method 5: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

Gionee offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no Gionee-specific backup advice turned up in any source beyond a computer copy, Google Drive, or Google Photos — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead; and its "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p). 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and Google accounts only offer 5 GB of free storage by default since May 2026, with the full 15 GB available only after verifying a phone number on the account. 3. Google backup is what Android itself names: personal settings back up to Google's servers with a Google account, and are restored to a new phone when you sign in with that account. It is a real backup — but it backs up settings, not a folder of photo files on a drive you hold. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2, 3 or 4 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What to avoid

  • Buying a USB-C OTG adapter for a micro-USB Gionee phone. Twelve covered models take micro-USB — only the S6 and S6 Pro take USB Type-C — so a USB-C adapter does not fit twelve of them. Buy a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it, unless your unit is an S6 or S6 Pro.
  • Assuming the F205's OTG listing is settled. Gadgets360 lists USB OTG, but gadgetsnow and smartprix leave the line blank. Plug a drive in and look for the mount notification before counting on the one-tap route on it.
  • Assuming the S8's OTG or card slot is settled. Gadgets360 says No to both, but phonebunch and smartprix say OTG Yes and smartprix, phonearena, lowyat and naijatechguide say it takes a card. Either way the S8 runs Android 6.0 and cannot run SnapDisk, so the disputes are not a reason to buy an adapter for it — but they are a reason to check your unit's SIM tray before counting on the card-reader route.
  • Assuming the S6 Pro's OTG listing is settled. Gadgets360, smartprix and gadgetstouse say OTG Yes, but a gadgets360 user comment says "there no OTG". Either way the S6 Pro runs Android 6.0 and cannot run SnapDisk, so the dispute is not a reason to buy an adapter for it.
  • Assuming the M6's OTG listing is settled. Gadgets360 lists USB OTG No, ubergizmo lists USB OTG. Either way the M6 runs Android 6.0 and cannot run SnapDisk, so the dispute is not a reason to buy an adapter for it.
  • Assuming the S11's 3600 mAh battery or 16 MP + 8 MP rear camera is the retail S11. Gadgetsnow, cashify and 91mobiles list 3600 mAh and 16 MP + 8 MP rear, but smartprix and umatechnology list 3410 mAh, and umatechnology's retail S11 listing says 16 MP + 5 MP rear — the 3600 mAh and 16 MP + 8 MP figures belong to the bigger S11S. Either way it runs Android 7.1.1 and lists OTG Yes, so the mix-up changes nothing about the backup.
  • Assuming the S10's 6 GB RAM or 256 GB ceiling is settled. Smartprix, androidheadlines and kenyaprice say 6 GB and up to 256 GB, phonesdata says 4 GB RAM with a 256 GB ceiling, and androidheadlines says up to 128 GB. Either way it runs Android 7.0 and lists OTG Yes, so the disputes change nothing about the backup.
  • Assuming the M5's 128 GB ceiling is settled. Gadgets360 says up to 128 GB, gsmarena.com.bd says up to 256 GB, phonebunch and mobilegizmo say up to 64 GB. Check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 64 GB for that model.
  • Assuming VaultDrive or FotoSafe runs on an Android 7.0 or 7.1 Gionee. VaultDrive needs Android 8 or later per its own site, and FotoSafe needs Android 9 — so neither runs on the F6, F205, S10, S11 or M7, and FotoSafe runs on none of the fourteen, since the newest covered model runs Android 8.1.
  • Mistaking a spec sheet or a product page for a phone-to-USB guide. The gadgets360 and smartprix listings are real spec pages — but they only list an Android version, a processor, a camera, or a USB port type. None of them describes copying your camera roll straight to a USB drive from the device.
  • Mistaking a drive-locked app for a general backup. Photo Backup Stick DC is a real one-tap backup app — but it only works with genuine Photo Backup Stick OTG drives. If the drive you already own is any other brand, that app cannot use it: SnapDisk, Photos to USB, and FotoSafe work with any drive.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the device and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026 — and on the eight models below Android 7.0, the two that list OTG No, and the F205 where the drive mounting is the test, that route may not exist, so the honest answer is that a computer may be needed.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On a Gionee phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the device — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. The file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the device. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip. Automate the copy, check it on the drive, then delete from the phone.
