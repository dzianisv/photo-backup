Method 1: SnapDisk — proven on twelve

Method 1 is the one-tap version. It uses SnapDisk, the free backup app this site is about — SnapDisk. Free version: manual backup, up to 5 GB per run. Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase and adds auto-backup on connect plus unlimited size. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, which twenty-six covered Homtom phones clear — the lowest clears it exactly, at 7.0 — so the app installs on twenty-six, and the one-tap backup is proven on twelve: each one lists USB OTG Yes.

  1. Check the Android version first. Settings > About phone > Android version. The HT3 Pro, HT6, HT7, HT7 Pro, HT8, HT10, HT16, HT16 Pro, HT17, HT17 Pro, HT20, HT20 Pro, HT27, HT37, Zoji Z6 and Zoji Z7 run below 7.0 — SnapDisk will not install on those sixteen, so stop here and use Method 3 or Method 4 instead. The HT37 runs 6.0 shipped with a 7.0 upgrade reported, but it lists USB OTG No, so even upgraded there is no one-tap route on it. The HT16S, HT26, HT37 Pro, HT50, HT70, HT80, S7, S8, S9 Plus, S16, S17, S99, S99i, C1, C2, C8, C13, H1, H3, H5, H10, P30 Pro, Zoji Z8, Zoji Z9, Zoji Z11 and Zoji Z33 all run 7.0 or newer — the HT16S, HT26, HT37 Pro, HT50, HT70, S7, S8, S9 Plus, S16 and Zoji Z8 run 7.0 or 7.1; the HT80 runs 7.0 or 10 depending on the listing; the S17, S99i, C1, C13, H1, H3, H5 and Zoji Z11 run 8.1; the S99 runs 8.0; the C2 runs 8.1 or 8.0; the C8 runs 8.0 or 8.1; the H10 runs 8.0 or 8.1; the Zoji Z9 runs 8.0 or 8.1; the Zoji Z33 runs 8.0 or 8.1; and the P30 Pro runs 9.0. On the phone, allow installing from unknown sources when asked. It needs Android 7.0 or later, which twenty-six covered Homtom phones clear — the lowest clears it exactly, at 7.0 — so on a Homtom phone the APK installs on twenty-six, and the one-tap backup is proven on twelve.
  3. Plug in the USB drive. On six covered phones that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in — no adapter, no cable. On the thirty micro-USB models that is a micro-USB OTG adapter between the phone and the drive. A plain sync cable is the wrong cable: it connects a phone to a computer, not a drive to a phone.
  4. Look for the mount notification. On the twelve proven phones — the HT26, HT50, HT70, HT80, S8, S99, S99i, C2, H5, H10, Zoji Z9 and Zoji Z33 — Android notices the drive and offers it as storage, and SnapDisk picks it as the To folder. On the seven mounting-test phones — the S7, S9 Plus, C8, P30 Pro, HT16S, C1 and Zoji Z11 — if nothing appears, check Settings > Storage: if the drive is listed there, tap it and mount it, then run the backup. SnapDisk copies the photos byte-for-byte: the file on the drive is the same file the phone took, with the same pixels. There is no re-encoding step, so nothing is lost in the copy — the only place quality gets lost is a cloud backup with a "Storage saver" setting.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter

Method 2 is the manual version of the same thing — no app, just the phone's file manager and a drive. The file manager on a Homtom phone is Files, or Files by Google, per devicesfaq — so this method names it directly. It works on the twelve proven phones, and on the seven mounting-test phones if the drive shows up in Settings.

  1. Plug the drive into the phone. On six covered phones that is a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in. On the thirty micro-USB models that is a micro-USB OTG adapter between the phone and the drive.
  2. Open Files and find the DCIM folder. Your camera photos live in the DCIM folder — on most phones that is Internal storage > DCIM > Camera. Screenshots live in Pictures > Screenshots, and WhatsApp photos in Android > media > com.whatsapp > WhatsApp > Media > WhatsApp Images.
  3. Select the photos and copy them to the drive. Long-press one photo, select the rest, tap Copy, navigate to the USB drive and tap Paste. Copy, not Move — see "What to avoid" below.
  4. Wait, then check. Copying takes time — thousands of photos can take half an hour or more. Open the drive in Files and count before you delete anything.

This is the same copy Method 1 does, done by hand. It copies the original files as they are — no re-encoding, no quality loss.

Method 3: through a computer

Method 3 is the fallback — and the one the per-model backup FAQ calls the most economical method. It works on all forty-two, including the sixteen below-7.0 phones, the seven mounting-test phones if the drive never shows up on the phone, and the seven OTG-No phones. Plug the phone into a computer with the USB cable, open the phone as a drive, and drag the DCIM folder onto a USB drive. Copy, not Move, and check the copy before deleting anything. It works — it just takes the manual step Method 1 does for you.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route

Method 4 is the card route, and on Homtom it is a real one at full scale: a microSD card fits all forty-two phones for certain, so this route is proven on all forty-two — unlike Vernee, where it is proven on twenty of twenty-one, and unlike RedMagic, where it is proven on none of the fourteen.

  1. Put the card in the phone. The slot is dedicated on the HT20 Pro, the S17 and the C8, hybrid on the HT16 and the C2, and shared with the second SIM on the Zoji Z33. Slot types were not stated in the sources found for the other models. Card ceilings are contested on twenty-eight phones — so buy at or under the lower number if you want the ceiling every source agrees on. The worst are the S99 (32, 128, 256 or 500 GB) and the C8 (32, 64, 128 or 256 GB). The H1, the H3 and the H5 are three numbers each — 32, 64 and 128 GB.
  2. Set the camera to save to the card. Open the Camera app, go to Settings, and set Storage to the SD card. New photos then land on the card, not the internal storage.
  3. Move the photos already on the phone. Open Files, select the DCIM folder, and move it to the card. The photos already on the phone do not move themselves — they live in internal storage until you move them.
  4. Take the card out and read it in a card reader. Plug the reader into the computer, or into the phone with an OTG adapter, and copy the photos to a USB drive.

Two warnings: the photos already on the phone live in internal storage, not on the card — so moving the card alone backs up nothing until you move them — and the card is only a backup if the photos end up on it, so check the card before you delete anything on the phone.

Method 5: Google Photos, or the cloud

Method 5 is the cloud route — and the one Homtom's own per-model FAQ names for photos alone: Google Photos uploads them automatically when on Wi-Fi. It works on all forty-two, it is free up to the Google account's 15 GB, and it is the only method here that backs up without a cable. But it is not a USB backup: the photos live on Google's servers, not on a drive you hold, and the "Storage saver" setting compresses them — so check that setting before trusting a cloud copy. If you want the originals on a drive you own, Method 1 or Method 2 is the route.

What this does not do

  • It does not install below Android 7.0. Sixteen covered Homtom phones run below Android 7.0 — the HT3 Pro, HT6, HT7, HT7 Pro, HT8, HT10, HT16, HT16 Pro, HT17, HT17 Pro, HT20, HT20 Pro, HT27, HT37, Zoji Z6 and Zoji Z7 — so on those sixteen there is no SnapDisk route at all, and no rival-app route either: VaultDrive needs Android 8 and FotoSafe needs Android 9, so on a below-7.0 phone the only routes are Method 3 and Method 4.
  • It does not work if the phone does not host a drive. The seven mounting-test phones — the S7, S9 Plus, C8, P30 Pro, HT16S, C1 and Zoji Z11 — may or may not mount a drive, because no source settles OTG on them. If the drive never shows up on one of those seven, Method 3 is the route. The seven OTG-No phones — the HT37 Pro, S16, S17, C13, H1, H3 and Zoji Z8 — will not mount a drive at all, so Method 1 and Method 2 are closed on those seven even though SnapDisk installs.
  • It does not delete photos. A backup copies the photos to the drive; deleting the originals is a separate step, and only after you have checked the copy.
  • It does not back up your settings, contacts or messages. It backs up photos and videos — the files in your camera roll.
  • It does not back up photos from apps that hide them. Photos saved inside an app's private storage — WhatsApp's hidden media, or a locked album — may not be in the DCIM folder, so check the app's own export.
  • It does not compress or shrink your photos. The copy is the original file — the same pixels, the same size.

What to avoid

  • Don't move instead of copying. Move deletes the original as it goes — if the copy fails halfway, the photos are gone. Copy first, check the copy, then delete.
  • Don't unplug the drive until the copy is done. Unplugging mid-copy corrupts the files being written. Wait for the progress to finish, then eject the drive in Settings before pulling it out.
  • Don't buy a micro-USB adapter for a Type-C phone. Six covered phones take USB Type-C — the HT70, HT80, H10, P30 Pro, Zoji Z9 and Zoji Z11 — a USB-C flash drive plugs straight in, and the adapter is the wrong buy.
  • Don't buy a USB-C drive expecting it to plug into one of the thirty micro-USB phones. Those thirty are micro-USB, and a USB-C drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter between them.
  • Don't buy a card bigger than the contested ceiling without checking. Twenty-eight phones have two, three or four card ceilings — so buy at or under the lower number if you want the ceiling every source agrees on.
  • Don't buy a card bigger than 32 GB for the S99 or the C8 without checking. The S99's ceiling is four numbers (32, 128, 256 and 500 GB) and the C8's is four numbers (32, 64, 128 and 256 GB) — 32 GB is the only number every source agrees on for both.
  • Don't count on VaultDrive's AppBrain floor on any Homtom phone. Its own site says Android 8, which sixteen clear — but AppBrain lists Android 12.1, which none of the forty-two clears, since the highest shipped version is 10 on the HT80 listings and 9.0 on the P30 Pro.
  • Don't count on FotoSafe on shipped software on any Homtom phone. It needs Android 9 — the HT80 clears only on the 10 listings, and the P30 Pro is 9.0 with OTG contested.
  • Don't trust a single HT80 listing. Its Android version, its card ceiling and its chipset are all two numbers — check the unit, not the listing.
  • Don't trust a single S99 or C8 listing either. The S99's card ceiling is four numbers and the C8's is four numbers, with one source contradicting itself on the C8.
  • Don't confuse the HT10 with the H10. The HT10 is a 2016 Helio X20 phone on Android 6.0; the H10 is a 2018 MT6750T phone on Android 8.0 or 8.1. Only the H10 clears the floor.
  • Don't let one folder hold every photo. A backup is only a backup if the copy exists somewhere else — a drive, a card, or the cloud. One copy is not a backup.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a copy. A backup finds your photos, copies them all, and tells you when it's done. On a Homtom phone the honest position is simple: forty-two phones, twenty-six clear Android 7.0, twelve list USB OTG Yes, forty-two take a microSD card — the one-tap backup is proven on twelve, and the card-reader route is proven on all forty-two.
