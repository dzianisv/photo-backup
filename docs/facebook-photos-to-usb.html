Back up Facebook photos and videos to USB from Android

Your Facebook photos are files on your phone — in the Downloads folder or a Facebook folder, which you can point a backup app at. Plug a USB drive into the phone and copy them straight to it, no computer, no upload.

November 22, 2026 · 6 min read

TL;DR: Plug a USB drive into your Android phone, point SnapDisk's From folder at the folder where your Facebook photos landed, and tap Backup: your Facebook photos and videos copy oldest-first to the drive, with no computer and no upload in between. The honest limits, up front: SnapDisk copies media files only — the photos and videos in the folder you pick. It does not back up your Facebook account itself, your messages, comments, or the posts you only saved inside the app (Facebook's Save is a bookmark, not a file — it stays inside Facebook and vanishes if the post is deleted). And the folder is the whole problem with Facebook, unlike Instagram: there is no single Facebook folder. The guides disagree — Downloads, Pictures/Facebook, or DCIM/Facebook, depending on your phone and Facebook version — so this article tells you to check all three in the Files app first and back up whichever ones actually hold your photos, instead of naming one folder and being wrong on your phone. SnapDisk's default run copies DCIM only, recursively, so files in DCIM/Facebook are covered by the default run — but files in Downloads and Pictures/Facebook are outside DCIM and are missed entirely, so the Facebook route is the From-picker folder route, not the default run, and this article says so in the steps, not in a footnote. Your whole account at once is a different job: Meta's official export sends you a zip of everything, but the zip lands on your phone and must be unzipped before anything can copy to USB — that two-step route is Method 2 below. There is no bulk on-device Facebook-to-USB export: nobody offers one, and this article does not pretend SnapDisk is one. SnapDisk copies, it never deletes — deleting from the phone afterwards is your step, only after verifying a few photos on the drive. Free version: 5 GB per run; run it again past the cap and it picks up where it stopped, because files already copied are never copied twice. Screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified — keep the phone awake for the run. As of November 22, 2026, no top search result offers phone-to-USB Facebook backup in one tap: the top results download Facebook media to the phone only, export it as a zip that still needs a copy step, or copy by hand in a file manager without naming any Facebook folder.

What the search results actually offer

Search for this job and the results split into four shapes, none of them the one-tap phone-to-USB copy. The first shape is the generic OTG guide: itechguides' transfer-photos-to-a-memory-stick guide, the two YouTube OTG walkthroughs, mefmobile's transfer-without-a-computer guide, the EyeFi forum thread, and Wondershare MobileTrans' phone-to-USB guide all copy by hand in the Files app — select in DCIM, Copy to, paste on the drive — and Facebook is never named, no Facebook folder path is given, and the copy is manual select-copy-paste with no progress, no resume, and no verification. The second shape is the Facebook downloader app: All Media Downloader and Bulk Photos Videos Downloader on Google Play download Facebook photos, videos, Reels, and whole albums — Bulk Photos Videos Downloader promises "entire media albums from Facebook directly to your Android device" — and the Dailyhunt roundup of the top six FB downloader apps (FvidGo, FastVid, Snaptube, VidMate, and two more) does the same: copy a link, paste it, the file lands on the device. Every one of them stops at the device. The file is on the phone; the USB step is missing, and none of them names a USB drive at all. The third shape is Meta's official export, covered by solveyourtech, androidgreek, and mokubetech: Accounts Center → Your information and permissions → Export your information, choose "Export to device", wait for the email, and download a zip within four days. That is the only bulk route Facebook itself offers — but the zip lands on the phone, and the guides stop there: the zip still has to be unzipped and copied to a drive by hand. The fourth shape is the one-tap whole-library app: Photos to USB: OTG Backup copies the whole gallery in one tap — "Back up every photo and video on your phone to a USB pen drive, with one tap", "from your camera, WhatsApp and every other folder" — and MyBackup backs up to an OTG USB card too. But neither names Facebook or any Facebook folder, so neither tells you which folder holds your Facebook photos or that the folder varies by phone. VaultDrive, the rival this site compares against, also does phone-to-USB with no computer and lets you pick files and folders — and its page, read in full this run, never mentions Facebook either, so there is no Facebook-specific guide there. Two more guides do the first half only: umatechnology's and HowToGeek's “How to Keep a Local Copy of Facebook Photos on Your Phone” both get Facebook photos onto the phone — download each photo, or bulk-export with Download Your Information — and stop there, with no USB step. That is the gap: the folders are named in download-to-phone guides that disagree with each other, the USB copy is done by hand in generic guides, the downloader apps stop at the device, and nobody puts the two together in one tap.

Where Facebook keeps your photos and videos

Facebook saves what it saves on your phone as ordinary files — but, unlike Instagram, in no single folder you can count on. The guides disagree with each other, and the honest thing is to say so rather than pick one and be wrong on your phone:

  • Downloads — where tecnobits, Mobikin, and GBTimes all say Facebook's "Save to phone" puts the photo: "The photo will automatically be saved to the downloads folder on your Android device" (tecnobits), "Select Save to phone (or Download). The photo will appear in your phone's gallery under Facebook or Downloads" (Mobikin), "Facebook downloads typically land in your device's Downloads folder" (GBTimes).
  • Pictures/Facebook — a Facebook folder inside Pictures, per eathealthy365 ("Facebook: Creates a 'Facebook' folder inside your main 'Pictures' or 'DCIM' folder") and solvemix ("Android phones often use Pictures or Downloads"). Your gallery shows saved Facebook photos in a Facebook album whatever the folder underneath is.
  • DCIM/Facebook — Internal storage/DCIM/Facebook, per guiaalfa ("Facebook: Downloads in Internal storage/DCIM/Facebook"). This is the one folder the default run already covers — see below.

What is not in any of those folders is just as important. Facebook's Save (the bookmark) saves nothing to the phone: tapping Save on a post adds it to your Saved items inside Facebook, and if the post is deleted it vanishes from that list too — a bookmark is not a file, and it cannot be copied to a drive. What does put a file on the phone is Save to phone (also labelled Download on some versions): open the photo full-screen, tap the three-dot menu, and choose Save to phone — that is the download, and it lands in one of the three folders above. Videos and Reels work the same way through the downloader apps: copy the post link, paste it into the downloader, and the MP4 lands on the device — FvidGo and the other tools in the Dailyhunt roundup save to your Android's default download location, which is the Downloads folder unless you changed it. And a photo you only viewed is not on the phone at all: Facebook shows you a copy from its servers, and only a deliberate Save to phone, a downloader, or an export puts a file in a folder above. Check all three folders in the Files app first — open Files, look in Downloads, in Pictures for a Facebook folder, and in DCIM for a Facebook folder, and note which ones actually hold photos. If none of the three exists on your phone, nothing has been saved there yet, and the fix is Save to phone on the photos you want to keep, not a different backup app.

What you need

An Android phone (Android 7.0 or later for SnapDisk); a USB drive your phone can read — a USB-C flash drive, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter (most modern Android phones support OTG; if the phone doesn't see the drive, try a different adapter); and enough space on the drive for the Facebook photos and videos you want to keep. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 can't hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit. And one check before you start: open the Files app, look at the sizes of the three folders above — Downloads, Pictures/Facebook, DCIM/Facebook — and divide by 5 GB — that is how many free runs the copy needs, and the arithmetic belongs before you plug the drive in, not halfway through the copy.

Method 1: SnapDisk (one tap, background copy)

SnapDisk is a free Android app that does exactly this job and nothing else. Install it from the v0.1.2 APK (it's not on Google Play yet), then:

  1. Check the three folders first — do not guess. Open the Files app and look in Downloads, in Pictures for a Facebook folder, and in DCIM for a Facebook folder. Note which ones hold your Facebook photos. The guides disagree about where they land, and your phone decides — the copy below runs once per folder that has photos in it.
  2. Open SnapDisk and set the From folder — do not run the default blind. The app opens to a settings screen with a From folder and a To folder, each with a folder picker. The default From folder is /sdcard/DCIM — the camera folder — and the copy walks it recursively, so files in DCIM/Facebook are covered by a default run. But your Facebook photos in Downloads and Pictures/Facebook are outside DCIM, so a default run misses them entirely. Tap the From folder picker and navigate to the first folder that holds Facebook photos — Internal storage > Download, or Internal storage > Pictures > Facebook — and tap it.
  3. Run each Facebook folder in turn. After the first run finishes, pick the next folder that holds Facebook photos as the From folder and run it again. Files already copied are never copied twice, so the runs do not duplicate anything — and if your Downloads folder holds other files too, the image and video files copy and the rest are simply skipped.
  4. Set the To folder to the USB drive. Plug in the USB drive, tap the To folder picker, and choose a folder on the drive. The copy mirrors the source folder structure, so your Facebook folder stays a Facebook folder on the drive.
  5. Plug in the USB drive. SnapDisk offers a backup in a notification: "External storage is connected. Backup photos?"
  6. Tap Backup. Photos and videos copy oldest-first — your earliest Facebook photos first — in the background, while you use the phone normally.
  7. Watch the progress notification — real percent, copied / total size, speed, and time left — with Pause and Stop. You can pause and resume any time.
  8. Wait for "Backup complete." Every file is size-verified after copying, so the counts are proof, not a promise. Files that already copied are never copied twice — so if the free 5 GB per-run limit stops a big Facebook folder partway, just run it again and it picks up where it left off.

If you unplug the drive mid-copy, the backup pauses with the full error shown and resumes where it stopped when the drive is back. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. Method 2: the Meta export — your whole account as a zip

Method 1 copies the Facebook media that is already on your phone as files. If you want everything you ever posted — including the originals of photos you never saved to the phone — the only bulk route Facebook itself offers is Meta's official export, and it is a two-step job: the export gets the files onto your phone, and then they still have to be copied to the drive. Here is the whole route:

  1. Request the export. In Facebook, open your profile, tap the menu, tap Settings & privacy, then Settings, then Meta Account (some accounts still show Accounts Center), then Your information and permissions, then Export your information. Tap Create export, pick your Facebook profile, and choose Export to device — not a transfer to Google Drive or Dropbox, which sends your archive to a cloud account instead of your phone. Pick what goes in it (Photos and Videos covers your uploaded photos and videos; Posts covers your timeline posts), all time, and high media quality, then start the export and enter your password when asked.
  2. Wait for the email. Meta prepares the archive and emails you when it is ready. Most arrive within hours to a few days. The download link in that email is valid for four days — download the zip as soon as the email arrives, or request it again.
  3. Download and unzip the zip on your phone. The zip downloads to your phone — usually the Downloads folder. Open it in the Files app and extract it: the photos and videos inside are ordinary files in folders (photos_and_videos, with your_photos and your_videos inside), and only now are they files SnapDisk can copy. The zip itself is not an image or video file, so SnapDisk would skip it — unzipping is not optional, it is the step that turns the export into a backup.
  4. Point SnapDisk at the unzipped folder and run Method 1 on it: From picker to the unzipped folder, To picker to the drive, tap Backup. The copy runs oldest-first with progress, Pause, and verification, exactly as above.

Two honest notes. First, the export is slow and partial by nature: you wait on Meta, the link expires, and what arrives is your content plus messages, comments, and account data — the messages and comments are text in the archive, not photos, and SnapDisk copies only the image and video files, so they stay on the phone. Second, the export does not include photos other people posted that you are only tagged in — Facebook's own export categories say it will not include information someone else shared — so a tagged photo lives with the person who posted it, and this route cannot fetch it. If your Facebook folders are empty because you never used Save to phone, the export is the route; use Save to phone as well, so future photos save to the phone and the next backup is a one-tap folder copy instead of another export.

Method 3: by hand in the Files app (no app needed)

If you'd rather not install anything, Android's built-in Files app can copy the Facebook folders to the USB drive: 1. Plug in the USB drive. A notification or a "USB drive" entry should appear in the Files app under Storage devices. 2. Open Files, browse to Internal storage > Download — and, for the other two possible folders, Internal storage > Pictures > Facebook and Internal storage > DCIM > Facebook. 3. Select the photos (or long-press the Facebook folder to select the whole thing), tap ⋮ → Copy to, then choose the USB drive as the destination. 4. Wait for the copy to finish — and don't unplug the drive until the Files app says it's done. This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason SnapDisk exists: no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time Facebook photos pile up — and you have to remember which of the three folders holds them, because the Files app will not tell you that Downloads is not where Facebook puts them on every phone. For a Facebook folder that grows every day, the app's oldest-first background copy is the calmer option.

SnapDisk's free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run — verified in the repo as FREE_BACKUP_BYTES_LIMIT, 5 GiB — and runs can be repeated. Files already copied are never copied twice, so a second run picks up where the first stopped. For Facebook, 5 GB goes further than it does for a camera library: Facebook photos are compressed when you post them, and the folders are mostly images, so a whole Facebook folder is often one free run. The videos are the part that eats the cap — a Downloads folder full of Reels and downloaded videos can pass 5 GB on its own, and that is the count to do before you start: open the Files app, look at the sizes of the three folders above, divide by 5 GB, and that is how many free runs the copy needs. What this does not back up

This is the part people get wrong, so here it is plainly. Copying the Facebook folders backs up the files — the photos and videos. It does not back up:

  • Your Facebook account. Your profile, your friend list, and your account itself live on Facebook's servers, not in a folder on your phone. Copying files does not copy the account, and if the account is deleted or banned, the drive holds the photos, not the account.
  • Messages and comments. Messenger messages and comments are text in Facebook's database, not files in a Facebook folder. The Meta export in Method 2 includes them in the archive as text — SnapDisk copies image and video files only, so they stay on the phone.
  • Saved posts. Facebook's Save is a bookmark inside the app, not a file. A saved post from someone else's account is not on your phone at all, and it vanishes from your Saved list if the poster deletes it. This article's route does not back it up — it was never yours to copy.
  • Photos you are only tagged in. A photo someone else posted that tags you belongs to their account, and Facebook's export does not include it. If it matters, ask the poster for the original, or save it to your phone with Save to phone while it is still up — that puts a file in one of the three folders above, which this article's route does cover.
  • Other people's posts. Downloader apps like All Media Downloader and Bulk Photos Videos Downloader pull anyone's public post onto your phone, one link or one album at a time. This article covers your own media already on your phone. Downloading someone else's posts without their permission is against Facebook's terms, and a drive full of other people's photos is not a backup of your account.
  • Anything still only on Facebook's servers. A post you never saved to the phone, never downloaded, and never exported is not a file on your phone. Method 1 copies what is there; Method 2 is the route for the rest.

If you want the whole account — posts, photos, messages, and media together — the Meta export in Method 2 is the route, and it is the only one Facebook itself supports. This article's one-tap route is for the photos and videos as files on a drive you hold.

What to avoid

  • Running the default and thinking Facebook came along. The default run copies DCIM — the camera folder — recursively, so it covers DCIM/Facebook if that is where your phone puts them. But your Facebook photos in Downloads and Pictures/Facebook are outside DCIM, and a default run that finishes green has backed up your camera roll, not your Facebook — check all three folders before you delete anything.
  • Trusting one guide's folder and skipping the check. The guides disagree — Downloads, Pictures/Facebook, DCIM/Facebook — and your phone decides. Open the Files app and look in all three before you start, or you will back up an empty folder and think the job is done.
  • Treating the Save bookmark as a backup. The Save button saves a post to a list inside Facebook, not to your phone. If the poster deletes the post, it vanishes from the list. A backup is a file on a drive you hold — a bookmark is a link to someone else's server.
  • Downloading the export and stopping there. The Meta export zip on your phone is not a backup — it is one file on the same phone that could be lost, and its download link expires in four days. Unzip it and copy the files to the drive, or the export was only a download.
  • Copying the zip and calling it a backup. SnapDisk copies image and video files, not zip archives — the export zip would be skipped. Unzip first, then copy the folder.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Assuming the copy keeps running with the screen off. Unverified — no wake lock, no battery-saver handling in the repo. Keep the phone awake for the run or check it.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.
  • Backing up other people's posts and calling it your archive. Downloader apps make it easy to pull anyone's public post onto your phone. Your archive is your own media — the posts, photos, and videos you made. Someone else's post is theirs, and Facebook's terms say so.

Rule of thumb: the photos are files in three possible folders — check all three, back up the ones that hold photos. The account is on Facebook's servers — it follows your sign-in, not your drive. The bookmark is a link — it follows nothing. Do the folder copy, check the copy on the drive before you delete anything, and run the Meta export as well if the posts you never saved to the phone matter.
