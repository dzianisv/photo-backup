Method 1: SnapDisk on the PQ29, PQ39, PQ51, PQ52 and PQ54 (the one-tap route)

On the five OTG-listed phones, the automated route exists. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later, and all five clear it — the PQ39, the PQ51 and the PQ52 run exactly 7.0, the floor, not a comfortable margin — and all five list USB OTG:

  1. Install SnapDisk. Until the Play listing is live, install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on every covered Sigma Mobile phone — including the PQ34 and the PQ37, which can run the app but never see a drive.
  2. Plug in the USB drive with the right OTG adapter. The PQ29, the PQ51 and the PQ52 take a micro-USB OTG adapter; the PQ39 and the PQ54 take a Type-C OTG adapter. Buy the adapter that fits the port you can see, not the one a spec table guesses — the PQ37 proves spec tables can list both.
  3. Tap Backup in the notification. SnapDisk offers the backup when the drive connects, then copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  4. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. Its limits: it works with a drive only on the five OTG-listed phones — not on the PQ34 or the PQ37, which list OTG No — and on the PQ36, the PQ38, the PQ39 Max, the PQ39 Ultra and the PQ54 MAX it installs but the drive may never mount, because OTG is unconfirmed there. On the PQ34 and the PQ37, Methods 3 and 4 are the routes.

Method 2: the built-in file manager and an OTG adapter (works on the five OTG phones)

This is the stock route, and the only phone route on the five OTG-listed phones besides SnapDisk. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive, and OTG is confirmed on the PQ29, the PQ39, the PQ51, the PQ52 and the PQ54:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with an OTG adapter that fits your unit. On the PQ29, the PQ51 and the PQ52 that is a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain micro-USB sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. On the PQ39 and the PQ54 that is a Type-C OTG adapter. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive.
  2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the stock file manager on a Sigma Mobile phone, so use the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On these phones the photos are the 13 MP shots from the rear camera on most models — 8 MP on the PQ29 — plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on the OTG phones: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. On the PQ34 and the PQ37 there is no phone route at all — the manual copy needs a drive the phone can host, and those two list OTG No.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the Sigma Mobile phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. It is also the fallback for the PQ34 and the PQ37, whose USB ports never host a drive, and for the PQ36, the PQ38, the PQ39 Max, the PQ39 Ultra and the PQ54 MAX, where OTG is unconfirmed. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 1 or 2 is the route, or Method 4.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (works on every model)

Every covered Sigma Mobile model takes a microSD card — up to 128 GB on the PQ29 and the PQ39 Max per the sources, with no ceiling stated for the rest — and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old phone manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. One warning: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in the file manager.

Method 5: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

Sigma Mobile offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — the only Sigma-specific backup advice found is the hard-reset page telling you to export photos to a PC via USB or use Google Photos — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead; and its "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p). 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. What this does not do

A few honest limits, so the manual copy is not oversold:

  • No automated backup exists for the PQ34 or the PQ37. Both can run SnapDisk — they clear Android 7.0 — but both list USB OTG No, so neither can host a drive, and no backup app can change that. The computer copy and the card-reader route are manual, one batch at a time, with no progress detail, no resume, and no verification.
  • Photos and videos only, from the folder you pick. By default that's DCIM — your camera photos. Photos in other folders (Downloads, app folders) only copy if you select them too, and photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the microSD card are not on the card for the Method 4 route.
  • Files over 4 GB on FAT32 are a problem. Very long videos on a FAT32 drive will not copy — copy those to a computer instead, or reformat the drive to exFAT, noting that a phone this old may not read exFAT, so FAT32 is the safer format for the phone route.
  • It never deletes. The file manager's Copy only copies. Freeing space on the device is copy, check the copy on the drive, then delete the originals yourself — never the other way round.
  • OTG is confirmed on five phones, listed as No on two, and unconfirmed on five more. The PQ29, the PQ39, the PQ51, the PQ52 and the PQ54 list OTG; the PQ34 and the PQ37 list OTG No; and no source found states OTG for the PQ36, the PQ38, the PQ39 Max, the PQ39 Ultra or the PQ54 MAX — not even the PQ54 MAX's official manual — so on those five the drive mounting is the test, and the computer copy in Method 3 is the reliable route.
  • Every covered Sigma clears SnapDisk's Android 7.0 minimum — but four of them fail VaultDrive's. The PQ34, the PQ39, the PQ51 and the PQ52 run Android 7.0, which is SnapDisk's floor (API 24), not a comfortable margin — and VaultDrive needs Android 8 or later per its own site, so those four cannot run VaultDrive. AppBrain lists VaultDrive at 12.1 or later, which no covered Sigma phone clears either.
  • The PQ51's memory is two numbers in the sources found. devicesfaq and mobilexfiles list 2 GB RAM and 16 GB storage; findpare lists 3 GB RAM and 32 GB storage. Check Settings > About phone on your unit before sizing anything around either number.
  • The PQ37's connector is two numbers in the sources found, too. findpare lists both USB Type-C and micro USB 2.0 for the same phone. The connector changes nothing about the backup — the PQ37 lists OTG No either way — but look at the port on your unit before buying any cable for it.
  • Only two models have a sourced microSD ceiling. The PQ29 and the PQ39 Max take microSD up to 128 GB per the sources; the other ten list a microSD slot with no ceiling stated in any source found. Check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 32 GB.
  • No source found names the stock file manager on a Sigma Mobile phone. No source found names one, so Method 2 uses the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit.
  • A microSD card is a second copy, not a USB drive. All twelve covered models take microSD, so a card is a second local copy you can count on here. But a card is not a USB drive: it does not plug into any computer on its own, and this article is about the drive — the card only becomes a backup once its photos are copied onto one.
  • If yours is an older X-treme, the route is different. No source in this run's searches names an X-treme model below the PQ29, so this guide covers the PQ29 onward only. If yours is older, check Settings > About phone for its Android version first: anything below Android 7.0 cannot run SnapDisk at all, and the computer copy in Method 3 is the reliable route.
  • Newer Sigma phones can still fail to host a drive. The PQ37 runs Android 8.1 and the PQ38 runs Android 10, but the PQ37 lists OTG No and the PQ38's OTG is unconfirmed. A newer Android version is not a promise the port hosts drives — on any Sigma model, plug in a drive and look for the mount notification.
  • The PQ29's IP69 and the PQ34, PQ39 and PQ54's IP68 ratings are old seals. The ratings are real — IP69 on the PQ29, IP68 on the other three — but the seals on phones from 2018–2019 are years old. Do not test water resistance with a phone whose photos you have not backed up yet.

What to avoid

  • Assuming SnapDisk backs up to a drive on every Sigma phone. SnapDisk installs on all twelve covered models — every one clears Android 7.0 — but it can only see a drive on the five that list USB OTG: the PQ29, the PQ39, the PQ51, the PQ52 and the PQ54. Do not buy Pro for a PQ34 or a PQ37 expecting a drive backup, and do not trust a store listing that says "compatible" without checking the OTG listing first.
  • Assuming OTG because the port is Type-C — or because the phone is new. The PQ39 and the PQ54 are Type-C with OTG, but the PQ37 is Type-C with OTG listed as No, and the PQ38 is Type-C with OTG unconfirmed. Plug in a drive and look for the mount notification before buying an adapter.
  • Buying the wrong adapter for a micro-USB phone. The PQ29, the PQ51 and the PQ52 take a micro-USB OTG adapter; the PQ39 and the PQ54 take a Type-C OTG adapter. A micro-USB adapter does not fit a Type-C port, and a Type-C adapter does not fit a micro-USB port — and on the PQ37, check the port on your unit first, because the sources list both.
  • Assuming the PQ36, PQ38, PQ39 Max, PQ39 Ultra or PQ54 MAX hosts a drive. No source found states OTG for any of the five — not even the PQ54 MAX's official manual. On those five the drive mounting is the test, and the computer copy in Method 3 is the reliable route.
  • Assuming the cloud still works on Android 7.0. No source found confirms the current Google Photos or Drive app still installs on the PQ34, the PQ39, the PQ51 or the PQ52. If the cloud app is not already on your phone and working, do not plan a backup around installing it now.
  • Assuming your photos are on the microSD card. On these phones the camera may save to internal storage or to the card depending on the setting. If you pull the card for Method 4 without checking, you may copy an empty DCIM folder while the photos sit in internal storage. Check where the photos live first.
  • Assuming the PQ51's memory or the PQ37's connector. The PQ51 is 2 GB RAM and 16 GB storage per devicesfaq and mobilexfiles, and 3 GB RAM and 32 GB storage per findpare; the PQ37 lists both Type-C and micro USB 2.0. Check Settings > About phone and the port on your unit before sizing anything around any of these numbers.
  • Assuming Sigma Mobile has a backup app. No Sigma Mobile backup app to a USB drive turned up in any source. The one-tap route on the five OTG phones is SnapDisk, not a Sigma app — and on the PQ34 and the PQ37 there is no app route at all.
  • Assuming an old IP68 or IP69 rating still holds. The PQ29 is IP69 and the PQ34, the PQ39 and the PQ54 are IP68 — but the seals on phones from 2018–2019 are years old. Do not test water resistance with a phone whose photos you have not backed up yet.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the device — never the other way round. The file manager only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. A file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the device. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish before unplugging, and eject the drive from the notification or Settings > Storage first.
  • Mistaking a cloud backup for a photo copy. Google Drive and Google Photos back your device up to Google's servers — if you want to plug the drive into a computer and open the pictures, you need a plain copy, that is Method 1, 2, 3 or 4, not a cloud backup. And Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and since May 2026 Google accounts get only 5 GB free by default, with the full 15 GB only after verifying a phone number. On the Android 7.0 Sigmas the cloud apps may not even install any more.
  • Mistaking a hard-reset page for a phone-to-USB guide. The firmwarespro PQ29 page is a real document — but it is a hard-reset page, and its backup advice is a PC via USB or Google Photos and Drive. It never describes copying your phone's camera roll straight to a USB drive from the phone. The generic Android guides in the search results are the closest thing to a phone-to-USB guide, and they say nothing about Sigma Mobile.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the device and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy or a card reader is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On a Sigma Mobile phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost — and on the Android 7.0 Sigmas those apps may not install at all any more.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip. On the PQ29, the PQ39, the PQ51, the PQ52 or the PQ54, SnapDisk picks them for you; on the PQ34 or the PQ37 there is no app that does, so copy the whole DCIM folder in one go, check it on the drive, then delete from the device; on the PQ36, the PQ38, the PQ39 Max, the PQ39 Ultra or the PQ54 MAX, plug a drive in first and look for the mount notification before buying anything.
