Save GoPro photos to USB from Android

Your GoPro photos live on the camera, not on your phone. Download them with Quik, save them to the phone — they land in your photo album — then copy them to a USB drive, no computer.

November 28, 2026 · 12 min read

TL;DR: A GoPro photo is not on your phone until Quik saves it — and the save is two steps, not one, and the first step alone does not count: downloading the photo from the camera puts it in Quik's own Media tab, and a photo that has only reached the Media tab is a file in the app, not a file in the phone's gallery, so no backup app on the phone can see it yet. GoPro's own support page gives both steps with their real names: pair the camera to Quik, tap View Media, pick the photos, tap Download — they land in the Media tab — then open the Quik tab, select the photos, tap the arrow, tap Save to phone, make your quality selections, and GoPro's own page says to open your photo album to view your saved content. The wall: a GoPro photo is not on your phone until you save it — twice

Start with the two mistakes this job turns on, because they look like the same tap and they are not — and with GoPro the first one is worse than in the messaging-app cases, because Quik gives you two places a photo can sit, and only the second one is on the phone in the sense a backup can use. The first mistake is the quiet one: opening Quik and seeing the photo does not put the photo on your phone. The photo you are looking at lives on the camera — a box with a lens and a microSD card inside it, holding the files — and the copy the app fetches to show you the preview is a streamed view, not a file on the phone. A camera full of photos you have browsed and looked at is, from the phone's point of view, a camera full of nothing: the files are on the microSD card, not yours on the phone, and the day the card is full, the camera is off, or the card corrupts, the photos are unreachable. If you have never downloaded a photo from the camera, there is no GoPro folder on your phone at all, and pointing SnapDisk at a folder that does not exist copies nothing. That is the wall, and it is quieter than in the messaging-app cases: WhatsApp at least saves the photo to the phone when it arrives, and the work there is finding the folder. Quik saves nothing on arrival and nothing on viewing — the save is a step you take, one batch at a time, and the step is the whole first half of this article.

The second mistake is stopping after the first of the two saves. Quik's download has two halves, and they have to happen in order: the download, which puts the photos in Quik's own Media tab, and the save to the phone, which puts them in the photo album. The first half is the work most guides describe — pair the camera, tap View Media, tap Download — and it is real: after it, the photos are in the app, on the phone, and you can edit them, share them, and watch them in Quik without the camera nearby. But they are in the app, not in the phone's gallery: GoPro's own support page draws the line itself, with two separate sections — "How to download Media from a GoPro Camera to Quik (Mobile)", which ends with the files in the Media tab, and "Download Media to device photo gallery", which is the second step, Save to phone. A photo that has only reached the Media tab is a file in Quik's app storage, and app storage is private to the app: no gallery app lists it, no file manager browses it, and no backup app can read it. Running SnapDisk's default backup with photos only in the Media tab copies every photo in the gallery — and zero GoPro photos, because from the media index's point of view there are no GoPro photos to copy. That is the trap this article exists to prevent, and it is a trap the app itself sets up: the download feels finished, the photos are right there in Quik, and the second step looks like an optional extra. It is not optional. It is the step that writes the file where a backup can reach it.

Two scope facts, because they decide what this article can and cannot promise. First, the save is one batch at a time, and the batch is yours to choose: GoPro's own support page documents selecting one photo or several — tap Select, then tap each additional photo — and its own HERO13 manual documents the bulk version, tap Download to download all of your new footage to the app, one tap for everything new since the last download. A shoot organised into days is a few taps; a camera that has never been downloaded is one long first download, and a weak connection stalls it the same way it stalls any download. Second, not every photo in Quik is reached the same way. A photo on the camera downloads with the steps in Method 1. A photo already in Quik's cloud — uploaded by Auto Upload on a subscription — downloads from the cloud to the app with Download to app storage, and then still needs Save to phone before a backup can see it. And if the camera is not reachable — the phone is away from the camera, or the camera is off — Quik cannot even list the photos, let alone download them: the camera has to be on, paired, and connected, and that setup is the precondition for everything below.

And a word on whose photos these are, because GoPro photos are usually yours in a way Pinterest pins are not. The photos this article covers are the ones on your own camera — the ride, the dive, the trip, the day out — and saving them to your own drive for your own records is the job. They are also, in the plain sense, sometimes photos someone else took: a friend's camera, a borrowed GoPro, a client's shoot. Saving a file does not grant permission to reuse it — so re-posting a photo from the camera as if it were yours, or sharing it onward as your own work, is a different act, and the same courtesy applies here as anywhere: the person who took it is the person to ask before the photo leaves your phone for anywhere else. This article's methods copy the file to your drive; what you do with it afterwards is yours to decide, and asking is the honest step before anything leaves your phone. Only save content you have permission to keep — and this article makes no watermark-removal claim anywhere: SnapDisk copies the file exactly as Quik saved it.

Where downloaded GoPro photos land — the Quik Media tab, the photo album, and the gallery check

This section exists because the folder is the one fact a guide can get wrong in a way that wastes an evening — and because the folder decides which SnapDisk route works. The answer is short, with one fork in it and one honest unknown, and the fork is the two saves. A file downloaded from the camera with Download lands in Quik's Media tab on the phone, and you find it inside Quik itself: open Quik, tap Media, and the Quik tab shows it. GoPro's own support page says so in its own words: "Tap [Download], and the files will be in the [Media] tab." Inside the Media tab, the files sit under two tabs of their own — Quik and Phone — and the Quik tab is the one that holds what you downloaded from the camera. That agreement is the honest centre of this article: unlike the Pinterest case, where the guides disagreed about the folder, the GoPro docs agree, and the destination is named by GoPro's own support page as well as by its own camera manual.

The second landing spot is the photo album, and it is the one the backup uses. A file saved from Quik with Save to phone lands in the phone's photo album, and you find it in your gallery app: open the gallery and look for the photo. GoPro's own support page says so in its own words: "Tap [Save to phone]. Make your Quality selections and tap [Continue]. The files begin downloading. Open your photo album to view your saved content." The page names the destination as the photo album — not a folder, not a path — and that is the destination this article names too. The check that settles it is the gallery check, and it is the same check the whole series uses: open your gallery app and look for the photo. A saved photo that shows in the gallery is an ordinary indexed file, and the gallery and SnapDisk read the same media index, so a photo the gallery shows is a photo the default run sees. If it is not in the gallery, look in Quik under Media, then the Quik tab: the file is still a file on the phone, in the app, and Method 1's second save is the step that moves it out. Do not look in DCIM/Camera for a Quik save — that is where the camera puts its own photos, not where Quik puts downloads, and a GoPro save will never be there.

The honest unknown is the absolute path, and this article states it rather than inventing one. GoPro names the destinations in the app — the Media tab, then the photo album — but none of GoPro's pages read this run gives the absolute Android path either one sits at, no /sdcard/DCIM/GoPro, no /storage/emulated/0/... path, nothing a file manager can be pointed at by address. So this article does not give one. Worse, the third-party guides disagree about it: one guide says the saved files sit in Internal Storage, then DCIM, then a folder named GoPro; GoPro's own community answers say the save goes to wherever the gallery save location is set, or usually in a folder called GoPro exports, or usually in a folder called DCIM. Three answers from GoPro's own community, and they do not agree — which is itself the evidence that there is no one path to give. The gallery check is what proves the default run sees a save, not a path. The one honest uncertainty in this section is whether a given phone's gallery shows saved GoPro photos: most gallery apps show them under On this device, and Google Photos shows them in the device folders, but the gallery is the check, not the promise — if the photo is in the Media tab and not in the gallery, the default run may not see it, and the Save to phone step is the route. That is the same check the whole series uses, and it is stated here rather than assumed.

One more scope fact for this section, because it decides what the drive copy holds: the download from the camera is the footage, and the save to the phone offers quality selections. GoPro's own support page gives the Save to phone step as "Make your Quality selections and tap [Continue]" — and the page does not say what the options are, or whether the highest one is the file exactly as the camera recorded it. So this article makes no byte-for-byte claim for the phone save, and says to choose the highest quality offered. The camera-to-app download carries no quality selection in GoPro's docs: the HERO13 manual gives it as "Tap Download to download all of your new footage to the app", with no quality step in between. If a saved photo looks smaller than the original, the save is the suspect, not the download: the photo was saved at a lower quality in the Save to phone step, or it was recorded small in the first place. SnapDisk copies the file exactly as Quik saved it — byte-for-byte, no conversion, no quality change — but it cannot improve a file that arrived small. And one GoPro-specific wrinkle worth naming here, because it looks like a failed download when it is not one: Quik downloads from the camera over the camera's own Wi-Fi, and a large batch takes a while — so a shoot that has not appeared yet may still be downloading, not failed. Give it time on a steady connection before downloading it again.

What you need

An Android phone — Android 7.0 or later, which is SnapDisk's minimum — with Quik installed and paired to your GoPro, plus one USB drive your phone can read: a USB-C flash drive, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter. Quik itself is free from Google Play — GoPro's own support page says the basic version is free, and the fully-featured version is $9.99 a year, or included with a GoPro Subscription at $49.99 a year — and the camera download and Save to phone steps on GoPro's own support page carry no subscription note: the subscription features the page names are cloud backup and premium editing, not the download. SnapDisk itself is installed from the v0.1.2 APK — it is not on Google Play yet — so install it by allowing "install unknown apps" for the browser or Files app you download it with, which is standard Android. You need nothing else for the main route: no file manager with hidden files, no extra app, no account beyond the Quik login, no third-party downloader. The save steps are inside Quik itself, and the backup is SnapDisk's default run. Check the drive's space first: GoPro photos are small — a photo is a few megabytes at most — so space is almost never the problem for the GoPro half, but look anyway, because a whole-library default run copies everything, not just GoPro. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 cannot hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit, and exFAT is the format to use for a photo drive.

Before any method, settle the two questions that decide whether the phone route works at all: can the phone reach the camera, and does it see the USB drive? Open Quik and check the camera appears as paired — if the camera is off, or the phone has never paired to it, the download cannot start. Then plug the drive in and open Settings > Connected devices or Settings > Storage — the drive should appear there. That check is the test, and it takes ten seconds. If the drive does not appear, try a different adapter first — cheap OTG adapters are the usual failure — then a different drive, and a powered hub if the drive is a spinning hard drive rather than a flash drive. And one warning up front, because it applies to every route below: do not delete anything from the phone — or from the camera — until the USB copy is done and verified. The photos on the camera are the originals, and the photos in the photo album are your only copy outside the camera, and deleting from either before the check is how a photo ends up nowhere at all. The same goes for the camera's card itself: do not format the card until the drive copy is checked, because the card is the only route back to a photo you have not saved yet.

Method 1: Save the photos in Quik, then back up with SnapDisk

This is the route to use, because it is the only one that joins the whole job — the GoPro photo, out of the camera and into the photo album, onto a USB drive plugged into the phone, no computer — using only the apps already on the phone plus SnapDisk. It has two halves, and they have to happen in order: the download, which puts the photos in Quik, and the save to the phone, which puts them in the gallery. The first half is the work. The second half is the bridge. Skipping the second half is the mistake this article exists to prevent: running SnapDisk's default backup with photos only in Quik copies every photo in the gallery — and zero GoPro photos, because from the media index's point of view there are no GoPro photos to copy.

  1. Download the photos from the camera into Quik. Open Quik on your phone. Pair the camera to Quik if it is not paired already — Quik pairs over the camera's own Wi-Fi and Bluetooth — then tap View Media. Tap the photos you want to download: to select more than one, tap Select, then tap each additional photo you want. Tap Download, and the files land in the Media tab. For a whole shoot at once, GoPro's own HERO13 manual gives the bulk version: connect the camera to Quik and tap Download to download all of your new footage to the app — one tap, everything new since the last download. Work through the shoots that matter first; the rest can wait for the next sitting. Quik runs each download as a transfer from the camera, so a large batch takes a while and a weak connection stalls it the same way it stalls any download.
  2. Save the photos from Quik to the phone. Open Quik, tap Media to view all of your photos and videos, and tap the Quik tab. Select a photo — tap and hold the icon until it turns blue, and tap more photos individually or by using the date bubble to take a whole day at once. Tap the arrow, then tap Save to phone. Make your quality selections and tap Continue: choose the highest quality offered, because GoPro's page does not say the saved file is byte-identical to the camera original. The files begin downloading. Open your photo album to view your saved content — that last line is GoPro's own, and it is the check that the save worked.
  3. Check the photo is on the phone. Open your gallery app and look for the photo. If it is there, the save works — and this is also the check that proves the default run will see it, because the gallery and SnapDisk read the same media index. If it is not in the gallery, open Quik, tap Media, then the Quik tab: the file should be there by name, which means the download worked and the save did not — run the Save to phone step again, watching for the download to finish. A save that never completes is usually a connection problem, not a Quik problem: the file is coming from your camera, and if the phone cannot reach the camera — the camera is off, or out of range — nothing downloads at all.
  4. Do not look for an auto-download setting — there is not one. GoPro's current support page documents the manual Download tap, and no auto-download setting that pulls camera photos onto the phone by itself. Auto Upload is the other direction: it uploads to the GoPro cloud on a subscription, with the originals staying on the camera. Microsoft's Outlook has the same shape — no auto-download — and Gmail has the setting, but Quik does not: every photo still on the camera needs the same Download tap each time you connect. There is no setting in this article that makes the job smaller over time instead of bigger, and this article does not invent one: the photos you shoot next week arrive as camera files, not as phone files, and the next backup picks them up only after a download session first. That is the honest difference from the Gmail case, and it is the reason the download session matters more here.
  5. Plug in the drive and run SnapDisk. Plug the USB drive into the phone. SnapDisk offers the backup in a notification — tap Backup — or open the app and tap Start. The run copies oldest-first, verifies every file by size after copying, and shows progress with Pause and Stop in the notification. The free run covers up to 5 GB, and GoPro photos are small, so one run is almost always enough for the GoPro half — though a whole-library run copies everything else too, and that is the point: the saved photos come along with the rest, with no folder to pick and no step to remember. Android 15 and later give a dataSync foreground service 6 hours in 24 hours — if the copy runs past that, Android pauses it, and opening the app resets the timer. Screen-off continuation under battery saver is unverified — the repo holds no wake lock and no battery-saver handling — so keep the phone awake for the run or check it.
  6. Skip the share links — or download them another way. Quik lets you share a photo by link or straight to social — but a link is not a file on your phone. A share link opens the photo in a browser or in the app, and the USB leg still has not happened. Do not use a link as the save step: open Quik and use Save to phone, which is the step that writes the file. A photo that only exists as a link is still a photo that lives on the camera until you download it, and the USB leg still has not happened.

Two honest limits. First, the save is not retroactive in the other direction either: saving a photo today does not put the photos you only looked at yesterday onto the phone — a photo you viewed in Quik is still on the camera until you download it and save it. That is steps 1 and 2's job, and it is a manual job: one tap per batch, with no auto-download setting to catch the ones still to come. Second, the 5 GB free cap is per run — a GoPro shoot will never reach it, but a whole-library default run might, and running it again picks up where it stopped, because files already copied are never copied twice. Third, SnapDisk copies, it never deletes: deleting the photos from the phone after the check is always your step, never the app's — and deleting them from the camera is Quik's, not SnapDisk's, and it does not free the drive copy. The drive copy is the backup; the phone copy is the original until you decide otherwise.

Copying only some GoPro photos? If you would rather copy just a few photos than the whole library, the From folder picker still takes a folder, not single files — so save the ones you want, find them in the gallery, and copy the whole library, or use the picker on the folder the gallery shows them under. The picker route keeps only recognised photo and video extensions — GoPro photos are .JPG files and GoPro videos are .MP4 files, both on the list. If the drive is unplugged mid-copy, the backup pauses with the full error shown and resumes where it stopped when the drive is back. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device.

And the check that matters for this article: after the run, compare the count against the photo album. A green backup that copied the whole library has still copied zero GoPro photos if nothing was ever saved from the camera — which is exactly what happens when the photos were only downloaded into Quik and never saved to the phone. Open the drive and look for the GoPro photos by name: the default run mirrors the source tree, so the photos should be there under their own names. If they are missing, the save is the suspect, not the run — download the photos in Quik and save them to the phone first, then run again. SnapDisk copies, it never deletes — the only files it ever deletes are its own half-finished .part files — so deleting the originals after the check is always your step, never the app's.

Method 2: Why there is no picker route for Quik's app storage

If you would rather copy just the GoPro photos and nothing else, the honest answer is that there is no picker route for them — and this article says so rather than inventing one. The From folder picker is the system's folder picker, and it can only point at folders the system picker can reach. A photo downloaded to Quik's Media tab lives in Quik's app storage, and app storage is private to the app: scoped storage guards it, no file manager browses it, and the picker cannot open it. SnapDisk does not request all-files access — no MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE anywhere in the code — which is the permission a file manager uses to browse another app's folders freely, and the absence is the reason SnapDisk cannot simply walk into Quik's storage the way a file manager can. The code has no GoPro path handling anywhere — zero matches for "gopro" in the app's source — so there is no Quik folder to navigate to, and no folder name to give you. The Save to phone step is the bridge, not an optional extra: it is the step that moves the file out of Quik's private storage and into the photo album, where the gallery — and the default run — can see it.

That is the same shape as the Gmail and Outlook cases, and the difference from the QNAP case is worth stating, because it decides which method to use. In the QNAP case the Download Folder sits on shared storage, outside Android/data, so the picker route is code-supported — untested, but supported. In the GoPro case the Media tab sits in app storage, inside Android/data, so the picker route is not supported at all, and no test could make it supported: the folder is not a folder the picker can reach, and the photos in it are not files the default run can see. Do not go digging in Android/data for your GoPro photos: that folder holds Quik's working data — cached fragments in a temporary folder, not your photos — and scoped storage guards it, so a file manager may not even show it. The photos are in the photo album once saved, or in Quik until then, and those are the only two places this article's methods touch. For a whole camera, use Method 3 — this article is only about the photo and video files. One more note for the photos that show in the gallery: those are already covered by the default run, so no picker route adds anything for them — the default run reads the media index and the gallery is the index.

Method 3: Bulk routes — download all new footage, the wired USB-C route, and the microSD card reader

GoPro has a bulk download of its own on the phone, and two bulk routes off the phone — and they are worth naming honestly, because they are the first results for every bulk-save search — and only one of them is this job. On the phone, the bulk route is the all-new download in Quik: GoPro's own HERO13 manual says to connect the camera to Quik and tap Download to download all of your new footage to the app — one tap, everything new since the last download. That is the phone bulk route, and it is the one to use for a shoot — one tap per session, and the shoots you care about most first. There is no documented per-photo multi-select limit to work around: the support page documents selecting one photo or several with Select, and the date bubble takes a whole day at once, so a scattered library is a few taps, not an evening of them.

Off the phone, the first bulk route is the wired connection. GoPro's own HERO13 manual gives it its own section — "Saving to your phone with a wired connection: use a wired connection to transfer photos and videos from your GoPro to your phone faster and more reliably" — and the cable for an Android phone is a standard USB-C to USB-C cable: plug the camera into the phone, start Quik, and follow the on-screen instructions. A third-party guide for the same route says the phone's file manager then shows the camera as a connected device, with the photos in DCIM, then 100GOPRO — the folder the camera writes on its card. But none of that is a phone-to-USB backup by itself, and the difference is where the files land. The wired route puts the files in Quik or in the file manager's view of the camera — and the USB leg still needs the Save to phone step and the SnapDisk run. So use it for what it is: the faster way to get a whole camera off the card in one sitting, for the day the wireless download is too slow, standing at the camera. For plain photo files on a drive you can open anywhere, save the photos to the phone first — Method 1 — and copy them to a USB drive with SnapDisk, no computer.

The second bulk route off the phone is the microSD card reader. GoPro's documented route to a computer is to take the microSD card out of the camera and put it in a card reader — and the same reader with a USB-C plug works on the phone: the reader appears as a connected device in the file manager, and the photos sit in DCIM, then 100GOPRO, the folder the camera writes on the card. That is the way to get a whole camera off the card in one sitting without Quik at all, for the day the camera will not pair or the app will not download. But it is not a Quik route, and it is not a SnapDisk route either: the card reader is a file-manager job, and the USB leg still needs the photos on the phone first — copy them from the card to the phone, or copy them straight from the card to the drive with the file manager, and SnapDisk is not involved. The two routes are not rivals; they are different shapes, and this article is about the plain-files one. And the same permission rule applies to both: only save content you have permission to keep.

Can SnapDisk read Quik's downloads directly?

This is the question the whole article turns on, and it deserves a straight answer in three parts — what the code proves it can do, what it cannot do, and what would prove the rest. The first part is proven, from the code at the current base and from how Android's media index works — and it is the same shape as the Gmail and Outlook cases. SnapDisk's default run reads the phone's media index — it queries the MediaStore images and videos tables, oldest-first by date taken, with no folder filter. A GoPro photo saved to the phone is an ordinary indexed file: it shows in the gallery, and gallery apps read the media index. The photo's presence in the gallery is the evidence the file is indexed — if the media scanner had skipped it, the gallery would not show it at all; it does show, so there is no such skip. The query is in PhotoQuery.kt, the no-folder-filter shape is the code's own design — the query call passes no selection at all — and the gallery evidence is the platform fact that closes the loop. So the default run sees saved GoPro photos, and it sees them with no step beyond the save. That is not an assumption: it is the code's query shape plus the gallery evidence, stated as such. And the default run copies the whole library, not DCIM only — the query reads every indexed image and video on the phone, whatever folder it sits in, so the photo album is covered the same way DCIM is. GoPro photos are .JPG files and GoPro videos are .MP4 files, and both extensions are on SnapDisk's recognised list, so the folder walk and the index query both keep them.

The second part is a no, and this article marks it as a no rather than guessing in either direction: whether SnapDisk can read a photo that is only in Quik. It cannot — and the reason is the platform, not a missing feature. A photo downloaded to the app but never saved to the phone lives in Quik's app storage, and app storage is private: scoped storage guards it, the media index does not list it, the gallery does not show it, and SnapDisk has no all-files access with which to reach it. The picker is the system's folder picker — ActivityResultContracts.OpenDocumentTree, in MainActivity.kt — and it opens with no folder pre-selected: there is no initial-folder hint anywhere in the code, and no GoPro path handling anywhere in the app — zero matches for "gopro" in the app's source. The navigation chain is an ordinary folder for a saved photo — the photo album, not dot-prefixed, so not hidden from the picker the way a dot-folder would be — but for an app-only download there is no navigation chain at all, because the folder is not a folder the picker can open. The Save to phone step is the only route out of Quik, and the joined workflow in Method 1 is the route that is proven to work, and it is the route this article gives first. The third unknown in this section is GoPro's own, not SnapDisk's: GoPro publishes no absolute Android path for where Save to phone lands, so this article cannot state where the picker will find it by address — only that it sits in the photo album, and that the gallery check is what proves the default run sees a save.

The third part is what the code does prove about the rest of the job, so the limits above are not mistaken for a wider doubt. The manifest requests the media-read permissions (READ_MEDIA_IMAGES and READ_MEDIA_VIDEO, plus the older READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE capped at Android 12), the notification permission, and foreground-service permissions — and no INTERNET permission at all, zero occurrences, so the photos physically cannot leave the device. It does not request all-files access — no MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE anywhere — which is the permission a file manager uses to browse another app's folders freely, and the absence is the reason SnapDisk cannot simply walk into Quik's storage the way a file manager can; the Save to phone step is the route instead, and it needs your tap. SnapDisk does not talk to the camera at all: there is no GoPro handling anywhere in the code, no camera API, and no network permission with which to reach one. The backup itself is the verified core: oldest-first by date taken with a date-modified fallback (PhotoQuery.kt), every file size-verified after copying (BackupEngine.kt), Pause and Stop in the notification (NotificationHelper.kt, BackupService.kt), files of 4 GB or more on a FAT32 drive skipped with their reason instead of stalling the job (BackupService.kt), and the free run capped at 5 GB with Pro a one-time $9.99 purchase (ProPrefs.kt). None of that changes the app-storage verdict — it bounds it. The backup engine is proven; the end-to-end save of this exact app is not yet tested on a real phone.

Camera photos vs photos already in Quik

The route is the same for both, but the starting point is not, and it is worth saying which one you are saving before you start. Photos already on the camera — the case this article is really about — are the ones the two saves govern: with a download and a save they land in the photo album, and without them they are not on the phone at all, and the methods above are written for them. Photos already in Quik are different, because they are already past the first save: a photo in Quik's Media tab, or in Quik's cloud on a subscription, still needs the Save to phone step before a backup can see it — the cloud copy downloads to the app with Download to app storage, and then still needs Save to phone, because a cloud file in the app is a file in the app, not a file in the gallery. Back that up instead of downloading it again: open Quik, tap Media, tap the Quik tab, select the photo, tap the arrow, tap Save to phone, and the default run picks it up like any other indexed photo. That matters more than it sounds, because the photos already in Quik are often the ones you care about most: the shot from the last trip, the clip you already edited, the photo you saved to the app and never saved to the phone. The original on the camera is the one worth keeping, and the copy in the app is the one worth saving to the phone.

One related corner is worth a line, because GoPro users hit it and it looks like a shortcut that is not one: Quik keeps its working data under Android/data, and a guide may tell you to dig in Android/data for a downloaded photo. That is a different thing from saved photos — the cached copies are fragments keyed to the installation, in a temporary folder, not your photos in any usable sense — and Android/data is guarded by scoped storage, so a file manager may not even show it. Do not go digging in Android/data for your GoPro photos: they are not there. The photos are in the photo album once saved, or in Quik until then, and those are the only two places this article's methods touch. And one GoPro-specific note for the cloud half: because Auto Upload runs camera to cloud, a photo in the GoPro cloud is a copy, not a move — the original stays on the camera unless you deleted it yourself, and Free Up Space-style cleanup is not a Quik feature to watch for here.

Checking the drive after the copy

The check has two halves, and they have to happen in order: first that the saves actually produced a file on the phone, then that the drive holds it. After the SnapDisk run finishes, open the drive on a computer or on the phone itself and count — by file name, not by folder name, because GoPro publishes no absolute path for where Save to phone lands and this article will not guess what the drive copy's folder is called. The drive should hold the GoPro photos you saved, under their own names, and a few opened at random should render fully. Open a GoPro photo itself — not just any photo on the drive — because a run that covered the rest of the library would still look complete on a random check while the one set you cared about was missing. If the count is short, the run is not the first suspect — the saves are. Check the photo album in the gallery: is the photo actually there, and does it hold the photos you expected? If the album is empty, the photos were never saved — download them in Quik and save them to the phone, and run again. If the album holds them and the drive does not, the run is the suspect, and the run needs repeating, not a different folder.

If the drive's free space ran out mid-run, the run stops with the full error shown; free space by moving the drive's contents to a bigger drive first, or back up to a second drive. If the run stopped at the 5 GB free cap — unlikely for a GoPro shoot alone, possible on a whole-library default run — run it again and it picks up where it stopped, because files already copied are never copied twice. And the check that matters most for a photo that only existed on the camera: confirm the drive copy before you delete anything from the phone or the camera. The copy in the photo album is an ordinary file and will survive; the copy on the camera depends on the card keeping it, and a formatted card or a failed drive ends that. The drive is a plain folder of plain files. Anyone who picks up the drive can open them. That is the price of a copy you can open anywhere, and it is worth stating before the photos leave the phone.

What to avoid

  • Assuming downloading a photo into Quik puts it on your phone. It does not — downloading the photo puts it in Quik's Media tab, and a photo that has only reached the Media tab is a file in the app, not a file in the gallery. A one-tap backup copies zero GoPro photos until you save them to the phone with Save to phone first.
  • Assuming viewing a photo in Quik saves it. It does not — viewing the photo shows you a streamed copy from the camera, and Quik saves nothing on viewing. A photo you only viewed lives on the camera, not in any folder. A one-tap backup copies zero GoPro photos until you download them and save them first.
  • Assuming the From picker can reach Quik's downloads. It cannot — a photo downloaded to the app lives in Quik's private app storage, and the system picker cannot open another app's private folder. The Save to phone step is the bridge, not an optional extra; there is no picker route for app-only downloads.
  • Assuming Auto Upload downloads to the phone. It does not — Auto Upload sends photos from the camera to the GoPro cloud on a subscription, with the originals staying on the camera. A cloud photo still needs Download to app storage and then Save to phone before a backup can see it.
  • Assuming a share link is a save. It is not — a Quik share link opens the photo in a browser or in the app. A link is not a file on your phone. Save to phone is the step that writes the file; a link is the step that does not.
  • Assuming the saved photo lands in DCIM/Camera or DCIM/GoPro. It does not — Quik saves land in the photo album, and GoPro publishes no absolute Android path for where that is. DCIM/Camera is where the camera puts its own photos, and DCIM/GoPro is one third-party guide's claim, contradicted by GoPro's own community answers. Looking in DCIM for a Quik save is looking in the wrong folder — and do not trust a guide that gives you one path.
  • Assuming the saved file is the file exactly as the camera recorded it. It may not be — the Save to phone step asks for quality selections, and GoPro's page does not say the saved file is byte-identical to the camera original. Choose the highest quality offered, and do not plan a backup around a byte-for-byte claim this article does not make.
  • Assuming the default SnapDisk run covers photos you only downloaded into Quik. It cannot — proven from the code and the platform: the default run reads the media index, and a photo that was never saved to the phone never enters the index. A green backup copies zero GoPro photos from a camera you never saved from.
  • Assuming the wired route skips the Save to phone step. It does not — the wired connection gets the files into Quik or into the file manager's view of the camera faster, but the USB leg still needs the photos saved to the phone first. A faster download is still a download, not a save.
  • Assuming the card-reader route is a SnapDisk route. It is not — the card reader is a file-manager job, and SnapDisk is not involved in it. Copy the photos from the card to the phone first, or straight from the card to the drive with the file manager, and run SnapDisk for the phone half.
  • Looking in Android/data for your GoPro photos. That folder holds Quik's working data — cached fragments in a temporary folder, not your photos — and scoped storage guards it. The photos are in the photo album once saved, or in Quik until then.
  • Assuming a download that has not appeared yet has failed. It may still be running — Quik downloads from the camera over the camera's own Wi-Fi, and a large batch takes a while on a weak connection. Give it time before downloading it again, or you will download it twice.
  • Assuming screen-off continuation under battery saver. UNVERIFIED — the repo holds no wake lock and no battery-saver handling. Keep the phone awake for the run, or check it.
  • Using a FAT32 drive for a library with long videos. FAT32 cannot hold a single file over 4 GB, and a library with long 5.3K clips will hold one. SnapDisk skips those files and lists them — use exFAT.
  • Assuming the free 5 GB cap covers the whole library. It is per run. A library over 5 GB needs several free runs — run it again and it picks up where it stopped — or one $9.99 Pro purchase.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this. An unplug mid-copy leaves a half-copied library that looks finished until you count it.
  • Checking the drive by opening any photo. Open a GoPro photo itself — not just any photo on the drive. A random check passes even when the one set you cared about was never copied, because the photos that did copy render fine.

Rule of thumb: Download first, save second, then back it up — downloading a photo into Quik puts it in the app, Save to phone is the step that puts a file in the photo album, and the album is the bridge to the drive. The download is one tap per batch; the save is one tap per batch; the backup after it is one tap for all of them.
