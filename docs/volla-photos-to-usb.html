TL;DR: The one-tap route is proven on six of seven phones — the Phone X, Phone 22, Phone X23, Quintus, Plinius and Plinius Plus all list USB OTG Yes, so a USB-C flash drive plugs straight into any of them and SnapDisk copies the whole camera roll in one tap. On the original Volla Phone no source states OTG at all, so the drive mounting is the test there. All seven clear Android 7.0, so SnapDisk installs on every one of them — from the GitHub APK, not from Play, because Volla OS ships without the Google Play Store. The copies are the original files either way: a file-manager copy moves the files as they are, never recompresses them, and the only place quality gets lost is a cloud backup with a "Storage saver" setting. Method 1: SnapDisk on the six — and the test on the seventh

On the six phones that list USB OTG Yes, SnapDisk installs and the one-tap backup works: the Phone X runs Android 10, the Phone 22 runs Android 11, the X23 runs Android 12, the Quintus runs Android 14, and the Plinius and Plinius Plus run Android 14 at the base — all above SnapDisk's 7.0 floor — and all six host a drive over USB-C. On the original Volla Phone the version test passes — it runs Android 9 — and the OTG test is where the route is unproven: no source states OTG for it, so the drive mounting is the test:

  1. Install SnapDisk from the GitHub APK. Volla OS ships without the Google Play Store, so there is no Play install on any Volla phone — install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page instead. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on all seven covered phones, and the one-tap backup to a drive is proven on six of them, because six list USB OTG Yes. On a phone running Ubuntu Touch instead of Volla OS there is no SnapDisk route at all — use Method 3 or 4 instead.
  2. Plug in the USB drive. All seven take USB Type-C, so a USB-C flash drive plugs straight into any of them, or a regular USB drive needs a USB-C OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction.
  3. Look for the mount notification. On the six that list OTG Yes the drive should mount: tap Backup in the notification and SnapDisk copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. On the original Volla Phone no source states OTG, so the drive mounting is the test: if a drive does mount on your unit, tap Backup the same way — but do not buy a drive expecting that outcome. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  4. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. Its limits: it works only on phones that run Android 7.0 and host a drive — and on the original Volla Phone no source says it hosts one. Every covered phone's port is USB 2.0, so the copy runs at USB 2.0 speed on all seven — a full camera roll takes a while, and that is the hardware, not the app.

The copies are the original files. SnapDisk streams each file byte-for-byte from the device to the drive — the code reads the file and writes the same bytes out, with no decode, no recompression, and no resize — and then checks that the copy on the drive is the same size as the original. Other apps copy originals byte-for-byte too; what SnapDisk adds is the automated whole-library run: oldest-first order, progress with Pause and Stop, resume where it stopped, and skip-if-already-copied on the next run. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter (works wherever the drive mounts)

This is the stock route, and the only phone route that could work on the original Volla Phone at all. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive. On the six that list OTG Yes — the Phone X, Phone 22, Phone X23, Quintus, Plinius and Plinius Plus — the drive should mount. On the original Volla Phone no source states OTG, so the drive mounting is the test:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with an OTG adapter. On all seven that is a USB-C OTG adapter, or a USB-C flash drive plugged straight in, with the OTG wiring inside the adapter — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive — and if the drive never shows up, the phone does not host it, and Method 3 or 4 is the route.
  2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the file manager on a Volla phone — use the file manager app on your unit, whatever it is called.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On the six where this method should work, the photos are the 13 MP shots from the rear camera on the Phone X, the 48 MP shots on the Phone 22 and the X23, the 50 MP shots on the Quintus, and the 64 MP shots on the Plinius and Plinius Plus, plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick. On the original Volla Phone the photos are the 16 MP shots — but on it the drive mounting is the test, so those photos move by Method 3 or 4 if the drive never mounts.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

This works fine for a one-off copy, if the drive mounts at all. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on the six phones: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. And it is proven on six of the seven — on the original Volla Phone the drive mounting is the test.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the Volla phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no Android-version question and no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. It is also the only phone-to-drive route for the original Volla Phone if the drive never mounts on it — and it is the route for a phone running Ubuntu Touch instead of Volla OS, where there is no SnapDisk and no Android file-manager route at all. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 2 is the route on the six where the drive mounts, or Method 4 on the six that take a card.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (works on six of seven)

Six of the seven covered Volla phones take a microSD card — the original Phone up to 256 GB, the Phone X up to 512 GB, the Phone 22 up to 512 GB, the X23 up to 1 TB, the Plinius up to 1 TB and the Plinius Plus up to 1 TB — and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old phone manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. Three warnings: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in the file manager. The Quintus takes no microSD card at all, so there is no card-reader route on it — use Method 1, 2 or 3 instead. And the Phone 22's slot is a hybrid slot sharing the second SIM slot, so check your unit's SIM tray before counting on the card-reader route without removing a SIM. Unlike Wileyfox, where the route is proven on all eight, and unlike Zopo, where it is proven on thirty-four of thirty-six, six of the seven covered Volla phones take one — so this route exists on six, with no OTG question at all on any of them.

Volla offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no Volla-specific backup advice turned up in any source beyond a computer copy or its own cloud — so the stock options are Volla's cloud, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2, 3 or 4 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What this does not do

  • It does not back up your settings or your contacts. SnapDisk copies photos, videos, and any files you pick — the files themselves, exactly as they are. App settings, call logs, texts, and your accounts stay with your phone and your Volla account. For those, Android's built-in backup is the tool — it backs up personal settings to Google's servers with a Google account — not SnapDisk.
  • It does not delete photos from your phone after copying. Deleting is always your choice, after you check the copy on the drive. SnapDisk never deletes originals — it only copies.
  • It does not work if your phone does not host a drive. On the original Volla Phone no source states OTG, so if the drive never mounts on your unit, no backup app of any kind can copy your photos straight to a USB drive on it. If the drive never mounts, that settles it for your unit — use Method 3 or 4 instead.
  • It does not install from Google Play on a Volla phone. Volla OS ships without the Google Play Store, so SnapDisk installs from the GitHub APK. On a phone running Ubuntu Touch instead of Volla OS there is no SnapDisk route at all.
  • It does not back up photos from apps that hide them. Some apps keep photos in private folders SnapDisk cannot read. Those stay on the phone — check each app's own export or share option.
  • It does not compress your photos. It copies the original files byte-for-byte, so the copy is the same size as the original. On the Plinius and Plinius Plus, with their 64 MP cameras, and the Quintus, with its 50 MP camera, that is real storage on the drive — bring a drive with room.

What to avoid

  • Don't move files instead of copying them. The file manager's Move deletes the original as it goes. Copy first, check the copy on the drive, and only then delete the originals from the phone if you need the space.
  • Don't unplug the drive until the copy is done. If the copy is still running — SnapDisk shows progress, speed and ETA — unplugging leaves half-copied files. Wait for it to finish, then eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it.
  • Don't buy a micro-USB adapter. All seven covered Volla phones take USB Type-C — there is no micro-USB phone in this article at all. A micro-USB adapter does not fit any of them.
  • Don't assume the drive will mount on the original Volla Phone. No source states OTG for it, so a no-mount settles it for your unit. Use Method 3 or 4 instead.
  • Don't buy a microSD card for the Quintus. The Quintus takes no card at all — Volla's own page lists no card slot and Volla's help page gives separate "Quintus without SD card" instructions — so there is no card-reader route on it, whatever one secondary source claims.
  • Don't let one folder hold every photo. SnapDisk copies oldest-first, and the copy is the same size as the original, so a full camera roll on a phone with a 64 MP camera — the Plinius and Plinius Plus — fills a small drive fast. Bring a drive with room, or back up in batches and check the copy before deleting anything.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a copy. A backup finds your photos, copies them all, and tells you when it's done. On the Quintus there is no card-reader route at all, so the one-tap backup or the computer copy is the backup there.
