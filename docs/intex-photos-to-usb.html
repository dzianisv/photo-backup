Method 1: SnapDisk on the two (the one-tap route)

On the two phones that clear Android 7.0 and list USB OTG, the automated route exists. SnapDisk needs Android 7.0 or later — the Lions 3 and Style III run 7.0 exactly — and both list OTG:

  1. Install SnapDisk. Until the Play listing is live, install the v0.1.2 APK from the GitHub release page. It installs on Android 7.0 and later, so it installs on the seven phones that clear 7.0 — the Lions 3, Style III, S3, S1, Lions 2, A4 and T1 — but the one-tap backup to a drive only works where the phone hosts a drive: the Lions 3 and Style III list OTG, the S3 and S1 have no OTG stated, and the Lions 2, A4 and T1 list OTG No. It does not install on the other seven: they run Android 6.0 or older, below the minimum, so Method 3 or 4 is the route on those.
  2. Plug in the USB drive with a micro-USB OTG adapter. Both take micro-USB, so a regular USB drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A USB-C flash drive does not plug straight into either of them.
  3. Tap Backup in the notification. SnapDisk offers the backup when the drive connects, then copies the whole camera roll oldest-first in the background, showing progress, speed and ETA, with Pause and Stop. The free version backs up manually up to 5 GB per run; Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase for auto-backup on connect and unlimited size.
  4. Check the copy, then delete from the phone if you need the space. SnapDisk verifies every file's size after copying, but open a few photos on the drive yourself before deleting anything.

This is the only method in this article that takes the whole library in one tap, with progress, pause, resume and verification. Its limits: it works only on phones that run Android 7.0 and host a drive — and only two covered Intex phones do both. On the other twelve, Method 2, 3 or 4 is the route — and on the seven below Android 7.0, only Methods 3 and 4 exist.

The copies are the original files. SnapDisk streams each file byte-for-byte from the device to the drive — the code reads the file and writes the same bytes out, with no decode, no recompression, and no resize — and then checks that the copy on the drive is the same size as the original. Other apps copy originals byte-for-byte too; what SnapDisk adds is the automated whole-library run: oldest-first order, progress with Pause and Stop, resume where it stopped, and skip-if-already-copied on the next run. If a file is corrupt or too big for FAT32, it's retried once, then skipped with its reason listed — it never stalls the whole backup. The free version backs up manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase (no subscription) that starts the backup automatically when you plug in a disk and removes the size limit. The app has no internet permission, so your photos physically cannot leave your device.

Method 2: the file manager and an OTG adapter (works on the two)

This is the stock route, and the only phone route on the two besides SnapDisk. It works if — and only if — your phone hosts the drive, and OTG is listed on both:

  1. Plug in the USB drive with a micro-USB OTG adapter. Both take micro-USB, so a regular USB drive needs a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it — a plain sync cable is the wrong cable, it connects the phone to a computer, which is the opposite direction. A notification should appear saying the USB storage was added; tap it to open the drive. If nothing appears, check Settings > Storage for the drive.
  2. Open the file manager and confirm the USB drive shows up under storage devices. No source found names the stock file manager on an Intex phone, so use Files by Google or the built-in file manager, whatever it is called on your unit.
  3. Go to Internal storage, DCIM, Camera, where your camera photos live. On these phones the photos are the 8 MP shots from the rear camera on the Lions 3 and the 5 MP shots on the Style III, plus whatever image files sit in the folders you pick.
  4. Select the photos (long-press the first, tap the rest, or select all). Tap Copy, then select the USB drive, and wait for the copy to finish — don't unplug the drive until the file manager says it's done. Eject the drive from the notification or from Settings > Storage before unplugging it. Use Copy, not Move: Move deletes the originals as it goes, so a bumped cable halfway through loses photos.

On the S3 and the S1 the drive mounting is the test: no source found states OTG for either, so plug a drive in with a micro-USB OTG adapter and look for the mount notification before counting on this method. This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason a backup app exists on the two phones: you pick files one by one, there is no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time photos pile up. And on the other twelve it does not work at all as a phone route — the Lions 2, A4 and T1 list OTG No, and the seven below Android 7.0 cannot run a modern file-manager backup flow to a drive without an app that installs on them.

Method 3: through a computer (the reliable route)

If a PC or Mac is handy, this is the route to trust: plug the Intex phone into the computer with its USB cable, tap the USB notification and choose File transfer — and drag the DCIM folder onto the USB drive. This works on every covered model, with no Android-version question and no OTG question at all: the computer reads the phone as storage, and the drive plugs into the computer. It is also the only phone-to-drive route for the seven models below Android 7.0, which run Android 6.0 or older and cannot run SnapDisk at all, and for the three that list OTG No — the Lions 2, A4 and T1. Its only cost is the second device in between: if the phone and the drive are all you have, Method 1 or 2 is the route on the two, or Method 4 on any of the fourteen.

Method 4: the microSD card reader route (works on all fourteen)

All fourteen covered Intex models take a microSD card — up to 128 GB on the Lions 3, S1 and GenX, up to 64 GB on the Style III, S3, Lions 2, A4, T1, S7 and Y2, up to 32 GB on the S2, 4G+, Star II and Power HD — and the card-reader route needs no OTG, no computer connection to the phone, and no app: 1. Power the phone off and remove the microSD card. 2. Put the card in a USB card reader and plug the reader into a computer, or into a USB drive with a built-in card slot. 3. Copy the DCIM folder from the card to the drive. 4. Put the card back and power the phone on. This is the same route the old phone manuals describe — remove the memory card, place it in a card reader, and let the reader transfer the pictures — and it works identically for a phone card. Two warnings: if the card holds photos the phone has not yet written to it, or photos stored on the phone's internal storage rather than the card, those photos are not on the card — check where your photos live first, in the camera app's storage setting or in the file manager. And the ceilings are disputed on two models — the Lions 2's slot is up to 64 GB per gadgets360 and the Indian Express and up to 32 GB per smartprix and cashify, and the Star II's storage is two variants with different RAM — so check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 32 GB for the Lions 2. Unlike Karbonn, where the Hexa takes no card at all, every covered Intex phone takes one — so this route exists on all fourteen.

Method 5: Google Photos, Google Drive, or Google backup — not phone-to-USB plain files

Intex offers no backup tool to a USB drive at all — no Intex-specific backup advice turned up in any source beyond a computer copy, Google Drive, or Google Photos — so the stock options are Google's, and none of them writes your photos as plain files to a USB drive automatically. 1. Google Photos is Google's own photo backup app: it automatically backs up photos to your Google account. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and photos sync through Google Photos instead; and its "Storage saver" setting compresses photos (to a maximum of 16 megapixels, videos to 1080p). 2. Google Drive is the cloud backup Android names by default — it backs your data up to Google's cloud. It is a real backup — but it is a cloud backup, not a USB copy, and Google accounts only offer 5 GB of free storage by default since May 2026, with the full 15 GB available only after verifying a phone number on the account. 3. Google backup is what Android itself names: personal settings back up to Google's servers with a Google account, and are restored to a new phone when you sign in with that account. It is a real backup — but it backs up settings, not a folder of photo files on a drive you hold. None of them is a one-tap way to copy your camera roll to a drive as plain files. All of them work fine for what they are. Their limit, for photos specifically: none of them gives you a folder of plain photo files on a USB drive you can plug into any computer and open. If what you want is a copy of your photos you can open anywhere, Method 1, 2, 3 or 4 is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026.

What to avoid

  • Buying a USB-C OTG adapter for an Intex Aqua phone. All fourteen covered models take micro-USB — there is no USB-C model in this guide — so a USB-C adapter does not fit any of them. Buy a micro-USB OTG adapter, with the OTG wiring inside it.
  • Assuming the S7's or S2's OTG listing means a phone route exists on it. Gadgets360 lists OTG Yes on both — but both run Android 6.0, so SnapDisk cannot install on either, and the computer copy in Method 3 is the route on both.
  • Assuming the Power HD's planned Lollipop upgrade fixes the backup. Intex promised Android 5.0 for the Power HD, and phonebunch lists it as planned upgrade to 5.0 — but 5.0 is still below SnapDisk's 7.0 minimum, so the upgrade changes nothing about the backup.
  • Assuming the GenX's OTG listing is settled. Gadgets360, mobilegizmo, gadgetstouse and bmobile say OTG Yes and findpare says OTG No. Either way the GenX runs Android 5.1 and cannot run SnapDisk, so the dispute is not a reason to buy an adapter for it.
  • Assuming the Lions 2's 64 GB ceiling is settled. Gadgets360 and the Indian Express say up to 64 GB, smartprix and cashify say up to 32 GB. Check the card slot on your unit before buying a card larger than 32 GB for that model.
  • Assuming VaultDrive or FotoSafe runs on an Android 7.0 Intex. VaultDrive needs Android 8 or later per its own site, and FotoSafe needs Android 9 — so neither runs on any covered Intex phone, since the newest covered models run Android 7.0 exactly.
  • Mistaking a spec sheet or a product page for a phone-to-USB guide. The gadgets360 and gadgetsnow listings are real spec pages — but they only list an Android version, a processor, a camera, or a USB port type. None of them describes copying your camera roll straight to a USB drive from the device.
  • Mistaking a drive-locked app for a general backup. Photo Backup Stick DC is a real one-tap backup app — but it only works with genuine Photo Backup Stick OTG drives. If the drive you already own is any other brand, that app cannot use it: SnapDisk, Photos to USB, and FotoSafe work with any drive.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. Drag-and-drop through a Windows PC works — but it needs the PC in between. If the device and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 19, 2026 — and on the seven models below Android 7.0 and the three that list OTG No, that route does not exist, so the honest answer is that a computer is needed.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying. On an Intex phone the cloud routes are Google Photos and Google Drive — and Google Photos' "Storage saver" setting compresses photos, which is the one place quality actually gets lost.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the device — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Moving instead of copying. The file manager's Move option copies and then deletes the originals in one step. If the copy fails halfway, photos are already gone from the device. Copy first, verify, then delete.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.

Rule of thumb: if you have to pick the files yourself, it isn't a backup — it's a chore you will skip. Automate the copy, check it on the drive, then delete from the phone.
