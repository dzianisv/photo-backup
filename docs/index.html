How it works

Plug in a USB disk.

SnapDisk offers a backup in a notification: “External storage is connected. Backup photos?”

Tap Backup.

Photos and videos copy oldest-first, in the background, with a live progress notification — percent, size, speed, time left — and Pause / Stop.

Trust the counts.

Every file is size-verified after copying. Unplug mid-copy and it pauses, then resumes where it stopped. Already-copied files are never copied twice.

What you get

One tap

Disk connected → notification → Backup. No account, no cloud, no upload — everything stays on your devices.

Oldest first

Your earliest photos copy first, and the folder structure is kept (Camera, Screenshots, …).

Honest progress

The notification shows real percent, copied / total size, speed and time left. Pause and resume any time.

Proves it copied

Every file is size-verified after copying. A bad file is retried once, then skipped with its reason — it never stalls the whole backup.

Full errors, not one line

A file too big for FAT32 is skipped and listed, not a dead end. Errors show the full error with Copy.

Simple settings

Open the app and you get a settings screen only: a From folder and a To folder, each with a folder picker.

Questions

Is SnapDisk really free?

Yes. The free version backs up photos and videos manually, up to 5 GB per backup run, with no ads, no account, and no cloud. SnapDisk Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase that adds auto-backup when a disk is connected and unlimited backup size. There is no subscription.

Does SnapDisk upload my photos anywhere?

No. Copying runs entirely on your device, from your internal storage to the external disk you choose. The app has no internet permission, so your photos cannot leave your device.

How does a backup start?

Plug in a USB disk and SnapDisk offers a backup in a notification. Tap Backup and walk away — photos and videos copy oldest-first, in the background, with a live progress notification.

What happens if I unplug the disk mid-copy?

The backup pauses with the full error shown and resumes where it stopped when the disk is back. Files that already copied are never copied twice.
