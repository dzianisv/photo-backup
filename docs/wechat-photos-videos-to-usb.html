Where WeChat keeps your photos — and why the sources disagree

WeChat photos live in two different places, and the guides that name only one of them are the reason this is confusing. Here are both, plainly:

  • The saved copies — in your gallery. When you save a photo or video from a chat, WeChat writes a copy your gallery app can see, under a WeChat album. The phone's own help pages describe the pattern as Pictures/[app] for photos saved from a messaging app, and the saved copies show up among your most recent gallery items. This is the copy a backup can use — and the one this article is about.
  • WeChat's own storage — /sdcard/tencent/MicroMsg/[account]/. This is where WeChat keeps everything it received, in subfolders for images, videos, and voice files, plus a diskcache folder holding cache.data files — several JPEGs packed into single files with no extension. Those are not plain photos: no gallery app shows them, and no file copy of them gives you your photos back. Do not go digging here for a backup.

There is a third place people point at that does not work at all: the app's private folder, Android/data/com.tencent.mm. On Android 11 and later, scoped storage closes other apps out of Android/data — the system file picker cannot even grant a folder there, and a file manager shows it empty or refuses it. So no backup app and no file manager can read WeChat's private folder from the phone, and any guide that tells you to browse it is describing an Android that no longer exists. The reliable route is the saved copies in the gallery: save first, then back up the saved copies. If a photo never got saved — the thumbnail in the chat, never downloaded, or a Moments or Channels post with no save option — it is not on your phone as a file yet, and there is nothing to copy. Save it first, or it is not backed up.

What you need

An Android phone (Android 7.0 or later for SnapDisk); a USB drive your phone can read — a USB-C flash drive, or a regular USB drive with a USB-C OTG adapter (most modern Android phones support OTG; if the phone doesn't see the drive, try a different adapter); and enough space on the drive for the WeChat photos you want to keep. One format note: drives formatted FAT32 can't hold a single file over 4 GB. That only matters for very long videos — SnapDisk skips those files and lists them instead of failing the whole backup. A drive formatted exFAT has no such limit.

Step 0: save the WeChat photos to your gallery first

This is the step the other guides skip, and without it there is nothing to back up. WeChat keeps received photos inside the app until you save them. Two ways to save, both inside WeChat:

  1. One photo at a time. Open the chat, tap the photo so it opens full screen, then long-press it and choose the save option (worded "Save to phone" or similar — the wording changes between WeChat versions). The copy lands in your gallery under the WeChat album.
  2. Up to 9 at once. Open the chat, tap the icon in the top-right corner for the chat details, tap Search Chat History, then tap Media — the photos and videos from that chat appear together. Choose the select option, tick up to 9 photos or videos (WeChat sets a maximum of 9 per action), and choose the option to save them to your device's album. Repeat per chat.

Two things to know before you start saving. First, the first time you save, your phone asks whether WeChat may add items to your photos — if you said no earlier, the save fails without much warning, so check WeChat's Photos and videos permission in your phone's app settings first. Second, some things cannot be saved at all: other people's Moments and Channels posts give WeChat no save option, so those stay inside WeChat — a screenshot is the only copy you can take of those. Once the photos are in your gallery, they are ordinary files, and the backup below treats them like any other photo on your phone.

Method 2: by hand in the Files app (no app needed)

If you'd rather not install anything, Android's built-in Files app can copy the saved WeChat photos to the USB drive — after Step 0, exactly as for any other photo: 1. Plug in the USB drive. A notification or a "USB drive" entry should appear in the Files app under Storage devices. 2. Open Files, browse to Internal storage → Pictures, and find the WeChat folder (or the folder your phone used for the saved copies — check the gallery album name if Pictures has no WeChat folder). 3. Select the photos (or long-press the WeChat folder to select the whole thing), tap ⋮ → Copy to, then choose the USB drive as the destination. 4. Wait for the copy to finish — and don't unplug the drive until the Files app says it's done. This works fine for a one-off copy. Its limits are the reason SnapDisk exists: no progress detail beyond a spinner, no resume if you unplug mid-copy, no verification that every file actually arrived, and you have to repeat the whole manual selection next time WeChat photos pile up. For a WeChat album that grows every day, the app's oldest-first background copy is the calmer option.

What this does not back up

This is the part people get wrong, so here it is plainly. Copying the saved WeChat photos backs up the files — the photos and videos you saved to your gallery. It does not back up:

  • Chat history. Your messages live in WeChat's own database, not in a photo folder, and SnapDisk only copies image and video files. WeChat's own backup route is to a computer, through WeChat for Windows or Mac — unlike WhatsApp, it has no Google Drive chat backup, and no chat backup to a USB drive from the phone.
  • Voice messages. Voice messages are audio files (.opus/.amr), which SnapDisk does not copy.
  • Stickers kept inside WeChat. Stickers live in WeChat's own storage; they only copy if they sit inside the folder you picked, and only if they are image files.
  • Documents and files. PDFs and other files sent in WeChat are not image or video files, so they are skipped.
  • Photos you never saved. A photo that only ever lived inside a chat — never saved to the gallery — is not a file on your phone, so no backup of any kind can copy it. Save it first (Step 0), or it is not backed up.
  • Moments and Channels posts. Other people's posts give no save option in WeChat, so they stay inside the app. A screenshot is the only copy — and a screenshot is a photo like any other once it is in your gallery, so the default run picks it up.

If you want the whole WeChat — chats and media together — the computer backup is the route, and it is the only one WeChat itself supports. This article's route is for the photos and videos as files on a drive you hold.

What to avoid

  • Backing up before saving. The most common failure here is running a backup and finding no WeChat photos on the drive — because they were never saved to the gallery. The backup can only copy files that exist. Save first, then back up, then check the drive.
  • Digging in tencent/MicroMsg. The cache.data files there are not your photos — they are several JPEGs packed into single files with no extension. Copying them gives you nothing a gallery can open, and SnapDisk skips them for exactly that reason.
  • Chasing the Android/data folder. On Android 11 and later it is closed to other apps by design. A guide that tells you to browse Android/data/com.tencent.mm is describing an old Android, and the time spent fighting the permission prompt is time the save-and-backup route would have taken.
  • PC-only tools when you have no computer. The top search results for WeChat backup are Windows/Mac programs (Coolmuster and similar) that need a computer in between. If the phone and the drive are all you have, a phone-to-USB copy is the route — that is the gap this article fills, as of November 22, 2026.
  • Cloud backup dressed as USB backup. If the app needs an account and an upload before your photos are safe, that's a cloud subscription with a USB step. Your photos sit on someone else's server, and the backup stops working the day you stop paying.
  • Deleting before verifying. A backup is only a backup once you've confirmed the copy. Copy, open a few photos on the drive, then delete from the phone — never the other way round. SnapDisk only copies — it never deletes — so the delete step is always yours, and always after the check.
  • Assuming the chats came along. The photo files are not the conversation. If the chats matter, run WeChat's own computer backup too — the folder copy does not replace it.
  • Unplugging mid-copy. Pulling a USB drive out while files are still writing can corrupt the file in flight. Wait for the copy to finish, or pause it first — SnapDisk's Stop button exists for exactly this.

Rule of thumb: the photos are files in a folder only once you save them — save them to the gallery first, then back up the folder. The chats are a database — back them up in WeChat, to a computer. Do both, and check the copy on the drive before you delete anything from the phone.
